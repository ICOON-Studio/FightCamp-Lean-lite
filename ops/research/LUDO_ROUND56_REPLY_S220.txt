ROUND 56 (FightCamp, S220) — STATION WEAR MECHANICS & STATION CARD AUDIT
1. MECHANICS VERDICT: APPROVED WITH FIXES
Evaluation Against Real Boxing Gyms & Deep Management Sim Standards

The core design of having athlete-hours directly drive wear with tiered integrity bands affecting training efficiency multiplier and injury risk is mechanically solid and completely in line with Motorsport Manager (part wear per lap) and Two Point Hospital (machine degradation per use).

However, several specific wear rates and edge rules are mathematically uncalibrated for a 1-to-3 boxer roster training 20 to 30 hours a week across a standard 52-week operating calendar. Furthermore, leaving recovery/analysis at 0.0 creates a dead mechanic for late-tier apparatus.

========================================================================================================================
CURRENT VS. CALIBRATED WEAR RATES (PERCENTAGE POINTS PER ATHLETE-HOUR)
========================================================================================================================
STATION TYPE                  CURRENT   PROPOSED   HOURS TO 50% (WORN)  HOURS TO 20% (FAIL) REAL-WORLD ANALOGUE LIFESPAN
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Speed-bag swivel              0.40      0.45       111 hrs (~4-5 mos)   177 hrs (~7 mos)    High-friction mechanical bearing; grease dries, pins shear.
Slat treadmill                0.55      0.35       142 hrs (~5-6 mos)   228 hrs (~9 mos)    0.55 was far too fast. Slat belts outlast rubber belts.
Power rack / Squat stand      0.05      0.04       1250 hrs (~4-5 yrs)  2000 hrs (~8 yrs)   Heavy gauge structural steel; J-cups and safeties wear.
Grappling mats (Boxing Demo)  0.30      0.10       500 hrs (~2 yrs)     800 hrs (~3 yrs)    Boxing calisthenics/stretching only; zero friction throws.
Heavy bags (Leather / Foam)   0.25      0.20       250 hrs (~10 mos)    400 hrs (~16 mos)   D-rings, strap webbing & foam settling at the strike zone.
Other cardio (Air Bike)       0.60      0.40       125 hrs (~5 mos)     200 hrs (~8 mos)    Fan belt tension, crank bearings, pedal threads.
Strength (Barbell / Plates)   0.15      0.08       625 hrs (~2.5 yrs)   1000 hrs (~4 yrs)   Bushing spin, knurling flattening, sleeve collar drops.
Ring canvas + ropes           0.20      0.15       333 hrs (~13 mos)    533 hrs (~21 mos)   Canvas duck cloth tension, turnbuckle slack, rope wraps.
Recovery / Analysis items     0.00      0.06       833 hrs (~3 yrs)     1333 hrs (~5 yrs)   First-aid cabinet / ice-tub seal / sensor calibration wear.
========================================================================================================================

Justification for Calibrated Rates
Slat Treadmill (Reduced from 0.55 $\rightarrow$ 0.35):
Problem: At $0.55$ points/hr, a single boxer running 5 hours a week burns $2.75%$ integrity weekly. In just 18 weeks (4.5 months), a brand new $$4,500$ Woodway-style slat treadmill degrades into DAMAGED ($50%$), and hits FAILING ($20%$) in 29 weeks. Slat treadmills are selected by commercial gyms precisely because they use vulcanized rubber slats and ball bearings that outlast conventional belt-over-deck treadmills by $5\times$. At $0.35$, it requires ~142 hours of foot-pounding to hit $50%$, which matches realistic belt adjustments and lubrication cycles.
Heavy Bags (Reduced from 0.25 $\rightarrow$ 0.20):
Problem: At $0.25$, two fighters sharing a bag line degrade bags to WORN in 12 weeks. In a boxing gym, commercial heavy bags (Title, Cleto Reyes, Ringside) take continuous club abuse for 9 to 12 months before sand settling makes the bottom rock-hard or hanging D-rings saw through their straps. At $0.20$, 250 athlete-hours takes a bag to $50%$ integrity.
Grappling Mats in a Boxing-Only Demo (Reduced from 0.30 $\rightarrow$ 0.10):
Problem: $0.30$ represents aggressive wrestling and judo takedowns with abrasive canvas shoe friction and gi scraping. In our boxing sim, this mat is strictly utilized for abdominal core work, calisthenics, neck bridges, and mobility stretching. A rate of $0.10$ reflects foam compaction and vinyl seam wear without false MMA friction.
Ring Canvas + Ropes (Reduced from 0.20 $\rightarrow$ 0.15):
Problem: Ring canvas degradation occurs during sparring and ring craft work. At $0.15$, it hits $50%$ after 333 athlete-hours. For a gym running 6 hours of ring work a week, this requires ~55 weeks (roughly 1 game year), aligning with an annual canvas flip/replacement schedule common in regional boxing facilities.
Recovery / Analysis Items (Fixed from 0.00 $\rightarrow$ 0.06):
Problem: Zero wear makes these assets static "install and forget" stat boosters. A rate of $0.06$ per athlete-hour simulates consumable exhaustion (restocking ice, gauze, tape, sanitizing solutions, recalibrating scales/sensors).
Band Thresholds & Penalty Rules Calibration

The four integrity bands are conceptually clear, but the injury jump from $0.35%$ to $2.0%$ at $49%$ is too steep a cliff, and the identical efficiency multiplier ($x0.65$) between DAMAGED and FAILING removes tactical granularity.

========================================================================================================================
INTEGRITY BANDS & OPERATIONAL EFFECTS (CALIBRATED)
========================================================================================================================
BAND NAME   INTEGRITY RANGE  TRAINING GAIN  INJURY RISK / SESSION   BREAKDOWN RISK  SYSTEMIC STATUS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRISTINE    90% – 100%       x1.05          0.00%                   0.0%            Optimal condition; small bonus
GOOD        75% – 89%        x1.00          0.05%                   0.0%            Standard working order
WORN        45% – 74%        x0.85          0.40% (Minor blister/strain) 0.0%       Noticeable wear; training drag
DAMAGED     20% – 44%        x0.65          1.25% (Wrist/joint sprain)   0.0%       Severe degradation; audible squeaks
FAILING     01% – 19%        x0.40          3.50% (Tear / structural)   5.0% / session Breakdown risk; hazard
OUT OF USE  00%              x0.00          LOCKED (Cannot train)   100.0%          Station broken down; training halted
========================================================================================================================

The "Pristine" Band ($90\text{--}100%$): Adds an incentive for managers to keep top-tier equipment fresh by awarding a modest $+5%$ training gain bonus (x1.05).
The "Failing" Gain Multiplier ($x0.40$ vs $x0.65$): A bag with collapsed filling or a treadmill shuddering at $15%$ integrity severely impairs workout cadence. Lowering the training gain from $0.65$ to $0.40$ gives a real operational reason not to leave machines in the red.
Breakdown Resolution: When a 5% breakdown roll triggers on a FAILING station during an active training tick:
Training session terminates immediately for the athlete on that station.
Station integrity drops to $0%$ (OUT OF USE).
Athlete suffers a non-trivial trauma roll: $60%$ minor joint sprain ($7\text{ days}$ recovery), $35%$ moderate ligament strain ($21\text{ days}$ recovery), $5%$ severe tear ($45\text{ days}$ recovery).
Station can no longer be serviced via routine maintenance; it requires a mandatory Emergency Overhaul costing $1.5\times$ base service cost or a full REPLACE.
Service & Replace Economics Rule
SERVICE Mechanics:
Restores integrity by $+50$ percentage points, capped at a maximum ceiling of 95%.
Formula: $\text{New Integrity} = \min(95, \text{Current Integrity} + 50)$.
Why: Routine servicing does not make worn equipment factory-new; it tightens bolts, restuffs or flips bags, greases chains, and replaces tape. A heavily degraded station at $22%$ serviced once reaches $72%$ (WORN), requiring a second service or a full replacement to hit pristine levels.
REPLACE Mechanics:
Destroys existing asset and spawns a brand new unit: 100% Integrity.
Purchase price equals base catalogue cost ($1.0\times$).
Any accumulated fatigue or wear history on the station is purged.
2. CARD VERDICT: APPROVED WITH FIXES
Evaluation of the Identified Problems (a, b, c)
Problem (a) [OVERRULE THE 640px HEIGHT & OFF-GENRE TEXT]: Completely agree with Claude. A $640\text{ px}$ tall card on a $1080\text{ p}$ canvas consumes almost $60%$ of the screen height, blocking crucial contextual viewports of adjacent stations and fighters. A 6-line prose block for YOUR FIT is visual fluff in a high-density management sim.
Problem (b) [OVERRULE FLOATING X TAB]: Completely agree. Floating close buttons detached from the parent modal look like web-browser popups or disconnected collision bounds. The X close trigger belongs inside the card's header bar, aligned to the top-right corner.
Problem (c) [OVERRULE ACTIVE SERVICE AT 95%]: Completely agree. Offering a $$220$ action that produces zero numerical change ($95% \rightarrow 95%$) is a player-hostile money trap that invites misclicks.
