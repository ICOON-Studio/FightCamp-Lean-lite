1. Data Source, Licensing & Precedent
Geographic Dataset: Natural Earth (Vector & Raster).
License: Public Domain (100% free for commercial use, zero attribution required, legally bulletproof for Steam commercial distribution).
Assets Needed:
Land/Ocean Vector Mask (for vector boundary lines).
4K Albedo Relief Map (stylized, desaturated sepia/slate to match our leatherette palette).
Country ID Pick Map (16-bit integer texture where each country’s landmass is filled with a unique flat RGB/Hex index matching its ISO-3166 numeric code).
2. Technical Implementation Architecture (Godot 4.7)

Do not import 195 separate 3D country boundary meshes. That is a performance and collision nightmare. Instead, use the UV-Projection Shader Technique:

[Sphere Mesh (1,440 tris)] 
    └─ Material: Standard Material 3D with Custom Shader
          ├─ Albedo Texture: Stylized Earth (Sepia/Dark Slate #1A1C20 & Land #2B2F36)
          ├─ Highlight Shader Parameter: Selected Country ISO ID
          ├─ Atmosphere Rim Shader: Soft Amber glow (#E5B842, Power 2.4, Rim 0.25)
          └─ Mouse Raycast -> UV Coordinates -> ID Texture Reader -> Trigger Country Select

Geometry: A single UV-Sphere primitive (MeshInstance3D), radius $2.2\text{ m}$, subdivided to 64 rings $\times$ 64 segments (4,096 triangles total).
Interactive Picking (Raycast to Country):
The camera casts an unprojected ray from mouse screen coordinates onto the Sphere collider.
get_collision_point() computes the intersection point in local sphere coordinates.
A 3-line mathematical transformation converts the 3D unit normal $(X, Y, Z)$ to Equirectangular UV coordinates: $$\text{longitude} = \text{atan2}(X, Z), \quad \text{latitude} = \text{asin}(Y)$$ $$U = 0.5 + \frac{\text{longitude}}{2\pi}, \quad V = 0.5 - \frac{\text{latitude}}{\pi}$$
The engine samples the 1-pixel color at $(U, V)$ from an in-memory Image of the pre-baked 2K Country ID Index map.
The sampled ID immediately triggers the UI state update: highlights the country border via shader uniform uniform int selected_country_id, and pulls the country's simulation facts onto the Right Inspector Panel.
Camera & Interaction Controls:
Left-Click + Drag: Rotates the sphere smoothly (Euler yaw and pitch with spherical quaternion slerp damping, inertia decay factor 0.92).
Mouse Wheel: Zooms camera field of view between $35^\circ$ and $60^\circ$ (clamps radius; keeps the globe tactile and responsive).
Search / Quick Select Box: An autocomplete dropdown at the top-left allows keyboard navigation ([Search Country...]). Typing "Japan" smoothly interpolates the globe's rotation quaternion to Japan's geographic center $(\text{lat } 36.2, \text{lon } 138.2)$ over $450\text{ ms}$ using a Cubic ease-out transition.
3. Visual Styling (Fighting Maria's Art Bible)
Ocean: Deep dark charcoal tone #141619 with a subtle grid of latitude/longitude lines in #22262C ($15^\circ$ intervals).
Landmasses: Warm dark stone #2A2B30. Unselected countries have crisp 1px borders in #3A3E48.
Selected Country: Filled with warm brass gold #E5B842 at 40% emission, bordered with a 2px glowing gold line (#FFD700).
Pin Marker: A physical chunky 3D brass needle with an illuminated ruby head (#C62F2A) planted perpendicular to the sphere surface at the capital city coordinates.
Atmosphere: An inverted-hull Fresnel rim shader that casts a gentle warm amber atmospheric glow (#E5B842) around the limb of the sphere, seamlessly tying into the 3D boxing gym backdrop behind it.
4. Performance & Prefab Check
Godot Asset Library Options:
Godot-Globe-3D (MIT): Available on GitHub. Provides basic lat/lon conversion, but relies on unoptimized multi-mesh boundaries.
Recommended Build Choice: Build custom in-house (Shader + UV sampler). It is less than 120 lines of GDScript and 40 lines of Godot Shading Language.
Performance Budget:
Draw Calls: Exactly 2 draw calls (1 for Globe Sphere, 1 for Capital Marker Pins MultiMeshInstance3D).
VRAM Overhead: $< 16\text{ MB}$ total for the three 2K textures.
Frame Time: $< 0.12\text{ ms}$ on GTX 1060 / Steam Deck.
5. Honest Cost & Verdict
Is it worth it? YES, 100%. Country selection is the literal "first 60 seconds" of a player’s franchise journey. A spinning tactile globe immediately tells the player: "This is a global tactical boxing empire sim, not an indie spreadsheet."
Cheapest Shiplike Version: Build the single sphere with UV texture sampling + camera lerp. Do not build country elevation displacement or realistic water reflection. Total implementation cost: 3.5 days engineering, 1 day technical art.
PART B: THE CALENDAR STRIP — ARCHITECTURAL RULING
1. The Verdict: KILL THE 12-DAY STRIP IN THE TOP TIME BAR

Look closely at Screenshot 2 (Bottom):

[ $10,530 ]  [ ◀ || ▶ ▶▶ ]  [ Mon 9 Feb ]  [ Mo 9 | Tu 10 | We 11 | ... | Fr 20 ] (🔔)  [ NO BOUT BOOKED ]


The 12-day micro-strip (Mo 9 ... Fr 20) sitting between the date and the bell is an ergonomics failure. It must be REMOVED from the top time bar immediately.

Why the Mini-Strip Fails Inside the Top Bar:
Severe Information Jamming: Cramming 12 date boxes into the center of a fixed $1920\text{ px}$ bar leaves each day only $22\text{ px}$ wide. It is impossible to show event types (sparring, weigh-in, bout, contract expiry, travel, medical suspension) with legible icons.
Destroys Responsive Scaling (1280×720 Breakpoint): At $1280\text{ px}$ (Steam Deck native), the Top Bar suffers a catastrophic overlap: the 12-day strip smashes directly into the financial ledger pill on the left and the Bout Status card on the right.
Temporal Confusion (Clock vs. Schedule): The Top Bar is a real-time execution engine (Pause, Play, Fast Forward, Current Hour, Current Day Ticker). A 12-day calendar strip represents future planning. Mixing immediate execution controls with a 2-week scheduling timeline creates cognitive friction.
2. Where the Calendar Actually Lives: THE DEDICATED SLIDE-OUT DRAWER

The calendar does not disappear from the game—it moves to its proper home: The Right-Edge Calendar Tab (visible on the right edge of Screenshot 2).

Clicking the Right Calendar Tab (or pressing hotkey C) smoothly slides out the Full 4-Week Tactical Campaign Drawer ($480\text{ px}$ wide):
Full Height Days: Every day has an $80\text{ px} \times 96\text{ px}$ tile.
Clear Multi-Icon Event Stacks: Shows exact icons:
🥊 Bout Day: Crimson #C62F2A banner with opponent headshot and weight division.
⚖️ Weigh-In: Brass Gold #E5B842 scale icon with target weight limit.
🩺 Medical Clearance / Suspension: White #ECEBE6 cross on red badge.
✈️ Travel / Camp Relocation: Slate Blue #2A5F9E directional arrow.
💰 Payroll / Upkeep Due: Amber coin badge #D98039.
Interactive Scheduling: Clicking any future day opens the day's training itinerary and allows setting camp rest protocols.
3. The New Top Bar Layout Specification (Clean, Robust, Uncrowded)

By removing the 12-day strip, the Top Bar becomes clean, perfectly proportioned, and responsive from $1280\text{ px}$ to $4\text{K}$.

Defect 1: The Crammed Top Bar Overlap
The Error: The 12-day calendar strip occupies the exact horizontal screen territory needed for legible time display and bout countdowns. Text is running into borders.
The Fix: Delete the 12-day strip from the Top Bar as specified in Part B. Anchor Cluster 1 Left, Cluster 2 Center, and Cluster 3 Right with dedicated minimum spacing ($48\text{ px}$ buffer).
Defect 2: Raw Debug Text "DEV JUMP 303" Floating in 3D Space
The Error: A massive bright white monospace debug string (DEV JUMP 303) is rendered directly over the canvas above the boxing ring, destroying spatial depth and production polish.
The Fix: Encapsulate all debug identifiers into a single collapsible developer console (F12), completely hidden in standard production builds (#if DEBUG compilation flag).
Defect 3: Orphaned Bottom-Left Floating Objective
The Error: Text reading "Stamina above 50 at the bell" with an icon floats arbitrarily at $X = 30\text{ px}, Y = 840\text{ px}$, detached from any UI card or fighter profile. The player cannot tell which fighter this objective applies to.
The Fix: Attach objective pills directly to the active fighter’s floor nameplate billboard, or pin them cleanly inside the left-hand Active Session Drawer under the fighter's face thumbnail.
Defect 4: Bottom News Ticker Truncation & Overlap
The Error: The scrolling league news wire at the screen bottom (Alejandro Tanaka defeated Hans Vargas...) is trapped inside an awkward cut-out panel with an unfinished dashed border, overlapping the bottom viewport margin.
The Fix: Convert the bottom ticker into a streamlined, edge-to-edge $32\text{ px}$ translucent dark leatherette bar #1E1713. News events scroll cleanly from right to left with bold yellow fighter names (#E5B842) and crisp result chips (KO R4, DEC 3-0).
Defect 5: Unlabeled Left-Hand Vertical Navigation Strip
The Error: A stack of 6 tiny, unpadded white line icons sits flush against the left screen boundary ($X = 12\text{ px}$). They lack visual hierarchy, active state indicators, or keyboard shortcut hints.
The Fix: Place icons on rounded dark square tiles ($42\text{ px} \times 42\text{ px}$) with $8\text{ px}$ separation. Add clear Godot tooltip bindings showing Section Name and Quick Hotkey (e.g., Roster [F1], Training Floor [F2], Staff [F3], Finances [F4], Schedule [F5]).
Defect 6: Unmasked Black Void Outside Gym Perimeter Walls
The Error: The camera frustum renders raw black void outside the north and west perimeter walls of the gym, breaking the physical illusion of a real urban boxing club building.
The Fix: Implement a static low-poly urban exterior vista (adjacent brick warehouse walls, fire escapes, alleyway asphalt, and subtle city silhouette) outside the windows and wall tops, blocking the black engine clear-color completely.
Defect 7: Detached "GUIDE -> Hire a coach" Bottom Button
The Error: At the bottom-right corner, an isolated yellow pill button (GUIDE -> Hire a coach) floats without contextual grounding or completion tracking.
The Fix: Integrate onboarding and tutorial objectives into a unified Club Directive Widget in the top-right corner beneath the Bout Card, featuring a clear step progress indicator (OBJECTIVE 1/4: HIRE A COACH [VIEW CANDIDATES]).
