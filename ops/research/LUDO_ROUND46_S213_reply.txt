SECTION A: AUDIT & SYSTEM REVIEW
1. What Works
Tree Structure: The 54-node technique tree with explicit prerequisite branching (stance_guard $\rightarrow$ catch $\rightarrow$ block_elbow $\rightarrow$ long_guard) mirrors classical combat sport pedagogy.
Derived Fight IQ: Calculating fight_iq from fight volume, discipline depth, and repertoire width prevents power-levelling it on a treadmill.
Motor-Sequence Consolidation: Sleep-based micro-breakthroughs reflect actual motor-skill learning mechanics.
Physical Wear & Region Model: The six-region model (head_neck, torso_core, left_arm, right_arm, left_leg, right_leg) provides the necessary anatomical constraints for deep training management.

The Five-Silo Disconnection:
The current architecture tracks Stats, Technique Unlock Progress, Station Drill Pips, Combo Tightness, and Move-Card Minutes as separate registries that never talk to each other.
In the live fight engine, discipline_proficiency and combo_tightness are ignored. As a result, 60% of player inputs are completely discarded at the ring ropes.
The "Binary Cliff" Flaw:
A technique is either Locked or Learned. Once learned, a move behaves identically in its first live round as it does after five hundred rounds.
Real combat sports do not work this way. Unconscious competence requires progressing from controlled rehearsal to chaotic, unscripted combat.
The Tenth-of-a-Point Fallacy:
Presenting DEFENCE 54.0 -> 54.4 provides zero visceral feedback to the player. In Football Manager, a change in defensive positioning manifests as a fullback visibly tracking an inside run; in Motorsport Manager, a setup change alters high-speed turn entry speed.
Showing fractional stat growth without visible tactical consequences on the canvas breaks the core gameplay loop.
Tiers 1–2 Auto-Granted to All Fighters:
Granting all Tier 1 and Tier 2 moves by default strips the game of its early-career identity. An amateur with four bouts shouldn't slip-counter like a world champion just because the move was unlocked out of the box.

Concrete Mechanics Across Key Archetypes
1. Straight Punches & Combinations

Example: boxing_one_two moving from Stage 1 to Stage 4.

Stage 1: The fighter pauses briefly between the jab and cross ($220\text{ms}$ reset). If the opponent slips the jab, the cross is often canceled.
Stage 4: The rear foot drives forward the instant the jab lands or touches guard. The reset window drops to $75\text{ms}$. Stamina cost decreases by 20%, and the punch combination reliably tracks moving targets without breaking form.
2. Defensive Slipping & Counters

Example: boxing_slip and boxing_slip_counter.

Stage 1: The fighter only attempts slips when stamina is above 80%. When tired, they fall back on a basic arm block, taking chip damage and guard wear. Counter window is narrow ($75\text{ms}$).
Stage 4: The fighter reads the opponent's shoulder dip automatically. Slips are executed smoothly even below 30% stamina. The counter window opens to $230\text{ms}$, allowing the fighter to plant and drive through with boxing_cross before the opponent can recover their guard.
3. Footwork & Ring Control

Example: boxing_pivot_footwork and boxing_cutoff_ring.

Stage 1: The fighter resets directly backward into straight lines, backing themselves toward the ropes when pressured.
Stage 4: When cornered, the fighter steps off the centerline at an angle, sliding along the ropes while keeping the opponent trapped in the center.

Turning Defeats into Progression Assets
Unlocking High-Yield Drills from Defeat:
When a fighter is stopped by a specific tactic (e.g., absorbing 6 clean boxing_overhand strikes), the post-fight debrief reveals an actionable vulnerability: "STRUCTURAL DEFICIENCY DETECTED: Opponent exploited lazy return on boxing_cross."
This unlocks an accelerated learning focus for the next 21 days: +40% XP toward boxing_shoulder_roll or boxing_slip when working with the head coach.
Film Study Mechanics:
Dedicating half a day on Monday to film review awards no physical conditioning, but grants two key benefits:
It preserves physical condition while reducing wear on recovering body parts.
It converts fight data into +1.2 to +2.5 derived fight_iq, reinforcing defensive reads against that opponent's archetype in future matchups.

1. Scouting & Camp Preparation
Eight weeks out from a bout, the player scouts the opponent's tendencies (e.g., Julian "The Viper" Cruz: 72% right-hand output, vulnerable to inside body hooks).
The player builds a focused camp plan:
Hire a specific sparring partner who throws heavy looping rights.
Focus pad drills on boxing_slip_counter and boxing_body_hook.
Every training decision is aimed directly at countering the opponent's stylistic habits on fight night.
2. Plateaus and Coaching Synergies
The Biological Ceiling: When a fighter's attribute reaches within 5 points of its ceiling, progression slows down: $$\text{Effective Gain} = \text{BaseGain} \times \left(\frac{\text{Potential} - \text{Current}}{5}\right)^2$$
Breaking Plateaus with Staff Specialisation:
Breaking through an attribute plateau requires targeted coaching expertise:
An elite trainer ($>!75$ rating) with a matching tactical philosophy adds a Breakthrough Multiplier (1.5x–2.2x) to plateaued skills.
Sparring against styles outside the fighter's comfort zone helps unlock advanced nodes like boxing_drawing and boxing_counter_combination.
3. The Fatigue vs. Readiness Trade-Off
Pushing a fighter to train through heavy fatigue ($>!75$) yields diminishing returns:
Skill progression drops by $60%$.
Soft-tissue wear rates double.
If a fighter enters fight night with residual fatigue, their in-ring speed and stamina drain rates suffer severe penalties.
Players must carefully manage the trade-off: train hard enough to sharpen skills, but taper in time to enter the ring completely fresh.

1. Skill Acquisition Curves: The Logarithmic Reality

Skill acquisition is governed by a logarithmic progression curve:
$$\text{Progress} = K \cdot \ln(1 + \beta \cdot \text{Reps})$$

Stage 1 (Theoretical): Takes 1 to 2 weeks of dedicated padwork (300–600 reps). Fast initial progress.
Stage 2 (Drilled): Takes 3 to 6 weeks of partner drills and heavy bag work (1,500–2,500 reps).
Stage 3 (Sparring-Hardened): Requires 20–35 live sparring rounds over 2 to 3 months. Moves only stick after surviving real ring pressure.
Stage 4 (Signature Engine): Takes 1 to 2 full years of active professional competition, demanding 40+ clean landings under pressure.
2. Physical Detraining & Skill Rust
If an athlete halts training entirely (due to injury or neglect), skills and conditioning degrade steadily:
Weeks 1–3: Physical sharpness and attributes hold steady.
Weeks 4+: conditioning decays at $-0.6$ points per week.
Technique Rust: Skills do not drop entire stages, but inactive Stage 3/4 moves temporarily fall back to Stage 2 execution speed until the fighter logs 3 tune-up rounds on the pads.
3. Age-Related Athletic Decline
Ages 18–23: Fast recovery and rapid physical gains; lower baseline composure and fight_iq.
Ages 24–29: Prime athletic window: balanced power, speed, and recovery.
Ages 30–34: Pure physical attributes (speed, conditioning) slowly decline by $-0.4$ points per year, while mental mastery (fight_iq, technique) reaches its peak.
Ages 35+: Physical decay accelerates ($-1.2$ to $-2.0$ points per year for speed). To remain competitive, veteran fighters must lean heavily on efficient Stage 4 counters, defensive positioning, and energy conservation.

SUMMARY OF THE DESIGN TRANSFORMATION
BEFORE:
- 5 disconnected tracking systems running in parallel.
- Stats change invisibly by +0.1 without clear in-ring impact.
- Default Tier 1/2 techniques perform identically from day one to retirement.
- Post-fight review provides no meaningful training insights.

AFTER:
- 1 unified technique mastery pipeline (Stages 1 through 4).
- High-mastery techniques unlock faster reactions, tighter execution, and lower stamina costs.
- Fighters struggle with new moves early on, mastering them through real sparring and ring experience.
- Post-fight telemetry highlights structural flaws, feeding directly into the next week's training camp.


This design turns skill training into an authentic, deeply readable management loop. Every hour spent drilling on the pads directly shapes the action inside the ring, delivering the long-term planning, clear consequences, and strategic depth expected of a premier sports management simulation.
