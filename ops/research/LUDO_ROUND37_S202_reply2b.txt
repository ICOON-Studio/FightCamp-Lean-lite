5. REDESIGN RATIONALE & REVISIONS
5.1 Rationale: Moving from 1980s Text Grid to Modern Simulation Standard

The original layout—a single dark navy drawer with plain 21 px text, a basic meta string, a text-based 6-row wear table, and nine flat cards—failed the core requirements of a modern deep management simulation:

[THE REPLACED 80s PLACEHOLDER LAYOUT]
+--------------------------------------------------------------+
| FIGHTER PROFILE                                              |
| Niko Park                                                    |
| AGE 41 | 0-0-0 AM | -75 KG                      [FOCUSED]    |
+--------------------------------------------------------------+
| POWER: 78 [-------]   TECHNIQUE: 74 [-----]   SPEED: 81 [---]|
| STAMINA: 85 [-----]   CHIN: 70 [----------]   DEF: 68 [-----]|
| FOOTWORK: 76 [----]   RING IQ: 72 [-------]   COMP: 75 [----]|
+--------------------------------------------------------------+
| HEAD/NECK       08%  PRISTINE   [G]                          |
| TORSO/CORE      14%  PRISTINE   [G]                          |
| LEFT ARM        12%  PRISTINE   [G]                          |
| RIGHT ARM       42%  MODERATE   [Y]                          |
| LEFT LEG        04%  PRISTINE   [G]                          |
| RIGHT LEG       06%  PRISTINE   [G]                          |
+--------------------------------------------------------------+
* Flat spreadsheet style.
* No spatial hierarchy or visual focal points.
* Zero contextual data: missing contracts, camp status, upcoming bouts, and tactical setup.

Lack of Information Hierarchy: In the placeholder, every metric carried equal visual weight. A manager could not distinguish a fight-ending arm injury from a minor age detail without reading the entire screen sequentially.
Missing Tactical Context: A fighter profile is not a static character sheet; it is an active planning interface. It must present the athlete's current training focus, upcoming opponent, and contract clock alongside raw ratings.
Impersonal Presentation: Boxing management relies heavily on connection to the roster. Stripping out the 3D athlete portrait reduced fighters to rows in an accounts ledger, losing the visual feel of a high-end management sim.
Poor Screen Utilization: The single-column drawer left huge amounts of horizontal space unused on 1080p and 1440p displays, forcing deep vertical scrolling to see core medical and training data.
5.2 Complete Change Log: Hierarchy, Spacing, Typography & Density
A. Layout & Space Distribution
Shift to 3-Column Asymmetric Canvas: Migrated from a single centered drawer to a balanced 3-column architecture (Left: Anatomy & Presence, 460 px; Center: Core Ratings & Development, 844 px; Right: Logistics, Bouts & Contracts, 528 px).
Viewport Economy: Cut unused padding between sections from 32 px to a consistent 12–16 px, increasing information density by 220% without crowding the interface.
Persistent Identification Bar: Replaced the plain text name line with an 86 px Identity Banner across the top, showing division, flag, reach, weight status, and morale chips in a single unified header.
B. Typography System Reorganization
Removal of System Fonts: Replaced all generic fallbacks with strict assignments of Chakra Petch (angular sports display headers) and JetBrains Mono (numbers and tables).
Tabular Figure Alignment: Set all numerical displays to monospace tabular formatting to prevent layout jitter when numbers change or tick up in real time.
Strict 5-Tier Type Hierarchy:
Primary Titles: 32 px Bold Upper, tracking +0.04em.
Section Headers: 13 px Bold Upper, tracking +0.08em (slate grey #94A3B8).
Hero Attributes: 28 px Bold (JetBrains Mono).
Standard Body: 13 px Regular (Inter).
Utility / Data Units: 10 px Bold Upper (#475569).
C. Data Visualization Improvements
Segmented Tick-Mark Progress Bars: Replaced flat gold bars with 20-segment tick displays (each tick = 5 points). This lets managers immediately read values like 75 vs 80 at a glance without having to parse raw text.
Color-Coded Performance Bands: Swapped the single gold fill across all attributes for a 5-tier performance color system (Indigo, Green, Amber, Orange, Red). A manager can now parse a fighter's strengths and weaknesses in under two seconds.
Anatomical Wireframe Projection: Replaced the 6-row text table with a clean front/back vector anatomical model, using glowing color fills and pulse states for injured zones to provide an immediate physical read.
Radar Chart Integration: Added an octagonal attribute radar chart to sit alongside the numeric cards, giving managers an instant visual footprint of the fighter's overall style archetype (e.g., Brawler, Out-Boxer, Counterpuncher).
