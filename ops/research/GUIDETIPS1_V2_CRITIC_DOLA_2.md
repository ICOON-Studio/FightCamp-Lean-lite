4.1 The week and training plan - BUILT
Choice: for each fighter a week of seven mornings (a session each: roadwork, intervals, heavy bag, weights, speed bag, slip rope, shadow, neck work, or rest;
each at Light, Normal or Hard) and seven evenings (Pads, Sparring, Rest, Film). The coach writes the week from the camp phase and repeats it; the player edits
exceptions (click a day, a popover). "Back to the coach's week" resets. Diet: Hold or Cut 500 kcal a day.
Phases counted back from a booked bout: BASE (no bout), BUILD (3+ weeks out), PEAK (2 weeks out, hardest, sparring twice), TAPER (fight week: light, three rest
days, no sparring).
Changes: each morning adds a small amount to one stat (pace 1.0 per focused week x coach factor x headroom clamp((potential-stat)/25, 0.10, 1.25), a morning
is a fifth of it, Light x0.6, Hard x1.3, less when tired); kcal and weight; fatigue (0-100, "Week tiredness"; from 80 she is overreached: gains x0.6 and
injury risk x3). The Week tab shows plan totals, the goal line, the coach's one change with a "Do it" button, and the weeks to fight night.
Coach factor: 0.5 + rating x 0.7 per cent; no coach = 60%. Strength coach head-coach edge: body mornings +10%, craft mornings -10%.
Twist card: once a week the coach draws one (sore wrist, rain, weights room shut, partner ill, head cold, visiting pro, quiet week; or a choice card such as
tight knee, scale will not move, night off, heating breaks, combo clicking, southpaw in town, old coach visits, sponsor photoshoot, rival talks trash). Two
answers each with a visible price; unanswered = the coach's pick on Monday. Planned vs actual is shown on the review.
Training load: acute vs usual load; a spike (ratio 1.5+) raises injury risk 2-4x.
Two fighters in camp competing for the coach's pad nights: AGREED, NOT BUILT (see 4.2).
4.2 Pads, combinations and mastery - BUILT (pad nights for two fighters AGREED, NOT BUILT)
Evenings are nights that carry drill cards. A pad night is 75 minutes split over its cards; a sparring night gives every card already in her hands a share.
Standard week: 3 pad nights; camp 2+1 / 2+2 / 3+2 pad+spar for Light/Standard/Hard.
Choice: drag a drill card (a move or combination) onto a pad night; split a night 50/50, 70/30, 30/70; remove or clear; pin cards in the tray; "+ drill it".
Mastery gain per hour of drilling: pads 2.1, heavy bag 1.05, shadow 0.85, sparring 1.5, speed bag 0.9, double-end bag 1.2. Undrilled moves fade slowly.
A drilled combination also holds its punches. Tiers: New, Raw (under 30), Usable (30), Sharp (60), Signature (85). A move at 30+ is a weapon.
Today a planned line fires every time once it is usable; sharp is worth about 1 win in 100 over usable. In the game but SWITCHED OFF (S427,
measured on 400 bouts a cell): a planned line fires 1 use in 2 at usable, 3 in 4 at sharp, always at Signature (sharp then +2.8 wins in 100
over usable, Signature +2.7 over sharp). BUILT for professional bouts (S458-S462, ProRead): the opponent's corner studies your boxer's
last two professional fights and waits for the punch thrown most; two real weapons or a feint line blunt it. Amateur bouts: not read across bouts.
A combination in a fight costs energy (longer costs more); a flurry is limited by hand speed and tiredness; a signature (drilling, not length) may run one
punch past the speed cap. Own combinations built in the Playbook (Combos tab) get their own pad mastery.
AGREED, NOT BUILT: with two fighters in camp the coach's pad nights are shared; the top row of "Learn on the pads" shows how many go to each (cost to the
other fighter), only while two are in camp; a needs flag says when the second camp starts.

4.7 The game plan (Playbook) - BUILT

4.8 Fight night - BUILT
A bout is 3 rounds of 3 minutes (amateur), 4 (pro debut), more later. A round is six 30-second exchanges, each settled by dice on an edge (training, plan, corner call,
pressure, tiredness). The opponent fights one way at a time (Pressing, Boxing at range, Waiting to counter, Loading up, Tiring, Hurt), shown as a clue.
Every 30 seconds the player may shout one of the 3 calls that fit, or say nothing: Jab and move, Go to the body, Draw the lead, Hands up move your head, Let your
hands go, Breathe pick your shots, Tie up, Go for the finish. Each has a best / ok / wrong match to the opponent's mode, needs stats, costs or gives energy; only
two instructions per minute get through fully. Pace (Push / Steady / Save) and Aim (Own game / Head / Body / ribs / the hurt head) persist from shout to shout.
A set-up "NOW!" fires a learned combination into a gap the opponent opens: big edge, or a cost if wrong.
Corner minute between rounds: the corner card (what worked, what hurt, the call), the plan call, ONE CHANGE for the next round (a habit fix, or Elbows in: half the body punches go to the head, saving their energy and adding head damage, or Gloves high: the reverse; moved punches change where she is hit and what it costs, measured not to change who wins), RECOVER with 3 points (water, ice on the ribs, clear
her head when hurt), SET-UP (a combination x a gap), TALK (calm / fire up / the truth), change one plan arrow, cutman job (close the cut, enswell an eye, nothing).
Trust: a wrong call she followed costs trust; a Low fighter ignores shouting.
Knockdowns and counts come from power and damage vs chin, heart for the count; amateur RSC at 3 counts in a round or 4 in a bout. Injury roll after the bout.

0. The short answer
The games praised for tooltips answer four questions in a small card: what is this, why is it this value now, what moves
it, what can I do. The games hated for tooltips repeat the label, pile numbers into one long line, or hide the thing you
needed.
The guides players praise let you play first and speak only when the thing is in front of you (Civilization V, RimWorld's
learning helper, Plants vs. Zombies, Portal). A scripted start is praised when it is short and blended into play (Two Point
Hospital, Against the Storm); it is hated when it is long, takes the mouse away and cannot be left (Motorsport Manager's
first race, Football Manager's "barrage").
We already own most of the machine. scripts/ui/Tips.gd (S359, from study J90) is an index card that sits beside the
control, wraps at 330 px, locks so the pointer can travel onto it, and opens a sticky note for a game word
(scripts/rules/Glossary.gd, 10 words). Only 8 files use it. The other 268 tooltips are plain one-line strings written one
by one: of the 146 that are fixed text, 75 are five words or fewer. No rule said what a tooltip must contain and no test
checked one. That is why three earlier studies (J52, J90, K84) changed so little on screen.
So this standard is mostly a CONTENT rule plus a TEST that fails the build, on the card we have, and a guide that waits.

2. THE TOOLTIP STANDARD
2a. Four kinds, and only four
Kind	For	Built with
SHORT card	a plain value: a fixed price, a count, a date, a name of a thing. Nothing the player does this week moves it and it has no bands	the same card, a title and one or two lines
FULL card	a value the player's choices move, or one with bands, causes or a decision behind it: tiredness, mastery, loyalty, a coach's real cost, weight, load, an offer	Tips.gd card through one helper (section 5)
BUTTON line	a control whose label already says what it is	the same card, one line
MOVE card	every move, step and combination name, anywhere	MoveTip.gd (exists: plays the move on a loop on her own body)
2b. The SHORT card
A title and WHAT, plus one more line only if there is something to do. Two lines at most, 20 words at most.
Signing fee $1,000
Paid once, the day you hire this coach.
Which card a thing gets is decided by the question "can the player move this, or does it have a line he must not cross?"
Yes = FULL. No = SHORT. About half the numbers on screen are SHORT.
2b-2. The FULL card answers four questions, in this order
Line	Answers	Rule
WHAT	what is this, or what does this money buy	one sentence, 14 words or fewer, no number unless the number IS the thing
NOW	why it has this value today	the value and its causes. Two or more causes = rows (label, amount, + or - in colour), 4 rows at most, with the total last
MOVES IT	what makes it go up and down	one sentence, 14 words or fewer
YOUR MOVE	what the player can do about it, here, now	starts with a verb; names the page or the button. Shown in the title colour
A card may drop NOW (a thing with no value) or MOVES IT (a fixed price). It may never drop WHAT.
If nothing can be written for YOUR MOVE, the thing is information the player cannot act on: by the owner's S398 rule ("unless
the player can really do something about it") it is cut from the screen, or the line says honestly "Nothing to do: it clears by
itself in 3 days".
A game word in a card (Tiredness, Mastery, Usable, Camp, Fight week, Reputation, Dues, Runway, the towel line...) is underlined;
resting on it opens its sticky note. One note per word, the same note everywhere, from Glossary.gd.
Guardrails on game words: only words this game gives its own meaning (never "week", "coach", "money"); at most TWO underlined
words on a card; never the card's own title word; a note is plain text with nothing to hover, so there are exactly two levels
(Old World's warning). The test checks all four.
The lines carry no printed labels ("WHAT:", "NOW:"): the order and the look say it (plain, rows, quiet, title colour).
2c. Size
Text column 330 px on a 1920 screen (the card is about 360 px, under a fifth of the screen). Never wider. Every line wraps.
45 words is the ceiling on a FULL card, not counting rows, not a target: shorter is better. One number per sentence; three
numbers or more go into rows.
The card sits beside the control, never over it or under the pointer (Tips.gd does this today).
Opens after 0.3 s (today's setting); sweeping along a row of tiles swaps cards at once.
2d. The BUTTON line
One sentence, 6 words or more, that says what happens when you click, and what it costs if it costs something:
"Loads this combination back into the builder to change it." A greyed button says why and what would unlock it.
"Click: 50/50 -> 70/30 -> 30/70" becomes "Changes how the round is split between attack and defence. Now 50/50."
2e. A tooltip must NEVER
repeat its label or the words printed beside it (a test compares them);
run as one long line, or wider than 330 px;
pile three or more numbers into a sentence;
name a person - "your boxer", "the opponent", "your coach" (owner S448);
use a word the game has not taught - code names, short forms, "x1.3" (owner S422);
hold the only copy of something the player must know to act (that goes on the screen);
be empty where a number is shown: every number on a screen has a card.

3. THE GUIDE STANDARD
3a. What changes for the player
Today	Standard
Every page dims at once and a ring points at the next click.	EXPLORE FIRST. No dim and no ring on a first visit. The old trainer says one line: what this page is FOR.
The guide is a list of steps in a fixed order.	The first week keeps its four jobs (sign, coach, week, Continue), because the days cannot run without them. They show as a small list in the trainer's corner, in any order. Everything after week 1 is a LESSON that arrives when its thing happens.
Help is pushed.	Help is asked for, or earned by being stuck: a SHOW ME button on the bubble lights the ring; after 20 s with no click on a page that holds the next job, the ring pulses softly, with no dim. The dim appears only after SHOW ME.

The guide ends silently.	The trainer says it is over and where to find him.
3b. Rules for every line the trainer says
11 words or fewer (today's rule; Fan: eight on screen). One idea. The reason first when there is room.
Never blocks a click, never stops the days, never opens while a pop-up or a fight is on screen (owner S275).
One lesson at a time; a second one waits. At most 3 new things on a page before the player has used one (Hodent).
When two lessons are due together: a job that blocks the days first, then the thing under the pointer's page, then the oldest.
At most one new lesson per page visit and one per game day. A lesson the player dismissed never returns by itself; it
stays in "Ask the trainer".
Off switches that exist today stay: "Skip the guide" on the bubble and the title screen's switch. Skipping keeps "Ask the trainer".
Adaptive: a lesson whose thing the player has already done is marked learned and never shown.
The lesson's MORE opens the same sticky note the tooltip uses. One text per game word, written once.
No names: "your boxer", "the opponent" (S448).
