**2. STAMINA/RECOVERY IN 60 S**

Only a modest fraction of the round's metabolic cost is recovered in 60 s. Phosphocreatine (PCr, key for explosive power) recovers ~54–63% in 60 s post high-intensity (closer to 80–85% of full tank in some trained groups after training). Blood lactate remains very high (>12–15+ mmol/L post-bout; clearance slow—only ~11% removed in 3 min active recovery in related combat sports; 60 s removes far less). Perceived exertion (RPE) drops noticeably with proper breathing/cooling, but performance in the next round shows cumulative fatigue (punch force down 4%+ in later rounds; muscle re-saturation slows across rounds). The 60 s is not a reset—fighters start the next round at high intensity. Water, controlled breathing (e.g., ~6 breaths/min or 5 s in/5–7 s out), and towel waving ("aerating") provide measurable perceptual and minor physiological benefits (better HR recovery in some postures, lower RPE, slight convective cooling), but effects on lactate or next-round power are small/measurable mainly in comfort/output sustainability rather than full metabolic reset.[[3]](https://themmatrainingbible.com/punching-power-cardio-mma/)[[1]](https://fighters-corner.com/blogs/fighters-guide/how-do-boxers-recover-between-rounds)
