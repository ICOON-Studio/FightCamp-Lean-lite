2. AS A GAME — Score: 8/10
Clarity & Usability
✅ Clear in ~5 seconds: The weekly calendar is the dominant visual—drag sessions to days, click cards to adjust evenings, use Light/Normal/Hard toggles. Green +WIN / red –WIN tags make trade-offs instantly visible. The tiredness curve is an excellent at-a-glance reference.
✅ Art & Consistency: Session icons match the ink-style red/blue/motion language from the Playbook movesheet. Photographic backgrounds for each day balance warmth against functional clarity—consistent with the Camp Plan screen’s tone.
❌ Small friction: The bottom sessions panel feels slightly crowded; "what stat does what" relies on the colour key rather than direct labels on hover. "Coach's plan" / "Your plan" distinction is clear but the 51% → 55% change lacks a quick "why" tooltip.
Comparison: Cleaner and more visual than Boxing Manager’s text-heavy week view; far more detailed than Punch Club; less spreadsheet-like than Football Manager training but retains comparable depth. The drag-and-drop + live-preview tiredness curve beats most peers.
3. DEPTH — Score: 9/10
Genuine trade-offs everywhere: Every choice costs something—Hard sessions boost gains but spike fatigue and raise injury risk; swapping sparring for rest preserves condition but delays move mastery; pushing for a stat goal risks peaking too early. No single "correct" week—adjustments cascade across weight cut, energy, injury chance, and fight readiness.
Phase logic works: Peak/Build/Recover/Taper phases frame decisions meaningfully; the coach’s baseline plan gives a safe starting point players can tweak rather than reinvent.
One gap: No visible opportunity cost when reallocating pad time between moves—only the direct gain/loss for that one pair, not what you gave up elsewhere that week. Still, trade-offs are real and meaningful.
