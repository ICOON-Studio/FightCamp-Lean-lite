# The no-sparring rule after a bout: too harsh (measured S442)

Today, after a bout that was NOT stopped, the rule locks sparring by how much the head took:
5 days (fine), 10 days (hurt), 21 days (badly hurt).

Measured over 200 bouts (a boxer who loses 4 in 5, none stopped):

| Setting | 5 days | 10 days | 21 days |
|---|---|---|---|
| Today (2.2 a point) | 27 | 25 | 148 |
| 1.2 | 72 | 52 | 76 |
| 1.0 | 106 | 31 | 63 |
| 0.8 (proposed) | 124 | 68 | 8 |
| 0.6 | 189 | 11 | 0 |

Real boxing: after an ordinary points bout a club rests sparring for some days to a week; the long lay-offs
(about a month) are the medical suspension after a stoppage with head shots, which the game already has (30 days).
So 21 days should be rare after a bout that went the distance: a real beating, not three bouts in four.

Proposal: 0.8. Three in five bouts cost 5 days of sparring, a third cost 10, and a real beating (4 in 100) costs 21.
The 30-day suspension after a stoppage stays as it is. The injury roll is NOT changed by this.
