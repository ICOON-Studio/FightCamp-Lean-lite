**My best fix: a slow punch after a feint finds the hand coming back.** The feint keeps everything it opens for the cross and the body cross, and the overhand after a feint drops from 4 to 2.

**1. The hand comes back for slow punches (build this first)**
- **Rule:** after a feint pulls a hand, a fast or medium punch finds the line fully open. A slow punch (the overhand or the rear uppercut) still lands, but the pulled hand is back in time to take 2 off. So the overhand after a feint does 2, or 3 as a counter.
- **The real boxer:** a feint opens a split-second gap. A short straight shot goes through it, but a looping overhand is too slow, and the guard is half back when it arrives.
- **Why both stay worth choosing:** the feint still fully opens the cross (3) and the body cross (2). The overhand still does a full 4 on a line that was never held, for example against a man holding his lead hand high and his rear hand low.
- **The feedback loop:** power punches (the cross) still go through a feint, so guards don't simply freeze, and bouts don't drag.
- **What could go wrong:** step in, feint, cross, clinch could become the new loop.
- **Number to watch:** that loop's win rate, which must stay under 30%, while the feint stays at 3% of beats or more.

**2. A lead-hand feint draws only the lead hand**
- **Rule:** the feint can pull only his lead hand, held high or low, so it opens only the straight or body line. His rear hand, guarding hooks and uppercuts, never reacts to it.
- **The real boxer:** a jab feint makes the man parry or drop his front glove, and his back hand stays glued to his chin.
- **Why both stay worth choosing:** the feint still opens the cross and the body cross. The overhand is still the answer to a man who keeps his lead hand high and drops his rear hand.
- **What could go wrong:** the feint and the overhand stop meeting at all, so the overhand could drop under 3% of beats.
- **Number to watch:** the overhand's share of beats.

**3. Backing off doesn't bite on the feint**
- **Rule:** a man who backs off in the same moment as the feint isn't fooled, so his held hands stay where they are for the rest of the beat. He pays as usual: 1 stamina and one point of ground given.
- **The real boxer:** you don't bite on a feint by stepping out of range instead of reaching with your gloves.
- **Why both stay worth choosing:** the counter costs ground, so on the ropes (two points of ground given) there's no answer, and the feint is a ropes weapon there. The overhand still lands on a man who stays.
- **The cap on Long growing:** backing off already gives ground, and two points of ground given puts you on the ropes, so no one can back away from feints all bout.
- **What could go wrong:** it could kill the feint the way fix E did.
- **Number to watch:** the feint's share of beats, which must stay at 3% or more.
