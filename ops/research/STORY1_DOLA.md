## PART A — DOLA DEEP SURVEY: Story in Sports & Management Sims
*(8–12 titles reviewed; player sentiment sampled from Steam, Metacritic, Reddit, GOG)*

### A1 — Games Surveyed
| Title / Series | Genre | Narrative Approach | Player Praise | Player Criticism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fight Night Champion | Boxing sim | Linear cinematic story; fixed protagonist | Emotional stakes; corner voice feels personal; loss hits hard | Scripted outcomes override player skill; railroaded choices; cannot deviate from path |
| Punch-Out!! (Wii) | Arcade boxing | Character-driven episodic arc; rival personalities | Memorable opponents; learning patterns = story progress; light tone stays consistent | Very short; no branching; purely linear |
| Football Manager 24 Career | Sports management | Emergent narrative only; no written campaign | Players *write their own story*; longevity; every save feels unique | No guidance; new players feel adrift; no emotional throughline |
| FIFA / EA FC — The Journey | Sports sim | Scripted rise + fall; cutscene heavy | Production value; personal relationships; short-but-satisfying arc | "Movie with QTEs"; player agency illusion; forced drama; disconnect from gameplay |
| Motorsport Manager 1–2 | Team management | Light framing; rivalries + sponsor pressure | Early tension building; resource limits = genuine stakes | Abandoned halfway; no payoff; characters vanish |
| Rocky / Creed: Rise to Glory | Boxing action | Film retelling; underdog-to-champion arc | Iconic structure; known beats feel earned; clear goal | No originality; outcome predetermined; shallow mechanics |
| Wrestling Empire | Pro wrestling sim | Sandbox + generated drama; rivalries + angles | Chaos = charm; freedom; anything-can-happen energy | Poor pacing; no tight arc; scenes feel unconnected |
| Hades | Roguelike / character | Recurring cast; relationship layers; failure = story fuel | Characters evolve; choices persist; repetition becomes meaning | N/A — cited for *structure*, not genre |
| Persona 3–5 | JRPG / calendar | Fixed calendar; social links; time as finite resource | Every week matters; "one more week" pull; bonds = gameplay bonuses | Too rigid; cannot pause calendar; long unskippable scenes |
| NBA 2K MyCareer | Basketball sim | Career ladder + industry drama | Industry pressure feels real; teammates matter | Monetisation interrupts flow; narrative overshadows player team |

### A2 — Key Findings & Sentiment Summary
**What players consistently PRAISE:**
- A **clear ladder** (local → regional → national → world) gives direction without removing freedom
- **Personal stakes** — characters you recognise, relationships that shift, grudges that carry across fights
- **Failure as fuel** — losing a fight does NOT end the campaign; it changes the story
- **System integration** — story choices use in-game money, morale, reputation, not just "yes/no"
- **Calendar rhythm** — knowing what comes next; being pulled forward by a visible upcoming event

**What players consistently HATE:**
- **Scripted results** — "I won the fight but the story said I lost" = instant disconnect
- **Railroading** — only one path; ignoring the story breaks the game
- **Disappearing cast** — characters introduced then never referenced again
- **Interrupt fatigue** — too many cutscenes; cannot get back to playing
- **Unrelated stakes** — drama that has no effect on money, roster, or gym state
- **One-and-done endings** — nothing carries over; campaign feels like a tutorial

**Source tags (sentiment aggregated):** Steam reviews 2024–2026 / r/ManagementGames threads / Metacritic user consensus

### A3 — 10 Rules for FightCamp Story Design
Derived from above findings + FightCamp's unique "simulated outcome + gym management" identity:

| # | Rule | Core Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Never script a fight result** — story sets stakes, context, and consequences; the engine decides the winner | Players trust the simulation; losing must feel earned, not forced |
| 2 | **Story is a layer, not a cage** — every beat can be delayed, ignored, or declined; sandbox remains fully playable | Sandbox is the core product; campaign is the welcome mat, not the prison |
| 3 | **The calendar is the spine** — story beats anchor to weeks, not arbitrary timers; "one fight out" > "three months later" | Visible pull = player motivation; matches existing weekly loop |
| 4 | **Every choice touches a real system** — money, morale, heat, contract terms, injuries; never flavour-only dialogue | Consequences build trust; drama = gameplay pressure |
| 5 | **Failure reshapes the arc** — losing sends you down different paths; setbacks create rivalries, comebacks, new allies | Career longevity feels real; mirrors real sports |
| 6 | **The cast stays** — characters recur across the whole campaign; rivals return; mentors advise; debts come due | Builds emotional continuity; Hades/Persona strength |
| 7 | **UI leads the story** — menus show what matters next; the gym itself is where scenes happen; minimise cutscenes | Immersion; no "game → movie" jarring shift |
| 8 | **Rivalry is a slow burn** — heat builds over months: sparring, press, close fights, callouts; instant hate feels fake | Real grudges take time; 0→100 scale is perfect for this |
| 9 | **Carry over is king** — ending state (fighters, funds, reputation, rivals) becomes your free-play baseline | Campaign = foundation, not prologue |
| 10 | **Respect the operator's vision** — campaign shapes the menus and loop; the story *is* how you play, not just what you watch | UI + narrative unity = distinct identity |

> Structure reference used: **three-act underdog arc** (common to Rocky, Fight Night, The Journey) + **calendar-driven episodic flow** (Persona) + **emergent persistence** (Football Manager). No copyrighted plot material used.

## 1. LOGLINE + TONE
**Logline:** A retired boxer returns to their old neighbourhood to open a modest boxing gym; with one raw, talented unknown and almost no money, they climb from amateur hall shows to world-title contention — while a corporate-backed rival gym tries to buy, bully, or bankrupt them out of the sport entirely.

**Tone:** Gritty but hopeful. Realistic about injury, money pressure, and the business side of boxing, but rooted in loyalty, pride, and the belief that a fighter — and a gym — is more than a brand.

**At the 10-hour mark:** You have weathered your first major setback. Your first fighter has fallen short of a national title, you turned down a buyout offer that would have cleared your debts, and your reputation has split the sport — half the industry respects you, half wants you gone. You are tired, behind on bills, and more determined than when you started.

**At the credits:** You stand in your gym — bigger now, but still yours — having proven that independent roots can reach the top. The belt is real, but the true win is the team you built and the doors you opened. The story ends not as a finish line, but as the beginning of your legacy.

---

## 2. THE SPINE

### Protagonist — The Gym Owner
You were a competent pro boxer who retired young after a bad hand injury ended your shot at the big time. You spent years away, working ordinary jobs, until you came back to the neighbourhood where you grew up — and found the old community gym boarded up. With savings, a small bank loan, and stubborn hope, you reopened it. This gym is not a business venture; it is unfinished business. You do not just want to produce champions — you want to give local talent the shot you never truly got.

### The First Fighter — Jax "The Spark" Marlow
A 21-year-old construction labourer who grew up three streets over. He trains in the evenings because he loves it, not because he expects to make it. Fast, fearless, technically raw — he hits harder than his size suggests but lacks discipline. He is your first, your longest, and the one the story revolves around.

### The Antagonistic Force — Apex Performance Group
A corporate-backed gym chain with deep pockets, TV deals, and a stable of already-prospected fighters. They do not just want to win — they want to dominate the market. They see your gym as inefficient, underfunded, and *ideological* — and therefore dangerous. Their head of operations treats boxing as entertainment first and a business second; fighters are assets to be traded, not people to be developed.

### Three Acts Mapped to the Career Ladder

| Act | Ladder Tier | Core Theme | Apex's Move |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Act I: The Foundation** | Amateur → Regional | Survival & proving you belong | Dismiss you; then try to poach Jax |
| **Act II: The Climb** | Regional → National | Integrity vs. opportunity | Offer buyout; pressure promoters; court Jax harder |
| **Act III: The Summit** | National → World | Legacy & what you stand for | Sabotage bid; attempt to strip ranking; fight on your terms |

## 5. CHOICES THAT MATTER
*(All playable if ignored — story slows, does not stop)*

| Choice | Immediate Effect | Long-Term Consequence | If Ignored |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Take Apex buyout (Beat 4)** | Debts cleared instantly | Campaign ends early; "corporate" ending; Jax's career no longer yours to manage | Game proceeds; harder funding path; Apex hostility grows |
| **Rest vs. push through loss (Beat 7)** | Timeline shifts 1–2 weeks | Rested = healthier long; pushed = higher injury risk through Act III | Jax trains default schedule; story progresses slower; no special penalty |
| **Promoter allegiance (Beat 9)** | Purse size + broadcast reach | Apex = money but worse medical coverage; Lila = fairer, slower growth | Auto-assigns most viable option; story proceeds without unique dialogue |
| **Second medical opinion (Beat 10)** | Apex heat +30; fight nearly pulled | Integrity skyrockets; rival fighter's team later faces scandal; your reputation as safe grows | Fight proceeds as scheduled; no extra heat, no reputation bonus |
| **Final merger decision (Beat 12)** | Campaign ending chosen | Independent = harder free-play start, unique "legacy" flags; merge = funded but constrained | Defaults to independent; full free-play unlocks |

## 6. FIGHT-NIGHT MOMENTS
*(No outcome scripting — atmosphere + stakes only)*

1. **Corner reminder — the personal stake**  
   Between rounds 3–4, Elena: "He's fighting for more than a belt — don't let him forget it, but don't let him break himself." No stat change; player feels the weight.

2. **Entrance contrast**  
   Apex fighter: full production, lights, entourage. Jax: walk-on music from his sister's playlist, no frills. Crowd reaction bias shifts (cheers louder for Jax) — no in-fight modifier, just atmosphere.

3. **Grudge round — heat visible**  
   If rivalry > 80, on-screen text: *"Round 6 — this isn't just points anymore"*. Fighter AI may press harder or take more chances — style shift only; engine still decides success.

4. **Injury callback**  
   If Jax ignored rest earlier: mid-fight, animation favours his bad side; corner notes it. *"His left's slowing — remember we talked about this?"* Higher injury risk *stat* but no forced stoppage.

### (f) UI Rules the Story Implies
1. **Icons first, text second** — Morale = faces; money = coin; heat = flame; hover for full context
2. **The gym is your interface** — Every action happens in a room; no abstract menus disconnected from space
3. **One clear next action** — Always pinned; secondary options below
4. **Respect the player's time** — All scenes skip; all interrupts dismiss; "Remind me later" option
5. **Consistent tone** — Clean, gritty, professional; no flash over substance — just like the gym you build
