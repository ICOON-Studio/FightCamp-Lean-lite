# FightCamp money design v2.3

Changes from v2.2 to reach 8/10 against shipped Steam management games. Every number has a real-world anchor (GBP x 1.25) or is marked GAME.

## 1. Number and rule changes (six)

**Coach scaling.** From: one sessional coach runs any size gym. To: one coach per 50 members carried; each extra coach $450/week (real: Level 2 coach GBP 30-35/h, 10 hours = $375-440; GAME size $450). Over the coach cap, members leave 5% a month (GAME). A 250-member club building needs 5 coaches at $2,250/week total, not $450. Fixes critic A #1 (nothing grows with success), B #2 partial (staff checklist). The number it moves: a year-5 gym's coaching bill from $450/week to $2,250/week.

**Club show cooldown.** From: every 12 weeks, always full turnout. To: if the last show was under 16 weeks ago, turnout -20% (GAME); the gym's own boxers on the bill lose a training week (injury recovery paused that week). Real: small clubs burn their audience and volunteers at 3-4 shows a year; England Boxing's calendar has 2-3 club shows plus championships. Fixes A #3 (cash machine every 12 weeks). The number it moves: a grown club's show income from +$3-5k every 12 weeks to +$2-4k twice a year.

**Contract talks with real risk.** From: two talks (top 10, belt), bonus always pays back in 3-4 bouts. To: after the first top10/belt talk, the pro's firm asks yearly for a 2% share of last year's gym earnings from that pro (GAME); refusing carries a 25% chance the pro leaves for a promoter's gym and is lost for the rest of the career (GAME). A champion (belt holder) lifts the gym's ticket cut by 10% and unlocks a third sponsor tier at $1,200/week (GAME). Fixes A #4 (talks are a calculator), B #4 (no risk in refusing). The number it moves: a top-5 pro's gym net per bout from flat $1,200 to $1,000-1,400 with a real decision.

**Kit budget rebalance.** From: Low saves $25/week but loses 3 members and +15% risk; S&C -25% risk; floor -35%. To: Low saves $25/week, no member loss, +25% injury risk (GAME); S&C -15% risk (was -25%); floor stays at -35%. Real: a worn glove causes more cuts than it saves; a good S&C coach prevents overuse injuries but not all contact injuries. Fixes A #2 (Low kit never picked, S&C always hired, floor wastes buys). The numbers it moves: Low kit is now a real gamble for a cash-poor gym; S&C is no longer an automatic first hire.

**Strength corner costs capacity.** From: $3,600 + $22/week, +10% power/conditioning gain, no trade-off. To: same price and upkeep, but costs 10 member capacity (the racks, bars and sled take 400-600 sq ft; real: a strength zone needs that space in a 1,500 sq ft unit). Fixes B #2 (upgrades are a checklist with no scarce resource). The number it moves: a unit with the strength corner carries 60 members, not 70; the player must choose between a bigger roster and a stronger one.

**Dues bands.** From: fixed $35/month. To: the player sets Cheap ($25), Standard ($35) or Premium ($50) a month; Cheap: +2 members/week join rate (GAME); Premium: -1 member/week leavers (GAME); Standard: no change. Real: gyms from GBP 20/month community clubs to GBP 50/month boutique boxing studios all exist; the price moves who walks in. Fixes B #3 (player cannot set dues). The number it moves: a gym chasing volume runs Cheap and nets $10 less per member but fills the premises faster; a reputation gym runs Premium and trades members for margin.

## 2. New mechanics (two, under the three cap)

**Promoter's licence.** After reaching the club building (tier 2), the player can buy a promoter's licence for $40,000 (real: a small-hall promoter's bond, ring hire and insurance setup GBP 15-25k x 1.25 + buffer). The licence costs $500/week in upkeep (a part-time administrator). It stages 4 pro shows a year, each with an $8,000 up-front cost (hall, officials, sanctions; real: a 6-bout small-hall card GBP 4-6k x 1.25). The gym takes 10% of the opponent's purse and all ticket revenue above $5,000. A card with at least one ranked boxer and 150+ reputation nets $8-15k; a thin card (no ranked names, low reputation) can lose $3-5k. This is the ONE late ambition: a year-4 gym with a ranked pro and 200+ members has a real reason to grow, and a real risk of a bad night.

**Grant applications.** Once a year in January, the player may apply for a grassroots sport grant of $3,000-5,000 (real: Sport England small grants GBP 250-5,000, x 1.25). The application has a 60% chance of acceptance (GAME). If accepted, the money arrives in week 8 but carries a condition: the gym must run a youth section or a women's/no-gi session for 52 consecutive weeks. Dropping the condition before 52 weeks claws back the full grant. A gym that already has the youth section upgrade can apply for a "women's session" grant ($3,500, same terms); the two conditions stack. This is a year-1 choice with teeth: free money, but it locks a spending line for a year and a failed clawback can crater cash.

## 3. Year 1 / year 2 / year 5 money sketch (a sensible gym)

**Year 1 (weeks 1-52):** 30 to 60 members. Weekly in: dues $115, sponsors $250, evening classes $350, one club show net +$800 (amortised $60/week). Weekly out: rent $415, head coach $450, fees $12, power $100 (winter average), insurance $24, kit $45, stipend $250 for one prospect. Net: roughly breakeven to +$50/week. Cash at week 52: $16-18k (1.5-2 months of costs). Owned: matted floor, youth section. Saving for: bigger unit ($12,000) or fitness kit and assistant.

**Year 2 (weeks 53-104):** 90 to 130 members, moved to bigger unit. Weekly in: dues $330, sponsors $400 (Thamesgate), fitness classes $420, juniors $115, one pro debut camp bill covered by purse. Weekly out: rent $785, two coaches $730, S&C $100, kit $45, show costs (amortised $85/week). Net: +$200-300/week. Cash at week 104: $22-28k (2-2.5 months). Owned: strength corner, fitkit. Saving for: club building ($25,000) or promoter's licence ($40,000).

**Year 5 (weeks 209-260):** 220-250 members, club building, one ranked pro. Weekly in: dues $720-800, sponsors $800 (Northline), fitness $420, juniors $115, pro purse share $400-600/week (amortised). Weekly out: rent $1,270, five coaches $2,250 (scaled!), physio $75, kit $80, show costs $170/week, promoter's licence $500/week. Net: +$100-300/week (the pile is gone; coaches and the licence eat the surplus). Cash at week 260: $30-45k (2-3.5 months). The gym is saving for: a second promoter's licence (staggered shows), or simply building reserves against a pro injury or a bad show.

## 4. Bounds a 260-week simulation must meet

- Cash in months of costs at weeks 104, 156, 260: band 1.5-4.0 for a sensible player. Below 1.0 at any point: the design is too harsh. Above 6.0 at any point: the pile has returned and the design has failed.
- The gym never closes for a sensible player (one who books sensible bouts, keeps sponsors, does not stack stipends).
- At least two real spending choices per year: the player can point money at upgrades, staff, premises, the promoter's licence, or reserves, and each path has a visible trade-off.
- A careless player (no sponsors, unsafe bouts, hard camps, every stipend on) closes by week 30-40, same as v2.2.
- The promoter's licence is reachable by week 180 for a sensible gym and is the dominant year-4/5 sink; without it, cash drifts above 4 months.
- Coach scaling removes 60-70% of the year-5 surplus that v2.2 left on the table.
- A grant application in year 1 is a genuine bet: a cash-poor gym can afford the clawback risk; a comfortable one may skip it.
