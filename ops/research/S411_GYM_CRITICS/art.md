# Gym art review (S411) - art director / environment artist

Seen: home_layout_A, home_layout_B, fighters_page, versus_page, choose_your_building (all 1920x1009 renders). Measured by eye from the pixels, so give or take 5%.

## 1. Presentation score: 5/10 (capped by two hard fails)
Hard fails:
- HF1 Scale break across the set: gym boxers are about 60-70 px tall; Fighters-page boxers are about 250 px and Versus boxers about 650 px. The same person appears at three sizes with three renderings (tiny flat sprite, lit 3D bust, full-body 3D). It does not read as one game.
- HF2 Two worlds in one frame (layout B): a flat, evenly lit, hard-edged diorama floats over a dark, moody, photographic gym. The photo is a different light, scale and mood, and the diorama has no shadow on it, so it looks pasted on. It also breaks the standing rule "bright, alive, modern" (the backdrop is the old-garage look).

Without those, the building itself is a 6.5-7: clean blocky shapes, good ring, readable zones, bright floor colours.

## 2. Why it reads small (the owner is right)
Numbers, layout A (B is identical in size):
- Building bounding box x 354-1558, y 108-790: 1204 x 682 px. That is 63% of the width and about 68% of the 1009 px height, but a diamond fills only about half its box: roughly 400k px, **about 20% of the screen is the gym**. Corners of the screen (left 350 px, right 360 px) are empty or backdrop.
- The 24 x 18 plan gives a cell of about 50 px wide on screen (1204/ (24+18) * 2 ~ 57 px). A boxer is about 1.2 cells tall. Ring is about 380 px wide, about 7 x 7 cells, so a ring is as large as a quarter of the floor. Fine for a ring, but the room is 24 x 18 cells and holds: 1 ring, 9 bags, 3 weight racks, 3 mats, 2 locker banks, 1 desk. About 20 objects in 430 cells, so it reads as a sparse, roomy hall, not a busy club.
- Wall height is about 50-60 px (about one figure), and it is cut away on the near sides entirely. Real gym walls give the room height and a feeling of enclosed space; a one-figure wall reads like a model, a board game, a dollhouse floor plan. Nothing hangs on the walls (no posters, no mirrors, no windows with light, no belts, no flags).
- The people: 3-9 figures, at 60 px against 380 px of ring. The figures are the only moving thing, and they are tiny, so the place feels empty and quiet. 30 members and 2 + 30/5 = 8 people on the floor should feel crowded; here they spread over a floor of 430 cells (one per 50 cells).
- Floor: big flat colour blocks (a cream-wood field, a red bag strip, a blue mat strip, a charcoal weights strip, a blue ring apron). No texture, no wear lines, no rug, no shadow under equipment. The blocks look like map regions not a floor.
- Lighting/depth: no ambient occlusion, no cast shadows, no light shafts from the glass blocks on the wall, no gradient. Everything is the same value, so there is no depth and no focus.
- Empty areas inside: the cream floor between reception, ring and lockers (the upper-left room) is a large empty region of about 300 x 150 px.
- Layout A's backdrop (pale pastel wash) is calm and matches the setup page, but the gym floats in a void with nothing around it: no street, no ground, no edge. Layout B's backdrop adds mood but fights the diorama.

Verdict: it reads small because (1) only a fifth of the screen is gym, (2) the room is sparse and the people tiny, (3) low walls and flat light give no volume, (4) it floats in a void.

## 3. One game with fighters_page and versus_page?
Not yet. Fighters and Versus share: dark warm photo room, strong coloured spot-lighting, big lit 3D bodies, parchment cards with brush-script titles, saturated red/blue. The gym shares only the top bar, the brush title and the parchment cards below it (layout A). The gym art is flat vector-like pieces with white chrome and light-blue glass blocks; a paint-textured look is only on the mats and ring canvas.
Also: the set has two light worlds: Fighters/Versus are dark with a rim light, the gym (A) is pale and washed, the setup page is pale pastel. Layout B's dark backdrop moves toward Fighters. Choose one.

What should change so the set is one style:
- Decide the shared recipe: lit 3D-ish forms with soft shadows and rim light, warm key light, a deep teal/dark-red surround, parchment UI cards. The gym should be a lit, shaded object, not a flat sticker.
- Same boxers, same render: the gym figures must be the same MetaHuman characters (same kit colours: red top + lime shorts for Lucy) shrunk by rendering, not redrawn.
- Gym "bright and alive and modern" can still sit on a dark surround: bright interior pooled in warm light, dark surround, like the Fighters page spotlights. Bright refers to the gym's inside; the surround should be a dark soft vignette, not a photo of an old garage.
- Brush title and parchment cards continue as is; that part already works.

## 4. Labels and pins
- Size: the pill labels (icon disc 56 px + label 40 px tall, 110-130 px wide) are about as big as a boxer (60 px) and a bag, so they cover equipment (Fights sits on the ring apron, Playbook covers the mats, Reception covers the desk). The icons are web-UI style white-on-navy chips; they do not look part of the world.
- Fit: Reception sits on the desk (good), Club office on the office desk (good), Fighters on the bags (good), Fights on the ring (good). Playbook is on the mats: acceptable but it covers the thing the mats show. **World does not fit**: it is a pin floating at the end of the bag wall, on no object, near the edge of the picture and half clipped by the red wall; the owner is right. Playbook is the mats but looks like a floor decal; and Playbook is not a place, it is a book.
- Always on? No. Five permanent labels at the same weight is noise and, after the first week, redundant with the top bar (the same Fighters / Fights / Club / World pills). Suggested: labels hidden by default, a thin name tag fades in on hover (object outlines glow, cursor changes), all five labels show only in the first-hour guide or when the player holds a key (Tab) or after 3 s idle. Keep a small badge (a number or a dot, 18 px) on a place only when it needs the player (offer at the ring, walk-in at reception, bills at the office): this is "actionable info".
- World: it is not a room, so it should be an object in the room that stands for the outside: a window or door to the street on the back wall with a view (city skyline, poster of the rankings), or a wall-mounted TV/rankings board, or a map poster. Click there for World. Even better: the gym sits on a street edge and the street/sign on the left leads to World (rival gyms across the road).
- Playbook: a physical book. A whiteboard/tactics board on the wall next to the mats and ring, or a coach's lectern with a clipboard, with a paper corner. Click it: Playbook. Best if it is a tall object (whiteboard 90 px high) so it is visible from the Home view and sits against a wall, not on the floor.
- Fights: ring, keep. Add the bout poster on the wall for a booked bout (the ring itself shows a "date" flag).
- Both pages need space off the floor: wall furniture is where more places go without covering the floor.

## 5. What is reachable with the current approach, and what is not
Reachable with separate isometric pieces from data, one angle (Ludo-generated):
- Bigger on screen: scale the same plan to fit the work area (see list). No new art needed; set the cell at about 70 px for a 1000-1050 px wide plan, or crop the plan to 20 x 14.
- Higher walls (a 150-170 px wall on the back two sides with windows, posters, a roof beam, light shafts), a floor texture and shadows. Shadows can be a soft dark ellipse sprite under each piece, baked into the pieces by Ludo (consistent light from top-left).
- More objects (bags, heavy bags, speed bags, plyo boxes, mirrors, water, skipping ropes, benches, towels, a TV, banners) and more people.
- Wall furniture for World and Playbook.
- Scene-level light: one warm overlay gradient and a vignette in a shader, a few additive light cones.
Not reachable well with that approach:
- Real shadows and lighting consistent with the Fighters/Versus pages; boxers in the same render as those pages; person scale that matches 3D bodies; camera pan, zoom, a rotating angle; build mode with arbitrary placement (the pieces must be baked from one angle and one light, and every new piece costs a Ludo round with a style-drift risk).
- A different approach: render the gym in 3D with the game's own MetaHuman boxers and the existing 3D gym assets, orthographic camera at 30 deg elevation / 45 deg yaw (true isometric 35.26 deg elevation if wanted), real lights (a warm key, soft shadow, rim light), baked to an image or live. It matches Fighters/Versus exactly, gives true shadows, free zoom and pan, and makes build mode and four buildings a matter of data. It costs: a 3D gym kit (about 25 pieces), a gym scene built from GymPlan data, a headless render for the layout stills, and the cost of live 3D on the hub. CLAUDE.md says the 3D gym comes later; this review is that case for pulling the plan forward, with the hybrid: keep the data plan (GymPlan), keep the 2D pieces for now, and make the 3D view in the same data.
- Ideas from real games, for inspiration only: Two Point Hospital / Museum (high cutaway walls, dense items, lit rooms, people everywhere), Prison Architect (flat but dense), Dungeon Keeper style room labels that only show on hover, Kairosoft (small but busy), Game Dev Tycoon (tiny people but lots of equipment), Sims 4 (wall panel cut-away, shadow sprites), Cities Skylines (hover labels, badge icons only on problems), Football Manager's club hub (tiles, not a scene; the opposite).

## To reach 10/10
Most important first.

1. **Fill the screen with the gym.** Make the plan fill about 1600 x 900 px of the 1920 x 1009 work area (about 45% of the screen, was 20%): a cell of about 75 px (was about 55), or crop the plan to 20 x 14 cells and scale to 90 px. Place the building centred, with the top bar and the bottom 100 px for status only. Move the Job card, news and notice board off the picture, into a drawer or a bottom strip of at most 120 px. Layout A pushes everything under 700 px; fix it by compressing, not scrolling. The gym should be the screen.
2. **Fix the figure scale and make the people the same render as Fighters and Versus.** Boxers 100-110 px tall (door height 150, ring 480 px wide), rendered from the same MetaHuman models and kits as the Fighters page (Lucy in red top, lime shorts), with soft contact shadows. About 70 px at minimum. Show 8-12 people on the floor, always doing something (bag work, shadow boxing, skipping, stretching, a coach holding pads), plus 2-3 visitors at reception.
3. **Give the room walls and volume.** Back walls 160-200 px high (about 3 figures) with windows (real light shafts, 20% opacity white wedges), mirrors on one wall, a banner, framed posters, a pull-up rig, a belt cabinet, fluorescent strip lights; a cutaway front edge 14 px thick (floor slab with a visible edge and a shadow below it). Cast shadows: every item and person gets a soft shadow (black at 25%, blur 6 px, offset 8 px to the lower right). No more "model on a table".
4. **Choose one lighting world and make the surround match.** Replace the photo backdrop and the pastel void with a dark teal/charcoal vignette (same as the Fighters-page surround: a dark blurred gym with spotlights, but heavily darkened and blurred 20 px, so it is quiet and cannot compete). Pool a warm light on the gym (a radial gradient of +10% brightness centred on the ring, vignette -25% at the corners). The inside stays bright and modern (white walls, light wood, blue/red accents). Remove the dark garage look from the backdrop. Add a ground slab and a street edge so the building sits on something instead of floating.
5. **Triple the density of equipment.** From about 20 objects to 55-70 in the same 24 x 18 plan: a second row of bags, speed bags, a double-end bag, kettlebell rack, a bench with plates, a stretching mat, a water cooler, benches, shoe racks, towels, mirrors, a skipping-rope hook board, a scale at weigh-in, a TV with the rankings. Zones keep their rule (no bath, board or chairs on the training floor) but each zone needs a clear edge: skirting, a rubber floor, a painted line.
6. **Floor materials, not colour blocks.** Wood floor with plank lines at 8% contrast, a rubber floor with a pebble texture for weights, mats with seams and a worn centre, a ring apron with canvas stitching and logo, taped lines and a gym logo on the main floor, a wear patch near the bags. Keep the colours (they are the good part) but break each block with texture and a 10% light/dark variation.
7. **Labels: off by default, hover to show, badges only for needs.** Remove the five permanent pills. On hover, a place gets an outline glow (3 px, warm white) and a name tag 26 px high in the brush font or the card font, with the actual state ("1 offer", "2 walk-ins"). A red/amber/blue dot badge 18 px on the object only when the player must act. Tab (or the first-hour guide) shows all labels for 4 seconds. The top bar already carries the same destinations; do not repeat them in the scene all the time.
8. **Put World and Playbook in the world.** World = a wide street window or glass door on the back wall with a skyline/street view, and a rankings poster board next to it (a 160 x 110 px object), clickable as one hotspot; a rival gym's sign visible across the street. Playbook = a tactics whiteboard (140 x 120 px) on the wall beside the mats with arrows and a boxer silhouette, plus a clipboard on a stand. Both on walls, both visible without a pin. Fights = the ring plus a poster of the booked bout on the wall. Club office = the desk plus a safe and a ledger. Reception = desk plus a door and a visitor bench.
9. **Make the plan size real.** The four buildings should differ in footprint, not only in layout: front of house 24 x 18 but a bigger reception; two rings 28 x 18; bag wall 24 x 20; conditioning 26 x 18. A bigger building feels bigger on screen, and the "Move premises" feature gains meaning. On screen, fit by scaling, and optionally allow mouse-wheel zoom (0.8x to 1.4x) and drag-pan within the frame (the data supports it; it is only one transform).
10. **Add life that moves and sounds as scale cues.** Ambient animation: bags swing, a skipping rope loop, a ceiling fan, steam from the shower door, a TV flicker, dust in the light shafts, a member entering at the door; and a day/evening light colour shift. Movement of 8-12 people across the whole floor sells the size better than any art.
11. **Same chrome as the other pages.** Put the Job / Since last Monday cards in the same parchment style as the Fighters cards, at the same size, and title the page in the brush script like the others. Drop the white-on-navy web pill style from the scene (it is the only element that looks like a web form).
12. **Choose the approach by a test, then commit.** Spike for two days: render the gym from the existing 3D pieces and MetaHuman boxers with an orthographic camera (yaw 45 deg, elevation 30-35 deg), a warm key light, soft shadows and a rim light, from the same GymPlan data. Compare it side by side with the 2D version at the same screen size. If it clearly beats the 2D version in the Fighters-page set test, make it the hub (the pieces stay the same data; the 2D one becomes the thumbnails on the building choice page, rendered from the same scene so they match). If not, do items 1-11 in 2D with Ludo pieces baked with a fixed light, and do not mix photo backdrops with flat pieces.
13. **Building choice page.** Its thumbnails (about 260 px wide) are the best-looking version of the gym, because small flat isometric is where this style works. Keep them, but render them from the final hub so they match, with a rotating bonus tag; and on picking, show the real gym large (a 900 px preview) in place of the four equal small cards.

Rough target after 1-8: 8.0. After 9-13 and the 3D spike resolved: 9-10. A 10 needs the gym to look like part of the Fighters/Versus set and the player to feel they own a busy, lit, full club on first sight.
