# Progressive knowledge: player review (S411)

Reviewer: a player with 40 years of management and sports games. Read: CRITIC_PACK.md, KNOWLEDGE_S411.md.

## 1. Score: 6.5 / 10. Hard fails: none that cap it, but two near-fails (below)

The idea is good and I would enjoy it. "The range never lies" is a clean promise, and chin-tested-by-fire is the best line in the document. It is a feature that makes me care about a signing, because I have a stake in what I do not know. But as written it is a number-hiding system with one reward moment, and nothing yet says what I do with the doubt.

Near-fails (each would cap at 5 if left as written):
- **Doubt with no decision attached.** If the only thing I do is "train and wait for the bar to narrow", it is a loading bar. Rule 5 ("nothing new to manage") is the danger: no decision, no fun. Games like Football Manager's fuzzy scout stars work because I choose who to send, where, and whether to buy at 3 stars.
- **The bar being a lie by offset.** A hyped prospect centred high is fine, but a range whose centre is always wrong breeds distrust. If I learn the centre is biased, I will ignore the centre and use only the width. Needs a visible honest cue (see changes).

What is good: the truth is always inside; the fight reads the true number; goals read the truth (so I am never blind on my plan); old saves start fully seen.

Options missing:
- **A deliberate test, such as "test his chin"** (hard sparring with a harder partner, a risky bout). Changes the game: knowledge becomes a spend (injury risk, fatigue, morale) versus a gain.
- **Choose what to learn.** A "focus the coach's eye on X" pick on the week board. Changes: scouting becomes planning.
- **Trial scale on prospects**, where the trial buys width, not a flip from range to exact. Changes: Find a fighter becomes a shopping decision.
- **A second opinion from another coach** for money (costs a fee, narrows one range). Changes: money buys information, which is the pillar "every week costs something".
- **Sell/trade a signing before he is known** (pass him on to another club). Changes: a hyped flop can be offloaded, a gem can be kept quiet.
- **Coach's lean in words** on a wide range ("I'd bet on a good chin, not sure"). Changes: gives me a hunch to follow or doubt.

Useless or weak as written:
- Injury meter from the Physio as an "instrument": fine, but the physio is already a hire for a number; do not turn every hire into a viewer. Keep two instruments, not five.
- Cutman showing "marks easily" before the first cut: so small nobody will notice. Fold it into the Physio.
- Per-attribute `seen` for six measurable stats separately: the player will never track six sharpening timers. Show at most 3 lines at once on the Monday.

## 2. First week with a new boxer

What I see: a signing card with stars as a range, one first impression, soft bars. Strength, speed, stamina as a range about 16 wide; skills 20 wide; chin, heart, composure, fight IQ "unknown". The room looks like a fighter I have not met yet, which is right.

What I do differently:
- I would not put him straight into hard sparring with my best fighter. I would test him cautiously: pads, then a controlled spar with a lighter partner.
- I would write a plan that does not depend on his chin: I do not know it yet.
- I would delay a bout offer I cannot read (the win chance band is wide), and I might take an easy one to see him under fire cheaply.
- I would hire or keep the strength coach before the physio, because exact numbers on the body let me plan.
- I would watch the Monday review with real attention.

That is a good first week. But only if the game lets me choose a cheap test versus an expensive one. With "nothing new to manage", my week-one choices are the same as today. So the week one gain is attitude, not play.

## 3. The chin test and the flop

Chin tested: this is the best moment in the design and it must be treated as one. A chin is found out when he gets hit: a knockdown or a hard round. Good moment IF it lands on screen when it happens, not on Monday in a line of text. A KO in round two that reveals a glass chin should feel like a punch to me, not a report. If the Monday line is the only place it appears, I will have lost the moment two days before.

Hyped signing flops: infuriating if he is simply bad and I never had a say. A flop is a good moment only if I can see afterward that the signs were there ("the coach said he was unsure about the chin; his centre led high") and that I chose to gamble. A flop with no clue to read is unfair; a flop where I ignored a hint is a story.

What the game should do at that moment:
- Stop the clock on the reveal, with the fighter's face, one plain line, and the bar snapping tight, in the moment of the fight or the spar, not buried in the week report.
- Show the old range ghosted beside the new one ("you thought 60 to 76; it is 52").
- Name what it changes: "Your plan has no answer for a stoppage danger. Change the corner call?" One button to go act on it.
- For a flop: a line from the coach in the voice ("I told you I wasn't sure"), and a choice: keep, move on, rebuild around what he does have.
- For a gem: a polaroid up on the wall, and the news "this one is better than you hoped".

## 4. When I stop looking at the ranges, and year three

I stop looking at a range when it becomes a number, which is the stated design. For an active fighter that is within two or three months for the body and about six bouts for the under-fire ones. After that the ranges are gone for him and the feature is only alive for new signings.

Year three: only if something is still hidden. As specified, the ceiling stays "never exact", which is the one lasting hook, but it is words only. If a year three roster is all numbers and every new recruit is a wide bar for four weeks, the feature turns into a routine that I click through. Things left to find in year three as written: ceiling for old boxers, new traits on rare moments. That is thin.

What would give year three life: the unknown is a recurring pressure, not an opening phase. New things that stay unknown: how he fights at a higher level (the step up from regional to national rewidens the under-fire range), the opponent's true ability, a rival gym's prospect, and what age and wear are doing to a known number (it goes back to a range after a brutal fight: "has the last fight cost him his chin?"). That last one is the great addition: knowledge can decay, and a damaged fighter becomes uncertain again.

I would also stop looking sooner if sharpening is automatic and the same for everyone. The player must be able to speed it by a choice.

## To reach 10/10

1. **Give the player decisions to buy knowledge.** A "Test him" option (harder spar, a risky bout, with a named cost in fatigue, injury or morale) and a "Coach's eye on X" pick on the Week board. A cheap safe test that narrows slowly versus an expensive one that narrows fast. Remove "nothing new to manage" as a rule; replace it with "nothing you must do, much you can choose".
2. **Make the reveal a live moment.** When a range snaps (a chin held, a glass jaw found, a gem seen), stop at the moment in the spar or bout: face, plain line, old range ghosted beside the new one, and one button to act. Do not leave it to the Monday text.
3. **Let knowledge go stale.** A heavy fight, a long layoff, age, or a step up in level widens a known range again ("did that stoppage cost him his chin?"). This keeps year three alive and links to the existing damage ledger.
4. **Show the coach's lean in words on every wide range**, and show the hype honestly: tell me when a prospect's reputation is ahead of what has been seen ("everybody likes him; nobody has seen him hit"). Keep the offset, but give a readable clue so a flop is a read I missed, not a rigged roll.
5. **Prospects and trials as a shopping choice.** Trials buy width, scouts and second opinions cost money and narrow one range; passing a prospect on or selling a signing before he is known is possible.
6. **Limit what is on the board.** At most three live "unknown" lines per boxer on the Monday; group the rest ("body: known, skills: mostly, under fire: not yet"). One progress word for each group, not six timers.
7. **Win chance must say why it is wide**, in the same line ("wide because you have not seen his chin or hers"), and the corner card must flag a plan line that depends on an unknown.
8. **Cut the hire-as-viewer list to two instruments** (strength coach: body exact; physio: injury risk). The rest are minor; do not turn every hire into a viewer.
9. **Opponents and rivals get the same treatment** at higher levels so the unknown never fully ends: the world reads the truth today, but a ranked rival's chin should be a range until I see it tested.
10. **Prove it with the bots test before building the screens**: scouting bot against blind bot, 5 more top-10 finishes per 60 worlds. If it fails, add the decisions in item 1 first, since ranges without choices are decoration.
11. **Show width, not a fake bar of precision.** No digits until narrow, a soft fade, and a hover that says where the coach thinks it sits (low, mid, high), so I can bet.
