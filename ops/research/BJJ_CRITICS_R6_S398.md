## What both say (the agreements)
- THE SAME BIGGEST FAULT, independently: white belts are passive. A side whose few owned moves give it no arrow in a position
  HOLDS, so low skill reads as stalling: 0.66 penalties a white match (0.04-0.08 at every other belt, a tenfold cliff), 27%
  of white matches 0-0, white even subs 0.22, the Guard player wins at white because the Wrestler holds. Both: NOT
  fixable by tuning - it is how a beat works. Critic 9: a side with no good arrow attempts a low-odds scramble; a hold means
  protecting a lead or a pin. Critic 10: attempt a grey or felt arrow at low odds and risk the miss.
- Stalling should come from protecting a lead (late), never from having nothing to try.
- Pace too low: 2.9 attempts an athlete at white, 5.7 at black (critic 9: a real black match has 15-30 committed actions
  each) - one landed arrow decides too much. Critic 9: two steps a beat or ~15-s beats.
- Black referee decisions 0.02 (real ~7%).
- Styles: no counters, one dominant style per belt (white Guard player 0.58-0.61 against all; purple Passer); white Passer,
  Half-guard and Leg locker identical. Both: a matchup cycle (Wrestler > Guard player at low belts, ...), lanes drawn from
  the style at every belt, IBJJF legality per lane (a white Leg locker has the straight ankle lock and little else).
- The belt table: both judge against the rating proxy (one belt ~ a 100 gap, 66-75%) and call blue>white 0.88 and the two+
  belt cells too high; both say a four-stripe vs fresh row should be worth well under a belt (critic 9: 0.58-0.63; critic 10:
  time at the belt <= 60% of a step) - 4-stripe brown over fresh brown 0.75 > black over brown 0.64.
  OUR NOTE: the fresh-vs-four-stripe row spans the whole belt by construction ("a ready white = a fresh blue", S397 critic
  b: no jump at a promotion), so it equals a belt step; the critics of rounds 2-6 split on this (no jump vs a jump). Held
  until the beat change is measured: more attempts a match change every gap.

## Verdict
Step 3 (the approved structural change) answers the fault both name: the ~11-s two-sided beat with WORK (frames and grips,
never a stall) for a side with nothing worth trying, IDLE only on the late-lead hold (built in BjjBeat after a first
measurement: idling every no-pick beat drew 2.4 penalties a white match). Re-scored after it is fitted.
