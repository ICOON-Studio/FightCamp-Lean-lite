# J_SOUTHPAW1: orthodox against southpaw (the open stance), and what changes

This is research only. Everything was accessed on 25 Nov 2026 (Asia/Nicosia) unless a page gives its own date.

How each claim is tagged:
- **SOURCED:** a measured or counted number.
- **COACH:** a coach's or instructor's teaching.
- **MEMORY:** my own recall, not checked.
- **DERIVED:** my own arithmetic on sourced numbers.
- **CHECK:** needs a second look.
- **NOT FOUND:** searched for, and no number found.

"Open stance" means one orthodox boxer against one southpaw. "Closed stance" means both boxers in the same stance.

**The big caveat:** I found no large punch-by-punch count of open-stance bouts. CompuBox hasn't published a league-wide split by stance (NOT FOUND). The only punch-type count by stance is one 2013 undergraduate dissertation on 24 bouts [S1]. Everything else is CompuBox notes on single boxers [S2–S5] or coaching pages [S6–S11]. Only the win/loss results (Q5) rest on large samples, from BoxRec [S13–S15].

## Short answers

**Q1. What lands more or less in the open stance?**
- **The orthodox jab, from CompuBox notes on single boxers:** it lands less against southpaws every time.
  - One boxer landed 11.9% against a southpaw, against 22.4% against an orthodox opponent [S4] (SOURCED; the 22.4% is DERIVED).
  - Another landed 11% against a southpaw, against 17% over seven tracked bouts [S3] (SOURCED).
  - A third landed 9.8% against two southpaws [S2] (SOURCED).
  - For scale, the junior-middleweight average was 22% [S3] (SOURCED).
- **The jab in the one counted study:** mixed. The two open-stance groups landed 41.3% and 29.8%; orthodox-only bouts landed 31.5% [S1] (SOURCED, small sample, CHECK).
- **The rear straight:** it's thrown more in the open stance and its land rate barely moves [S1] (SOURCED).
  - Thrown per bout: 66.6 and 75 in the open-stance groups, against 53.3 and 54.3 in same-stance groups (p = .013).
  - Landed: 47.2% and 40.4% in the open-stance groups, against 45.5% in orthodox-only and 39.2% in southpaw-only bouts.
- **The lead hook:** boxers throw more hooks with whichever hand is in front.
  - Left hooks were thrown more when an orthodox boxer was in the bout, right hooks more when a southpaw was [S1] (SOURCED).
  - The left hook landed 59.2% in one open-stance group, against 41.1% in orthodox-only bouts [S1] (SOURCED, CHECK).
  - Coaches say the orthodox lead hook is awkward in the open stance and the southpaw's jab beats it [S6, S7] (COACH).
- **Body punches:** the open-stance groups landed 74% and 75%. In same-stance bouts, head and body land rates were "very similar" [S1] (SOURCED). A single orthodox-only body figure is NOT FOUND.
- **Power punches overall, single boxers:** no pattern. One orthodox boxer landed *more* against a southpaw, 29.1% against 19.7% [S4]. Another landed fewer, 29% against 52% [S3] (SOURCED).

**Q2. What stops what?**
- **In the closed stance (coaching):** the rear hand catches the other boxer's jab and lead hook. The lead glove or lead shoulder catches the rear straight and the overhand [S7] (COACH).
- **In the open stance (coaching):**
  - Against the jab, coaches teach the *lead* hand: parry it or slap it down, then jab back with the same hand [S8, S6]. A rear-hand block is also taught [S8] (COACH).
  - Against the rear straight (the southpaw's straight left), the orthodox boxer's *rear* (right) hand has to stop it. That hand has to move between the straight and the overhand, because one position no longer covers both [S7, S6] (COACH).
- **Your guess 1, that the lead hands get in each other's way so the jab is easier to stop:** coaches back it.
  - They describe both boxers trying to "out-paw" each other with the jabbing arm, and an "inside jab" that keeps the other jab from landing [S6].
  - The single-boxer CompuBox drops in orthodox jab accuracy point the same way [S2–S4].
  - A pooled count is NOT FOUND.
- **Your guess 2, that the rear straight comes down the middle past the lead hand so the rear hand must stop it:** coaches back it [S6, S7] (COACH), and the rear straight is thrown more in the open stance [S1] (SOURCED). Counted blocks by hand are NOT FOUND.

**Q3. Lead foot outside.**
- **What it gains, in plain words:** the boxer whose lead foot is outside can reach with both hands, while the other reaches with neither or only the rear hand. The outside boxer also stands off the line of the other boxer's rear hand and can move any way, while the other can only back off [S6, S7, S8] (COACH).
- **Counts in boxing:** NOT FOUND.
- **The closest count, from MMA (not boxing):** in UFC bouts ended by punches from 2020 to 2022 (271 contests), the most common finishing pattern between opposite stances was outside front foot plus a rear hook, 16 cases, 22.8% [S12] (SOURCED).
- **How often foot position decides an exchange:** NOT FOUND.

**Q4. The body.**
- **An orthodox boxer against a southpaw:** the southpaw's liver (right side) is on the near side.
  - Coaches teach a rear right to the liver or solar plexus, ideally with the lead foot outside [S10] (COACH).
  - One page says the orthodox lead left hook reaches the southpaw's liver on a "less obstructed" path [S11] (COACH; CHECK the source's quality).
- **A southpaw against an orthodox boxer:** the orthodox boxer's liver is on the far side.
  - The southpaw's lead right hook to the body lands on the spleen side, so reaching the liver takes the southpaw's *rear* left hook or uppercut, which is "more difficult" to land [S11] (COACH, CHECK).
  - One coach singles out the southpaw's rear uppercut to the body [S6] (COACH).
- **Counted:** body punches landed 74–75% in the open-stance groups [S1] (SOURCED). Liver hits by stance are NOT FOUND.

**Q5. Do southpaws win more?**
- **Among top-rated professionals, no** [S13] (SOURCED). This is the records of the top 340 boxers on BoxRec in June 2013:
  - orthodox boxers won 88% of their bouts and southpaws 87% (p = 0.71);
  - *both* stances won more against orthodox opponents (89%) than against southpaws (85%, p < 0.002);
  - a boxer's own stance and the opponent's stance didn't interact (p = 0.66).
- **Across a wider range of ability, yes, slightly.** Among 10,445 active male professionals on BoxRec in January 2019, a random southpaw had a better win share than a random orthodox boxer 52.4% of the time (p = 0.0007) [S14] (SOURCED).
- **Rated boxers, 1924 to 2012 (2,403 of them):** left-oriented boxers had better loss-to-win ratios, with no difference in the share of knockouts [S15] (SOURCED in the abstract; the exact numbers are NOT FOUND).
- **A small Turkish sample (22 boxers, measuring handedness, not stance):** left-handers did better (p < 0.01) [S16] (SOURCED).
- **MMA (1,468 fighters):** no difference (p = .33) [S17] (SOURCED).
- **A direct tally of orthodox-against-southpaw results:** NOT FOUND.

**Q6. The one rule** is at the end of the memo.

## Detail by question

### Q1. Punch land rates
- **The only counted split by stance [S1].**
  - The rear straight was the one punch that changed significantly in the open stance: it was thrown more (p = .013). The author puts this down to "the different angles" and to coaches telling the orthodox boxer to lead with the rear hand.
  - Jab land rates went up in one open-stance group and down in the other, so there's no clear effect on the jab. The number of jabs thrown didn't differ significantly either (SOURCED).
- **CompuBox notes [S2–S5].**
  - Each covers one boxer. All three orthodox boxers landed jabs at about half their usual rate or the division average, or less, against southpaws: 11.9% [S4], 11% [S3] and 9.8% [S2] (SOURCED).
  - The southpaws facing boxer C landed 21.3% of their jabs [S2] (SOURCED).
  - Power punches went both ways [S3, S4] (SOURCED).
- **The lead hook.**
  - Coaches say the orthodox left hook "has a hard time landing" in the open stance, because the front feet get in each other's way and the southpaw's jab gets there first. They call the southpaw's lead right hook hard for orthodox boxers to see [S6, S7] (COACH).
  - The one count doesn't back the coaches on the left hook: it landed 59.2% in the OS group [S1] (SOURCED, CHECK).
- **Uppercuts:** the least thrown punch in all four groups [S1] (SOURCED).
- **NOT FOUND:** any amateur or Olympic punch count split by stance, including open-stance land rates in IBA or Olympic boxing.

### Q2. Which hand stops what
- **The closed stance, as coaches teach it [S7] (COACH):**
  - "the opponent's jab and left hook ... can be blocked by the orthodox opponent's right hand";
  - "the opponent's right hand and overhand right ... can be blocked by the left glove or the left shoulder";
  - so one guard covers everything.
- **The open stance:**
  - The orthodox boxer "has to move his right hand in order to block both the straight left and overhand left" [S7] (COACH).
  - Southpaws "can easily throw straight through or around your right glove, so you'll have to keep moving your right hand back and forth" [S6] (COACH).
  - Against the open stance you can't block every angle "with a static guard" [S7] (COACH).
- **Against the jab, coaches teach these (COACH):**
  - **Slap it down with the lead hand:** the southpaw parries the orthodox jab down "with your lead hand ... slapping the opponent's glove down", then jabs over the top with the same hand. Blocking with the rear hand is also taught, as the "safer" choice [S8].
  - **Bait the slap:** the orthodox boxer can "feed him a lazy jab and let him slap it down with his right glove", then roll into a left hook [S6]. This assumes the southpaw stops the jab with the lead hand.
  - **The inside jab:** from the inside foot position, "your inside jab will keep his from landing" [S6].
  - **The outside parry:** a lead-hand parry lets a southpaw "force an orthodox jab off to your left" [S9].
- **Verdict on guess 1:** supported by the coaching and by the counts on single boxers. No pooled number exists (NOT FOUND).
- **Verdict on guess 2:** supported by the coaching, and the extra rear straights in [S1] fit it. Blocks counted by hand are NOT FOUND. Our own J50 counted defences in a women's amateur final, but not by stance.

### Q3. Lead foot outside
- **What it gains (COACH) [S6]:**
  - "the boxer with the front foot on the outside can punch with both hands whereas the other boxer can't reach with either";
  - so the outside boxer is in punching range while staying out of the other's;
  - that boxer can move any way, while the other "can only retreat";
  - from behind the other boxer's lead shoulder, the inside boxer's lead punches can't reach, and the rear hand "is too far away and also blocked by his own right arm".
- **What other coaches say (COACH):**
  - A southpaw on the outside has a "2-to-1" position: both hands against only the orthodox boxer's rear hand, because the orthodox lead arm is "out of range" [S7].
  - A southpaw who doesn't win the outside gets "caught out by the orthodox boxer's right hand" [S8].
  - "When one fighter's front foot is behind the other, more punches will be thrown. When the stances are even, both fighters are more likely to prod cautiously" [S6].
- **Counts:** NOT FOUND for boxing.
- **The MMA count:** in 271 UFC KO/TKO contests from 2020 to 2022, the most common finishing pattern between opposite stances was outside front foot plus a rear hook, 16 cases, 22.8% [S12] (SOURCED).
- **A figure not to use:** a search summary said the finisher had the outside foot in about 49–52% of opposite-stance finishes. I couldn't open the full paper (it returned a 403), so it's CHECK and not used.

- **Where the liver is:** on the right side of the body [S11] (COACH; anatomy, and MEMORY agrees).
- **An orthodox boxer against a southpaw [S10] (COACH):**
  - "The good thing with southpaws is that their liver is located on the front side of their stance."
  - The writer shows an orthodox boxer hurting a southpaw "by landing a lead right hand to the liver" with the front foot outside.
  - The writer also lists a jab then a right cross to the body, aiming at "the liver and the solar plexus".
- **A southpaw against an orthodox boxer:**
  - The southpaw's lead right body hook lands on the spleen side. Reaching the liver needs the rear left hook, "a more difficult punch to land" [S11] (COACH; CHECK, because the page is unsigned and dated 2026).
  - One coach tells orthodox boxers to watch the southpaw's rear uppercut to the body [S6] (COACH).

## Q6. The one rule
The finding with both counts and coaching behind it is this. In the open stance, the rear straight comes down the middle and only the rear hand stops it, while the two lead hands meet each other.
- **The count:** rear straights are thrown significantly more in open-stance bouts [S1].
- **The coaching:** the defender's rear hand has to move to cover both the straight and the overhand, where in the closed stance the lead glove or shoulder covered the straight [S7, S6].
- **The jab:** it ends up fighting the other man's lead hand, which fits the lower orthodox jab land rates against southpaws [S2–S4].

So if Ring Switches adds only one rule for a southpaw opponent, make it **a swap of which held hand stops straight punches in the open stance**. The rear straight (the cross) is then stopped only by the rear hand held high. The lead hand held high keeps stopping the jab.

Having the lead foot outside is what coaches stress most [S6–S8], but the game doesn't track foot position, and no boxing count backs it. That makes it a second step, not the one rule.
