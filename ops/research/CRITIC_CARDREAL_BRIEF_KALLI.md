I'm reading the walked bout turn by turn first, then I'll do the move table, the legal pairs, the scores and the 10/10 screen.

`SCORE real=6 depth=5 play=6` — all three are below the bar of 8.

Each man having his own position fixes the core fault. Standing out of the guard in two moves, with an up-kick as the boxer backs away, is exactly how a real fight goes. But the paper still lacks four things any fight fan expects: a knockdown, punching down from standing in the guard, punches from the jiu-jitsu fighter on top, and posture inside the guard. And five of the bout's turning points come down to "he had no card for it".

- **Real fight, 6:** the stand-out, the sit-down and the up-kick look right. The knockdown is missing, and so are the punches a fan would expect to see. Several turns in the walked bout are wrong (below).
- **Depth, 5:** follow him down or not, stand out or stay, pull or sit are all real choices. But the turns that decide the bout are settled by what's in each hand, not by a trade-off: no up-kick on turn 6, no sprawl on turns 9 and 19, no sweep on turn 12, no escape on turn 21. Posture isn't tracked, so punching from inside the guard costs nothing.
- **Play, 6:** the turns are quick and the words are plain. But 8 pairs of positions and 25 things to learn is heavy for the first screen, and the stand-ups after a single empty turn add filler turns.

**THE WALKED BOUT: turns where I'd ask "how is that possible?"**
The paper says there are none; I found nine.
- **Turn 6:** the referee stands the seated man up after one empty turn. A real referee waits a beat, calls "get up", and only then makes him stand. Make it two empty turns, with the call shown after the first.
- **Turn 8:** tie up and pull guard in the same volley. That makes "needs a tie he made earlier" meaningless, and it's the old pull from no grip under a new name. Either the tie has to stand for one of the boxer's turns, or the boxer's answer can hit the tie as well as the pull.
- **Turn 9:** "head pulled down", then he throws a right hand. With his posture broken in the closed guard, he can't punch to the head. He has to posture up first (a move), and that's the triangle's window.
- **Turn 12:** standing in a closed guard, then simply backing away. The legs are locked, so he has to break them open first. And standing in the guard is where a boxer does his best work: punching down at the man on his back. The model leaves that out.
- **Turn 15:** the rising man was at the edge of leg range, so he should end at punching range, not kicking range. Also, which of the jab and cross landed at the bell is never said.
- **Turn 16:** a body lock is the best takedown grip a grappler can get. A real jiu-jitsu fighter trips the boxer and lands on top; pulling guard from a body lock is a wasted grip.
- **Turn 18:** the pull "ends his volley" (table 4c), but he then lays a hip-bump sweep in the same turn. That breaks the paper's own rule. A hip bump also needs the top man sitting up, and right after a pull his posture is down. The scissor sweep or the flower sweep fits.
- **Turn 20:** mount to guard in one shrimp. The escape from mount leads to half guard (knee and elbow); full guard comes back from side control.
- **Turns 19 to 22:** the jiu-jitsu fighter has mount and never strikes. Under MMA rules, a fan expects punches from mount; a submission-only top player looks like a grappling match, not an MMA fight.

**THE MOVES (table 4c): wrong rows and what to write**
- **Tie up:** the boxer's only answer is to hit him on the way in. Add a frame (step off, a forearm to the collarbone) that stops the tie, and let the boxer clinch too (overhooks to smother); boxers do both.
- **Pull guard:** the window misses the punch as he falls, and Sprawl is the wrong word for it. Call the answer "Keep your base". After a failed pull, the tie is gone.
- **Follow him down:** the window is the up-kick only. Add a leg grab as the boxer steps in (ankle pick or sickle; the Sweep card already exists).
- **Get up in open space:** "STANDING at KICK" is wrong when the standing man was in leg range. Rising close ends at punching range.
- **Stand up in the guard:** the after-state is right. Add that standing opens a closed guard unless the man underneath pays to keep it locked ("Hold the legs"). Also allow punching down from here at full damage, answered by Sweep or by an armbar on the punching arm.
- **Recover guard:** from mount it should lead to half guard, and from side control to guard.
- **Ground punches from inside the guard:** each punch gives up an arm. Make it explicit: a punch from inside the guard opens that turn's window for Armbar or Triangle. That's the main risk the boxer weighs on the ground, and the paper doesn't spell it out.
- **The referee stand-up:** say whether standing in the guard (pair 4) counts as a ground position.
- **Right as written:** Sit down, Walk away, Punch down at half damage, Back away (with the up-kick and the ankle pick), Sweep, submissions, and escapes that keep both men down.

**THE LEGAL PAIRS (4b)**
- **Missing: the knockdown.** A boxer's punch drops the man, who ends seated or on his back, in leg range of the boxer. It's the commonest way a boxer gets the fight to the ground on his own terms. The boxer then decides: punch down, follow him down, or let him up. Without it, the boxer is never the one who chose the ground.
- **Missing: down, holding a leg.** The man on his back holds the standing man's ankle or leg. That's the moment between "in leg range" and a sweep. It can wait, but the Sweep card from under a standing man needs it to make sense.
- **Doesn't happen in this pairing:** pair 7 (one man on his knees). Neither man shoots a takedown, and getting up takes a single move. Pair 8 (both down, apart) only follows a failed sweep. Keep both in the engine, but neither needs a word on the first screen.

**THE RULES (4d) AND THE LOOPS (4e)**
- **Free stall:** holding a clinch with nothing laid. Rule 7 says an idle clinch is broken up, but table 4c has no row for it, so the jiu-jitsu fighter can hold the tie for as long as he likes, waiting to draw a pull. Add a row: if a tie is held through a whole exchange with nothing laid, the referee breaks it to punching range.
- **Free exit:** standing in the guard and doing nothing. If that counts as idle on the ground, the referee stands both men up and the boxer skips the up-kick that backing away would risk. Rule: from pair 4, the referee's stand-up leaves the boxer standing in leg range and the man underneath still down; he then gets up as normal.
- **Harsher than a real referee:**
  - A point off for the third sit in a round. Referees warn often and take a point almost never.
  - Counting pulls that lead to nothing as timidity. A pull is an attempt, not a stall.
  - The one-turn stand-up of the grounded man.

  Fix: the referee calls "get up", then stands him up after a second empty turn. Warn on the second time in a bout and take a point only after that warning. Never count a pull. The judges' control score already punishes sitting.
- **Fine as written:** recover guard and pass, and step in and step out. The referee stand-up on top stays.

**WHAT WAS LEFT OUT**
- **The paper can't look real without these, and none of them is listed:** the knockdown, posture in the guard (up or broken, one flag), the boxer punching down from standing in the guard, and the jiu-jitsu fighter's punches from mount and side control (a rule, no card).
- **I agree these can wait:** the fence, the turtle, leg locks against a standing man, other rule sets, the judges' order, and the wrestle-up. The body-lock trip (M20) is the one I'd move up: without it, turn 16 rings false.
- **The first screen could drop:** pairs 7 and 8, the August 2026 reading of "grounded" (it changes nothing for this pairing), and the closed, open and half kinds of guard. Show only closed or open; half guard only matters for the kimura.

**TO REACH 10/10**, most important first:
1. **Add a knockdown.** A punch for 6 or more that drops him to 10 health or under puts him SEATED in leg range. The boxer chooses: punch down (half damage), follow him down (up-kick window), or let him up. The referee steps in if punches land with nothing coming back for 2 turns. This is the boxer's own road to the ground.
2. **Make the basic defences floor moves paid in wind,** so the turning points are choices, not draws:
   - Keep your base (against a pull or a sweep): 2 wind.
   - Escape a hold: 2 wind.
   - Up-kick from your back: 2 wind.
   - Hold the legs: 2 wind.

   Cards then become stronger versions, for example a sprawl card that also leaves him on his knees. No more "he had no card for it".
3. **Add a posture flag in the guard, UP or BROKEN.** A pull leaves it broken. Posture up is a move, and the triangle and armbar can answer it. Punches to the head need posture up. The hip bump needs posture up; the scissor and flower sweeps work when it's broken.
4. **Each punch from inside the guard gives up an arm:** that turn, Armbar or Triangle may answer it. That's the trade the boxer makes on the ground.
5. **Let the boxer punch down from standing in the guard at full damage,** with Sweep or Armbar as the answer. Standing in the closed guard opens it unless the man underneath pays Hold the legs.
6. **Give the jiu-jitsu fighter punches from on top** (a rule, no card): half damage from mount or side control, answered by Cover up or Escape. Mount then threatens two things.
7. **Fix the rows:**
   - The tie has to stand for one of the boxer's turns before a pull, or the boxer's answer can hit both.
   - Pull guard truly ends the volley.
   - Get up from leg range ends at punching range.
   - Recover guard goes from mount to half guard and from side control to guard.
   - A failed pull drops the tie.
   - The boxer can frame against a tie, and clinch himself.
8. **Fix the referee's caps:**
   - A clinch held idle for one exchange is broken up.
   - The grounded man gets a "get up" call, then is stood up on the second empty turn.
   - A warning comes before any point off, and pulls never count as stalling.
   - Say whether standing in the guard counts as the ground, and keep the boxer's up-kick risk if the referee stands them up.
9. **Add the body-lock trip:** a tie that's a body lock lets the jiu-jitsu fighter trip to the top, answered by Keep your base.
10. **Re-walk the bout** with these changes and measure it. Count the bout's turning points decided by a missing card (the target is none), the share of the jiu-jitsu fighter's ground turns where Follow, Walk away or Punch down has a margin over the others, and the bout length (about 5 minutes, which needs a real test).
