# R28 Cutman - design map v3: DEPTH (S424, Kalli)

v2 scored 4/10 with the operator because the player makes one call, once, when the answer is already obvious. v3 turns the cut into a thread that starts at hiring, runs through the camp, is fought over round by round, and leaves a mark on the career. Every number marked **invented** is a design value for tuning. Every number marked **anchor** has a real-world source, and anything John has not yet confirmed is marked **CHECK (John)**.

## The idea in three lines
1. You never see the true cut, only a range. A better cutman shows a narrower range, and the range never lies.
2. Both corners see the cut. From the next round the other corner hunts it, so every minute is a race between closing the cut and their aim on it.
3. Every cut leaves scar tissue, and scar tissue makes the next cut likelier. The career choices are rest, style, surgery and who you hire.

## The decisions, in order

### A. Before the fight

**1. Which cutman you hire** (Club > People, a staff card with three tiers; you swap the tier between bouts, not during one)

| Tier | Amateur show | Pro night | What the corner sees of the cut | Closes a cut in one minute | Scar left by a closed cut |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| None | $0 | $0 | Only the band word (MARKED / CUT / BAD) | Amateur: nobody may work the cut (anchor: England Boxing 2.4.1). Pro: the coach works it at half strength | +25 |
| Club cutman | $50 | $150 | Range ±15 | 50% | +20 |
| Seasoned cutman | $120 | $300 | Range ±8 | 70% | +15 |
| Name cutman | pro only | $700 | Range ±4 | 85% | +10 |

The prices are invented; v2's $150 pro and $50 amateur are kept for the club tier.

- **The trade:** money every fight night against seeing the truth and against long-term scar.
- **Why no tier always wins:**
  - **None is right** for a boxer with no scar on a low-stakes club show. Cut chance is low (see 9), and $50 a show over a season of 10 shows is $500, which is serious money for a club that earns $35 a member.
  - **Club is right** for most amateur nights.
  - **Seasoned is right** for a scarred boxer or a regional final.
  - **Name is right** only for a pro on a ranked or title night, where the purse is $3,000 or more and a TKO loss costs a place.
  - A name cutman on a 4-round tune-up spends about a quarter of the gym's 25% share.

**2. The scouting tells "Cutter" and "Head-clasher"** (Fight prep > What your plan should answer, from tape study like any tell)
- **Cutter** (sharp, accurate punches): the cut chance per landed head punch ×1.5 (invented).
- **Head-clasher** (leads with the head on the way in, more often in orthodox against southpaw): an accidental head-clash cut can open in any round, 8% a bout against 1% normally (invented). Head-clash cuts follow a different rule at the stoppage (see 7).
- **The trade:** a tape session spent finding this tell is not spent finding the opponent's weapon or weakness. Tape sessions are scarce: one every 2 camp days, and the weapon opens first.
- **Why it is not dominant:** against a low-power volume puncher it wastes a session. Against a cutter with a scarred boxer it decides the night.

**3. The plan line "Protect the eye"** (Playbook > Game plan, a new situation: "When the eye is cut")
- Answers: **Hands high, cover it** (hits on the cut -40%, own output -10%), **Turn the cut side away** (hits on the cut -25%, footwork changes and the jab lands -10%), or **Fight on as normal**. All invented.
- The line only fires while a cut is open.
- **The trade:** the line uses one of the plan's 4 to 6 places, and those places hold the lines that win the fight.
- **Why it is not dominant:** for a boxer with no scar and an opponent with no Cutter tell, that place is better spent on a winning line. For a "Marks easily" boxer against a Cutter it is worth a place.

### B. In the round (live fight, FIGHTDISPLAY2 side panel)

**4. The cut opens and is hidden**
- A cut has a true depth from 0 to 100, which the player never sees.
- The panel shows the band word and, during the minute, the cutman's range (for example "CUT, 35-50").
- The doctor stops the bout at depth 70 or over. Each landed punch on it adds +4 to +8 depth (invented).
- Cut chance per bout: amateur 3 rounds 6%, pro 4-6 rounds 14% (invented). The direction is anchored: cuts are 51% of Nevada pro injuries (J55 3b), and amateurs see fewer. Whether women amateurs wear headguards and elite men do not is **CHECK (John)**.

**5. They hunt it, and you can hunt theirs**
- From the round after a cut opens, the opponent's Aim switches to "the cut". Their punches land on it at +1 per exchange, and their body work drops by the same.
- You see this as the clue "They're going for the eye".
- The same Aim, "The cut eye", is yours when their cut opens. The shout **Hands up, move your head** (existing) cuts hits on your cut by half for that exchange, at its normal energy cost.
- **The trade:** hunting their cut takes punches away from your plan's body work, and the plan's pressure only builds from planned answers.
- **Why it is not dominant:** hunting is right against a cut deeper than 40 with a round or more left. It is wrong when you are well ahead and only need to see out the bell, or when their corner has a good cutman and the cut will close anyway.

### C. The minute (the corner minute, dark and tight)

**6. Two pairs of hands across four needs**
- The cutman has 3 hands points for the cut and the swelling. The coach has 3 for the call, the breath and water, and talk. That folds in the existing RECOVER 3 points.
- Each extra point on the same need is worth less: the first is worth 100%, the second 60%, the third 30% (invented).

| Need | One point does | Who |
|---|---|---|
| The cut | Closes the cut at the tier's chance, or takes depth -8 if it doesn't close | Cutman only (in the amateur corner, by the rules) |
| The swelling | Halves next round's eye loss (v2's number) | Cutman, or the coach at half strength |
| The call | Plan change or a SET-UP (existing) | Coach |
| Breath and water | The existing RECOVER effect | Coach |

- With no cutman, the coach's 3 points must cover the swelling too, so the breath suffers.
- A cut and a closing eye in the same minute is the real squeeze: 3 cutman points never cover both fully.
- **Why no split always wins:** all 3 on the cut is right when it sits near 70 and the doctor looms. 2 on the swelling is right when the eye is closing and the cut is shallow. And 0 on the cut is right in the last minute of an amateur bout when you are clearly ahead and the cut is under 50, because the doctor won't stop it this side of the bell.

**7. Stop, send out, or race** (under a divider, never pre-selected, Ready waits for it)
- **What the player sees before choosing:**
  - The cut's range.
  - The doctor's chance next round, worked out from the range: under 50 is "unlikely", 50-69 "possible", 70 or over "will be stopped".
  - The cards as the corner reads them: "Ahead, probably 2 rounds to 1", a range, never the judges' real numbers.
  - The cut's cause: a punch or a head clash.
  - The stakes line, as in v2.
- **The rule a stoppage follows:**
  - A punch cut is a TKO loss for the cut boxer.
  - A head-clash cut stopped in pro boxing after round 4 goes to the scorecards as a technical decision; before then it is a no contest or a technical draw. This is an anchor (most commissions, the WBC and BBBofC style) but **CHECK (John)** for the BBBofC wording.
  - The amateur rule for an accidental cut is **CHECK (John)**. I don't know the World Boxing and England Boxing rule.
- **The options:**
  - **Pull out.** As in v2: a loss, the eye is safe, the least scar.
  - **Send out to survive.** Pace Save and the line Protect the eye switches on for this round. Their hunt still runs.
  - **Send out to race.** Pace Push plus Go for the finish while there is time. Energy costs more, a stoppage chance is real, and so is a wide-open counter.
- **Why no option always wins:**
  - **Pull out** is right when the doctor is close, you're behind and there's a scar (each new cut on scar sets up a lasting one).
  - **Survive** is right when you're ahead and the head-clash rule will send it to the cards. In pro boxing, getting past round 4 turns a "no contest" into a decision win.
  - **Race** is right when you're behind with a punch cut, because the doctor will end it anyway and the only way to win is a stoppage.
- **The boxer's say:** a line in the boxer's own words, as in v2. Heart and morale decide whether it is pride or truth, and that matters after the bout (see 9).

### D. After the bout and the career

**8. Scar tissue per eye, 0-100, on the profile under "Marks easily"**
- Each cut adds the tier's scar (see 1), and each punch landed on an open cut adds +2.
- **The cut chance per bout is the base × (1 + scar/100).** So a scar of 60 makes cuts 1.6× likelier.
- "Marks easily" shows when scar reaches 40 or more.
- Scar drops by 1 a week by itself, and by 3 in a rest week (invented).

**9. Healing choices** (the week review and the goal talk, as actions)

| Choice | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Rest | 4 weeks with no offers | Scar -12 |
| Change style | A goal: drill a Defence goal plus "Hands high" for 6 weeks, about 6 points of output skill not trained | Base cut chance -25% for good |
| Scar revision surgery | $2,500 and 8 weeks out | Scar -40 |
| Hire a better cutman | Fight-night price | Closes cuts with less scar, from 1 |

- The surgery option is anchored in that scar revision is done in boxing, but its size is invented. **CHECK (John).**
- **Why no choice always wins:** rest costs a show and the money from it. Surgery costs most and fits a pro before a title run. A style change is slow and a pressure fighter loses edge. The cutman is fight-night money every time.

**10. Trust after the call** (existing morale-as-trust)

| What happened | Morale |
|---|---|
| Pulled out a boxer who was telling the truth ("I'm fine") | -6 |
| Pulled out a boxer whose line was pride | 0 |
| Sent out and stopped by the doctor | -4, or -8 if Heart is low |
| Sent out and won | +5 |

All invented. Trust feeds the existing 1.05-0.7 factor on the corner's word next fight, so a bad call carries forward.

## Dominant-strategy check, short
- **Always hire the best cutman:** no, because the money only pays back on scarred boxers or big nights.
- **Always all points on the cut:** no, because the breath and the eye cost the round.
- **Always send out:** no, because race and survive need the cards and the cause on your side, and a pulled-out boxer avoids scar.
- **Always pull out:** no, because it gives away fights the head-clash rule would have won on the cards.
- **Always protect the eye:** no, because the line costs a plan place.
- **Always hunt theirs:** no, because it costs the planned body work and their cutman may close it.

5. **Rival gyms remember:** a known scarred boxer draws Cutter opponents more often in future offers, so scar shapes matchmaking.
6. **The picture, before any build:** a mockup of the CUT card with its range bar, the hands split, the cards read and the three buttons, judged by the coach critic.
