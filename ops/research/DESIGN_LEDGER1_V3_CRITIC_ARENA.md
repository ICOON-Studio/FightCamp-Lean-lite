# Boxer contracts — independent design review

SCORE depth=7 general=6

**DEPTH: 7/10.** The best choice is where to put the next dollar or week of training, not whether a boxer has earned back the last one. Paying a stipend can protect preparation; splitting it can keep two boxers at the cost of both training less; moving it protects one at the other's expense. Going pro trades an amateur goal and a known weekly bill for signing costs, a different schedule and uncertain earnings. Buying back a purse share trades cash before the next bill for income over later bouts. These are real choices. The warning rules work against that depth: an amateur paid $250 each week needs a $450 bout more often than once every 1.8 weeks just to cover the stipend from tickets. At one bout every two weeks the shortfall is already $50 before camp costs. Yet three consecutive losing weeks may never occur because each bout resets the streak. Conversely, a good prospect on a compulsory layoff can look like the obvious cut. The map doesn't make either case a good coaching decision.

**GENERAL DESIGN: 6/10.** Separate an offer to take your boxer from an offer to fight your boxer: that is the right cut. Reusing the existing booking, departure and deal rules makes this buildable in pieces. But knowing a constant is not the same as having a ledger. $35 monthly dues and eight members added for a win do not establish eight incremental paying members forever, especially near capacity. Weekly net alternates between recurring-only and actual income less costs; “net worth” is neither cash nor worth. Orange can mean six weeks without a booking or six weeks losing money, red alternates between no bout and no bout in the next three weeks, and the chart uses yet another threshold. The always-$250 release saving is wrong for an unpaid boxer, a split stipend or a pro. This misses the 7/7 bar.

**PLAY: 6/10.** The good Monday is “the bout landed; the stipend got your boxer through camp; here's what actually came in.” The good deadline is “can you pay the bonus and still make the next bill?” Right now the quiet Monday brings another chart, and the bad Monday puts a cut button next to a losing streak. Give the player one thing to look forward to and report how the previous decision turned out. Don't make them clear every boxer every week.

## What the options change

| Option | What changes, and when it matters |
|---|---|
| Keep / Release | Keeping alone changes nothing and guarantees no income. Releasing frees a roster place and removes only costs that actually stop; it can cost cash, reputation and teammates' loyalty. Past spending does not change. A booked bout can block release. |
| Pay / Pause stipend | Trades the actual weekly amount for training availability. Show the effect on the next Week and booked camp, and the first bill affected. Idle loyalty drift is not a penalty caused by the toggle; show it in both paths if the schedule is unchanged. |
| Match / Half / Move | Match answers the ask and gains loyalty; for a stipend ask it commits the full payment. Half splits a payment, slows both boxers and costs the teammate loyalty. Move transfers it, puts the teammate on a day job and can start a grievance. Show both boxers' outcomes and the lock end date; Half and Move need an eligible teammate and the right kind of ask. |
| Promise / Let Go / Tell It Straight | Promise spends cash and commits a show slot and a bout; a broken promise can cost the boxer. Let Go starts departure rather than matching. Tell It Straight is a gated, once-per-approach loyalty intervention that can land or fail; it isn't a sixth answer or free reassurance. Show eligible choices and compare them before committing. |
| Accept / Not This Show / Pass | Accept books the show and replaces the waiting sheet offer; compare the date, weight and other available bout first. Not This Show spends the once-per-season free refusal with that rival, not a rebooking. Pass, including lapse, normally transfers ladder points; show the free exemption when already booked or hurt. |
| Turn pro / Stay amateur / Ask for terms | Turn pro ends the stipend and starts a dated debit and an uncertain debut return. Staying keeps the amateur route open and delays the next call. Where offered, asking can change date, opponent or split, not just pay: reuse the existing promoter comparison and its one-ask rule. Different ticket thresholds are not interchangeable. |
| Keep share / Take lower share | Pay cash now for the difference in future gym receipts, or keep the cash and give up the difference. At top ten, 5 percentage points need $50,000 in eligible gross receipts just to recover $2,500; after taking 20%, paying $5,000 to keep it instead of taking 17% needs about $166,667. Neither implies a loyalty gain. If the current and lower share are equal, paying a bonus buys nothing. |
| Find Bout / Review / Dismiss / Compare | Find Bout opens existing eligible booking choices, not a guaranteed payday. Review sets a Monday reminder, not a contract change. Dismiss hides a notice, not the risk. Compare is useful when two boxers compete for one payment or roster place; sorting is navigation, not strategy. |

**Missing:** “Keep & review after recovery/the bout” should write a dated plan, with a reason and next legal bout date. “Compare pay and pause” should show the changes to gym cash and preparation before the toggle. “Release after the bout” should be a reminder and a fresh quote, not automatic release. For each deal, show the lowest club cash before the next bill and how much eligible income makes paying worthwhile. These add decisions without inventing a transfer market, injury status or replacement fee.

**Useless or misleading as drawn:** Bare Keep duplicates doing nothing. Blind bulk toggles hide locks and teammate effects; cut them. Replace separate sparklines, a full trend and a break-even card with one expandable history; an unknown break-even is not a date. Cut 5/10-bout totals presented as forecasts and any fixed quote for “future purses lost.” A reminder and Dismiss need distinct return rules or only one should survive. Put boxer and rival identifiers in card or column headers; the Approach copy and challenge details should not repeat names in action text.

## To reach 10/10

1. **Make the cash true first.** Record each actual receipt or payment once, with date, category and boxer ID when directly linked. Weekly net includes all posted income and costs that week; cumulative net sums weekly net and persists across years and departures. Keep unallocated dues and shared bills in the club column, not in a boxer's cash total. If showing an allocated fee or estimated membership effect, label it separately and never add it back into club cash. Reconcile the sum of boxer rows and shared cash to the club book. Fix annual fees, per-bout charges and first-time debut costs before building alerts. Use “Cash in / Cash out / Net,” not “worth.” This needs transaction attribution where the current books don't supply it.
2. **Compare next moves from the same state.** Put Keep as is, Pay/Pause, and Release (when legal) on one eight-week comparison. Show next bill, lowest projected club cash, next booked bout, avoidable spend, exit fee, roster place and relevant loyalty changes. Use dated bills and actual stipend/lock state; put booked but unsettled returns in a conditional case, with a no-bout case alongside. Don't extrapolate an exact break-even from unbooked bouts. For deals use current terms and incremental receipts; for debut reuse the existing offer's dates and ticket assumptions. Update the preview before commit and keep a receipt afterward. Forecasting training and cash together is new work; reuse the Week outputs where available and don't invent precise gains where they aren't.
3. **Turn warnings into coaching jobs.** Use one state table everywhere. For example: yellow after two completed four-week blocks with negative direct net, or when the next bill leaves cash below zero; orange when eligible and unbooked for six weeks with no valid booking in the next three; red when the projected no-bout cash path threatens the gym's eight-week closure rule. Show the cause, not just the colour. Stop the idle count during compulsory rest or blocked entry windows and show the return date; cash risk still applies. Review snoozes yellow/orange to the chosen Monday unless the reason clears or severity rises. Red stays visible without forcing a release; a pause or booking only clears it if the underlying cash risk clears. Link to legal offers, Week or the relevant bill. On the review date show what changed against the earlier plan.
4. **Wire conditional choices, not fixed columns.** Use the rule-provided availability and rejection reason for each answer. Show deadlines and lapse outcomes before choosing, actual costs and teammate effects for each affected boxer, and the booked show and broken-promise consequence before Promise. Keep certain loyalty changes separate from conditional drift; mark assumptions and never predict a departure date from an unchanged schedule as a certainty. Preserve the existing deal and debut screens as the only places to commit those deals. Home flags point there; Monday stays a summary, not a new modal or a second control panel.
5. **Test decisions, not a preferred strategy.** Match starts and random seeds while changing one decision, then repeat across a cash-rich gym, a cash crisis, a suspended prospect, two boxers sharing a stipend and a pro already on a reduced share. Start pro fixtures at each milestone; a 12-week fresh start need not reach a belt. Check the warning lifecycle, cash reconciliation to rounding, locks and booked-bout guards, release quotes, and actual versus forecast on the review date. Require examples where paying and taking the lower share each win, where pausing saves the gym but costs preparation, and where keeping a recovering boxer beats cutting. If a policy wins everywhere, fix the decision model rather than celebrating the pass line.

In the main area, use roughly two thirds for the current decision: side-by-side paths with cash on bill day, booked bout, preparation and loyalty on matching rows. Use the other third for the reason, deadline and one primary action, with a review-date control underneath. Below, show a single eight-week cash plot with actuals solid and conditional paths dashed; open the dated in/out entries from it.
