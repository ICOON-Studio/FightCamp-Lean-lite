The game, as much as you need
Two boxers plan a "beat" at the same time, then it plays. In a beat each man may use his lead hand, his rear hand and his
feet, in an order he picks (up to three moments). A hand that does not punch is HELD high or low and stops one kind of punch:
lead hand high: straight punches to the head (jab, cross); lead hand low: body punches;
rear hand high: punches from the side (lead hook, overhand); rear hand low: uppercuts.
Three ranges: Inside, Mid, Long. Health 16-18, stamina 8 (a held hand gives 1 back, a punch costs 1-3).
The punches (damage / stamina / ranges): jab 1 / 1 / Mid, Long; body jab 1 / 1 / Mid, Long; lead hook 3 / 2 / Inside, Mid;
lead uppercut 3 / 2 / Inside; body hook 2 / 2 / Inside, Mid; cross 3 / 2 / Inside, Mid; overhand 4 / 3 / Inside, Mid (slow);
rear uppercut 4 / 3 / Inside (slow); body cross 2 / 2 / Inside, Mid.
The FEINT (lead hand, 1 stamina, no damage): if the other man is holding a hand against the line you then punch on, the
feint pulls that hand away for the rest of the beat, so the next punch finds the line open.
The problem, measured
One loop wins too much: step in, FEINT, OVERHAND, then clinch to get stamina back. A bot playing only that beats the three
opponents in 38%, 63% and 70% of bouts (the bar is under 30%). Without the clinch it wins 7-13%, so the feint-then-overhand
is the real exploit: nobody can cover it. The overhand after a feint lands 83-94% of the time.
Three fixes were measured and each one killed the feint itself:
D: the feint opens the overhand only at Inside -> the feint and the overhand both drop to 1-2% of all beats (floor 3%).
E: a punch that lands on you in the moment you feint wastes the feint -> feint 1-2% of beats; the nearest (8 of 9 bars).
F: both -> the loop falls to 22-25% (passes), but the feint and the overhand are nearly never chosen.
So the fix must cut the feint -> overhand opening WITHOUT making the feint worthless.

THREE BEST FIXES — Best First
✅ FIX 1 — Feint shifts guard PARTIALLY, not fully
1. The rule
When feinted upon, the defender's relevant held hand shifts partially: it no longer fully blocks that line, but still gives 50% chance to block any follow-up punch on that line for the rest of the beat. It does not fully pull away.
2. Real boxing parallel
A feint makes your opponent twitch/hesitate and their guard shifts but doesn't completely vanish — they're still covering partially, just slower and less secure. You see the opening but it's not a free pass.
3. Why both stay viable
Feint still good: it still opens up all punches — you just don't get 100% guarantee. Great for setting up hooks, jabs, uppercuts — you just don't get a guaranteed overhand anymore.
Overhand still good: it still hits hard (4 damage) — you just don't land it 90%+ off a feint. It rewards genuine reads, not a single combo loop.
4. Risks & number to watch
Risk: If partial block is too strong, feints disappear entirely; too weak and the exploit stays.
Watch: Overhand land rate after feint — target 45–55%, not 83–94%.
Stability note: Since guards still mostly work, power punches don't get nerfed overall — bouts stay snappy, Long range doesn't dominate because jabs still get interrupted by partial coverage.
✅ FIX 2 — Feint has RANGE LIMITS on what it can open
1. The rule
A lead-hand feint can only pull/partially disable the lead-side defensive hand — it cannot affect the rear hand at all. Since the overhand is blocked by the rear hand high, a lead feint NEVER removes that protection.
2. Real boxing parallel
You can only really move the hand you're threatening — feinting with your lead hand draws their lead hand; their rear hand stays home protecting the other side.
3. Why both stay viable
Feint still good: it still works perfectly to set up jabs, lead hooks, body shots — all the things a feint should open up.
Overhand still good: lands normally when you force a real mistake or catch them out of position — just not from a cheap lead feint.
4. Risks & number to watch
Risk: Feint usage drops too low if it no longer helps with your "big punch."
Watch: Feint frequency per bout — keep it above 8–10% of beats, not 1–2%.
Stability note: Jabs at Long range don't grow — feints still work there just fine, they just don't bypass the rear guard.
✅ FIX 3 — FEINT costs more if UNUSED, or expires QUICKLY
1. The rule
After you feint, the defender's hand is only distracted for ONE single punch. If you feint and then do something else — move, clinch, or wait — the hand snaps back instantly. You cannot feint → walk in → overhand → clinch in the same beat; the feint window closes immediately after one action.
2. Real boxing parallel
A feint creates a split-second opening — it vanishes in a blink. If you don't strike right then, the opportunity is gone. You can't milk one feint across multiple steps.
3. Why both stay viable
Feint still good: deadly when you commit and strike immediately — excellent for jabs, body shots, quick hooks. Just not for slow power punches that take time to set up.
Overhand still good: use it when they're already out of position or you've broken their stance another way — it's still your hardest punch.
4. Risks & number to watch
Risk: Timing becomes too punishing — players stop feinting altogether.
Watch: Feint → punch completion % — target 60–70% (was 90%+), meaning some get interrupted but most still connect.
Stability note: Clinching after feint dies as a tactic — stamina recovery no longer comes for free after feinting; pace stays balanced, no Long-range inflation.
