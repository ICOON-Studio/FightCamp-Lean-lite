# YOUR JOB (fresh critic): one fighter's Training screen, judged as a GAME screen, not as an information sheet

The owner wants: art where it matters, little text, and from the picture alone it is clear how you set the training, what your options
are and what each improves, with tiredness and intensity shown. The art must match the rest of the game's screens (the camp plan).

What the picture shows (the player's one boxer, 12 days before a club-show bout; this is the last week whose pad nights count for it):
- Top bar: the club, the date, money, a Weekly card flag (an event to answer) and RUN THE WEEK (the one pulsing button).
- Header: the fighter, the next bout and a countdown, a WIN dial (the coach's plan against the player's plan: the player's changes add
  +4), the fighter's goal bar, and what is known about the opponent (two habits; one found on film this week).
- THIS WEEK (the centre board): a week goal (Peak for Sat 28 / Build / Recover), Undo and Coach's plan, a line saying next week is the
  taper. Seven days, each with a MORNING card (picture, a round icon ringed in the colour of the stat it trains, what it trains, tiredness
  as flames, and its Light / Normal / Hard stop as a letter; the selected day shows the lever) and an EVENING card (pads with the coach -
  the punch drilled and when it becomes Usable, the level at which it can be thrown in a fight - film of the opponent, sparring, or an
  open night). Only the player's own changes carry a green tag: the points they add to the WIN dial. Under the cards, ONE tiredness
  curve for the week with named bands (Fresh, Working, Tired from 72, Overreached from 80). A sparring card is being dragged onto
  Saturday evening: a dashed line on the curve shows where tiredness would go, and a strip under the curve shows what the drop buys and
  what it costs before the player lets go.
- SESSIONS (bottom): every session as a round icon to drag, ringed in its stat's colour; mornings left of the divider, evenings right.
- Right column: ON FIGHT NIGHT, a checklist that updates as cards move (weight, the goal, the tiredness peak, the injury chance, the
  moves ready to throw) with one line tying the film to the plan; the coach's pick as a handwritten note with how sure; the clicked card
  (Wednesday evening's pads) with what it gives and gives up; USE COACH'S PICK.

Questions, in this order:
1. LOGIC CHECK: anything the game could not do at that moment, any number that contradicts another on the same screen, any name used
   outside a header.
2. AS A GAME, score 1 to 10: would you enjoy planning a week here? Is it clear in five seconds how you set the training, what your options
   are and what each improves? Is there art where it matters? Compare with screens you know (Football Manager training, Punch Club,
   Motorsport Manager, Two Point, Boxing Manager on Steam).
3. DEPTH, score 1 to 10: is the week a real decision (trade-offs that change), or is there one right plan?
4. Which options are missing, which are useless, what would each change?
5. END with "To reach 10/10", most important first. Then describe your 10/10 screen for this as a layout on 1920x1080, panel by panel.
