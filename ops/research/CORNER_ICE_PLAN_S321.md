# Corner ice and cut work on her figure (S321 plan, for the operator's OK)

Your idea (S321): the boxer on the side panel shows blood that runs, and you pick where to ice to calm her and win back condition.
Short answer: yes, it fits, and most of the machinery is already in the game. One part needs changing so it stays true to boxing.

## What ice really does in the 60 seconds (our own research, J55 + J68, sources listed there)
- **On a swelling (eye, cheek):** cold plus pressure for 10-45 s slows the bleeding under the skin, so the eye stays open next round.
  The cutman's tool is the enswell (cold steel) or an ice bag; one study found the ice bag cools the skin more than the steel.
- **On a cut:** ice does not close it. Pressure, then a swab of adrenaline 1:1000 held 10-30 s, then Vaseline to seal it.
- **On the back of the neck:** she feels cooler and less tired (lower perceived effort). It does NOT speed up the heart-rate drop in
  60 s in the studies we found. Her heart drops about 26-35 beats in the minute whatever you do; sitting and breathing do that.
- **"Cool the muscles to gain condition":** not real. Muscles do not recover from ice in a minute. So in the game ice must not refill
  stamina. It can calm her (composure) and keep a hurt place from getting worse.

## What the game does today (Lean, update 21-23)
- The cutman has ONE job a minute: enswell the left eye, enswell the right eye, close the cut, or nothing (his own pick is lit).
- The second's ice is placed for you, on the ribs. That ribs ice is our invention: real corners ice the neck and the swellings.
- The body map shows hurt places as colours. Nothing moves on it.

## The change I propose
1. **Tap her figure to choose.** Cutman: tap a cut or a swollen eye (one place). Second: tap the neck (calm, +composure next round)
   or a swelling (slows it). His own picks stay lit so a player who taps nothing still gets a sensible corner.
2. **Blood that runs.** An open cut drips on the figure, faster the worse it is; a swelling grows and the eye narrows. A closed cut
   stops dripping. You see the problem before you read a number.
3. **Not enough hands.** Pro bout: cutman and second work while the coach talks. Amateur bout (no cutman, one person in the ring):
   if the coach works the cut, he has time for only ONE instruction instead of two. That is the real trade (J55: cut work and coaching
   compete for the one person in the ring).
4. **Stakes next round.** A cut left open bleeds into her eye (more misses on that side) and at the next stoppage the referee can
   call the doctor; a swelling left alone closes the eye over two rounds. A doctor stop loses the fight.

## Will it be fun?
- **Fun when it is a choice:** two bad places, one pair of hands, the clock running. That dilemma (save the eye or stop the bleeding?)
  is the drama a manager game lives on, and you feel the answer in the next round.
- **Not fun as a mini-game:** holding an ice pack for 15 s, a timing bar, or rubbing a spot. A fight has up to 12 corners and a career
  has dozens of fights; anything with hands-on dexterity gets skipped by fight five. So: one tap per job, the time it takes is
  shown, never played out by the player.
- **Not fun when there is one right answer.** Most corners will be quiet (nothing hurt: ice the neck, done in one tap or none). The
  choice only matters when two places are bad, and that is when it should feel tense.

## Size
Small: the jobs, the map and the round effects exist (BodyMap.corner, CornerOverlay._cutman_jobs). New: taps on the figure, the drip
and swelling drawing, neck ice -> composure, the amateur one-instruction rule, the doctor call at a stoppage. About one session.
