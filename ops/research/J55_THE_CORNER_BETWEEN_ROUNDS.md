## 0. Headline answer

**The real corner minute is about 45 working seconds, shared between a doctor-style job (water, breathing, cut, swelling) and a coaching job, and real coaches talk far more than the advice says they should. Boxing law is almost uniform on the clock and the headcount, and divided on coaching volume and on what a corner may put on a cut. The game should model one authored minute with a small instruction budget, a partial heart-rate recovery, and cut work that competes with coaching for the same person in the ring.**

1. **The clock is the same in every code.** Rest is 1 minute in all of them (England Boxing, IBA, World Boxing, USA Boxing, BBBofC, Nevada, ABC boxing and MMA). The corner must be clear of the ring about 10 seconds before the next round (a warning in every code; the BBBofC adds a second call, "Seconds out", 5 seconds before the start). Usable corner time is therefore roughly 45 to 50 seconds. SOURCED, rules table in section 1 (S1, [S3](https://www.iba.sport/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/20230209-IBA-Technical-Competition-Rules-1.pdf), S5, S6, S9, [S12](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nac/NAC-467.html), [S14](https://www.abcboxing.com/abc-regulatory-guidelines/), [S16](https://www.abcboxing.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Unified-Rules-of-MMA-8.2026.pdf)).
2. **Headcount is nearly uniform too.** Three seconds is the amateur norm (England Boxing 1 in ring, 1 on apron, 1 on floor; IBA and World Boxing 3 with 2 on the apron and 1 in the ring). Pro bodies allow 4 (BBBofC; California; Nevada, New Jersey and ABC for titles). One person inside the ropes is the rule nearly everywhere. Nevada is the outlier: in boxing "only a cutman may be inside the ring ropes during a period of rest" ([S12](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nac/NAC-467.html), NAC 467.635(2)). SOURCED.
3. **Coaching in the rest is allowed everywhere; coaching during the round is where codes split.** ABC says a second "may coach his/her boxer" in the rest ([S14](https://www.abcboxing.com/abc-regulatory-guidelines/)). During rounds the amateur codes ban standing, clapping and yelling; Nevada bans coaching "loudly or excessively"; BBBofC bans advice only "in such a manner as to cause distraction"; New Jersey bans all coaching "during the progress of any round" ([S17](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-46-7-2)). SOURCED.
4. **What a corner may put on a cut is the widest split.** England Boxing: only an accredited person may treat cuts, water only to drink (S1). World Boxing: adrenaline 1/1000 and non-petroleum jelly only (S5). IBA also lists Collodion, thrombin, Gelfoam and Surgicel and says "Ice bags, Enswell and Swabs are permissible" ([S3](https://www.iba.sport/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/20230209-IBA-Technical-Competition-Rules-1.pdf)). USA Boxing: jelly, nose plugs, swabs, ice bags (S6). Pro bodies: adrenaline 1/1000 (BBBofC, New Jersey, Nevada), with the BBBofC kit list including "(k) An eye iron" (S9). A thrown towel is **a foul** in Nevada ([S12](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nac/NAC-467.html), NAC 467.675 items 27 and 31) and is allowed in California ([S18](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/4-CCR-361)). SOURCED.
5. **Counted corners: real coaches deliver many statements.** Halperin and colleagues put lapel microphones on 12 coaches at the 2015 Australian amateur championships and logged **445 statements across 25 bouts and 57 rounds, mean 8 ± 2 per round**; 52.5% were controlling, 78.8% had no attentional focus, winners' corners were more positive (36% against 18.5%) ([S21](https://gwulf.faculty.unlv.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/Halperin_Wulf_coaching_cues_boxing_PSE_20161.pdf)). SOURCED (amateur, one nation, one event).
6. **The famous corners were short and emotional, not tactical.** Teddy Atlas to Michael Moorer, Moorer v Holyfield I, after round 8: about 111 words, **two actual instructions** ("back him up", "fight a full round") wrapped in a threat ("you're going to be cryin' tomorrow") ([S26](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2000/08/21/cornerman)). Angelo Dundee to Sugar Ray Leonard before round 13: "You're blowing it, son. You're blowing it!" ([S30](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2012-feb-02-la-me-angelo-dundee-20120202-story.html)). SugarHill Steward to Tyson Fury after round 7: one instruction, repeated five times ("jab") ([S35](https://talksport.com/boxing/1653040/fascinating-corner-footage-tyson-fury-deontay-wilder-brutal-ko/)). Ben Davison to Anthony Joshua before round 5 (Dubois): about 93 words from Davison, three distinct directives, one repeated four times; Joshua was knocked out in round 5 ([S34](https://www.mirror.co.uk/sport/boxing/anthony-joshua-ko-daniel-dubois-33733957)). SOURCED text; counts are PROXY (partial clips).
7. **Instructions: one to two is the working number.** A French savate survey (n=47) has coaches giving "two instructions maximum", and under 1% saying all of three were applied ([S23](https://e-s-c.fr/minute-de-repos-en-boxe.php)). Warwick/England Boxing found five recurring functions in the minute: settle and breathe, health check, confidence, work-ons (the "bulk"), refocus ([S22](https://www.englandboxing.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Coach-Communication-in-Boxing-Kieran-File-Warwick-University.pdf)). PROXY (perception data, not outcome data).
8. **The body does not recover in a minute.** Trained amateur boxers' heart rate fell only 13% to 19% (26 to 35 beats per minute) in the 1-minute rest, from 187 to 195 at the bell to 152 to 166 at the next bell (n=10, simulated 3 × 2 min); the authors say the rest "did not allow sufficient time" to decline ([S46](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3871409/)). In professional boxers, fast muscle re-saturation covers the first 40 to 50 seconds, so the 60 s break is not being used to full effect ([S48](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10858063/)). SOURCED (simulated bouts, small n). A sports doctor in The Athletic says that if breathing is not settled until the last three or four seconds "everything that's happened before it is probably not going into their brain" ([S25](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/7562114/2026/09/04/boxing-gassing-out-body/)), JUDGEMENT/opinion of one expert.
9. **Cut and swelling work has no outcome evidence.** I found no study that measures whether corner treatment prevents a stoppage. What exists: cuts are 51% of injuries in Nevada professional boxing ([S57](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16295814/)); in healthy volunteers an ice bag or saltwater pack cools facial skin more than a steel enswell ("does not support the clinical use of an enswell", [S47](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32325428/)); in surgical wounds epinephrine stops bleeding faster than thrombin (up to 2.5 minutes, [S59](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21745412/), PROXY). NOT FOUND for fights.
10. **Proposed model (section 4, JUDGEMENT, label `invented`):** a 60 s minute with a fixed 10 s clear-the-ring end, one in-ring person who is either the coach or the cutman, a budget of one main instruction and one optional second, a partial heart-rate reset (about 15% of peak, not a full refill), an uptake penalty for every statement beyond two, cut and swelling work that eats clock and coaching, and a retire/stop choice kept separate from the coaching choice. One rule for all fights, not a per-commission table.

### 1b. What the table says (SOURCED synthesis; judgement marked)

- **Rest length:** 1 minute in every row that states it (S1, [S3](https://www.iba.sport/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/20230209-IBA-Technical-Competition-Rules-1.pdf), S5, S6, S9, [S12](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nac/NAC-467.html), [S14](https://www.abcboxing.com/abc-regulatory-guidelines/), [S16](https://www.abcboxing.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Unified-Rules-of-MMA-8.2026.pdf)). No code in the set shortens it for later rounds; the BBBofC allows 2-minute rounds in bouts up to 10 rounds but the interval stays one minute (S9, 3.7).
- **Seconds cap:** 3 in amateur (EB, IBA, World Boxing; USA Boxing's rulebook is silent and its guide says 3), Nevada and ABC non-title; 4 in BBBofC, California, and title bouts in Nevada, New Jersey and ABC.
- **In the ring at rest:** one person (EB, IBA, World Boxing, BBBofC, ABC, New Jersey, California, Virginia). Nevada: the cutman is the only person allowed inside in boxing, and the trainer is only allowed in if no cutman is needed ([S12](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nac/NAC-467.html)). JUDGEMENT: that Nevada rule is the only one that writes the cut-versus-coach trade-off into law, which makes it the best rule to copy into a single game rule.
- **Warning signals:** all codes use a 10-second warning. Only the BBBofC (5 s "Seconds out") and California (also at round end) add a second call.
- **Coaching volume:** the amateur codes forbid noise during the round, not in the rest. Nobody forbids speech in the rest. MMA time-outs ban coaching; the rest is a different matter.
- **Doctor in the rest:** the BBBofC is the only rulebook read that gives the doctor a standing right to visit the working corner between rounds (3.8); Nevada makes seconds wait for the physician after a combatant injury (467.642(3)); the others call the doctor at the referee's request.
- **Towel:** amateur = automatic stoppage (ABD); BBBofC = manager or chief second only, never mid-round; Nevada = a foul and not a stoppage; California = permitted; New Jersey = no second may terminate the performance; ABC = mount the apron instead of throwing.
- **Cut agents:** the order of permissiveness is EB (accredited person only) < World Boxing (adrenaline + jelly) < USA Boxing (jelly, ice, swabs) < IBA (adds collodion, thrombin, Gelfoam, Surgicel) and pro codes (adrenaline, thrombin, Avitene, pressure plates). Enswell is mandatory in the BBBofC kit, permitted in IBA and World Boxing, "pressure plates" in New Jersey, and **not named** in Nevada's NAC.

### 2a. Counted corners (the "handful"): one dataset and seven named corners

Counting rules I used: a **statement** is one utterance or sentence by a corner voice; a **directive** is a distinct instruction about what the boxer should do (an instruction repeated is one directive with repeats); **words** are corner words only. **Reading (JUDGEMENT on the above):**
- A 1-minute rest returns heart rate by roughly **one sixth** (13% to 19%) of what a 3-minute or 2-minute round pushed it up, and later rounds start higher (60 s HR 152 to 166). A game that refills stamina in the minute is wrong in direction; one that gives a small, capped refill is right.
- Anything that makes the boxer's first 40 to 50 seconds of recovery more effective matters more than anything said in the first seconds ([S48](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10858063/)). This is the only rest-minute evidence that a slow start is real: professional boxers' muscles are still re-oxygenating for 40 to 50 s.

### 3b. Cuts, swelling, nosebleed, enswell

- **How common (SOURCED, PROXY for the corner's problem):** Nevada professional boxing, Sep 2001 to Mar 2003: 524 matches, 17.1 injuries per 100 boxer-matches (3.4 per 100 boxer-rounds); facial laceration 51% of injuries, hand 17%, eye 14%, nose 5%; losers carried about twice the injury risk ([S57](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16295814/)). Nevada MMA 2001 to 2004: 171 matches, 28.6 injuries per 100 participations, laceration 47.9% ([S58](https://www.jssm.org/jssm-05-CSSI1-136.xml-Fulltext)). Neither measures the effect of corner treatment. PROXY.
- **Enswell versus ice (SOURCED):** Beauregard and colleagues timed 60 s of cooling on 11 healthy men: ice bag, cold pack, ice cube, saltwater pack and a stainless-steel enswell changed surface temperature significantly; the ice bag and saltwater pack cooled more; "This study does not support the clinical use of an enswell to provide short-duration cryotherapy treatments to facial tissue, as ice packs are more effective" ([S47](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32325428/)). The measure is skin temperature in uninjured faces, not swelling or stoppage. PROXY. (Grok credited this to "Hawkins"; the first author is Beauregard.)
- **Topical haemostasis (PROXY):** a systematic review of 9 randomised trials on split-skin-graft donor sites found epinephrine stopped bleeding faster than thrombin (up to 2.5 minutes), saline or mineral oil (up to 6.5 minutes) ([S59](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21745412/)). It is a surgical wound, not a boxing cut.
- **Practice (SOURCED, ARCHIVE):** Dave Tenny: do not rub swelling side to side; "apply firm pressure with the Enswell or an ice bag"; "A minute between rounds isn't long, but it often enough time to make a difference"; for a nosebleed, a Q-tip soaked in adrenalin hydrochloride inside the nostril with pressure, breathing through the mouth; checked with the Nevada commission's doctors ([S60](http://web.archive.org/web/20060427200330/http://www.thecutman.com/tennyarticle.htm)). Wikipedia's sequence (cold towel on the cut, adrenaline swab, petroleum jelly, enswell on swelling; MMA promotions supply cutmen to prevent "greasing") agrees but is a weak source ([S61](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutman), COMMUNITY).
- **Rules that shape the practice (SOURCED, section 1):** Adrenaline only once a cut or bloody nose is present (NSAC, [S13](https://boxing.nv.gov/uploadedFiles/boxingnvgov/content/faq/sections/InspectorSpeech-2022.pdf)); no adhesives like Krazy Glue; BBBofC kit includes "an eye iron"; IBA says enswell permissible; Nevada NAC does not name it; World Boxing allows only adrenaline 1/1000 and non-petroleum jelly on cuts.
- **Outcome evidence (NOT FOUND):** no trial or registry that links corner cut treatment to fewer or later stoppages. The size of any benefit in the model is therefore invented.

## 4. Proposed model for FightCamp (JUDGEMENT on sourced evidence; every number is `invented` unless an anchor is given)

**Principle.** One authored corner rule for every fight (user decision, 2026-09-07). The rule is a **60 second budget with three jobs**: recover, treat, tell. The jobs share the clock and the fighter's attention. Rule differences between commissions (3 or 4 seconds, towel as foul, which cut agent) are text flavour only and are not modelled.

### 4a. The minute (anchors in brackets)

| Phase | Seconds | What happens | Anchors |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arrive | 0 to 5 | Fighter walks to the stool; no instruction lands; the ring is entered | "four to six seconds before that athlete sits down" ([S25](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/7562114/2026/09/04/boxing-gassing-out-body/)); 1 person in the ring (S1, [S3](https://www.iba.sport/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/20230209-IBA-Technical-Competition-Rules-1.pdf), S5, S9, [S14](https://www.abcboxing.com/abc-regulatory-guidelines/)) |
| Settle | 5 to 20 | Water, breath, gumshield out, health check ("you ok?"); cut and swelling work can start in parallel | Savate boxer's recall: 0 to 15 s the coach asks if he is OK and gives him a drink ([S23](https://e-s-c.fr/minute-de-repos-en-boxe.php)); Warwick: greeting, recovery, health check come first ([S22](https://www.englandboxing.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Coach-Communication-in-Boxing-Kieran-File-Warwick-University.pdf)) |
| Tell | 20 to 45 | Up to **two** instructions and one confidence line; other cues are noise | "two instructions maximum", 3 instructions under 1% all applied ([S23](https://e-s-c.fr/minute-de-repos-en-boxe.php)); Warwick work-ons "bulk of coach talk" ([S22](https://www.englandboxing.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Coach-Communication-in-Boxing-Kieran-File-Warwick-University.pdf)); Halperin 8 statements per rest as the thing to beat ([S21](https://gwulf.faculty.unlv.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/Halperin_Wulf_coaching_cues_boxing_PSE_20161.pdf)) |
| Finish | 45 to 50 | Last sip, gumshield in, stool out, final word | IBA referee checks gumshield and clear corner at ten seconds (S4) |
| Clear | 50 to 60 | Warning; ring and apron clear; fighter alone | 10 s warning in every code in section 1; BBBofC also "Seconds out" 5 s before (S9) |

This matches the existing 45 s `CornerPitStop` bar (S97, 2026-09-27): the working window is 45 seconds and the last ten are the clear-out. JUDGEMENT.

### 4b. Parameters (all `invented`; anchors only show the direction and rough size)

1. **Breath recovery:** the minute closes about **15% of the gap** between the end-of-round level and rest (13% to 19% observed; [S46](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3871409/)), so a boxer who finishes a round at 195 bpm starts the next at about 160. The gap is larger in later rounds. No full refill. Optional: +2 to +3 points of that gap if the corner stays silent through the Settle phase. Anchor: the 40 to 50 s fast-resaturation window ([S48](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10858063/)).
2. **Receptivity of the fighter to instructions:** rises from low (0.2) at 5 s to full (1.0) by about 40 s, scaled by how much breathing has settled. An instruction delivered before 20 s gets at most half uptake. Anchor: Usher ([S25](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/7562114/2026/09/04/boxing-gassing-out-body/), opinion), savate recall ([S23](https://e-s-c.fr/minute-de-repos-en-boxe.php), perception).
3. **Instruction budget:** slot 1 full uptake, slot 2 about 0.6 of it, slot 3 and beyond about 0.1 (noise). Anchors: savate figures (one instruction, two instructions "both applied" 47% of boxers, three <1%); Halperin (real coaches give ~8 per rest, so the game rewards restraint and a real corner will feel too chatty). The player picks cards; AI corners in the sim may over-talk (see 4d).
4. **Voice register** (maps to Halperin's categories and K72 card `SAY`): *calm/supportive* (more positive, higher effect on a fighter with high belief), *controlling/urgent* (the usual 52.5%), *wake-up/rhetorical* (Atlas, Dundee; high variance, can backfire on a hurt or low-heart fighter). Winners' corners are more positive (36% v 18.5%), which is a flavour cue for AI corners.
5. **Cut and swelling job:** needs the in-ring person. If a **cutman is on staff** he works inside while the head coach talks from the apron (EB and IBA geometry: 1 in ring, 1 on apron, 1 on floor). If there is no cutman, the head coach does the cut work and the Tell phase loses one instruction slot. This is Nevada's rule (467.635(2)) turned into a game rule. Treatment order: cold and pressure first; adrenaline 1/1000 only on an open cut or bloody nose (NSAC); enswell on swelling; stronger agents (thrombin, Avitene) as pro-only upgrades. Effect sizes are invented; direction anchors are ice > steel enswell for cooling in 60 s ([S47](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32325428/)) and epinephrine fastest in surgical haemostasis ([S59](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21745412/), PROXY).
6. **Nose and breathing:** the existing nose-blow dilemma in `CornerPitStopPanel` stays. Chest or neck ice gives no breathing bonus ([S56](https://rsdjournal.org/rsd/article/view/27984), PROXY).
7. **Stop decision:** separate from coaching and made before the Tell phase, as three outcomes: continue, doctor check (rare; the referee or doctor may order it; BBBofC allows a doctor visit to the working corner, S9), or retire (corner-ordered stop). One rule: the chief second may retire the fighter only in the rest; a corner's stop mid-round is not modelled. Do not copy Nevada's towel-as-foul or California's thrown towel; both are commission quirks. Check `FightStoppageRead` for a current mid-round towel path before building.
8. **Confusion penalty:** two corner voices disagreeing (Fury–Usyk, [S37](https://www.givemesport.com/tyson-furys-corner-cam-oleksandr-usyk/); Pacquiao's corner, [S41](https://wboboxing.com/boxing-news/koncz-backs-arum-pacquiaos-corner-didnt-strategy/)) is an optional event that wastes one instruction slot.

### 4c. The mic'd-corner option (S74), JUDGEMENT

- Lines per rest: at most two sentences from the Tell phase, each at most 60 characters (K72 cap) plus one Settle-phase line from the breathing family.
- Line families from the sourced quotes, each with a tone: *breathe* ("Breath, buddy, breath."), *fix one fault* ("Chin down, hands up."; Miller's "you're dropping your right hand. Every time."), *one target* ("Aim your hooks to his body."), *confidence* ("You are scoring good points."), *state* ("Be very calm and surgical now."), *score status* ("You need this round."), *rare wake-up* ("You're blowing it, son."). All quoted material above can be used as inspiration; do not ship third-party verbatim lines without writing new ones.
- Crowd noise and the corner mix: the broadcast mic'd corner is a recorded feed ([S45](https://www.avinteractive.com/news/audio/shure-wireless-victorious-mayweather-pacquiao-prize-fight-26-05-2015/)), not an in-game channel. Present the lines as captions over crowd audio; the player can switch the audio option off.
- `CornerAdvice.focus` (breathe, defence, close_it, body, output, cut_work) is speech only and is not wired to the action chooser (roadmap note). The Tell slots should be what finally wires it: one instruction biases the next round's action weights for a short time, scaled by uptake. Invented.

### 4d. What to test before building (JUDGEMENT)

- Do players find the 20 to 45 s window too short with two instruction slots? Halperin shows real corners say more, so the model is deliberately strict; confirm that it still feels fair.
- Is a 15% breath refill too punishing next to the existing 45 s bar? Tune against `FightTrauma` and round-by-round stamina.
- Whether the apron coach can talk while the cutman works (EB/IBA geometry) should be a simple, visible rule on the pit-stop screen.
