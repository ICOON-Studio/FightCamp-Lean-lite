# YOUR JOB (fresh critic): the Training scene (the club's four fighters, one week planned at a time), judged as a GAME screen, not as an information sheet
The owner wants: art where it matters, little text, and from the picture alone it is clear how you set the training, what your options
are and what each improves (by how much), with tiredness and intensity shown. The art must match the rest of the game's screens: the
camp plan's pictures, and session icons drawn like the Playbook's ink move pictures.

## Where the screen sits
Training is its own scene, opened by clicking the bag area on the gym floor (the office keeps mail, money, staff and the roster). The
fighter profile has a read-only Training tab (the week in one row, what it buys, fight night) with one button, Open in Training.

## What the picture shows (the club's four fighters; the picked one is a welterweight 12 days before a club-show bout)
Top bar: the club, back to the gym floor, the date, money, CONTINUE (to the next date the clock stops at: another fighter's fight night).
FIGHTERS (top strip): four portraits - three boxers in three weight classes and one BJJ fighter (she has her own no-gi coach) - each
with weight class, sport, the next event, the kind of week (peak week, card, fight week, mat week) and the pad-coach minutes this week.
Click one and the board below is that fighter's.
THE BOARD, seven days and a FIGHT WEEK column:
- COACH row: the club's one pad coach has 75 minutes an evening; each evening is a bar split by fighter (portrait chips, minutes);
  a sparring night (the coach referees), an evening the coach is in another fighter's corner (locked), Sunday closed. The picked
  evening (Friday) is outlined: drag the split.
- MORNING row: one session a day (picture, ink icon ringed in its stat's colour, ONE number: the gain, Light / Normal / Hard).
- EVENING row: pads (the move's gain; READY when it crosses 30), film (one more hidden tell), sparring (Fight IQ), rest. The two
  evenings the player changed from the coach's week are lit and numbered 1 and 2 ("was: pads", "was: 60").
- TIREDNESS: one curve, end of each day, into the fight week and the first bell (48, under the fresh line 50). Lines: 72 (injury
  chance x2 by 76) and 80 (overreached: gains x0.6). The coach's Hard week as a grey dashed ghost (peaks 78).
- NIGHTS switch under the curve: Light / Standard / Hard (the coach's) / Yours, each with the week's tiredness peak (70 / 70 / 78 / 70);
  Yours says how many evenings it changed and lights them.
- One hover is shown: the cursor on Wednesday's point names what made the 70 and the coach's week's 78. Every other detail is on
  hover; each tile shows one number.
FIGHT WEEK column: the coach's taper (3 pad nights, no sparring, light mornings), weigh-in on the day (amateur), first bell tiredness.
THIS WEEK BUYS: the two moves (bars to the cap 72, Usable at 30, the day each is READY), and one row YOURS vs COACH'S (win, peak
tiredness, injury chance, the last tell found or not). MORNINGS: the six stat gains and what they are worth (this bout +0.5%, ten
weeks like it +5%).
SESSIONS (bottom): every session as a round ink icon to drag, with its one number at Normal; mornings left, evenings right.
Right column: the chance to win as a stack (today 50%, each move made ready +1.5, the film's tell 0 to +2 only if a ready move answers
it; the coach's own week 52%), ON FIGHT NIGHT (weight cut, first bell tiredness, this week's peak, training injury chance, the stamina
goal), the clicked evening (Friday: 65 / 10 minutes; at 60 minutes the check hook misses READY by 0.1, at 65 it makes it; what the
other fighter on that night pays, by her portrait), the coach's pick as a handwritten note with how sure, and TAKE THE ADVICE.
The numbers come from the game's rules: a morning is a fifth of a focused week (x0.6 Light, x1.3 Hard), times the coach and the
headroom to potential, which is why one morning adds about 0.1 to a stat; a pad night's gain shrinks as the move nears its cap;
tiredness keeps 87% a night and adds the night's load.
