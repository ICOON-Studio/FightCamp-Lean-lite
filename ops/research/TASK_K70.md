## Why this exists — it is your own finding, followed up

K69 identified the retention killer in this genre as **outcomes players cannot
trust**, and K68 said buyers pay for *"management depth and honest fight
outcomes, not 3D."* Both are about the same thing and neither says what it is
MADE of. That is this task.

The distinction that matters, and it is not fairness: **a player will accept
losing to a huge underdog and will not accept an outcome they cannot explain.**
Those are different properties. We need the second one specified.

## The question

**What mechanisms make a simulated combat result feel explicable, and what
mechanisms make it feel arbitrary?** Answer at the level of a thing we could
build, not a principle.

Cover at least these, and add any you find:

1. **VISIBLE CAUSATION.** Does the game show the player WHY? A round-by-round
   breakdown, a damage log, a stat that moved? Which games do this and what
   exactly do they surface?
2. **PRE-FIGHT EXPECTATION.** Did the player know the odds going in? An upset is
   only an upset against a stated expectation, and a game that never states one
   makes every result feel random. How is this shown — odds, a prediction, a
   confidence bar, scout reports?
3. **CONSISTENCY UNDER REPETITION.** If the same two fighters met ten times, would
   the spread feel right? Communities notice when a 70/30 favourite loses half the
   time. Is there any documented handling of this?
4. **THE ROLE OF RARE EVENTS.** A flash knockout is real and also the single most
   trust-destroying outcome if it is not set up. How do games signal that a
   fighter has that capability BEFORE it fires?
5. **WHAT PLAYERS ACTUALLY COMPLAIN ABOUT.** Forum and review evidence. Quote the
   complaint shape, not one angry post — the recurring pattern.

## Sources to work from

Football Manager's match engine (the most complained-about and most studied
simulation in any management game), Out of the Park Baseball, TEW, UFC's own
career mode, EA's Fight Night legacy, and any fight-sim community you can reach.
Also worth having: the general literature on **procedural legibility** — why
players forgive a system they understand.

## THE TRAP

**Do NOT answer the question "how do we make outcomes more realistic".** That is a
different question and we already have an answer to it (a sourced anchor set of
real MMA figures). Realism and legibility are not the same property, and a system
can be perfectly calibrated to reality and still feel arbitrary to the person
watching. **This task is about the second one only.** If you find yourself
recommending a change to a probability, you have drifted.

**Second trap: separate what a game DOES from what a player BELIEVES it does.**
Several of these communities have elaborate folk theories about their match engine
that the developers have denied. Both are useful — the folk theory tells you what
the game failed to communicate — but they must be labelled differently. Mark each
as MECHANISM or PERCEPTION.

**Third trap, from J34:** if a figure is not published, say NOT FOUND rather than
estimating it. A number we build on and cannot source costs more than a gap.

## The one judgement I want

At the end, **one ranked list of the three cheapest things that would make our
fight outcomes feel honest**, cheapest first, each with what it needs to exist.
Cheapest means fewest new systems. We already compute a great deal internally that
the player never sees — if the leverage is in SURFACING what exists rather than
building anything, say so plainly, because that would be the best news in this
