# YOUR JOB (fresh critic): the Training scene (the club's four fighters, one week planned at a time), judged as a GAME screen, not as an information sheet
The owner wants: art where it matters, little text, and from the picture alone it is clear how you set the training, what your options
are and what each improves (by how much), with tiredness and intensity shown. The art must match the rest of the game's screens: the
camp plan's pictures, and session icons drawn like the Playbook's ink move pictures.

## Where the screen sits
Training is its own scene, opened by clicking the bag area on the gym floor (the office keeps mail, money, staff and the roster). The
fighter profile has a read-only Training tab (the week in one row, what it buys, fight night) with one button, Open in Training.

## What the picture shows (the club's four fighters; the picked one is a welterweight 12 days before a club-show bout)
Top bar: the club, back to the gym floor, the date, money, RUN THE WEEK.
FIGHTERS (top strip): four portraits - three boxers in three weight classes and one BJJ fighter - each with weight class, sport, the
next event and the state of the week (your week / the coach's week / fight week); one has an unanswered weekly card. Click one and the
board below is that fighter's. Right of it: the picked fighter's chance to win (coach's week 51%, the player's changes +1.5 to +4 =
53-55%, a range because the film can find a still-hidden tell), the bout and the countdown.
THIS WEEK BUYS: the moves (bars to the cap 72 with the Usable line at 30 and the coach's week as a grey mark: check hook 12 -> 32,
body hook 29 -> 32, moves ready to throw 4 -> 6), the stats with the week's gain each (a week moves tenths), the Stamina goal and the one
change that closes it, the opponent's habits and the tell still hidden.
THIS WEEK (an amateur bout: no camp; next week is the light fight week): NIGHTS (Light 2 pads + 1 sparring / Standard 3 + 1 / Hard 4 + 1,
each with the tiredness peak it gives; the coach's is Hard, the player's edited week shows as YOURS), Undo and Coach's week.
COACH'S PAD NIGHTS: the club's one coach holds the pad nights for all four fighters; each night shows whose it is, and a shared night
names the cost to the other fighter. Seven days: a MORNING tile (picture, ink icon ringed in its stat's colour, ONE number:
the gain, and Light / Normal / Hard) and an EVENING tile (ONE number: the move's gain, the film's win range, sparring's Fight IQ), and
under each day a TANK (the day's tiredness, the 72 line, red above it, the coach's week as a grey dashed mark). Changed days say what the
coach had ("was: Sparring"). A sparring card is being dragged onto Saturday evening: the tank shows the amber rise and a strip names
what the drop buys and costs (moves, Fight IQ, win this bout, tank, injury: hurt = pull out or fight hurt) before the player lets go.
SESSIONS (bottom): every session as a round ink icon to drag, with its one number at Normal; mornings left, evenings right.
Right column: ON FIGHT NIGHT (weight: Hold or Cut, ticked, with what each gives; the goal; the tank at the first bell; this week's
tiredness peak against the coach's; this week's injury chance; moves ready) and how the film counts (only once the Playbook plan answers the found tell with a Usable move); the coach's pick as
a handwritten note with how sure; the clicked card (Wednesday evening: one pad night split between two moves, the split, what each
gets, what the coach's night would have given); a button that applies the coach's advice.
The numbers come from the game's rules: a morning is a fifth of a focused week (x0.6 Light, x1.3 Hard), times the coach and the
headroom to potential, which is why one morning adds about 0.1 to a stat; a pad night's gain shrinks as the move nears its cap.
