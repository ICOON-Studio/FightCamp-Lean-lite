## 2. DECISIONS (evidence only)

1. **Do not treat Miarka “clinch without attack” 11.4–12.6 s as episode duration.** Denominator = time per round (cumulative phase time), sample = 2,814 UFC rounds across weight classes (Miarka et al., *Hum Mov Sci* 2017). Same caveat as J32.
2. **Do not equate Silva/Del Vecchio “Interaction” with MT clinch.** Interaction = high-intensity strike/exchange block; clinch/collar-tie is not separately timed.
3. **Do not invent a clinch-exit % table.** No Fightnomics/FightMetric published share of clinches ending in separate vs TD vs ref vs cage was found on live web reopen. Use only named proxies below, labelled **proxy**.
4. **Grip list for product/UI may use coaching taxonomy**, but must be labelled **COACHING CONSENSUS (not measured)** until a competition frequency study exists.
5. **Separation:** treat as **NO STUDY**. Functional “safety distance” (lead arm fully extended) and ABC “full arm’s length” are definitions/rules, not empirical distances.

## 3. ITEM 1 — MMA clinch EPISODE duration

### Status: **NOT FOUND**

No study in the reopened set reports median (or mean ± SD) duration of **one** clinch from engage to break/takedown/cage-transition with that denominator named.

### What *is* published (wrong denominator — do not misuse)

| Source | What was measured | Sample | Number | Denominator | Grade |
|--------|-------------------|--------|--------|-------------|-------|
| Miarka, Brito, Dal Bello, Amtmann 2017 (*Hum Mov Sci*; FightMetric-coded UFC) | Time in **clinch without attack** | **2,814 rounds**, all UFC weight divisions | Higher in Flyweight **11.4 s** and Half-middleweight **12.6 s** | **Cumulative seconds per round** (phase time), not per episode | A |
| Fightnomics / Kuhn (MMAOddsBreaker “Wrestlers Ruining MMA?”, 2015; FightMetric TIP) | Share of Octagon time standing at distance vs clinch/ground | UFC TIP corpus (multi-year; exact N not restated in article) | 2014: **69%** feet (distance + clinch), **31%** ground; distance share rising over years | % of **total fight time** | B |
| Miarka et al. 2016 (*J Strength Cond Res*) | Standing **low-intensity** combat | 645 outcome-paired rounds | Median R1 **2:33.5** (P25–P75 1:20–3:56); R2 2:37; R3 2:07 | Standing LI phase time per round — **not clinch** | A |
| Del Vecchio, Hirata, Franchini 2011 (*Percept Mot Skills*) | High- vs low-intensity stand-up/ground | 52 regional MMA athletes | EP (HI : LI+pause) **1:2 to 1:4**; HI stand-up often cited ~6–9 s by secondary summaries | HI **standing** blocks include strikes + grappling; **not clinch-only episode** | A/B (primary confirms EP; per-round HI stand-up seconds rely on secondary quoting of tables — treat cautiously) |
| 2026 lower-weight UFC position paper (CSS; DOI 10.62852/css/2026/243) | Mutual clinch as % of bout time | Lower-weight UFC matches (paper sample as published) | Mutual clinch **13.36%** of match time; cage time **20.91%** | Aggregate **% bout time**, not episode | B |

### Closest proxies (labelled)

| Proxy | Why closest | Caveat |
|-------|-------------|--------|
| **Boxing** Martusciello et al. 2025 — clinch (C) phase mean **4.06 ± 2.27 s** | True **episode-level** clinch duration (engage→unlock), pooled across 12 women’s World finals | **Boxing rules** (ref breaks prolonged holds); not MMA |
| Del Vecchio regional MMA HI standing ~seconds | Order-of-magnitude for short standing exchanges | Mixes punching and clinch; not engage→break clinch |

**INFERENCE (not DOCUMENTED):** If Fightnomics-era clinch is ~16–18% of total fight time (J32) and Miarka cumulative clinch-without-attack is ~11–13 s/round, average episode length would require **episode count per round** — that count was **NOT FOUND**, so no inferred seconds are reported here.

## 4. ITEM 2 — How clinch ENDS (percentages)

### Status: **NOT FOUND** (say loudly)

**No study** was found that tabulates MMA clinch endings as % separate / referee / takedown / knockdown / cage transition.

Searched: Miarka phase papers (2016–2017), Fightnomics blog archive reopen, FightMetric glossary, Paul Gift/Bloody Elbow FightMetric-derived “alternative stats,” UFC Stats public schema, BMC 2026 takedown-density paper. None publish a clinch-exit transition matrix.

### Closest published proxies

| Proxy | Sample / denom | Number | What it is **not** | Grade |
|-------|----------------|--------|--------------------|-------|
| Paul Gift / FightMetric-derived, UFC 245 preview (2019) | Fighter career P5M in clinch | Usman **63%** clinch TD success; Covington **53%** (both **6.9** clinch TD attempts per 5 min in clinch); Nunes clinch TD success **33%** (vs 9% from distance) | Success rate of **clinch TD attempts**, not % of clinches that end in TD | B |
| Miarka et al. 2017 (*Int J Perform Anal Sport*) — performance probabilities | **1,752 rounds / 584 UFC bouts** (2013–2014) | Takedowns landed among strongest win predictors (with distance head strikes, offensive passes); model accuracy **69.6%** | TD landed by phase counts exist in coding scheme; **published paper does not give % of clinches ending in TD** | A |
| BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil 2026 | **8,461 UFC bouts**, 1997–2025 | Winners vs losers: TD% median **50.0% vs 20.0%**; CTRL/TD **99.75 vs 78.88 s** | Overall TD success & control per TD — **origin position (clinch vs shot) not split** | A |
| Boxing Martusciello 2025 | 12 IBA women’s finals; AI = arbitral interruption | AI durations vary by weight (e.g. examples in tables ~3–7 s); heavyweights over-represented in AI frequency | Boxing **ref break** ends many clinches by rule — not transferable % to MMA | A |
| Ouergui et al. 2014 kickboxing WC | 45 elite male WAKO bouts | Referee pause mean **5.4 ± 4.3 s**; HIA **2.2 ± 1.2 s** | K-1/WAKO clinch broken almost immediately — pause ≠ MMA clinch exit mix | A |
| ABC Boxing Referee Manual (rev. ~2025) | Rulebook | Break at **full arm’s length**; boxers take **one complete step** back; never walk between | **Procedure**, not measured ending % | C (rules) |

**Loud conclusion:** Ending-% table remains **NOT FOUND**. Proxies above must not be rewritten as “X% of clinches end in TD.”

**Decision:** Keep Interaction medians as J32 did; do **not** relabel them as clinch episode duration.

### DOCUMENTED — clinch *action* frequency (still not grip type)

### COACHING CONSENSUS (not measured) — still useful

| Grip | Typical role (coaching) |
|------|-------------------------|
| Single collar tie | Head control; transitional |
| Double collar / plum | Posture break; knees |
| Underhook (single/double) | Angle, lift, TD setups |
| Over-under / 50-50 | Neutral pummel start |
| Body lock (front/rear) | High-control TD/throw base |

Couture-style coaching lists body lock, underhook, collar tie as “three most important” MMA clinch positions — **consensus, not measured**.

## 7. ITEM 5 — Separation distance / re-engagement

### Status: **NO STUDY**

No peer-reviewed or FightMetric/Fightnomics source reports metres or centimetres of separation after a clinch break, nor mean time to re-engage, in MMA or Muay Thai.

### What exists instead (not measurements)

| Source | Content | Label |
|--------|---------|-------|
| Martusciello et al. 2025 | **Safety distance** defined functionally: neither can land with fully extended lead arm; F→NF when a boxer retreats to that zone | Operational definition — **no metres** |
| ABC Boxing Referee Manual | On break: separate to **full arm’s length**; each takes **one complete step** backward; referee steps straight back; never walks between | **Rule procedure** — **NO STUDY** of actual cm |
| AIBA/IBA RJ regs (similar) | Same arm’s-length break language | Rules |

**Time-to-re-engage:** **NO STUDY** found for post-clinch re-entry latency in MMA/MT. Boxing NF phase durations (Martusciello tables; e.g. category means roughly ~2–6 s depending on weight) measure general no-fighting intervals, **not** specifically “after clinch break → next engage.”
