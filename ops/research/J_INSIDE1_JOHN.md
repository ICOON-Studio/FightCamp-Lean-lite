**What the papers do give:**
- **Clinching in the women's 2023 world finals** ([Martusciello 2025b](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12197034/), MEASURED). These are shares of punching-plus-clinching action counts, not time:

| Weight class | Clinching, rounds 1 / 2 / 3 |
|---|---|
| 54–57 kg | 2–10% |
| 48–50 kg | 18–26% |
| 81+ kg | 7.7%, 17.4%, 22.9% |

  - Weight class changes the mix significantly. The round makes no overall difference (p=0.944).
- **What gets thrown** (all ranges, not inside only):
  - Elite men: about 53% straight punches, 40% hooks and 7% uppercuts (Davis 2015, snippet seen via a [thesis](http://hdl.handle.net/10034/623170)).
  - Regional and national boxers (Thomson & Lamb 2016, via the same thesis): DERIVED 55% straights, 38% hooks and 7% uppercuts. The snippet doesn't say whether these were men or women.
- **Guard dropping** in elite men rises about 41% from round 1 to round 3 ([Dunn 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5747423/)). That figure covers all ranges, not just inside.
- **Long range now dominates** elite amateur boxing: since the 2013 rule changes it has been mostly straight punches from distance (Davis 2018, men, abstract only).
