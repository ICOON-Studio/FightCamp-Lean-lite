# TASK G137 - GROK - DOES WHAT THE CORNER SAYS CHANGE THE NEXT ROUND? (S100)
Owner: grok. Assigned 2026-09-27 (S100). Sourced research, no code. Grade every figure SOURCED (link) / PROXY / JUDGEMENT.

## Why
The game now lets a corner give a between-rounds READ ("his tell: the hand drops after the jab - counter over it";
"his exit: he slides the same way off the ropes") that helps for the next round only, and a pre-fight game plan whose
fit to the opponent is a small edge. Both sizes are AUTHORED today: a read = +3 percentage points on landing / on
defending for one round; a perfect plan = about +5 points, a bad one about -5. We want them grounded.

## What would a human do?
A head coach watches round 1, finds ONE pattern, and says it on the stool in a sentence; a fighter who executes it
lands the counter in round 2. Analysts check this with round-by-round punch stats (CompuBox / UFC Stats) - did the
landed rate move after an adjustment. Look for that evidence first.

## Questions
1. Is there any measured effect of between-round coaching on the next round (boxing, MMA, kickboxing, or other combat
   sports - also studies of coach feedback in timeouts, e.g. judo/taekwondo/basketball timeouts as a PROXY)? Size it.
2. Round-to-round variation in landed rate for the same fighter (CompuBox or UFC Stats): what swing is normal
   (so we know how big a +3 point next-round edge is against the noise)?
3. Game-plan quality: any evidence that a pre-fight plan fitted to the opponent (film study, style-matched sparring
   partners) changes win rate or accuracy? Even anecdotal expert estimates, graded JUDGEMENT.
4. One recommendation: the size of a correct between-round read, and of a well-fitted plan, as percentage points of
   landing / defence. Keep to one page.
