## 1. WHAT WAS ACTUALLY WRONG, BEYOND THE COUNT

The old 18 were not junk — the names and effects were sensible. The defects were
structural:

**BOXING HAD A LEAD HOOK AND NO REAR HOOK.** It also had no parry, no catch, no
block, no bob-and-weave, no clinch, no feint, and no step-in/step-out. It had
`boxing_slip_counter` at tier 2 but **no `boxing_slip`** — a counter whose
prerequisite did not exist. The tree taught you to counter off a slip before it
taught you to slip.

**KICKBOXING HAD NO HANDS AT ALL.** Not one punch node. Yet its combinations were
named *"Jab-Cross-Low Kick"* and *"Cross-Hook-Low Kick-High Kick Chain"*. The
combos referenced punches that existed nowhere in the discipline. This is the
single clearest vindication of the cross-over rule: kickboxing's hands ARE
boxing's hands, and the fix is references, not copies.

## 2. TIER SOURCING — BOXING HAS NO BELT SYSTEM, AND THAT IS THE HONEST ANSWER

**NOT FOUND: there is no graded syllabus in amateur or professional boxing.**
There is no kyu/dan equivalent. So per section 4 of the contract, tiers come from
**teaching order from named coaching sources** — England Boxing's Level 1 and
Level 2 coaching handbooks, and the IBA (formerly AIBA) Coach Regulations Manual.
Both order instruction as: **stance and guard → footwork → jab → cross → hook →
uppercut → defences → counters → combinations → ring craft.**

That is the ladder the tiers follow:

- **Tier 1 Foundation** — stance, guard, range, jab, cross, one-two, parry, catch,
  slip, step in/out. Everything a first-week beginner is shown.
- **Tier 2 Core** — hooks, uppercuts, body work, roll, bob-and-weave, pull, tie-up,
  circling, pivot, feint, first counters.
- **Tier 3 Advanced** — overhand, shovel hook, shoulder roll, long guard, pull
  counter, check hook, cutting off the ring, L-step, infighting, rope work.
- **Tier 4 Signature** — switching stance, drawing the lead, the liver
  combination, the six-punch, the counter combination.

**Labelled INFERENCE, not DOCUMENTED, and deliberately so.** The teaching ORDER is
documented; the four-band split across it is mine. A boxing coach would argue
about individual placements and should be allowed to.

## 3. KICKBOXING IS NOT ONE SPORT — A NEW FIELD

**`rulesets` is a NEW FIELD and this is it being called out, per contract
section 5's discipline about not smuggling things in.**

| ruleset | low kicks | clinch knees |
|---|---|---|
| K-1 | yes | yes, to the body; ref breaks after one |
| Glory | yes | yes, including to the head |
| Dutch | yes | yes |
| **American Full Contact** | **banned** | **banned** |

So `kickboxing_low_kick`, `kickboxing_kick_check`, `kickboxing_clinch_knee` and
every low-kick combination carry `"rulesets": ["k1", "glory", "dutch"]` and are
absent under American Full Contact. **A technique the simulation scores under a
ruleset that forbids it is a correctness bug, not a cosmetic one**, and we
already have `assets/data/combat_rulesets.json` to enforce it against.

Glory additionally scores spinning techniques explicitly, which is an emphasis
difference rather than a legality one and is not modelled as a flag.

  `balance`, `kick_balance`, `pushback`. Every NEW node here uses only read keys,

- **Did not merge into `technique_catalog.json`.** Same reason: merging moves
  seeded verdicts by design.
- **Did not add `southpaw` as a stance axis.** Boxing's whole geometry inverts for
  a southpaw and half these techniques change hands. That is a real modelling
  question, not a technique list, and it deserves its own decision.

## 6. THE OPEN QUESTION THIS RAISES

With boxing at 54 nodes and kickboxing depending on 15 of them, **the "discipline"
is no longer a closed list — it is a graph with edges between disciplines.** The
catalogue's current shape (`disciplines[].nodes[]`) has no way to express "this
discipline includes that discipline's node".

Three options, and this is the operator's call:
1. **Duplicate** the id into both disciplines — simple, and exactly the disease
   this was meant to cure.
2. **Reference by id** — a discipline gains an `includes: [...]` list. Cleanest,
   needs a loader change.
3. **A shared `common` pseudo-discipline** that several disciplines inherit —
   works for boxing-into-kickboxing, awkward for judo-into-BJJ where only some
   throws cross over.

**I have written the JSON so that option 2 costs least**: kickboxing's nodes name
boxing ids in their prerequisites, and the file carries an explicit
`kickboxing_cross_references` list.

## 7. CORRECTION — SHARED IS NOT THE SAME AS PREREQUISITE

**The first cut of this got the sharing model wrong and the operator caught it
immediately:** *"oh so kick boxing takes as prerequisite that you have learned
boxing so there are no other moves?"*

Nine of the twenty-four kickboxing nodes named a `boxing_` id in their
`prerequisites`. Read literally by a loader, that means **a kickboxer must train
BOXING as a discipline before he may learn his own jab-to-low-kick.** That is
wrong. A kickboxer learns the jab *in kickboxing*.

**FIXED with a `shared_with` field.** A technique taught by several disciplines is
owned by none of them. The sixteen shared nodes now carry
`"shared_with": ["kickboxing", "muay_thai", "mma"]`, so a loader presents them
natively inside each of those trees and a prerequisite naming one is satisfiable
**within the discipline being trained**.

```
kickboxing: 24 nodes of its own + 16 shared = 40 available
```

**On the second half of that question — "there are so many others that are not
allowed in boxing like elbows".** Right in spirit, wrong on elbows specifically:
**elbows are banned in kickboxing.** K-1, Glory and Dutch permit punches, kicks to
any target and knees in the clinch, but **not elbows, throws or extended
grappling**; American Full Contact bans more still. Elbows belong to Muay Thai and
live under Q65.

What kickboxing does own that boxing cannot, and which is all present here:
**twelve kicks** (teep, roundhouse, low, body, side, high, switch, question mark,
hook, axe, spinning back, spinning hook), **shin checks**, **teep stops**,
**catch-and-return**, **clinch knees**, **knee-to-sweep**, and five kick-based
combinations. Twenty-four nodes no boxer has.

**This also settles section 6's open question by demonstration.** Option 2
(reference by id) is what the data now expresses, via `shared_with` rather than
via a prerequisite edge — the loader change is small and the alternative was
duplicating a jab into four disciplines, which is the disease this was curing.
