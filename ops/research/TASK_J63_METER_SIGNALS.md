# J63 (S238) for John: when should a boxing coach CHANGE THE PLAN? Sourced signs and thresholds for a gains/attrition meter

Discipline: boxing (demo). No MMA, kickboxing or team-sport-only sources unless you flag them as such.

## Why
The operator wants the game's training loop built around two meters, GAINS and ATTRITION, which tell the player when to change
a fighter's training plan. Our lead designer proposed thresholds (acute fatigue over 75%, CNS stress over 80%, injury risk over
25%) with NO sources. We need real ones, or an honest "no number exists - here is the best proxy".

## Questions (answer each, with sources, a confidence level and the population behind it)
1. What signs do real boxing coaches and S&C staff use to decide a fighter is going stale or overreached in camp (session-RPE
   trends, morning HR/HRV, mood questionnaires such as POMS/RESTQ/Hooper index, grip or jump tests, sleep, sparring
   performance, weight)? Which are practical day to day?
2. Thresholds: for each usable sign, the published cut-off or change that marks functional overreaching (FOR),
   non-functional overreaching (NFOR) and overtraining (OTS) - e.g. Hooper index rise, HRV drop % vs baseline, CMJ drop %,
   sRPE weekly load rise %, monotony/strain (Foster). Say which come from combat sports and which are borrowed.
3. Gains flattening: how fast do boxing-relevant qualities improve over a 4-8 week camp (aerobic, punch force/speed,
   technique) and how quickly do they plateau or drop when load is too high? Typical % per week if any study gives it.
4. Recovery: after an NFOR warning, how many days of reduced load do coaches use, and how much does load drop?
5. Coaching effect: any evidence that a coach present (supervised training) changes adaptation or injury rates vs
   unsupervised training (any sport; flag it)?
6. The one-line rule a real boxing coach would give: "pull him off when ...".

## Format
Markdown memo. A "Key sources" list with links first, then one section per question, then a TABLE: sign | FOR | NFOR | OTS |
source | population | confidence. Say plainly what was NOT found. No invented numbers.

## What would a human do?
A boxing coach watches the fighter's sharpness on the pads, asks how he slept, checks his weight and resting heart rate in the
morning, and cuts the session short or gives him a rest day when the snap is gone two days running.
