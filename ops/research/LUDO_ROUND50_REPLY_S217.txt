C. WRIST ALIGNMENT, GLOVE GEOMETRY & ARTICULATION LIMITS

Glove roll and broken wrist angles ruin the visual weight of a strike. In boxing, if a wrist flexes or deviates on impact, the joint buckles under kinetic force (>3,000 N). The glove, wrist, and forearm must form a continuous, rigid structural column.

2. Lead Body Hook on Impact (0.267 s / Frame 8)
Palm Orientation (The Liver Hook Rule): MANDATORY SPECIFICATION: PALM FACING IN / THUMB UP (Vertical Fist / 90° Roll).
Biomechanical Rationale: A horizontal fist (palm down) requires the elbow to elevate above the shoulder plane (>105° shoulder abduction). At close body-shot range (52 cm chest-to-chest), lifting the elbow that high causes the punch to flare wide, hitting the receiver's defensive elbow rather than slipping under the rib cage. A vertical fist (thumb pointing straight up along +Y, palm facing back toward the attacker's own chest) keeps the elbow tucked close to the ribs (≈ 42° shoulder abduction). This allows the glove to slip underneath the receiver's right elbow and drive cleanly into the liver.
The Forearm-to-Knuckle Straight-Line Rule: V_forearm = P(hand_l) - P(lowerarm_l); V_hand = P(middle_01_l) - P(hand_l). Strict Alignment Requirement: Angle between V_forearm and V_hand must be <= 2.5° on every frame from strike release through impact (0.200 s to 0.333 s).
Elbow Angle Band: 88° to 94° flexion on contact. Angles >105° turn the punch into an ineffective slap; angles <75° crowd the strike and cause mesh clipping.

3. Attacker Rear Guard Hand During the Hook
