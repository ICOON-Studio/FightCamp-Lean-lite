# CARDREAL2 (S544): throws, takedowns, leg locks and the in-and-out striker, move by move
Researched 23 Nov 2026. It uses the same tags as CARDREAL1, and the source list is in part 4.

**Base rule set: unified MMA (ABC, Aug 2026) [S1].**
- Grounded means any body part other than the hands or feet on the floor.
- Kicks, knees and stomps to a grounded fighter's head are fouls.
- The only joint locks banned are on fingers and toes. So heel hooks, kneebars, toe holds and knee reaping are all legal (DERIVED).

## 0. Extra positions (each fighter has their own)
| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Clinch (open) / Clinch on the fence | Standing and tied up, in open space or with one fighter's back to the cage |
| Turtle | On knees and elbows, back up, facing the mat |
| Top on turtle / Riding | Behind or beside a turtled fighter, chest on their back or hips |
| Front headlock (top / bottom) | Top: in front, arm round the head. Bottom: head trapped, on knees or bent over |
| Half guard (top / bottom) | One of the top fighter's legs trapped |
| Back taken (top / bottom) | One fighter behind the other, with hooks or a body lock |
| Leg entanglement | Both seated or on the back, legs tied, one leg under attack |
| Standing in an entanglement | One fighter on the feet with a leg trapped, the other seated or on the back holding it |

## 1. Judo against a striker and against a wrestler

### 1a. Gripping with no jacket
Before, all fighters are standing. After a grip is won, both are in the clinch, open or on the fence.

| Grip | Throws actually seen in MMA | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Overhook or arm trap | Harai goshi, o goshi, kimura-grip wraparound throws (makikomi) | [S15; S17; S19] |
| Underhook plus elbow | Sasae tsurikomi ashi (ankle block), ko soto gake | [S21] |
| Collar tie | Sasae used to break posture, not only to score | [S21] |
| Head control, side-on | Hip throw attempt, then ko uchi gari when the opponent sits back | [S17] |
| Body lock | Trips (Harrison, twice in one fight), the "sambo drag" (Khabib) | [S22; S23] |
| Caught kick | Heel trip (kibisu gaeshi), single-leg drop (kuchiki taoshi) | [S19] |
| No grip, opponent shooting | Harai goshi turned in against a double-leg (Rousey vs Tate) | [S15] |

All SOURCED.
- **How long.** The grip fight can take seconds or most of a round. Once the grip is set, the throw takes under a second. ESTIMATE.
- **What the striker can do meanwhile.** Strike on the way in, then knee and elbow in the clinch [S1]. Lombard showed the judoka can use this too: a knee to the body made Shields brace, then the throw came. Lombard called it "Illegal in judo competition but works great for MMA" [S21].
- **What the wrestler can do meanwhile.** Drop the hips under the judoka's hips, which kills hip throws [S17]. Or switch to leg attacks, which IJF judo penalises and MMA allows [S8]. So judo competition never made the judoka train against leg attacks. DERIVED, CHECK.

### 1b. A throw that lands
| Throw | Thrower after | Thrown fighter after | Next 2 seconds | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Harai goshi landing on top (Rousey vs Tate, UFC 168, 28 Dec 2013) | Lands "in a near mount position" | On back | Mount or side control | [S15] |

- **A landed throw ends nothing in MMA** [S1]. It ends a contest only under two other rules:
  - judo, where an ippon throw wins [S8];
  - combat sambo, where a "perfect throw" (the thrower stays standing and the opponent lands flat on the back) is a total victory [S9].
- **Where the thrower ends up.** A throw "without falling" leaves the thrower standing over the opponent. A throw "with falling" (sacrifice and wraparound throws) puts both fighters down [S9].
- **The next 2 seconds.** Three examples:
  - Guardado recovered half guard, then full guard, then "pops back to her feet" [S22].
  - Evans "quickly recovered, got back to his feet" [S31].
  - Shields got up after each of Lombard's throws [S21].

### 1c. A throw that fails
| Failure | Judoka after | Opponent after | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hip throw blocked by dropped hips | Standing, back half-turned | Standing behind or beside, hips low | [S17] |
| Sumi gaeshi (sacrifice throw) with no hook | Underneath, on back. Lying back gives up side control, and rolling to the knees may give up the back | On top, circling to side control | [S16] |
| Rousey's recovery from that | Rolled onto the shoulders, came up on top, armbar from the turtle | On knees, then armbarred | [S16] |

- **Combat sambo.** A failed throw where the thrower lands on their own back scores nothing for the opponent unless the opponent counterattacks [S9]. In MMA nothing is scored either way, but the judoka is now underneath. DERIVED.
- **How often throws fail.** At the 2011 judo World Championships, 65.38% of actions were failed throws and 14.91% scored [S25] SOURCED. That was judoka against judoka, in the jacket.

### 1d. The pin under MMA rules
- **Unified MMA.** A pin scores nothing by itself: "Merely holding a dominant position(s) shall not be a primary factor… What the fighter does with those positions is what must be assessed" [S1]. If the pinner does nothing, the referee stands the fighters up.
- **Judo (IJF).** A 20-second hold-down is ippon and ends the contest; 10–19 s scores waza-ari and 5–9 s yuko [S8].
- **Combat sambo.** A 20-second hold-down scores 2 points [S9].
- **What a pin is worth in MMA.** It's a platform:
  - Harrison went from side control to the crucifix, a kimura attempt and mount, then ground strikes and an armbar [S22].
  - Rousey went from the throw to mount, then the armbar [S17].

All SOURCED.

### 2c. Riding and the turtle
| | Top (rider) | Bottom (turtled) |
|---|---|---|
| Strikes (unified) | Punches to the side of the head and body; no knees or kicks to the head; no strikes to the back of the head or spine [S1] | Almost none |
| Strikes (ONE) | The same, plus **knees to the head** [S2] | — |
| Main threats | Take the back, front headlock, crucifix (Khabib, Harrison [S23; S22]) | Stand up, sit out, switch, Granby roll [S39] |

## 7. What games get wrong about these four arts
1. **The throw ends the exchange.** In MMA, a landed throw starts the ground fight. Only judo ippon and combat sambo's perfect throw end a bout [S8; S9], and the thrown fighter is often back up in the same exchange [S22; S31].
2. **The thrower is always on top.** Sacrifice and wraparound throws put the thrower down too [S9; S19], and a failed sumi gaeshi leaves the judoka underneath [S16].
3. **Throwing without a jacket as if wearing one.** MMA judo works from overhooks, underhooks, collar ties, body locks and caught kicks, and opponents drop the hips to kill hip throws [S15; S17; S19; S21; S22].
4. **The pin is a win.** It scores nothing in MMA and gets stood up if nothing happens [S1]. It's worth points only in judo, sambo and grappling [S8; S9; S12].
5. **Every shot works.** Roughly 61–64% of UFC takedown attempts fail [S3; S6], and shots fail more often than clinch takedowns [S3].
6. **A stuffed shot is a reset.** It often leaves the wrestler on the knees with the head trapped. The guillotine is the second most common UFC submission [S4; S5], though only about 14% of attempts finish [S28].
7. **Takedowns land past the guard.** Most stall in full guard [S3].
8. **The turtle is a safe shell.** In ONE the rider can knee the head [S2]. Under every rule set the rider can take the back or the crucifix [S22; S23], and standing up from it invites a mat return [S23].
9. **The leg lock is a one-move win.** Leg locks are about 3% of UFC submissions [S4; S5]. In elite grappling, the fighter who starts the leg battle loses it more often than not from ashi garami and 50/50 [S27].
10. **The same leg locks are legal everywhere.** Heel hooks are legal in MMA, ADCC and IBJJF adult brown and black belt no-gi. They're banned in IBJJF gi, at lower belts, and in both kinds of sambo [S1; S9–S12].
11. **The same rules standing and on the ground.** In sport sambo, a leg lock stops when the defender stands [S10]. In combat sambo, a standing fighter can't strike a grounded one [S9].
12. **The karateka can't be hit going in or out.** The counter is timed to the entry, as Rua did to Machida [S33].
13. **The bladed stance costs nothing.** The lead leg gets kicked (49–3 at UFC 104 [S32]), and kicks get caught for takedowns [S36].
14. **The standing fighter always waits for the downed one.** That's true only for knockdowns in boxing, kickboxing and combat sambo [S7; S9; S14]. In MMA, judo and wrestling there's no wait [S1; S8; S13].
