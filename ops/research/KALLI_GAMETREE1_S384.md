1. **Strong: one map is both the match and the unlock tree.** Drilling turns a grey arrow yellow, and the next open walks that same arrow. The player sees cause and effect, which is the whole game.

2. **Strong: a miss is honest.** A failed move costs Ana only when the opponent owns the answer, so a loss names its reason ("no seatbelt escape") and its fix (a Drill button). The Winter Open example proves the loop: drilling the rear naked choke takes the finish from 10% to 32%.

3. **Wrong: the pace is too slow for a game.** Owning a move takes 9.4 weeks, and a 4-move route takes most of a year. That's true to real BJJ, but the player gets one yellow arrow every two months, so the weekly drill click is a chore with no reward. Fix: show the meter moving every Sunday, and give a felt step at 15 ("Ana tried it in rolling") before the 30 for owned.

4. **Wrong: the edge chip shows too many numbers.** Chance, where a miss lands, points and read pips on every edge, five times across, plus a node view and leaks, is a spreadsheet. Show one word on the chip (LIKELY / EVEN / LONG SHOT) with a red dashed corner when a miss is costly. Put the four numbers in the tooltip.

5. **Dominant strategy risk: the Goal tiles fill the plan for you.** If the strip always fills with Ana's best route, the player clicks Back every week and never plans. Make the coach's fill deliberately one step off for the scouted opponent, the same trick as the boxing whiteboard. Then a swap at a fork is a real decision.

6. **Unreadable: 28 holes and 49 nodes.** Showing only 3 leaks and one ring around Ana's reachable nodes is the right call. Hold to it: never add a "full chart" button in version 1.

7. **Too much text: every rule above is invisible machinery the player never needs to read.** That covers the late-lead HOLD, chain, the read, film, the mood multiplier and off-belt scaling. Keep each one to a single result-card line ("They'd seen the kimura trap twice; it was read at step 3"), and cut any rule that can't produce such a line.

8. **Add one thing: a rolling partner at the gym.** One fixed club partner rolls with Ana every few weeks, and the player watches the same tape against the same person. That gives a progress yardstick between opens (5 weeks apart) and a cheap test for a new route before a real bracket reads it.
