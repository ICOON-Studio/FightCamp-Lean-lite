# CARDREAL1 (S543): how position really changes between two fighters, move by move
Researched 23 Nov 2026. **Tags:**
- **SOURCED:** a source says it directly.
- **DERIVED:** I worked it out from sourced facts.
- **ESTIMATE:** coaching judgement, with no source that gives the number.
- **CHECK:** the source is weak, second-hand or conflicting.
- **NOT FOUND:** I looked and found nothing usable.

Source numbers [S1] onwards are listed in part 4.

**The core finding.** Each fighter has their own position, and when one fighter changes position, the other doesn't move with them. The fighter who stays down has to spend their own time and effort getting up, and that is when they're at risk. Real bouts break the prototype's rule on both counts: the bottom fighter isn't standing, and a standing boxer can barely hit a man lying on the floor [S19].

## 1. Boxer against jiu-jitsu fighter

Positions used:
- **Standing**
- **Seated**: upright on the floor, one hand posted
- **On back**
- **On knees**
- **In guard (top)**: kneeling or squatting between the other fighter's legs
- **Guard (bottom)**: on the back with the opponent between the legs
- **Past guard**: side control, mount or back

### 1a. The grappler pulls guard or sits to guard
| | Boxer | Grappler |
|---|---|---|
| Before | Standing | Standing |
| After a pull with a grip | In guard (top), pulled to the knees | Guard (bottom) |
| After a sit with no grip | **Still standing** | Seated or on back |

- **A pull needs contact first.** That means a collar tie, an overhook, a body lock or a shot that got to the hips. Without a grip, the grappler just sits down and the boxer chooses whether to follow [S28] SOURCED. The IBJJF penalises a pull or sit with no grip [S14] SOURCED.
- **How long.** The pull itself takes about 1 second. Getting through the punches to a grip can take several seconds or never happen. ESTIMATE.
- **What the boxer can do meanwhile.**
  - Before the grip: punch the grappler on the way in.
  - During the pull: sprawl the hips back and keep posture.
  - Once in closed guard: work from the top, or stand out of it (1c).

  DERIVED.
- **How it usually ends.** NOT FOUND for MMA. A pull from a collar tie or overhook breaks the boxer's posture and keeps the hands tied. A pull from double underhooks leaves the top fighter's hands free [S28] SOURCED.
- **The only number is from gi jiu-jitsu.** Among white and blue belts, the guard pull was 50% of takedown attempts and reached the ground 94% of the time [S29] SOURCED. That was against grapplers who weren't punching, so don't use it for a boxer. CHECK.
- **UFC 1 (12 Nov 1993) went the other way.**
  - Royce Gracie didn't pull guard on the boxer Art Jimmerson.
  - Gracie used feints and low kicks to make Jimmerson back off, then shot a double-leg at about 1:00.
  - Gracie moved to side control, then mount. Jimmerson tapped after about 55 seconds under the mount.
  - Jimmerson wore one glove, on the jab hand [S17] SOURCED.
  - Total time: 138 seconds by ESPN, 2:18 by Wikipedia [S18] CHECK.

### 1b. The striker refuses to follow the grappler down
| | Boxer | Grappler |
|---|---|---|
| Before | Standing | Seated or on back, feet towards the boxer |
| During (the "Inoki position") | Standing, circling outside leg range | On back, spinning on the hips, kicking at knees and thighs |
| After, under MMA rules | Standing | Stood up by the referee, or gets up by choice |
| After, under boxing rules | Not allowed | A downed boxer is any part other than the soles on the canvas, so it's a count [S7] |

**Ali vs Inoki, Nippon Budokan, 26 June 1976.**
- **The rules.** Inoki couldn't throw or tackle and could only kick with one knee down [S19; S20].
- **The standoff.** Inoki lay on the back for "all but the first 14 seconds" of round 1, scooting and kicking at Ali's knees.
- **The sweep.** In round 6 Ali grabbed an ankle. Inoki hooked Ali's calf with the other leg and tipped Ali over: a bottom-fighter sweep against a standing opponent.
- **The result.** Ali threw **six punches in 15 rounds**. It was a draw, and blood clots from the kicks to Ali's legs needed hospital care [S19] SOURCED.
- **CHECK:** accounts of the agreed rules differ, and Sherdog calls them "alleged" [S20].

**Anderson Silva vs Thales Leites, UFC 97, 18 Apr 2009.**
- Leites kept falling to the back.
- Referee Yves Lavigne stood Leites up **14 times**, seven in round 3, about once every 45 seconds [S22] SOURCED.
- The unified rules' tool for this is the stand-up, and avoiding contact is also the timidity foul [S1] SOURCED.

**Other caps on the standoff:**
- Combat jiu-jitsu allows 30 seconds of this "purgatory" per match before it costs overtime [S13].
- ADCC gives a minus point for going to the floor and staying there for 3 seconds or more [S15] SOURCED.

**What each side can do (unified MMA rules):**
- **The standing fighter** can kick the legs and body, step in to punch down, or walk away. Kicks, knees and stomps to the grounded fighter's head are illegal [S1] SOURCED.
- **The grounded fighter** can up-kick, spin on the hips, catch an ankle to sweep or leglock (Ali's round 6, Imanari [S32]), or stand up (1d). DERIVED.
- **How it ends.** Usually a referee stand-up or a stand-up by choice; there's no rate. NOT FOUND.

### 1c. The striker is in the closed guard and wants out
| Step | Boxer (top) | Grappler (bottom) |
|---|---|---|
| Start | In guard (top), head pulled down | Guard (bottom), legs locked, holding head or arms |
| 1. Posture | In guard (top), back straight, hands on hips or biceps | Pulling the head down, attacking arms (armbar, triangle, kimura) |
| 2. Stand up in the guard | Standing, legs still wrapped round the hips | Guard (bottom), hips off the mat, legs locked |
| 3. Break the lock | Standing, legs opened | On back, open guard, feet on the hips or knees |
| 4. Back away | Standing, out of leg range | **On back or seated: the grappler does NOT come up with the boxer** |

- **The key point for the cards.** After step 4 the grappler is still on the back and has to start their own stand-up (1d), and the boxer can attack in that window. DERIVED [S30; S1].
- **How long.** ESTIMATE:
  - posture: 1 to 5 seconds, often repeated;
  - standing up in the guard: 1 to 3 seconds;
  - breaking the ankles open: 1 to 5 seconds;
  - backing away: under a second.
- **The bottom fighter's chances in each step.**
  - **Posture:** an arm attack. Triangles and armbars come from here; they are 6.1% and 12.8% of all UFC submissions [S27] SOURCED. I found no share that counts only those from guard. NOT FOUND.
  - **Backing away:** an up-kick, an ankle catch to sweep (1e), or a stand-up.
- **Judging.** Punches and elbows from the top count for damage and dominance. Just holding a position "shall not be a primary factor", and a stalling top fighter gets stood up [S1; S6] SOURCED.
- **Leaving the top on purpose.** At UFC 128 (19 Mar 2011), Brendan Schaub couldn't do damage from the top against the striker Mirko Cro Cop. Schaub chose to stand up late in round 3 and won by knockout with an overhand right seconds later [S24] SOURCED.

### 1d. The bottom fighter gets back up
| Method | Bottom fighter: before → during → after | Top fighter: before → after |
|---|---|---|
| Technical stand-up, in open space | On back or seated → on one hand and the opposite foot, the other leg kicking or framing → standing in stance, a step back | Standing → standing |
| Wall walk, on the cage or ropes | Seated, back to the fence → back flat on the fence, feet walking in, hands on wrists or head → **standing, but still in a clinch on the fence** | In guard (top), kneeling or clinching → standing in the clinch |
| Wrestle-up from under top pressure | On back or seated → on the knees, fighting the hands → standing in a clinch, or still on the knees | In guard (top) → clinch, or on top again |

- **How the wall walk works.** Sit with the back on the fence and the feet under the hips, and control the opponent's wrists or head. The usual mistakes are turning the back and standing up without hand control. Being on your feet still doesn't give you room: you need an underhook and to circle off the fence [S30] SOURCED.
- **How long.** About 1 to 2 seconds for a technical stand-up in open space. 3 to 15 seconds or more for a wall walk with the opponent attached, and it often fails. ESTIMATE.

**When the stand-up gets punished, by rule set:**
- **Unified rules, Aug 2026 text.** "Grounded" means any part other than the hands or feet touching the canvas. So a fighter on hands and feet with no knee down can now be kneed and kicked in the head [S1; S6] SOURCED.
  - Mousasi vs Weidman, UFC 210 (8 Apr 2017): knees to a fighter with hands down were ruled legal under the 2017 rule, and the doctor stopped the fight [S5] SOURCED.
  - The definition has changed several times [S1–S4; S6]: before 2017 only the soles counted as not grounded; in 2017 both hands or a knee made you grounded; in 2022 one flat palm was enough; the 2025 and 2026 texts say hands don't count. Each commission adopts the change on its own [S5]. **CHECK.**
- **ONE.** One or both hands or a knee down means grounded, and **knees to the grounded head are legal** [S8] SOURCED.
- **Pride and Rizin.** Soccer kicks, knees and stomps to the grounded head were legal in Pride and still are in Rizin [S11; S12] SOURCED.
- **Boxing.** Not possible: a downed boxer is counted, and the other boxer goes to a neutral corner [S7].

**How it ends.** Best case, the fighter gets up and away. Common case, the fighter is up but pinned in a clinch on the fence. Worst case, dragged back down or hit on the way up. There's no rate for these. NOT FOUND. Fightnomics: about **one third** of position gains on the ground are later escaped or reversed [S24] SOURCED.

### 1e. Sweeps from the guard against someone standing up out of it
| Sweep | Bottom fighter's grip | Bottom: before → after | Top: before → after |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tripod | Hand on the heel, one foot on the hip, the other foot behind the heel | On back → on top or standing | Standing → sitting or on back |
| Sickle | Ankle grip, foot on the hip, the other leg chops the far calf | On back or side → on top or standing | Standing → seated or on back |
| Ankle pick from seated | Seated, one hand posted, grabs the heel | Seated → standing or on top | Standing → seated or on back |
| De La Riva or single-leg X | Legs hooked round one standing leg | On back → on top, or into a leg lock | Standing → seated or on back, or caught in a leg lock |
| Ali's ankle trap (from a real bout) | Hooks the calf while the ankle is held | On back → on top [S19] | Standing → on back |

- **Mechanics.** Control one ankle and the far wrist, put a foot on the hip, then push while pulling the heel. The sickle is the follow-up if the hook slips [S33] SOURCED (technique guides).
- **Why they work on a fighter standing up.** The weight is on the heels and one foot is close to the grappler's hands. DERIVED.
- **Why they're harder in MMA.** There's no gi to grip, and the standing fighter can punch down or kick the legs while the grappler's hands are busy. DERIVED [S1].
- **How long.** 1 to 3 seconds once the grips are set. ESTIMATE.
- **How it usually ends.** No MMA rate. NOT FOUND. A headline claims "50% of MMA sweeps come from half guard", but I only saw the headline [S34]. CHECK.
- **Leg locks against a standing striker.** Masakazu Imanari baits a kick or a check, grabs the leg and rolls under into a leg lock [S32] SOURCED. There's no bout-level rate.

**Notes on the table:**
- **ONE yellow card.** It costs 10% of the purse and can count in the decision [S8]. A fighter was red-carded for soccer kicks in a Muay Thai bout on 10 Oct 2025 [S10] SOURCED.
- **Pride.** Fights were scored as a whole, and the first round lasted 10 minutes. In some bouts with a weight gap, the lighter fighter chose whether kicks and knees to the head on all fours were allowed [S11] SOURCED.
- **Rizin** [S12] SOURCED:
  - fights are in a ring;
  - soccer kicks, stomps and 12-6 elbows are legal;
  - judges score damage first, then aggressiveness, then ring control.
- **ADCC** [S15] SOURCED:
  - a takedown that lands in guard scores 2, and a clean takedown past the guard scores 4;
  - a takedown scores nothing if the opponent turtles for 3+ s;
  - a shot followed by a guard pull within 3 s is a minus point.
- **IBJJF** [S14] SOURCED, via second-hand summaries of Rule Book v6.1, June 2024 (CHECK):
  - takedown, sweep and knee on belly score 2; a pass scores 3; mount and back score 4; each has to be held for 3 s;
  - white belts may not jump guard.

### One honest mixed rule set (proposal, as asked)
I'd base it on the **current unified MMA rules**, because they're what UK and US MMA use and they're fully written down [S1]. Each choice, and what it changes:
1. **Grounded means anything other than hands and feet touching the floor [S1].** The stand-up on hands and feet becomes a real risk window. The 2017 version (both hands down counts as grounded) would make the rising fighter safer.
2. **The standing fighter can punch and elbow to the head of a grounded fighter, and kick or knee the body and legs. No kicks, knees or stomps to the head [S1].** Refusing to follow is safe but slow. Adding ONE's knees to the grounded head [S8] punishes a kneeling or turtled fighter. Adding Pride's soccer kicks [S11] makes lying on the back against a striker a losing choice.
3. **Up-kicks to a standing opponent's head are legal; to a kneeling opponent's head, not [S1].** The boxer has to respect the range of the grappler's feet, as in Henderson vs Edgar [S24].
4. **A guard pull is legal but scores nothing. Rounds are judged on damage, then dominance, then duration [S1; S6].** A pull costs the grappler on the scorecards unless something comes of it. ADCC's minus point [S15] would make sitting down cost a point straight away.
5. **Standing over a grounded opponent is allowed. If the grounded fighter refuses to engage, the referee stands them up (Leites: about every 45 s [S22]). Repeated avoidance is the timidity foul [S1].** Nobody can stall forever in the Inoki position. Combat jiu-jitsu's 30 s purgatory clock [S13] is the stricter option.
6. **The referee stands the fighters up when there's no real effort to finish, and holding a position isn't effort [S1].** A grappler on top who doesn't strike or attack loses the position, so the boxer has a way back up.
7. **The clinch allows strikes and takedowns and is broken only for inactivity [S1].** The boxer has to fight out of it instead of waiting for a boxing-style break [S7]. Glory's rule (one strike, then let go [S16]) would keep the boxer safe and make grappling much harder.
8. **Both fighters wear MMA gloves.** Positions don't change; this only removes Jimmerson's one-glove oddity [S17].

## 3. The clinch
| Rule set | Strikes | Takedowns | How long the referee lets it run |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unified MMA | Punches, elbows and knees to the head and body (DERIVED) [S1] | Yes, but no spiking on the head | Broken only without "real, significant and/or sustained effort" [S1]; no fixed clock [S31] |
| ONE | The same, plus knees to the head once a knee or hand is down [S8] | Yes | "Action" call, then cards |
| Glory kickboxing (since Jan 2025) | **One** immediate strike, then release [S16] | No | Immediate |
| Boxing | Holding is a foul | Wrestling the opponent down is a foul | Broken at once: "Stop, Break", arm's length, step back [S7] |

**In MMA numbers** (Fightnomics, UFC 2007 to Apr 2013 [S24], SOURCED):
- The clinch takes up about **18%** of fight time.
- Clinch strikes land more often but hit softer. The knockdown rate per landed power head strike is **less than half** the rate from distance.
- In **64 KO/TKOs from the clinch**, the finishing strike was a punch 47% of the time, a knee 42% and an elbow 9%.
- Takedowns from the clinch succeed more often than shots from distance, which had "less than a one-in-three chance".

**How a boxer keeps a grappler off** (coaching guides, not bout data [S34; S30] SOURCED):
- **Sprawl** when the grappler drops levels: hips back and down, chest on the shoulders, win an underhook or whizzer, frame the head, then circle back to striking range.
- **On the fence:** don't stay flat on the fence. Win the underhook, post a forearm, control the head, and step off at an angle towards open space.
- **Punch the way in.** Gracie only got Jimmerson down after low kicks and feints stopped Jimmerson coming forward [S17].

**A real failure.** At UFC 118 (28 Aug 2010), Randy Couture took the boxing champion James Toney down "by the ankle" after a few seconds of circling. Couture mounted, punched, and won with an arm-triangle choke at 3:19 of round 1. Toney "held on, hoping to force a standup" [S21] SOURCED.

## 6. What games most often get wrong about style against style
1. **One shared position.** Each fighter has their own. When the top fighter stands up, the bottom fighter is still down and has to spend an action to get up [S30; S19].
2. **Free strikes at a grounded opponent.** A grounded fighter's head is at knee height and their feet are in the way: Ali threw six punches in 15 rounds [S19]. Under unified rules, kicks and knees to that head are illegal [S1].
3. **Getting up is free.** It's the riskiest moment on the ground, through knees to a fighter with hands down [S1], ONE's knees to the grounded head [S8], soccer kicks [S11; S12], or being pinned on the fence after a wall walk [S30].
4. **The guard treated as a pure loss or a pure win.** It's a real place to attack from [S27], but it loses rounds when nothing happens, and many takedowns stall in full guard [S1; S24; S28].
5. **Guard pulls with no contact.** Without a grip, a pull is just sitting down, and the striker can walk away [S28; S14].
6. **Takedowns that always work.** About 63% are stuffed [S26], and shots from distance do worse than clinch takedowns [S24].
7. **No referee.** Stand-ups for no progress, the timidity foul and restarts in the same position are a big part of the position game [S1; S8; S22].
8. **The same clinch everywhere.** Boxing breaks it at once [S7], Glory allows one strike [S16], and MMA lets an active clinch run with knees and takedowns [S1].
9. **Strikes from the bottom as strong as from the top.** Bottom fighters can't wind up with force; only an up-kick at a standing opponent is a real threat [S24].
10. **Top position as the end of the story.** About a third of position gains are escaped or reversed, and a top fighter who only holds gets stood up. Sometimes standing up on purpose is the best move [S1; S24].
11. **Assuming one rule set.** The same move can be legal, a foul or a minus point depending on the rules, and the definition of "grounded" has changed several times since 2016 [S1–S5].
12. **The boxer as a grappler with fists.** Boxers with no ground training (Jimmerson, Toney) had nothing to do on the ground except wait for the referee [S17; S21].

## What I couldn't verify
- **Guard pulls against strikers.** No rate for how often they end in a sweep or submission in MMA.
- **Ground exchanges.** No average length before a stand-up; the values in the Fightnomics chart were lost in the scanned text.
- **Half-guard sweeps.** I only saw the headline of the BJJEE "50% of sweeps from half guard" article.
