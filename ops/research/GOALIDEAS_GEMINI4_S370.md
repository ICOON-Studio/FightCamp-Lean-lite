1. **The 15-Second "Pad-Work Audition" (Borrows from: real amateur belt/grade assessments & *Football Manager* key highlights)**
Non-competing boxers never get a "fight night," so make Assessment Day their spectator payoff: cut from the 2D menu to a 15-second 3D clip of the coach calling out the grade's required combo on the focus mitts. Seeing her tangle her feet and drop her guard in Week 1, versus hearing three crisp leather *cracks* and a coach's nod before the PASSED stamp hits in Week 8, makes the menu planning visceral.

2. **Combos That "Click" and Cost Less (Borrows from: *Slay the Spire* card upgrades & real-world motor "chunking")**
When a boxer first slots a three-punch combo together, the move cards should feel taxed—high stamina cost and a visible pause between punches. Passing a Speed or Skill grade visually links those cards with a flow connector that shaves off stamina cost and tightens the transition frames, so the player literally feels the combo go from "thinking about every punch" to effortless muscle memory.

3. **The Gym Chalkboard & Club Records (Borrows from: real CrossFit/boxing gym whiteboards & *Hades* administrative hall)**
A 9.0 bleep-test score buried in a character sheet feels like a spreadsheet; that same 9.0 written in chalk on the gym wall feels like a trophy. Let recreational boxers claim spots on the club's physical record board (Bleep Test Queen, Heavy Bag Impulse, 3-Minute Skip Record), which stays on the wall and gives novices in the gym a small training boost when chasing "Sarah's 9.0."

4. **Heavy-Bag Sound & Chain Physics Tiers (Borrows from: *Rhythm Heaven* / *Punch Club* audio juice & real bag work)**
In a mostly 2D game, sound carries the weight of physical progress. Tie Power and Speed grades to distinct audio samples and bag reactions during weekly check-ins: a novice makes a flat, slapping *thud* that barely sways the bag, while a Gold-grade puncher produces a deep, room-filling *whump* that folds the leather and rattles the ceiling chains.

5. **"Day 1 vs. Today" Ghost Split-Screen (Borrows from: *Mario Kart* time-trial ghosts & real coaching phone-video review)**
Slow stat creep is hard to feel week-to-week because the player adapts to the new normal. On Test Day, show a brief side-by-side (or ghost overlay) of her throwing the assessment combo on Day 1 next to today—watching her present self snap back to guard while her Day 1 ghost is still winded and wide open makes months of quiet menu choices unmistakable.

6. **Club Pillar Roles for Non-Fighters (Borrows from: *Princess Maker* / *Monster Rancher* life-sim titles & real boxing club culture)**
In real gyms, dedicated non-fighters become the soul of the room: they get trusted to lead the skip warm-up, hold mitts for nervous beginners, or do light technical touch-sparring to help a competitor prep. Unlocking these club titles at Silver, Gold, and Platinum gives a non-competing boxer a real job in your stable and a reason for the player to care about her long-term mastery.

7. **The "Taper & Freshness" Landing Before Test Day (Borrows from: real S&C peaking & *Motorsport Manager* qualifying prep)**
If the "in 4 weeks on this plan" projection bar moves purely linearly, the player just sets a schedule and clicks Next Week four times. Make the test care about *freshness* on the day: grinding hard in Weeks 1–3 raises her ceiling, but pulling back two days before Sunday lets her legs recover so she actually hits her projected 8.8 on the bleep test instead of stalling at 8.3 with heavy legs.

---

**Strongest Two:**
* **#1 (The 15-Second Pad-Work Audition)** and **#2 (Combos That "Click")**: Together they close the loop between your 2D planning menus and your 3D viewer/card system. #1 gives the non-competing boxer a genuine "fight night" climax using the 3D assets you already have, while #2 makes passing a grade immediately change how her combo deck plays and costs stamina.

**One Thing to AVOID (The Trap):**
* **The Stat-Decay Treadmill (the worst habit of early *Punch Club*):** Never let earned grades or hard-won base fitness rapidly decay just because the player switches focus from Engine to Power for a month. If a recreational boxer's bleep test slides backward every week she spends learning head movement, training stops feeling like building an athlete and starts feeling like spinning plates to stop red arrows. Keep grades permanent, base conditioning sticky, and use short-term fatigue/sharpness for week-to-week tension instead.
