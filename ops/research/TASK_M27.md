## Why this, and it comes out of a real finding today

The operator asked how real fight plans are made, and we checked our own
research rather than guessing. `FIGHT_PLANS_RESEARCH.md` took **fifteen
disciplines** apart and found every fight plan has the same **seven parts**.

Our skill tree covers some of them and misses others. **The biggest miss is
part 6: THE SIGNATURE BATTLE** - *"the one duel that decides whether the plan
is viable"*. The research names it per discipline:

> the jab in boxing, kumi-kata in judo, guard-vs-pass in BJJ, the tie-up in
> wrestling, clinch entry in Muay Thai, the cage in MMA, distance in karate and
> savate, the catch in silat and sanda.

The tree shows WHERE the fight happens. **It never marks WHICH fight inside
that place decides it.** In the clinch column, the tie-up IS that duel and it
currently looks like one row of eighteen.

## THE TRAP

**Making it louder than the selection.** A player's pointer is the loudest
thing on any screen and must stay so. The signature mark is a standing property
of the technique; selection is where the player IS. If your treatment competes
with the pointer, the screen starts arguing with the person using it.

**Second trap:** dressing it as a warning. A signature battle is not danger. It
is the hinge - equally true when he is excellent at it.
