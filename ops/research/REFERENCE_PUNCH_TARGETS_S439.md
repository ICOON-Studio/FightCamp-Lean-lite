# Punch targets from two shipped boxing games (S439)

Measured read-only from the installed files into numbers only (median and range per punch type); nothing was exported. Each table was re-measured by a second reader. Use these as the targets our own clips are fixed against.

## Blood & Canvas: Bareknuckle Boxing (demo), Unreal Engine 5 (project BKKO), build at E:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/Blood&CanvasBareknuckleBoxingDemo. 59 punch clips on the UE5 Mannequin skeleton: 36 in the base set and 23 in the 'Necio' set. CHECKED by a second reader: 8 clips decoded again, 14 more cross-read, all montage events re-read.

The check passed on the main numbers, with corrections. I decoded 8 clips again key by key and recomputed turns, elbow and hips on 7, matching the first reader within rounding. Units are confirmed: Unreal cm (upper arm 27.8 cm, forearm 27.3), +Y forward, Z up, 30 Hz keys (60 Hz on two Necio hooks), speeds in m/s.
Corrected:
- Contact matches the stored ComboReady only on head forms. Body jab/cross and step cross are one key off, the step lead hook 2-3 keys.
- 'Drive' times include slow keys. The real fast part of a straight is 2-3 keys.
- Only straights stop dead. Hooks and overhands carry 9-10 m/s through contact.
- 'Peak at contact' is a per-key average and partly set by the contact rule.
- Shoulder turns are the upper-arm line. The clavicle line usually turns less (power lead hook 35 vs 52).
- Uppercut 'hold' figures are struck: the rule landed on the bottom of the dip.
Ten targets for fixing our clips, most useful first:
1. Snap the strike into the last 2-3 keys. Their jab covers 39 of 57 cm in the final 33 ms (1.2 -> 6.0 -> 11.8 m/s), and the power cross 40 of 69 cm (2.9 -> 7.9 -> 12.0). Make the last 1-2 keys before contact the fastest of the punch.
2. Time from first movement to contact: about 0.20 s for jab and cross, 0.27-0.30 s for plain hooks and uppercuts, 0.40-0.47 s for power hooks, uppercuts and overhands.
3. Fastest interval: aim for a last-key average of about 10-12 m/s (power uppercuts 14-16). Ours is 4.4-5.4 m/s, peaking 3-6 frames early, per CROSS_POWER_S439.
4. End of the strike by type. Straights stop at full reach (next key under 1 m/s), elbow 150-165, never locked. Hooks and overhands pass through at 9-10 m/s, elbow 105-130. Uppercuts land with elbow about 90-110.
5. No frozen wind-up. The hand moves from the first key. Load: straights draw back 5-12 cm, hooks swing out 22-45 cm, uppercuts dip 16-60 cm. Power forms pause briefly in the loaded spot before the drive.
6. No hold at reach. At most 1-2 keys there. Back in guard 0.30 s after contact for jab and cross, 0.33-0.50 s for hooks and uppercuts. That matches their ComboReset (0.53 jab, 0.60 hooks, 0.67-0.87 power).
7. Coil, then unwind. Hips and shoulders first turn away, then into the punch. At contact the hips are about 20 for jab and cross, 28-30 for hooks, 40-67 for power rear hook and uppercut. The shoulder girdle is about 40-60 by the clavicle line (up to 100 on the power rear uppercut). Power hooks wind back 45-65 first.
8. Modest hip travel in place, with no root motion: pelvis 2-4 cm forward for straights, about 10 cm for hooks and overhands, 16-19 cm for uppercuts. The head goes 4-8 cm on straights and 15-20 cm on hooks, overhands and uppercuts.
9. Guard hand. On plain punches it stays 15-20 cm from the head. On the cross and rear hook the lead glove closes in to 13-18 cm. Only power hooks, overhands and power uppercuts swing it out to 40-50 cm as a counter-balance.
10. Events tied to the motion. Hit detection is live from 0.07-0.10 s. Combo-ready falls on the contact key, give or take one. Feet are free at 0.43-0.70 s. Blends are 0.06 s in and out.
For lunges only, if we want them: the step forms carry 24-30 cm of root motion by contact and 62 cm by the end, with the pelvis 35-56 cm forward at contact. Their demo may never fire them.

Corrections made:
(1) The claim that 'contact agrees with ComboReady within 0.01 s in every set' is too strong. It holds for the head forms of plain, power and uppercut punches. It misses by one key on the jab and cross body forms (ComboReady 0.266-0.271 against 0.233), on the step cross (0.267-0.269 against 0.300) and on one power lead uppercut clip (0.467 against 0.433). It misses by 2-3 keys on the step lead hook (ComboReady 0.301 head, 0.467 body, against 0.367).
(2) 'Drive' durations include slow creeping keys. The fast part of a straight is only 2-3 keys (0.07-0.10 s). The jab covers 39 of its 57 cm in the final 33 ms, and the power cross 40 of 69 cm.
(3) 'Stops dead' is true for straights only (the next key is 0.6 m/s). Hooks and overhands pass through at 9.6-10.5 m/s on the key after contact.
(4) 'Peak at contact' is partly built into the contact rule (contact = the earlier of the nearest pass and the last key at half peak or more). At 30 Hz it is an average over the last 33 ms. For straights the true speed must fall to zero inside that key, so the SEEN fact is 'the last 33 ms before full reach is the fastest interval', not 'the fist lands at full speed'.
(5) 'Shoulder turn' is the upper-arm line. It includes the arm reaching. The clavicle line usually turns less: jab 41 against 46, power cross 59 against 71, power lead hook 35 against 52, rear hook 52 against 59. The power rear uppercut is the exception at 102 against 94.
(6) For uppercuts the 'hold at reach' rule measures the bottom of the dip, not the top. The knuckle's farthest point from guard is the dip (AS_UppercutLeftPower: 68 cm below guard at 0.400 s), so the uppercut hold figures are struck.
(7) On 26 of 30 body forms the knuckle missed the body target height by 10-30 cm. Their contact fell back to the 'last fast key' rule, so body contact times are less certain.
(8) The 'ours' numbers in the summary (4.4-5.4 m/s, peak 3-6 frames early, hold 0.25 s) come from ops/research/CROSS_POWER_S439.md, which covers our pro right cross only. They were not re-measured here. '0.3-0.7 s before the hand moves' could not be traced and is struck.
