# Research brief (S531): how often boxing bouts end early, by weight class and by sex

FightCamp is a boxing gym management game (amateur to first pro fights). Today a fighter's punch power and chin are
0-100 ratings read inside her own division, and bouts are only booked inside one sex and one division - so damage is
never scaled by weight or sex. We want real numbers to decide whether a heavier class or the men's side should stop
fights more often, and by how much.

Find, with sources (name each: BoxRec studies, England Boxing / AIBA / IBA results, academic papers, CompuBox):
1. Professional boxing: share of bouts ending by KO/TKO, by weight class (flyweight ... heavyweight), men.
2. The same for women's professional boxing, by class where available.
3. Amateur (Olympic-style, 3 x 3 min, headguards or not): share of RSC/stoppages, men vs women, light vs heavy.
4. Knockdowns per bout by class, if any study has it.
5. Debut and early-career pros (4-6 rounders): stoppage share vs the whole pro population.
Give a table per question, the year and sample size of each source, and say plainly where data is thin.
End with: the three numbers you would put in a game (stoppage share light / middle / heavy, women vs men, amateur vs
pro), and how sure you are of each.
