GAP 1 (CRITICAL POLISH): Medallion Head is Undersized & Needs High-Contrast Bezel
The Failure: The new medallion is a massive improvement, but at 1080p the coin head is rendering at roughly $36\text{ px} \times 36\text{ px}$.
At this size, the stamped gold fist emblem inside the red enamel is readable if you squint, but it lacks the heavy, proud, physical presence shown in the reference drawing (where the medallion reads as a collectible championship prize).
The outer brass bezel is currently a thin wire, so it blends into the red enamel disc from a distance.
Why It Holds It Back: In a management game, the selected object must look authoritative and rewarding. It should feel like slamming a heavy brass seal onto a contract.
The Exact Numeric Fix:
Scale Increase: Scale the entire marker assembly (coin head + banner) up by $+35%$:
Coin Diameter: Increase from current $\approx 36\text{ px}$ to $52\text{ px} \times 52\text{ px}$.
Needle Height: Increase from current $\approx 20\text{ px}$ to $30\text{ px}$.
Needle Width: $4\text{ px}$ at top, tapering to $1.5\text{ px}$ at contact.
Medallion Framing & Contrast:
Outer Bezel Rim: Set to $4.0\text{ px}$ width, Chamfered Brass #f0c233, Metallic 0.95, Roughness 0.18.
Inner Enamel Floor: Deep Blood Oxblood #781412 (slightly darker to increase contrast against the gold glove).
Gold Glove Emblem: Keep at $26\text{ px} \times 26\text{ px}$, Pure Championship Gold #f0c233, with a $1.0\text{ px}$ dark drop shadow #1a0505 around its perimeter.
Contact Shadow Tuning:
Enlarge the terrain shadow ellipse beneath the needle: $32\text{ px} \times 12\text{ px}$, opacity 70%, Gaussian blur 4\text{ px}.
GAP 2 (HIGH POLISH): The Backdrop is Still Pure Pitch-Black Void
The Failure: The concentric astrolabe rings look great, but the empty space between and behind them is pure #000000 void.
Because the globe sits on a pure black background, the dark side of the globe (the southern hemisphere and the unlit right flank) melts completely into nothingness.
It still feels slightly like a spaceship view rather than a globe in an antique, smoky brass-and-wood athletic office.
Why It Holds It Back: A pure black background flattens 3D depth. Shipped strategy games (Civ VI, HOI4) always have ambient environmental bounce and texture.
The Exact Numeric Fix:
Base Slate Canvas: Replace pure black #000000 with Aged Warm Cast Iron Slate: #131211 ($R: 0.075, G: 0.071, B: 0.067$).
Radial Cartographic Vignette: Add a fullscreen CanvasItem radial gradient behind the globe:
Center: Center of the 3D globe $(X: 680\text{ px}, Y: 540\text{ px})$.
Inner Radius ($0\text{ px} - 450\text{ px}$): Warm Charcoal Glow #1c1a18 (alpha 100%).
Outer Radius ($450\text{ px} - 1100\text{ px}$): Deep Vignette #0a0909 (alpha 95%).
Result: Instantly gives the dark side of the globe a soft back-silhouette, separating it from the screen edges.
Astrolabe Ring Polishing:
Rings are currently a bit stark: soften their color to Antique Brass #b8933b at 22% opacity with a soft additive blend mode.
GAP 3 (HIGH POLISH): Right Panel Vertical Distribution & Dead Space
The Failure: The right data card looks significantly better: the flag icon, the tier subtitle, the 3 stat progress bars, and the gold "CHOOSE MARKET" button are all styled properly.
However, all the content is crammed into the top $30%$ of the panel ($Y: 0 - 320\text{ px}$), leaving a massive empty black desert of dead space ($Y: 320 - 1016\text{ px}$) above the bottom button.
Why It Holds It Back: Professional UI balances vertical weight. Either panels size themselves to fit their content, or the content expands to fill the panel with rich, secondary tactical data.
The Exact Numeric Fix: Do not leave that $500\text{ px}$ gap empty. Use it to display secondary scouting intelligence:

GAP 4 (MEDIUM POLISH): Ocean Material Specular Highlight on Key Light
The Failure: The black ocean material has great depth, but it is currently rendering almost entirely matte.
In the reference concepts, the directional light catches the curvature of the North Atlantic Ocean with a tight, crisp, brassy/white specular glint that highlights the spherical shape of the earth.
The Exact Numeric Fix:
In the Ocean Spatial Shader:
Roughness: Lower from current $\approx 0.45$ to 0.18 (High-sheen black lacquer).
Metallic: Set to 0.08.
Specular: Increase to 0.75.
Directional Key Light: Ensure Light Energy = 2.2 with Specular = 0.85.
Result: As the player drags to spin the globe, the ocean produces a tight, sliding specular glint that makes the sphere feel like polished mahogany or lacquered stone.
GAP 5 (LOW POLISH): Name Tag Background Tone
The Failure: The name tag "UNITED KINGDOM" is currently rendered as pure clinical stark white (#FFFFFF).
Pure white jumps out of the 1970s warm palette and feels slightly like modern desktop OS text.
The Exact Numeric Fix:
Change Tag Background Fill to Aged Manila / Warm Cream: #f6f0e0 ($R: 0.96, G: 0.94, B: 0.88$).
Add a $1.5\text{ px}$ Dark Iron Rim: #2b2f36 with a $3\text{ px}$ chamfer on the corners.
Text Color: Change from pure black to Deep Oiled Charcoal: #1e1d1b.
