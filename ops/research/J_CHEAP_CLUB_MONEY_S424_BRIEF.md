# Question for John: what keeps a cheap community boxing club alive, money-wise?

For a boxing gym management game. The player picks the club's dues: cheap ($25 a month), standard ($35) or premium ($45).
Our 5-year sums show premium always earns the most once the gym is full, so the cheap community club never makes sense in
the game. Real clubs do run cheap on purpose. Find what pays for it. Real-world evidence only; say plainly where none exists.

1. Grants and funding open to a low-cost amateur boxing club in England: Sport England (Small Grants, Movement Fund),
   Club Matters, England Boxing's own funds, councils, the National Lottery, charities, police/youth-crime funds. For each:
   typical award size, how often a club can get one, and the conditions (juniors, deprived area, affordable pricing,
   accredited coaches, a safeguarding officer). Any figure on how many clubs receive one in a year.
2. Other money a cheap club relies on: free or cheap premises (council, church halls), volunteers instead of paid coaches,
   fundraising shows, sponsors. Any club accounts or case studies with numbers.
3. Premium boxing/fitness gyms: member churn compared with community clubs (any split at all, UK or US), and how many of
   their members ever compete.
4. Where competitive amateur boxers come from: share who started as juniors at the club they box for, vs joined as adults.
5. Same in brief for the US (USA Boxing clubs, PAL gyms, non-profit status, grants).
Give a number with its source wherever one exists, mark OFFICIAL / MEASURED / OPINION, and list what you could not find.
