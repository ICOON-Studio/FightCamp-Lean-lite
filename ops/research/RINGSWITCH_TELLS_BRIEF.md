# Research wanted: what a boxer READS before a punch, with numbers

You are the researcher. We are building a small boxing game (Ring Switches) in which both men choose at once: each
beat a man picks what his lead hand, his rear hand and his feet do. The playtest says the main guess (which of his
lines is open, which punch is coming) is blind: a coin flip, not a read. We want what REAL boxing says a fighter can
read before a punch, sourced, so the game's tells are true ones. Plain words, tables first, every number with its
source and whether it is a measured study, a coach's teaching, or a commentator's claim.

## What we need
1. THE TELLS. For each punch family (jab, cross, lead hook, rear hook / overhand, uppercuts, body punches) and for a
   step in / step back: what moves BEFORE the fist does (shoulder, hip, front foot, elbow, head, weight, eyes, breath),
   how long before (milliseconds, if a study measured it), and who says so.
2. HOW RELIABLE. Studies of anticipation in boxing or other combat sports (occlusion studies, expert against novice):
   how often an expert calls the punch right from the early cues, and how often a novice does. The numbers, the sample,
   the year.
3. WHAT A GUARD POSITION GIVES AWAY. If a man holds his lead hand low or his rear hand away from the chin, what do
   coaches say he is about to do, and what is he inviting?
4. HABITS. What counted studies or CompuBox-style numbers exist on how predictable a boxer is: the share of his punches
   that are his favourite punch, how often a combination repeats, whether a tired boxer becomes more readable.
5. FEINTS. What a feint is meant to make the other man do, how often fighters use one, and anything measured about
   whether it works.
6. STYLES. Is a pressure fighter (a slugger), a jabber who fights at range, and a body puncher each read by a different
   cue? Coaches' words, with the source.
7. WHAT IS NOT KNOWN. Say plainly where there is no study, only teaching.

## How it will be used
A designer will turn the three or four most reliable cues into things the player can see on the other boxer before he
chooses. So the most useful answer ranks the cues by how reliable and how visible they are from a side view.
