# A week worth playing - the weekly-loop design (S425, systems designer)

Lens: the Monday-to-Sunday loop. Rule I held myself to: every piece must give a reason to look forward to next Monday, a choice
the auto-button cannot make, and a reward that opens a new choice. I joined existing rows before inventing. Boxers are "the boxer"
or by name (sandbox rule); Lucy, Danny and Ana are the example club (Ana = no-gi).

## What I took from the four dead reviews
- Every mechanic that scored 4 had ONE stake (one eye, one week, one card) and a dominated option or a lookup. The fix is not a
  bigger number; it is a stake that CARRIES: today's choice changes what is on offer in three weeks.
- The measured facts I build on and do not touch: the coach's hands = one week on a card; an answer not yet usable on fight night
  costs 2 to 14 wins in 100; sharp pays only 1 point over usable; a plan-reader costs 6.5 points and nothing wins it back yet.
- That last fact is the hole in the game: pad time dies at usable, and a read line cannot be answered. Mechanic B below is built
  to fill both holes at once. That is why it is my strongest.

## 1. THE WEEK AS PLAYED

### Busy week (Lucy in camp, fight Saturday of week 4; Danny two weeks into base; Ana on the mat)
- MONDAY (Home, the Monday page). Rows land one at a time (R07, R11). What he looks at: the DOCKET (mechanic A): five or six
  dated lines, each one something he set going. "Jab-Jab usable Thursday." "Scale: 0.9 kg over, on track Friday." "Tape on
  Maria: one tell opens Wednesday." "Danny's ask: answer Thursday." Each line is green, amber or red against the fight date.
  He decides: the twist card (R05, two priced answers); the lay of Reid's three evenings (mechanic C); which plan line Lucy tests
  Thursday (mechanic B); Danny's ask (mechanic D). Then presses CONTINUE.
- TUESDAY-WEDNESDAY. Nothing stops the clock unless something needs him. Wednesday: the tape tell opens (a flag, one click).
- THURSDAY: the mid-week call. The clock stops ONCE, only if something landed: Lucy's sparring report (B), the answer to
  Danny's ask (D), the card that went usable (A). He answers what the week showed: rewrite a line, move Friday's pad night,
  take or refuse the offer. If nothing landed there is no stop: a quiet Thursday stays one click.
- FRIDAY-SUNDAY: the camp's long days run; Saturday week 4 is fight night. The corner minute uses lines he chose to show or
  hold (mechanic B). Sunday evening: the Why names which lines were seen, which won, and what the boxer's file now says.
- NEXT MONDAY: the docket closes: landed on time, landed late, surprise (a swing with its reason, R11). New lines open from it.

### Quiet week (no fight booked: the hardest week)
Today this is one click and nothing happens. With the pieces below it is the week the club grows:
- Monday docket shows a "Gym week" block: the coach's evenings have no pad claim, so mechanic C hands him the pool to spend on
  the gym: an induction course for the 6 newest members (the 90-day cliff, R74), the youth class (R71), or open sparring (a
  prospect trial for a Find-a-fighter name, R50). He picks one or two; each lands Thursday with a named result ("4 of 6 stayed;
  one has a quick left hand: invite to a trial?").
- He asks for a fight (D) so the next camp starts with the answer in hand. He drills a held line (B) in sparring, unseen.
- Reward he can name by Sunday: a member kept, a prospect found, a fight asked for, a line sharpened. The quiet week FEEDS
  the next busy one. That is the whole point: no week is empty because the pool of hours is never free, only spent differently.

## 2. THE FOUR MECHANICS

### A. THE DOCKET (Home Monday page + week review). Joins R92, R93, R07, R11, R14 (replaced), R05
The game already knows dated things: camp tape opens a tell every few days, a card goes usable in week N, the scale forecast, an
offer expires, an ask gets answered. Today they sit on separate pages. The docket is one list of "what I set going and when it
lands", on the Monday page, each line with a colour against the bell.
Decisions in order:
- Before: when he sets a thing going (drill, scout, ask, cut) the docket line appears with its landing day, so the price of
  starting late is visible ("start the cut Monday: lands Friday; Wednesday: misses by 0.4 kg").
- During: a line can be pulled forward at a price (the chosen answer needs a pad night moved, a scout bought for $250) or left.
- After: the week review reads the docket back. Landed, late, surprise. The surprise line carries its reason (R11).
- Across the career: lines he often leaves late become the coach's first-Monday note ("your cuts start late").
Case for each option: pull-forward is right when the bell is near and the line is red; leave is right when it is green or the
bell is far. Cut: "tease next Monday's twist" (R14): it had no choice behind it; the docket replaces it with things HE started.
Anticipation: he reads the docket on Monday and wants to see it land. Reward: a green board is visible progress; each landing
opens the next line (usable card opens the plan line that needs it; a found tell opens its answer).
Menus: Home (the docket itself, replaces "Since last Monday" order), Fighters > Training (each session shows its landing line),
Fight prep (the docket filtered to the bout), week review (landed/late/surprise), The Why (which lines landed in time).
Reuses: WeekCause, CampForecast, PadWeek, OppFile tape days, Needs, offers' dates. NEW: one Docket module that collects due
dates and states; a "Thursday stop" in Needs (only when something landed). SIZE: small-medium (it is a view plus one stop).
Numbers: dates, bands, fatigue 80 line, scale kg: FROM THE GAME. Thursday as the stop day: INVENTED (it is the middle of the week).

### B. THE FILM ON YOU: held lines and the Thursday spar report. Joins R17, R20, R21, R19, R25, R23, R46, R47, R55's step 1
This is the one that carries across weeks, camps and years. Everyone who fights you studies your public bouts, as you study theirs
in the Tape Room. A plan line is either UNSEEN or SEEN (seen = used 2+ times in one recorded bout, the game's own FREE_USES, and
the bout is public: a club show is public, a sparring night is not).
The decisions, in order:
1. WEEK, sparring night (R20, R46): pick the partner: a club mate (free, unscouted style), or an outside mate of the opponent's
   style ($160, exists today). Pick which plan line to test. A sparring night is private: the line stays UNSEEN.
2. THURSDAY, the spar report (the mid-week stop): plain lines: "the 1-1 won 5 of 6 on a presser; the step-back lost 3 of 4"
   (accuracy rises with the scouting done, R21: unscouted = a range). He answers: rewrite the weak line, drill the strong one on
   Friday's pad night (this is where mechanic C's evenings get their value), or leave it.
3. BOOKING (Fights, offer card): the offer now shows what the opponent could have seen of the boxer: "Maria saw the 1-1 at the
   club show, 3 weeks ago." HOLD OR SHOW: he can enter a line in the plan, or hold it back ("held" = the coach writes the plan
   without it). Showing wins more now; holding keeps it unseen for a bigger night.
4. FIGHT NIGHT (R25, rebuilt): a seen line against an opponent with the ring IQ to read it is answered from round 2 (the note,
   14 words, names the line). An unseen line is not. The corner's choice is the swap, and now it has a real answer: a second drilled
   answer to the same situation (the back-up line) is unseen, so a read line has a way back. This is the counterplay R25's own map
   says is missing. It must be measured before build (see kill list).
5. AFTER (the Why, History): the Why says which lines the world has now seen; History shows a "seen" mark per line and by whom;
   a rematch (R47) starts with the other corner's file on the boxer and the boxer's file on them, both visible.
6. CAREER: sharpness pays. A read line at Signature (85) loses only part of its edge; a read line at Raw loses all. So drilling
   beyond usable has a job again (the hole in R55's step 1: sharp pays 1 point today). Reward: a "house line" that opponents
   know and still cannot stop - the player's own style, earned, visible on the boxer's page.
Case for each option:
- Show the line: right when the win chance is under about 70% (you need it) or the opponent cannot read (low ring IQ).
- Hold the line: right in a tune-up against a much weaker boxer, where the win chance is above about 80% without it, and a
  bigger night (eliminator, nationals, title) is booked within the line's life.
- Test it Thursday in private: right whenever a bout is booked; the cost is one sparring night that did not drill a card.
- Outside style mate ($160): right when the opponent's style is scouted and unusual; wrong for an unscouted one (money wasted).
- Swap to a back-up line: right only if one was drilled to usable beforehand; the player who never drilled one learns why.
- Keep the read line: right only when it is Signature (the resist) or the reader is weak.
Menus: Playbook > Game plan (a "seen / held / unseen" tag per line, a back-up slot per situation), Fighters > Training (the
sparring night's partner and tested line), the Thursday spar report (a stop, not a pop-up: a flag then a card), Fights > offer card
("they have seen: ..."), Fight prep, the corner minute (the adapted note), the live fight (the opponent panel eye icon), The Why,
Fighter > History ("seen by" marks), World > rival file (what each rival has seen of you).
Reuses: PlanFight/PlanLines, PlanRead FREE_USES, PlanTest (state/PlanTest.gd), SparNights, OppFile, TapeRoom, CornerCard, FightWhy,
RivalPast. NEW: a per-boxer "lines seen" record written by public bouts; the held-line plan filter; the back-up line slot; the
Thursday report; the read-resist by mastery tier. SIZE: medium for the record, report and tags; LARGE for the measured read rule.
Numbers: FREE_USES 2 and "waiting costs 6.5 points" FROM THE GAME (R25 map, measured). Read-resist by tier (Signature keeps
half, Raw none): INVENTED, to be measured on the cover_probe harness first. Win-chance cut-offs 70 and 80: INVENTED, measure the
cost of holding a top line (it earns 64% of round 1's planned edge) per win-chance band. Real anchor for the idea: boxers study
each other's last bouts as a rule (tape study is the reason scouting exists in the game, J-notes on the Tape Room).

### C. THE COACH'S EVENINGS: one pool of hours, claimed by pads, sparring, class and the newcomers. Joins R55 (repaired), R73, R74, R46, R72, R71, R80
R55 as written is one clash (two boxers, one card) and two critics gave it 4. The repair is to make the evenings a pool that EVERY
week spends, with or without a fight, and give each claim a different price.
The pool: Reid's three evenings, the second club coach's three (hands 45, at the limit: single moves and the class, not
combinations: INVENTED, measure), Hale all seven. Each evening is claimed by one of: PADS for a boxer (or half and half), the
SPARRING eye, the CLASS (the $350 a week), the NEWCOMERS' induction (R74), the youth class.
Decisions in order:
1. MONDAY, the lay: drag claims onto evenings. Every drag shows its price before the click as "wins in 100" for the boxer in camp,
   money for the class, members kept for induction, tiredness for the boxer whose night moves (74 to 82 = overreached, x0.6 and
   injury x3, FROM THE GAME). The critics' missing free options are in: SPLIT the night (single moves land the same week on half
   a night, combinations a week late for both), a CLUBMATE holds pads (free, hands 40, costs that senior's own session),
   the BAG fallback (1.05 an hour against 2.1 on pads: a boxer without the coach is slower, not zero).
2. DURING: a boxer hurt gives nights back by himself; the pool re-lays and the Thursday stop shows who gained.
3. FIGHT WEEK: taper has no pads; "ready" means by the START of taper (the coach critic's catch). The docket shows the taper line.
4. AFTER: induction results land Thursday: "4 of 6 stayed". The 90-day list is real members with a first name each.
5. CAREER: members kept feed prospects (a quick left hand gets spotted in induction), prospects become signings, signings claim
   evenings. The loop closes: the quiet week's hours make the next camp's problem.
Case for each option:
- Class on, boxer on the bag: right when cash is short or the card is a single move.
- Class OFF for both boxers whole: right for a short overlap only; it costs members who miss the class (INVENTED: 1 in 10 of
  the class drifts per cancelled evening), so it is no longer free when cash is healthy (the coach critic's dominated case).
- Second coach: right when two boxers overlap most of the year; his price includes a roster place and the limit on what his hands
  can hold; not "always right".
- Hale: right from the third boxer, when evenings, not rating, are the shortage. With two boxers a second coach is cheaper and Hale
  is a bad buy: that is allowed to be true, the screen says so.
- Induction: right in a quiet week or when new joiners are many (the cliff is real); wrong in camp week 3.
Menus: Club > People (the rota, read AND edit: two homes would clash, so People is where hours are claimed; Training shows the
result), Fighters > Training (a Coach row per pad night: who holds it, a ghost face when denied), Fighters > Playbook (the star
and "needs 2 weeks of pads" on an answer not yet usable), Fights > offer card ("overlaps Danny's camp: both need Reid Monday"),
Home (the clash and the Gym-week block), Club > Money (the class line), Fighter > Overview ("feels second" and the promise, tied to
R58), week review and the Why (who held, what the lay cost).
Reuses: PadWeek, GameMoney classes, StaffRoles, Membership, Recruit, the promise in R58. NEW: PadHands (the R55 module), a claim per
evening, induction course and its list of newcomers. SIZE: medium for PadHands (as R55 already scoped) plus medium for the claims
and induction list.
Numbers: $115 an evening = $350 a week over three: FROM THE GAME. Pad 2.1, bag 1.05 an hour: FROM THE GAME. Hands 45, hands 40,
class drift, induction retention: INVENTED (industry talk of early-months attrition exists but the figure is the gym critic's
notes, partly from memory: do not quote as REAL). A pad session is 6 to 12 rounds of 3 minutes: REAL (J62, from the R55 map).

### D. THE ASK AND THE ANSWER: a fight asked on Monday, answered Thursday, with a reason. Joins R37, R39, R33, R47, R40, R52
The ask exists for pros today; amateurs have no way to ask. Make the ask a standing weekly verb, in time with the docket.
Decisions in order:
1. MONDAY: ask for a fight: level (tune-up / step up / long shot), a date window, and a named rival if known. The docket line says
   "answer Thursday". The offer window shows readiness from the docket: "card ready before taper: yes/no".
2. THURSDAY: the answer lands: a yes with a date; a yes later with the reason ("they take Lucy at 5 bouts: 2 to go"); a refusal
   with the reason ("weight: 1.2 kg over for that class"). A refusal is a checklist, not a slap.
3. SPONSOR AND SHOW REQUESTS (R33) arrive the same way: a sponsor wants a fight in 3 weeks; the docket shows what it costs (a card
   not ready, a camp cut short, money in). Take or refuse; both are on the docket.
4. AFTER: the Why and History log the ask and the result; a rematch (R47) is one tap when both camps have the other's file (B).
5. CAREER: refusal reasons accumulate into "what the organisers want next": a plain road with ticks (bouts, weight, rank), the
   same road as R40, now asked from the boxer's side.
Case for each option:
- Ask for a tune-up: right after a loss or an injury (confidence and the stipend); wrong when the club needs a ticket cut and the
  boxer is rested (a step up pays more).
- Ask for a step up: right when the docket is green and the Why said the plan worked.
- Long shot: right once, when a title or a rank is within a win (R44), wrong as a habit (the promoter refuses a duck twice).
- Take the sponsor's fight: right when the club is short of cash and the card is ready; wrong when it overlaps the other boxer's
  taper.
Menus: Fights (the ask under the offers, the answer card Thursday), Home (the ask on the docket), Fight prep, Fighter > Overview
(the road ticks), Club > Money (a sponsor's offer), the Why/History.
Reuses: ProLadder ask_rows, Matchmaking, ChampDraw, offers and dates, WeekTwist's short-notice card. NEW: the amateur ask, the
Thursday reply with reasons, the road ticks. SIZE: medium. The bot test from the S402 note (a smart ask beats a plain one) is still the
gate: if it fails the player can ask but the answer carries no real choice.
Numbers: the pro band (about 5 places above, unranked reach 15), the 3-fan rule for a top-5 name, purse and fee: FROM THE GAME.
Answer day Thursday: INVENTED. The 70-30 split of yes / yes-later / refuse: INVENTED, by readiness and rank.

## 3. KILL LIST (my own strongest objection to each, and my answer)
- A, the docket: "a dashboard is not depth". Answer: right, alone it is a 4. It is a frame: the lines have to be real decisions,
  which B, C and D supply. Without them I would not build it first. Honest.
- A, the Thursday stop: "another stop is a chore". Answer: it stops only when something landed. If a week lands nothing, no stop.
  If the first month shows it stops every week, drop it to the Monday page. No answer if every week has a landing: then it is
  Monday with a delay and should be cut.
- B, hold or show: "showing always wins; hold is dominated". Answer: only if win chance never saturates. At 85% with the line,
  holding may cost 5 points, not 13. That cost has not been measured. If it holds up dominated in the probe, the mechanic loses
  its central option and falls to a 4. I flag this as the first thing to measure.
- B, back-up lines: "the player never drills two answers to one situation, it is a chore". Answer: it is the only way to answer
  a reader and the Thursday report tells him which line is read; a player who does not care keeps the coach's plan (R22). No
  answer to "it is hidden depth he may never meet": the Why must name the back-up that saved a bout.
- B, read-resist by tier: "an invented number to rescue sharp". Answer: yes, invented; the harness decides, not me. If sharp
  still pays 1 point in the probe, I cut the resist and keep seen/unseen only, and B drops to about a 5.
- C, hands 45 for combinations: "invented, and cheap to ignore". Answer: measure first (R55 critics asked for it). If a combination
  lands the same week at 45, the second coach is dominated again and the rota is a lookup. No other answer.
- C, class drift: "a tax with no decision". Answer: the decision is when to pay it (overlap length, cash). Not a decision if cash
  is always healthy; then the drift has to scale with the member count so a small club feels it.
- D, the ask: "the bot says a plain ask is as good". Answer: then the reply carries the reason, and the reason is the choice.
  If every ask gets a yes the mechanic is a button. The gate is the S402 bot test; I would not build before it.
- D, a Thursday reply: "random replies feel like a slot machine". Answer: the reply is a function of readiness and rank shown on
  Monday; there is no dice in the yes or no, only in the date. Anything else I cut.

## 4. THE ORDER TO BUILD (each step already improves the week alone)
1. Probe, no screens: measure (a) the cost of holding a top line by win-chance band, (b) read-resist by mastery tier, (c) hands 45
   on a combination. These three numbers decide whether B and C are real. Also fixes R55's step 1 (sharp pays).
2. The Docket module and the Monday page: collects the dates the game already has. Better week the day it ships; no new rule.
3. The "seen" record per line and the History marks (a record only, no effect yet): the player starts to see what the world saw.
4. The Thursday spar report (needs PlanTest and SparNights, both built) with the rewrite / drill / leave answers.
5. Held lines in the plan + the offer card's "they have seen" line + the adapted note with a real back-up answer (R25 done right).
6. PadHands and the evenings pool with the free options (split, clubmate, bag) and the Coach row on Training.
7. Induction and the Gym-week block (the quiet-week answer), then the amateur ask with the Thursday reply.
Each of 2 to 5 can ship before the rules in 5 to 7 exist; 6 and 7 can ship in either order.

Overall I would not call any of the four a 7 before the probes. B is the one I would stake the weekly loop on.

The single strongest idea: make the world study the player's plan as the player studies the opponent's, so every line is either seen
or unseen and a held line, a back-up and a house line become the choices that lead from the week's sparring through the booking into
the corner minute and the next camp. It also gives pad time a job after usable and gives a read line a way back, the two holes the
four dead reviews and the measurements found.
