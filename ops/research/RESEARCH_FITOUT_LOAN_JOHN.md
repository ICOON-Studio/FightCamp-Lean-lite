# FITOUT_LOAN - real-world check of our gym fit-out loan (S531, John)

You are a researcher. Real-world numbers only, each with its source (link or publication) and year. Plain words.

## What the game does today
A small boxing club in England (amateur, England Boxing affiliated, ~70 members in a small unit, rent $1,800/month,
dues $35/member/month; our dollars = GBP x 1.25) can move to a bigger unit for $12,000 cash, or on a fit-out loan:
$3,000 down, then $750 a month for 12 months (paid by the week, $173), one loan at a time. That is $12,000 total, so the
loan has NO interest today. The bigger unit lets the name draw more members (cap goes from about 70 to about 140).

## Questions
1. What does moving a small UK boxing / combat gym to a bigger unit really cost (deposit, fit-out: mats, ring, bags,
   flooring, showers, legal fees)? Give low / typical / high.
2. How do small gyms really finance it: equipment finance / asset finance, small business loans (e.g. British Business
   Bank Start Up Loans), landlord rent-free periods, community grants (Sport England Small Grants, Lottery), member
   fundraising? Typical deposit %, term, interest rate (APR) for each in 2024-2026.
3. Is "$3,000 down, $750 a month for 12 months, 0% interest" believable? If not, what deposit, term and rate would a real
   lender give a club with about 70 members and two years of trading? Turn it into our dollars.
4. What goes wrong in real life (missed payments, personal guarantees, a club folding under a loan)? One line each, with
   how often if anyone measured it.
5. Any second option a manager would really weigh (a grant that takes months, a rent-free period, a sponsor paying for
   the ring) that would make the choice richer? Name its real terms.

End with a short table: option | deposit | monthly | term | total cost | catch | source.
