1. The 1-Pixel Screen-Space Border Without Linework Hierarchy
The Symptom: In the current screenshot, every border—whether it is the coastline of Norway against the Atlantic Ocean or the internal border between Slovakia and Hungary—is rendered at an identical, unvarying, semi-transparent 1-pixel cream thickness (#CCB899 at 55%).
Why It Reads Amateur: Real-world cartography and professional grand strategy titles never treat coastlines and political borders identically. Coastlines represent massive landform elevation and material changes (rock/earth meeting water); borders are human legal constructs. In the current build, the rasterizer produces aliased crawling sub-pixel noise across Western Europe. It creates a dense, vibrating wireframe cage that makes the land look like a wireframe sphere from a 1990s CGI demo rather than an authentic physical artifact.
2. The Generic "Google Maps" Pushpin Asset
The Symptom: The marker is a generic red ball atop a tan plastic toothpick shoved vertically into the United Kingdom.
Why It Reads Amateur: It imports the modern digital UX language of a mobile delivery app or Google Maps directly into a period-authentic, gritty, brass-and-leather 1970s boxing gym simulation. It looks like an untextured placeholder asset grabbed from a free asset pack in five minutes. Because it is oriented strictly along the surface normal without a shadowed mounting collar, base bevel, or physical presence, it appears to clip arbitrarily through the terrain mesh.
3. Flat Sticker Country Fill Without Shading or Material Depth
The Symptom: The picked country (UK) is rendered as a flat, opaque, pastel yellow-gold shape (#E5B842) overlaid on the surface, completely obliterating any land relief, surface roughness, or spherical curvature underneath.
Why It Reads Amateur: It ignores the spherical normal vector ($\vec{N} \cdot \vec{L}$) of the globe. Instead of looking like a illuminated territory or an embossed brass plate set into the sphere, it looks like a flat 2D sticker or MS Paint fill bucket application. The boundary outline is a blurry 3-pixel white fringe that exhibits visible bilinear interpolation blurring along the jagged raster edges of the equirectangular ID texture.
4. The Disconnected Grey Void ("The Cutoff Letterbox")
The Symptom: The 3D viewport is rendered inside a harsh, clipped rectangular letterbox surrounded by flat #7F7F7F window-clearing grey.
Why It Reads Amateur: In a shipped PC game, UI and 3D scenes are composited as a unified visual canvas. Here, the viewport looks like an embedded developer debug sub-window (SubViewportContainer) that was never skinned with decorative framing, cartographic background elements, or vignette post-processing.
5. Flat Incoherent Lighting and Rim Bleed
The Symptom: A weak, omni-directional orange rim light bleeds indiscriminately along the silhouette edge of the sphere, glowing brightest where there is nothing to illuminate (the empty Atlantic Ocean and the Arctic).
Why It Reads Amateur: Shipped 3D globe interfaces use a theatrical key-light that focuses visual drama on the player's active point of interaction (the selected market). In the current build, the sphere looks like a matte rubber ball under an uncalibrated default Godot DirectionalLight3D.
6. Typographic Disconnect on the Right-Hand Data Panel
The Symptom: The right-hand panel text "UNITED KINGDOM" floats ambiguously in empty space, set in a lightweight sans-serif font that lacks contrast, backing panels, or brass framing. Below it, "CHOOSE MARKET" is a bright yellow rounded rectangle with no chamfers, no metallic sheen, and no visual connection to Maria’s FightCamp aesthetic.
Why It Reads Amateur: The UI does not look designed; it looks placed. Shipped games use established anchor plates, textured cards, and consistent typographic hierarchies (e.g., DIN Condensed, Oswald, or League Gothic) with drop shadows and metallic fasteners.

1. The Globe Mesh & Spatial Shader Parameters
A. Base Sphere Geometry
Mesh Primitive: SphereMesh (Godot 4.7 built-in, or imported Quad-Sphere for uniform pole UV density).
Radius: $1.0\text{ m}$ in world space.
Tessellation: Radial Segments: 128, Rings: 64 (budget: $16,384$ triangles; zero silhouette facetting at 1080p/4K).
Transform: Position $(X: -0.22, Y: 0.0, Z: 0.0)$ in viewport space (shifted left by $22%$ of screen width to perfectly center the globe between the left screen edge and the $480\text{ px}$ right data panel).
B. Ocean Surface Material Spec
Visual Style: Deep, hand-rubbed black Japanese lacquer with deep oceanic bathymetric depth.
Base Color (Deep Trenches): Obsidian Charcoal #121417 ($R: 0.071, G: 0.078, B: 0.090$).
Shallow Water Color (Continental Shelves): Deep Indigo Slate #1b222c ($R: 0.106, G: 0.133, B: 0.173$).
Bathymetric Gradient Formula: Linear blend based on coastline distance field texture: $$\text{OceanColor} = \text{mix}(\text{DeepColor}, \text{ShallowColor}, \text{clamp}(1.0 - \text{DistanceToCoast} \times 4.0, 0.0, 1.0))$$
PBR Properties:
Roughness: 0.18 (High-sheen polished lacquer; produces a crisp, tight specular highlight from the key light).
Metallic: 0.10 (Subtle dielectric mineral depth).
Specular: 0.65.
C. Land Surface (Unselected Continents)
Visual Style: Warm vulcanized graphite-matte finish with subtle topographical relief and combat popularity tinting.
Base Land Color (Neutral): Dark Warm Graphite #242220 ($R: 0.141, G: 0.133, B: 0.125$).
PBR Properties:
Roughness: 0.78 (Ultra-matte chalk/leather feel; creates sharp material contrast against the glossy lacquer ocean).
Metallic: 0.00.
Combat Popularity Tinting ($0 - 100%$ Data Drive):
Rather than coloring the whole globe like a rainbow, use a subtle, sophisticated heat underlay:
Low Combat Popularity ($0 - 30$): Neutral Graphite #242220 (No tint).
Medium Combat Popularity ($31 - 70$): Subtle infusion of Oiled Saddle Leather Brown (#3a281e, $+15%$ warmth).
High Combat Popularity ($71 - 100$, e.g., UK, USA, Mexico, Japan): Infusion of Deep Burnished Bronze (#4d3319, $+25%$ warmth).
This subtle tint communicates global boxing density organically without looking like an arcade heatmap.

Complete GLSL Shader Code Snippet (Godot 4.7 Visual/Spatial Shader):
// --- COUNTRY SELECTION HIGHLIGHT PASS ---
uniform vec4 u_gold_rim_color : source_color = vec4(0.941, 0.761, 0.200, 1.0); // #f0c233
uniform vec4 u_velvet_oxblood : source_color = vec4(0.290, 0.078, 0.071, 0.45); // Deep Oxblood
uniform float u_selection_pulse_speed = 2.618; // 2.4 sec period (2*PI / 2.4)

// Check if current fragment matches active country ID
if (is_selected_country) {
    // Calculate normalized distance from fragment to country border (0.0 = border, 1.0 = deep interior)
    float border_dist = get_normalized_border_distance(uv); 
    
    // Rhythmic breathing timing (sine pulse)
    float pulse = 0.5 + 0.5 * sin(TIME * u_selection_pulse_speed);
    
    // Dual border linework
    if (border_dist < border_width_outer) {
        // 4px Brass Outer Rim with active emission
        ALBEDO = u_gold_rim_color.rgb;
        ROUGHNESS = 0.20;
        METALLIC = 0.95;
        EMISSION = u_gold_rim_color.rgb * mix(0.50, 0.85, pulse);
    } else if (border_dist < border_width_inner) {
        // 1.5px Inset Oxblood Registration Pinstripe
        ALBEDO = vec3(0.776, 0.184, 0.165); // #c62f2a
        ROUGHNESS = 0.40;
        METALLIC = 0.0;
        EMISSION = vec3(0.776, 0.184, 0.165) * 0.3;
    } else {
        // Interior Velvet Gradient (Translucent gold fading into deep oxblood)
        vec3 interior_color = mix(u_gold_rim_color.rgb * 0.7, u_velvet_oxblood.rgb, smoothstep(0.05, 0.6, border_dist));
        float interior_alpha = mix(0.40, 0.60, pulse);
        
        ALBEDO = mix(base_land_color, interior_color, interior_alpha);
        ROUGHNESS = 0.60;
        METALLIC = 0.15;
        EMISSION = interior_color * (0.15 * pulse);
    }
}

D. Edge-of-Globe Horizon Culling & Flipping Logic

To prevent the marker from looking glitchy or clipping through the sphere silhouette when the selected country rotates toward the horizon:

Normal Dot-Product Fade ($\vec{N} \cdot \vec{V}$):
Compute the dot product between the country surface normal and the camera view vector: $$\alpha_{\text{vis}} = \text{clamp}\left(\frac{\vec{N} \cdot \vec{V} - 0.15}{0.30 - 0.15}, 0.0, 1.0\right)$$
When $\vec{N} \cdot \vec{V} < 0.15$, the marker and name tag fade to $0%$ opacity smoothly.
Screen-Edge Auto-Flip:
If the marker’s screen $X$-position exceeds $X = 1380\text{ px}$ (approaching the right UI panel boundary), the Name Tag automatically mirrors its anchor to the left flank of the medallion ($X\text{-offset} = -(\text{Width} + 12\text{ px})$).
