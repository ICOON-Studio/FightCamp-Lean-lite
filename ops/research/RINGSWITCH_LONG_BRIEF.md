# Ideas wanted: LONG RANGE is a corridor - what should a man do out there, in a game of switches?

You are a game designer. We want your own ideas, not a review. Nothing below is a proposal to judge: it is the game as
it stands and the problem.

## The game (a small standalone prototype: Ring Switches, boxing, one man against one man)
- A bout is 3 rounds of 6 BEATS. In a beat both men set their switches at the same time, in secret, then the beat plays.
- Each HAND does one thing a beat: throw one punch, or stay home held HIGH or LOW. A hand at home blocks one line: lead
  hand high stops straight head punches, lead hand low stops body punches, rear hand high stops hooks, rear hand low
  stops uppercuts. A hand that punches is out of its guard only in the moment it throws.
- The FEET do one thing a beat: step in (1 stamina, one place closer), hold, or back off (1 stamina, one place further;
  you give ground, two and you are on the ropes; your next punch that beat is a pull counter, +1 if it lands).
- Three ranges: Long, Mid, Inside. EVERY ROUND STARTS AT LONG.
- A beat is three MOMENTS. The order you click your switches is the order you do them; your first action meets his first.
  When both strike in one moment the faster lands first, and a big one (3 health or more) that lands stops his slower one.
- Health 18, stamina 8 (+1 a beat, +1 for each hand held). The judges score each round 10-9 (10-8 when well ahead).

## The punches and where they reach (the page's own table)
| punch | hand | stamina | damage | speed | reaches |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| jab | lead | 1 | 1 | fast | Mid, Long |
| body jab | lead | 1 | 1 | fast | Mid, Long |
| lead hook | lead | 2 | 3 | fast | Inside, Mid |
| body hook | lead | 2 | 2 | medium | Inside, Mid |
| lead uppercut | lead | 2 | 3 | medium | Inside |
| cross | rear | 2 | 3 | medium | Inside, Mid |
| body cross | rear | 2 | 2 | medium | Inside, Mid |
| overhand | rear | 3 | 4 | slow | Inside, Mid |
| rear uppercut | rear | 3 | 4 | slow | Inside |
Also: a feint (1 stamina: his hand reacts, your next punch cannot be covered) and a push off (Inside).

## The problem (five playtesters, each in their own words)
At Long only the two jabs reach, for 1 damage each. So Long is a corridor you walk through: every round opens there, the
rear hand has nothing to do, and the only real choice is "step in now or poke first". All five testers named it; one
called it "a corridor where only 1-damage pokes reach". We do NOT want Long removed, and we do not want a long-range
bomb that ends rounds there. We want Long to be a place where a fight can be WON on points by a man who is better at
distance - the real outfighter - and lost by a man who walks in carelessly.

## What we ask you for
THREE different designs. For each:
1. THE RULE in at most five plain sentences (what changes, what the player sets, what it costs).
2. ONE BEAT walked through at Long: both men's switches, what happens moment by moment.
3. THE DECISION it gives the player at Long: what is he weighing, and when is each choice right?
4. WHO IT HELPS: the outfighter, the pressure fighter, or both? Should a pressure fighter still be able to get Inside?
5. HOW IT CAN BREAK: the lazy plan that would win too often, and the cap you would put on it.
6. THE REAL BOXING it stands for (one line: what real outfighters do at long range that the game misses).
Then: which ONE you would build first and why, and any published game (video, card or board) that handles range or
footwork in a way worth studying.

## Prior research
Our notes: in real boxing the outfighter wins at range with the jab, angles and the step-back counter; the pressure
fighter cuts the ring and pays to close distance. No design of ours answers this yet.

## What would a human do?
A designer gives each range its own job, makes crossing a range a cost or a risk the other man can punish, and checks
that each range is used in real numbers of beats.
