1. Would you move the sliders by feel, or does the coach's word + band give you enough to reason with? What is missing?
I’d work from the coach’s read and narrow bands rather than guessing blind—they anchor where to aim—but I’d never be fully sure. What’s missing is which sliders hurt which other reads: I know shifting range might lift Offence but tank Defence, but I don’t see the tradeoff ratios or which pairs conflict worst. Without seeing those links, every final tweak still feels like a calculated gamble, not a clean decision.
2. After the fight, what would you need to see to believe the plan MATTERED (not a hidden dice roll)?
Round-by-round breakdowns showing where the plan paid off: times my fighter landed the targeted shots, avoided the opponent’s strengths, or forced them into bad positions—directly matching what I’d set. If I see “outside range scored 8/12” or “defended 7/9 takedowns when keeping it standing” alongside the score difference, it’s real. If all I get is “win/loss + total points” with no link to my choices, it just looks like dice.
3. The corner read: does "helps next round only, costs 20 of the chief's seconds" feel like a real choice, or a button you always press? What would make you hesitate?
It feels like a real choice—not an auto-press—because the 20-second cost is genuine. I’d hesitate hard if the cutman work is urgent (bad swelling/cut) or my fighter’s gassed and needs clear instructions; trading “survive this round” for one round of intel is a terrible deal when you’re on the back foot. It only feels worth it if we’re close and I can see the opening.
4. What feels like cheating or like the game lying to you in this system?
If the bands narrow but the true answer shifts underneath without warning—like I locked in what looked right from film study, only for it to say “actually wrong” with zero new info or opponent change. Also if a “well-read plan” still loses 8/10 times with no visible reason: when the stated 15–55 point edge vanishes and there’s no matchup or style explanation behind it, it stops feeling like skill and starts feeling the game rigging the result.
5. The ONE thing to get right.
Make the feedback specific and visible. Every choice—slider or corner read—needs to show exactly how it shifted things: “pushing pace to pressure raised Offence 7 points but dropped Defence 4” or “that tell let you countered twice this round”. Trust dies when you can’t see the cause; it sticks when every effect is traceable.
