# Damage and recovery design: where it stands after six rounds (S438)

Your ask (S437): deepen the damage and hit system, and give the player options to act when a place is hurt.

The bar to reach your board is 7 from both. None reached it.

## Why round 3 went DOWN
Round 3 measured each choice honestly: how much does a player lose by following one simple rule instead of thinking?
- After the fight: almost nothing. "Train, spare or rest this week" is read off one number; ignoring it costs under
  one training day a case. The map cut its own care card. What is left is consequences, not choices.
- The fight: the corner minute is better (range call, liver or left ribs, their corner covering), but a player who
  learns "aim at the head from your best range, guard what the tape says" loses very little.
Earlier rounds scored higher because their checks could not see this.

## What both critics point to instead (the same three things, from both halves)
1. WHEN TO BOX AGAIN is the real choice after a fight: the same opponent on two dates (7 days or 21), each showing
   the chance to win, the place's state at the bell, and what passing costs with the matchmaker.
2. A carried sore place must cost the NEXT FIGHT (a sore rib tires faster when hit there; a sore eye is easier to
   find), not only days of training.
3. Tie it to the fight plan you already have: each plan line is priced against the sore place.

## The choice
- TRY ONCE MORE, ANOTHER WAY: three small designs written from scratch around those three points, the best one
  scored. About the cost of one round so far.
- STOP NEW DECISIONS: keep the ribs fix, and spend the effort on SHOWING damage (the face, the body picture, the
  result page), which you also asked about.
- PARK IT and go on with the Fighters page, the training scene and the clip fixes.
Claude's recommendation: try once more, another way. Six rounds of rewriting the same shape did not converge; the
critics agree on where the real choice is.
