- **judo_taisabaki_defense** — **REPLACE** with **Hip-block / cut-away** (elite throw-stopper). Tai-sabaki is a movement principle, not a named defence waza.
- **judo_kumi_defense** — **RENAME** to **Kumi-kata** (組手 / grip fighting), and split grip-break as a drill family — not one technique.
- **sambo_esc_bridge** — **KEEP**, display **Уход от удержания через мост** (bridge escape from hold-down).
- **sambo_esc_frame_roll** — **RENAME** to **Уход выворачиваясь** (turn-out / frame-and-roll from hold-down). “Frame and roll” is BJJ English.
- **sambo_esc_leglock** — **REPLACE** with **Защита от ущемления ахилла** and **Защита от рычага колена** (Achilles-lock and kneebar defences). Not one named entry-escape.
- **sambo_esc_shrimp** — **RENAME** to **Уход тазом / уход на бок** (hip escape). “Shrimp/ebi” is BJJ/judo Japanese, not FIAS Russian.
- **sambo_throw_defense** — **REPLACE** with **Защита от бросков** split: hip block, step-out/нырок, grip break — distinct from **ответные броски** (return throws).
- **sambo_grip_break_defense** — **RENAME** to **Срыв захвата** (KEEP as a real named action).
- **Q6.** Ukemi **protects the faller only**; it does not stop a throw scoring. Under IJF 2025–28, **landing orientation changes the score** (ippon / waza-ari / yuko / no score). Voluntary **bridge landing = ippon**. Head defence as uke: **allowed** seniors/juniors; **shido** (plus any score) for cadets. Diving = hansoku-make. Bridging during osaekomi does **not** by itself end the hold.
- **Q7.** What stops a throw **before** it lands: **grip break, hip-block/jam, posture/jigotai, taisabaki/step-around**. **Kaeshi-waza** is a counter-attack, not a pure defence (IJF: counter-attacker cannot use landing impact). Elite sources name hip-block/cut-away as the primary kill, then counter.
- **Q8.** FIAS **Sport SAMBO**: hold-down **20 s = 4 points**; **10–19 s = 2 points**. Escape (count stops): defender to chest/stomach/buttocks with scapular angle **>90°**, push attacker off, attacker switches to a painful hold, or off-mat. Combat SAMBO often **one scored hold-down per bout**.

# PART A — frequencies (MMA / kickboxing / Muay Thai)

## Q1. Jab share of all punches THROWN in pro MMA

### Decision

**NOT FOUND.** There is no published UFC/FightMetric/academic figure for the boxing jab as a share of punches **THROWN** in professional MMA. The 35% figure is amateur boxing **THROWN**, not MMA.

### What the public MMA databases actually record

**DOCUMENTED.** UFCStats / FightMetric do **not** classify standing punches as jab / cross / hook / uppercut / overhand on the public fighter or fight pages. They record, for **significant strikes**:

- result: landed vs attempted (THROWN);
- target: head / body / leg (landed **and** attempted appear on fight-detail tables as “X of Y”);
- position: distance / clinch / ground.

A Kaggle UFCStats scrape (jossilva3110, 1994–2026) states explicitly: **“Strike Targets & Positions (Only Landed strikes are tracked here)”** at fighter-average level; bout files carry `_Sig_Landed` / `_Sig_Att` but **no punch-type field**.

**DOCUMENTED.** Bloody Elbow “alternative stats” (FightMetric-derived, 2017–2020 fighter previews) define: *“A jab is just a non-power strike. Strikes are documented based on where they land or are targeted (head, body, legs), not the type that is thrown (punch, elbow, kick, knee).”*  
So a FightMetric “head jab” can be a light kick, a teep, or a boxing jab. **It is not the boxing jab.** Using those columns as “jab share of punches” would be a category error.

**DOCUMENTED.** Rami Genow, FightMetric, MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference 2012: FightMetric *does* internally distinguish jab vs power (lead-hand straight = jab; rear-leg head kick = power). That distinction was used in a victory-correlation model. **No slide or paper from that talk publishes a league-wide jab share of punches THROWN.**

**DOCUMENTED.** Miarka, Coswig, Amtmann et al., *Ido Movement for Culture* 20(3):9–17 (2020), “Ending an MMA combat bout…”, 304 UFC rounds 2012–2014, FightMetric-style coding. Standing **distance** medians (THROWN, per round, by outcome) include **Head Jabs Attempted** vs **Head Power Attempted**, e.g. Unanimous Decision: Head Jabs Attempted 7.0 (4.0; 14.0), Head Power Attempted 13.0 (8.0; 21.5). These are **not** convertible into a jab share of *all punches thrown* (clinches, ground, body, legs omitted; medians must not be added; “jab” ≠ boxing jab).

**NOT FOUND.** UFC Performance Institute *A Cross-Sectional Performance Analysis and Projection of the UFC Athlete* (2018; media.ufc.tv/ufcpi/UFCPI_Book_2018.pdf — URL failed to fetch on 2026-09-13; secondary reports: Forbes, MMA Fighting, FightBook). Published takeaways: average strikes **attempted** ~8.5/min; women’s SW/BW highest frequency. **No jab share.**

**Do not transfer 35% or 53.8% into MMA.** MMA distance includes kicks, takedown entries, and a FightMetric “jab” that is not a punch type.

**DOCUMENTED — finish-method evolution, LANDED.** Nogueira de Rezende Silva et al. with Miarka, “Twenty Years in the Octagon…” (2026 preprint/article): punches as share of striking finishes 77.4% (2003–04) → **86.1%** (2023–24); kicks 20.5% → **11.3%**. Still finishes, not thrown.

## Q3. Kicks by target in MMA/UFC: of kicks THROWN, share leg / body / head

### Decision

**NOT FOUND** for the quantity asked: **of kicks THROWN**, % leg / body / head.

Your existing **14–18% of LANDED significant strikes are to the legs** is a different statistic (all weapons, landed only). Do not treat it as kick-target mix.

### What *is* documented (all-strike target, usually LANDED)

**DOCUMENTED.** UFCStats fight-detail pages record Head / Body / Leg as **landed of attempted** for **all significant strikes** (punches + kicks + knees + elbows). Example schema: “17 of 18” leg = 17 LANDED of 18 THROWN **leg-targeted significant strikes**, not “kicks”. League-wide kick-only rollups are not published by UFC.

**DOCUMENTED (secondary, all significant strikes LANDED).** Dolex “The Rise of the Leg Kick” (UFC-data demo, 2026-03-04): head **64%**, body **20%**, leg **16%** of significant strikes **LANDED**; ~1 in 6 landed significant strikes target the legs; leg strikes per fight ~7 (2010) → ~14 (2021). This matches the order of magnitude of your 14–18% LANDED-legs figure.

**DOCUMENTED (secondary, mixed thrown/landed wording).** Undated FightMetric-derived PDF circulated as `eamm.mex.tl/.../jYrvOJmz.pdf` (retrieved 2026-09-13): “Currently, there’s an **80-10-10** percentage mix of head-body-leg strikes”; “**10% volume of strikes thrown at the legs**”; body share 6.1% (2002–07) → 9.2% (since 2008). The 10% legs line is explicitly **THROWN, all strikes**, not kicks. Treat as FightMetric-era commentary, not a peer-reviewed table.

**DOCUMENTED.** Miarka 2020 (same paper as Q1) reports **Leg Jabs Attempted** and **Leg Power Attempted** at distance (medians near 0–1 per round) alongside head and body. That is all-strike targeting, not a kick taxonomy, and medians cannot be turned into a kick-target share.

**NOT FOUND.** No UFCStats, FightMetric, UFC PI, or PubMed paper gives **kicks THROWN** split into low / body / head. Searches listed in the method log.

**INFERENCE (do not use as a number):** because head strikes dominate all-strike mixes (~64–80% LANDED), and many of those are punches, the **kick** mix is almost certainly more leg-heavy than the all-strike mix — but **no published kick-only thrown share exists**, so no figure is offered.

## Q4. Kickboxing / Muay Thai — CompuBox-style technique frequencies

### Decision

**No official Glory, ONE, K-1, Lumpinee, or Rajadamnern CompuBox** publishing jab / cross / hook / uppercut / teep / low-body-head kick / knee / elbow / clinch-knee as thrown and landed shares. Sports-science **notational** studies exist; they count **THROWN** (performed) techniques. Several also give landed/accuracy. Use those. Do not fabricate a unified “CompuBox KB” table.

### 4.1 Glory / K-1 professional (punches vs kicks, THROWN and LANDED)

**DOCUMENTED.** Zav’yalov, Grishin, Kolyada, *Theory and Practice of Physical Culture* (2014), “Quantitative and qualitative assessment of hitting actions in modern professional kickboxing ‘K-1’”. Video of **Glory World Series and Glory Superfights Tokyo**, 2012–2014, **>100 fights, 214 athletes**, custom “Electronic judge K-1”. Three-round (~6 min) fights.

From Tables 3–4 (per fighter, per fight):

| | Winners | Losers | Tag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Total strikes **LANDED** | **89.70** | 59.65 | LANDED |
| Total attacks **THROWN** | **174.75** | 167.40 | THROWN |
| Punches **LANDED** | **61.80** | 34.55 | LANDED |
| Kicks **LANDED** | 33.60 | 27.70 | LANDED |
| Punch accuracy | **46.67%** | 36.26% | LANDED/THROWN |
| Kick accuracy | **65.56%** | 53.77% | LANDED/THROWN |

Also **DOCUMENTED** in that paper:

- Attempted **punches exceed attempted kicks more than twice** (p<0.001). Boxing technique “dominates” K-1 at world level.
- Punches that **LANDED** exceed kicks that **LANDED** by ~1.3× (p<0.01).
- Kick **accuracy** exceeds punch accuracy by ~1.5× (p<0.001).
- Winner and loser each throw ~**170** attacks per fight ≈ **57 per round** (THROWN). Kick **LANDED** counts did **not** differ significantly winners vs losers.

**NOT FOUND** in that paper: jab vs cross vs hook vs teep vs low/body/head kick.

**DOCUMENTED.** Berbatovci et al. / Glory middle-heavy 2019, PubMed 39191384 (2024): 17 Glory matches, effort:pause ~**1.8:1**; winners more attacks, more 3-hit sequences, more **effective** (LANDED) attacks. No named-technique table.

**NOT FOUND.** Glory or ONE official CompuBox-style punch/kick-type dashboard.

### 4.4 Elbows, clinch knees, teep — ruleset note

## Q5. Is the distribution ever flat, or always dominated by basics?

### Decision

**Always dominated by basics.** No located competition sample in boxing, kickboxing, Muay Thai, or MMA finish data is close to uniform across the named arsenal.

**DOCUMENTED** examples (THROWN unless noted):

- Amateur boxing: jab 35% of punches THROWN; uppercuts 5+8 of 183 (Thomson 2016). Straights + lead hook dwarf uppercuts.
- Pro HW boxing: 53.8% of punches THROWN are jabs (Finlay 2022).
- Low-Kick elite: jab-cross 57 vs uppercut 7 vs axe/side 1 (Ouergui 2017, THROWN).
- Amateur/regional KB: straight ~50% or hook+low-kick ~28% each; uraken/spinning <1% (2014 analysis; Balkan 2021).
- K-1 observed: left straight 50% of punch quantity; right low kick 34% of kick quantity (2024 Balt J).
- Glory K-1: punches THROWN >2× kicks; “boxing technique dominates” (Zav’yalov 2014).
- Muay Thai: kicks > punches > knees; within punches, straight 18.7 vs uppercut 0.4 (Myers 2013, THROWN).
- MMA **LANDED** finishes: three punches (rear straight, two hooks) = 80% of punch KO/TKOs (Barley 2024); spinning backfist 0.4% of KO/TKO methods (UFCStats compilation).

**NOT FOUND:** any elite ruleset in which jab/cross/hook/uppercut/overhand/spin/elbow/knee/teep are within a few percentage points of each other.

**INFERENCE.** Energy cost, telegraph, and scoring rules all push volume onto the cheapest accurate weapons (jab/straight, low kick, roundhouse). Flat distributions would contradict every notational paper located.

### 6. `judo_kumi_defense` | Kumi-kata Defence | judo | defence | throw set-ups

**(a)** **Kumi-kata** (組手 / 組み方) is **real**: gripping method. **(b)** Grip fighting can **prevent throw set-ups** (stops **kuzushi/entry**, i.e. before landing). Breaking a sleeve (hikite) or lapel (tsurite) is a **grip break**. It does not protect the faller.

**(c)** **Beginner** (first lessons: right-hand lapel, left-hand sleeve) through **elite** (kenka-yotsu, cross-grip, belt, 30 s conventional-grip attack clock in 2025 cycle).

### 7. `sambo_esc_bridge` | Bridge Escape from Pin | sambo | escape | hold-downs

FIAS rules define the **bridge** as equivalent to **on the back** for **throw scoring** (landing in a bridge scores as a back fall) — different from using a bridge to **escape a hold-down**.

**(c)** **Beginner–intermediate.** Bridge is a wrestling/sambo fundamental; hold-down escapes are core parterre.

**(d)** **KEEP.** Display Russian **Уход от удержания через мост**. Do not conflate with judo throw-landing “bridging = ippon”.

# Q6. Ukemi, landing, score, penalised escape landings

### Does ukemi stop a throw landing?

**DOCUMENTED: no. Ukemi only protects the faller.**

Kawamura & Daigo / IJF-Kodokan classification: **Ukemi** = “General term for breakfalls designed to protect the body when thrown.” Kodokan list: mae, ushiro, yoko, zempo-kaiten. It is taught **before** nage-waza so uke can survive being thrown. A correct breakfall **does not** cancel tori’s technique. IJF SOR Article 14 (2024 PDF, pp. 144–145): a score requires a **recognised Kodokan technique (or variation)**, continuity, and a qualifying landing. “Just landing and rolling over and falling on the side/back in the process of the contest, **without applying a clear technique, is not enough to score**” — that sentence limits **tori**, it does not give uke a veto via ukemi.

**INFERENCE.** If uke slaps the mat in yoko-ukemi after a completed osoto-gari onto the back, the throw has still **landed** and is still scored. Ukemi changes **injury risk**, not **whether the throw happened**.

### Under current IJF, can landing (side vs back, rolling) change the SCORE of a throw that already happened?

**DOCUMENTED: yes. The landing *is* the score.**

Two stacked rule-sets must be distinguished. **Today is 2026-09-13**, so the **2025–2028 Olympic-cycle rules** (in force from Paris GS 2025; IJF.org “New Rules: What to Remember”, 17 Dec 2024, Lascau) are current. The **12 March 2024 SOR PDF** is the last full English book fetched here and is cited where unchanged.

**Ippon (unchanged 2024 → 2025)** — IJF.org 2024-12-17; 2024 SOR Article 14:

- Speed, force, **on the back**, control until the end of landing.
- Shoulders-to-hips line; shoulders ≥90° to tatami; shoulder + upper back can score.
- **Rolling = ippon only if there is no break** during landing. “It is Ippon! when uke rolls on his back.”
- A recognised Kodokan technique or variation must be identified.

**Waza-ari**

- **2024 SOR Article 15:** landing on the **whole side at 90° or more to the rear of the shoulder axis**; one shoulder + upper back; simultaneous two elbows / two hands / elbow+hand; four ippon criteria not fully achieved. Landings **not** waza-ari include some front-side / elbow-only pictures in the book.
- **2025–28 (IJF.org):** waza-ari covers **less than ippon but more than landing on the side to the rear**. Yuko takes the shallower side.

**Yuko — reintroduced 2025** (IJF.org 2024-12-17; German/Polish federation summaries of Istanbul seminar):

- Tachi-waza yuko: landing on the **side or close to the side towards the front**, **buttocks**, **upper back**, **side of the elbow**.
- Ne-waza yuko: osaekomi **5–9 seconds**.
- 2024 SOR had **no yuko** (only waza-ari / ippon).

**INFERENCE.** The same physical throw can be ippon, waza-ari, yuko, or no score depending on **which part of uke hits and whether the roll is continuous**. Landing is not a post-hoc modifier of a score already given; it **is** the criterion.

### Which escape landings are penalised (head defence, bridging)?

**Bridging after a throw — always ippon, not a successful escape.**

**DOCUMENTED.** 2024 SOR Article 14.2: “All situations in which one of the athletes **voluntarily** makes a ‘bridge’ (head and one foot or both feet in contact with the tatami) after having been thrown will be considered **ippon**.” Safety rationale: so athletes do not escape by endangering the cervical spine. “An attempt of a voluntarily bridge (arching the body) should be counted as a ‘bridge’.” Unchanged in 2025 cycle: “**landing in a bridge is still ippon**” (IJF.org; USA Judo Istanbul notes; German NJV 2025 sheet).

**Head defence as uke**

- **2017–2024 (2018 explanatory guide; 2024 SOR Article 18 family):** voluntary use of the head to avoid a score → **hansoku-make** in many senior interpretations (with pictures of uke diving the head into the mat).
- **2025–28 current (IJF.org 2024-12-17):**  
  - **Seniors and juniors: head defence as uke is ALLOWED.**  
  - **Cadets: NOT allowed** — uke **shido**, and **scores for tori are awarded if any**.  
  - **Diving head-first onto the mat remains hansoku-make.**  
  - Tori using the head to throw: allowed seniors/juniors; cadets shido.

**Bridging during osaekomi**

**DOCUMENTED.** 2024 SOR Article 17: referee **must not** call Toketa merely because uke’s back leaves the tatami in a bridge **if tori maintains the initial control**. Bridging in the pin is **not** a scored ippon for the throw (the throw already happened) and **not** automatically toketa.

**Kaeshi-waza / rolling after a failed throw**

**DOCUMENTED.** 2024 SOR, CARE notes under Article 13: the counter-attacker **cannot use the impact of landing** to finish kaeshi-waza. If both land without clear control, **no score**. Named counters (uchi-mata-gaeshi, harai-goshi-gaeshi, uchi-mata-sukashi, ura-nage, yoko-guruma, tani-otoshi, kosoto-gari/gake) **can** score if a proper **≥90°** landing is identified; a front landing or <90° plus a roll to the back is **transition to ne-waza**, not a tachi-waza score.

# Q7. What actually stops a judo throw before it lands?

Ukemi does not. Once uke is airborne onto a scorable surface, the throw has landed (Q6). Pre-landing defences:

### 1. Grip break / deny kumi-kata — **defence**

**DOCUMENTED.** Without a usable hikite/tsurite, most tachi-waza cannot start. IJF SOR penalises **passive** grip breaking without attack (shido / false attack definitions, 2024 Article 18.1 family; 2025: 30 s after classic grip to prepare an attack; non-conventional grips must be immediately active). So elite “defence” that is only stalling is illegal; elite “defence” that **breaks the grip and immediately attacks** is legal.

### 2. Hip-block / cut-away / jam — **defence** (primary elite kill)

**DOCUMENTED as coaching content, not a Kodokan waza name.** US international-level instruction (e.g. “Aggressive Defense Hip Block & Cut Away”, 2024 clinic transcript): *“this is probably the primary defensive movement in really elite judo”* — do not let the opponent’s hip inside yours; jam from armpit down the ribs; bend the arm, rip the gi tight, keep the pulling hand, kill the throw **then** look for a counter. Object: “completely shut them down”.  
This **stops landing** by preventing koshi-waza/seoi loading. It is distinct from kaeshi-waza.

**NOT FOUND:** a Kodokan official technique named “hip block”. The action is real; the name is gym English / “koshi o tomeru”.

### 3. Posture / jigotai / don’t give kuzushi — **defence**

**DOCUMENTED.** Jigotai (自護体) is the classical defensive stance. Kano/Kudo: natural posture that can respond. If uke’s weight is not broken (happo-no-kuzushi fails), the throw does not start. IJF: excessively defensive posture after kumi-kata without searching for an attack is **shido** (2024 18.1.1). So a static crouch is penalised; a **mobile** defensive posture that still threatens is the elite version.

### 4. Tai-sabaki / step-around / off the line — **defence** (movement)

**DOCUMENTED.** Tai-sabaki definition includes “reacting to your opponent’s techniques” (Q5 row 5). Stepping off the throwing line (especially vs seoi / koshi) prevents the load. Same as sambo step-out / нырок family.

### 5. Kaeshi-waza — **counter-attack, not pure defence**

**DOCUMENTED.** Kodokan kaeshi-waza (osoto-gaeshi, uchi-mata-gaeshi, uchi-mata-sukashi, harai-goshi-gaeshi, hane-goshi-gaeshi, tsubame-gaeshi, etc.). IJF 2024 CARE: attack vs counter-attack — the **first** to land on side/back in sutemi **cannot score** unless they take **clear tachi-waza control** and finish; the counter-attacker **cannot use landing impact**. Correctly applied uchi-mata-gaeshi etc. **can** score if ≥90° landing.

**INFERENCE.** Defence = throw does not land (or lands unscoreably). Counter = defender becomes tori and **scores**. Hip-block is defence; uchi-mata-sukashi is a counter that also nullifies the original throw.

### 6. Leg grab to defend a throw — **often illegal**

**DOCUMENTED.** 2024 SOR Article 10 pictures: kneeling uke **cannot grab the legs with hands/arms to defend the throw** → **shido**. 2025 cycle: gripping under the belt allowed **to the top of the inner thigh**, but **hooking/touching the legs** still **shido**. So “reach down and stop the sweep with your hands” is not a legal elite defence.

**NOT FOUND.** A peer-reviewed frequency table of which defences succeed at World Championships (hip-block vs step-around vs grip-break vs kaeshi). Coaching consensus is not a count.

# Q8. FIAS Sport SAMBO hold-down duration and escape rules

### Scoring time

**DOCUMENTED — Sport SAMBO (standard FIAS International SAMBO Rules, English texts 2015 eurosambo PDF; All-American Sambo Sport PDF; NZ SAMBO Federation summary “correct as of 25 May 2025”; Grappler HQ 2026 citing FIAS Sport book):**

| Hold-down duration | Score |
| --- | --- |
| **20 seconds** | **4 points** |
| **10–19 seconds** (incomplete) | **2 points** |
| Under 10 seconds | No hold-down points |

Definition (**DOCUMENTED**): a hold-down is a technique by which the attacker forces the opponent to lie **on the back** for a specified time and presses **torso to torso** (or to the opponent’s arms held to the torso). Torso = body excluding head, neck, limbs. Countdown starts when torso pressure is on and the defender is on the **shoulder blades**.

**Total victory** can also occur if the held athlete **gives up** (tap / verbal / requests interruption without attacker’s fault).

**8-point lead** ends the bout (technical superiority). A 4-point hold-down is a large chunk of that.

### Escape / when the count stops (the “escape rule”)

**DOCUMENTED.** The hold-down **ends** (count stops, “not counted” / “escape”) if:

1. The defender moves to **chest, stomach, or buttocks** (but **not** the lower back) such that the angle between the back at the **shoulder blades** and the mat is **more than 90°**;
2. The defender **pushes the attacker’s torso off**;
3. The **attacker switches to a painful hold** (hold-down count stops; painful-hold clock starts — **1 minute** allotted);
4. Both go **off the mat**;
5. Bout time expires.

If the **defender** starts a painful hold during the hold-down, both clocks can run; after the hold-down is scored, the painful-hold time continues (All-American / FIAS English).

**DOCUMENTED.** Sport sambo **does not** award ippon-by-pin the way judo 20 s osaekomi ends the match; 20 s is **4 points** unless it creates an 8-point gap or a submission.

### Combat SAMBO variants (do not mix with Sport)

**DOCUMENTED (secondary / older Combat texts):** some Combat SAMBO editions score a 20 s hold-down as **2 points** and allow **only one scored hold-down per bout** (Japanese Wikipedia combat section; one 2023 Combat PDF snippet: “Hold-downs shall be evaluated as 2 points (20 seconds). Only one (1) evaluated hold-down may be performed during a single bout.”). **For this Q8, use Sport SAMBO 20 s = 4 pts / 10–19 s = 2 pts** unless the product is Combat.

### Relation to the draft “bridge / shrimp / frame-roll” cards

**DOCUMENTED.** Those cards work by producing condition (1) or (2) above. A wrestling **bridge** that keeps the head and feet down with the back toward the mat is **still “on the back”** for **throw** scoring; for **hold-down** scoring, the relevant test is scapular angle **>90°** or breaking torso contact — a high bridge that peels the shoulder blades can stop the count, which is why sambo drills **уход через мост**.
