FIGHTCAMP — PRODUCTION SYSTEM SPECIFICATION

Engine: Godot 4.7.2 Stable (PC Target: x86_64, Windows / Linux / macOS)

Resolution Baseline: $1920 \times 1080$ (16:9 fixed scale factor, integer-snapped sub-elements)

Genre Class: Hardcore Sports Management Simulation (Football Manager, Motorsport Manager)

Design Mandate: Deep systems, explicit readability, high information density, zero mobile tropes. Every screen must anchor directly to a manager decision.

SECTION 1 — THE FIGHTER PROFILE AS A 3D "LOADOUT" SCREEN
1.1 Professional Critique & Strategic Verdict
The Reference Analogy

The producer’s proposal borrows from character-centric tactical games (Phoenix Point, Destiny 2, Guild Wars 2). In those titles, the player equips physical loot (weapons, armor modules, trinkets) onto a fantasy or sci-fi avatar. The visual feedback is immediate cosmetic and functional satisfaction: putting on a helmet changes the avatar’s silhouette.

Critical Risk vs. Benefit Analysis for FightCamp
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                      DESIGN TRADEOFF MATRIX                                        |
+----------------------+----------------------------------------+------------------------------------+
| DIMENSION            | RPG / TACTICAL SHOOTER ("LOADOUT")     | FIGHTCAMP (FM-CLASS MANAGEMENT)    |
+----------------------+----------------------------------------+------------------------------------+
| Core Interaction     | Equip physical items onto 3D body      | Allocate training hours & regimens |
| Screen Visit Rate    | 2–5 times per session (infrequent)     | 40–80 times per session (constant) |
| Dominant Data Need   | Item stats, rarity, aesthetic match    | Biometrics, wear curves, scouting  |
| Primary Spatial Cost | 3D avatar takes 45–55% screen width    | Spreadsheet / tables need width    |
| Evaluation Speed     | Visual inspection (does it look cool?) | Scan 9 attributes in < 1.5 seconds  |
+----------------------+----------------------------------------+------------------------------------+

Major Risks
The "Data Density Starvation" Trap: A 3D model occupying the center third ($640\text{ px}$ to $700\text{ px}$) forces all textual and tabular simulation data into narrow side columns. In a management sim, managers compare 9 attributes, 6 body-region degradation vectors, camp wear curves, morale, and contractual obligations simultaneously. Squeezing these into a $400\text{ px}$ left column turns vital tables into truncated labels or nested sub-tabs.
Workflow Latency / Session Fatigue: Football Manager players click through 15 fighter profiles in 90 seconds when triaging camp injuries or scouting an upcoming opponent card. If rendering a 3D model introduces viewport instantiation overhead or visual noise that slows down scanning attributes, the interface degrades the core management experience.
The Disconnect Between Boxing and "Equipment": Boxers do not equip an "Epic Hook." They master biomechanical motor patterns through repeated training. Treating techniques like an inventory screen misrepresents the sport unless the interface reinforces that techniques are long-term investments with execution trade-offs.
Major Strengths
Kinematic Literacy (Visualizing Technique Quality): In boxing, numbers like Lead Hook: Level 4 do not tell the tactical story as clearly as seeing the arc. Showing the fighter physically execute the move allows the manager to evaluate reach extension, elbow drop, and recovery balance.
Physicality & Emotional Attachment: Seeing a battered fighter stand in their actual physical condition—displaying taped ribs, a swollen left orbital bone, and visible posture fatigue—grounds the manager's roster in the physical realities of the sport far better than numbers on a spreadsheet.
Verdict: ADAPT (Tactical Dossier Model)
Do not adopt the RPG loadout model where the 3D model passively stands in the center while the manager clicks through small inventory icons.
Do not reject the 3D asset, because previewing motion clips solves the simulation problem of communicating reach, stance, and execution speed.
Adopt the "Tactical Biomechanical Dossier":
The 730 px Right Drawer remains the quick-look inspection tool on the gym floor (fast check without scene shifts).
The Full-Screen Profile ($1920 \times 1080$) adopts an asymmetric 3-column split:
Left (540 px): Biometrics, 9 core attributes, body wear, condition, contract.
Center (700 px): The 3D Biomechanical Stage (the athlete on a gym mat plinth, executing selected techniques on demand).
Right (680 px): The Technical Repertoire Tree (structured by punch class and tactical role, showing masteries, learning allocations, and counters).
Critical Anchor: This screen is where the manager sets Primary Tactical Focus and assigns Camp Drill Quotas. It is not a passive viewer; it is the command deck for technical development.

Relationship with the 730 px Gym Drawer

The two interfaces serve distinct operational needs:

The 730 px Drawer (In-Gym Inspection): A sliding panel overlaid on the live 3D gym floor at $x \in [1190, 1920]$. It does not instantiate a secondary 3D athlete. It is used during real-time training simulation to monitor station occupancy, fatigue buildup, and daily training outputs without pausing the gym simulation.
The Full-Screen Profile ($1920 \times 1080$): Opens when the player presses F on an athlete or double-clicks a roster row. It suspends gym floor simulation rendering, shifts the GPU pipeline to the dedicated character presentation stage, and provides deep planning tools for long-term development.

1.4 The 3D Stage: Lighting, Camera & Performance Architecture
Lighting & Rendering Pipeline

To give the 3D model clear physical presence without draining GPU resources, the screen uses an isolated, lightweight three-point studio lighting rig inside an independent World3D.

                  [Neutral Rim Light]
                  Angle: -145 deg, Elevation: +40 deg
                  Color: #38BDF8, Energy: 2.2
                           │
                           ▼
                     [Fighter Model]
                  Standing on Gym Plinth
                           ▲
             ┌─────────────┴─────────────┐
             │                           │
  [Key Light - Directional]    [Fill Light - Directional]
  Angle: +35 deg               Angle: -45 deg
  Elevation: +25 deg           Elevation: +10 deg
  Color: #FFF7ED, Energy: 2.8  Color: #64748B, Energy: 0.95

Plinth Base: A low, circular gym training mat ($2.2\text{ m}$ diameter, $0.06\text{ m}$ height) with a dark matte charcoal canvas texture (#1A1D24) and subtle red canvas boundary stitching. It grounds the fighter and provides a spatial reference for foot rotation.
Background Treatment: A neutral studio void. We avoid rendering a blurred gym floor here to prevent running a second complete 3D gym scene. The background is an unshaded dark gradient environment running from #0D111A at the top to #05070A at the base.
Camera Configuration:
Projection: Perspective, Vertical FOV: $34.0^\circ$ (flattens perspective distortion to accurately display body proportions and reach).
Distance: $2.40\text{ m}$ default, clamped to $[1.60\text{ m}, 3.20\text{ m}]$ on scroll-wheel zoom.
Target Height: Y-focus at $+1.15\text{ m}$ (solar plexus of standard $1.78\text{ m}$ model).
Interactive Drag-to-Rotate:
Left-click drag on the 3D viewport rotates the fighter around the Y-axis.
Rotation Limits: Free $360^\circ$ continuous orbit with an automatic ease-back to the front-three-quarter default if idle for $>8\text{ seconds}$.
Vertical pitch clamped between $-10^\circ$ and $+25^\circ$.
Idle Animation Architecture:
When no punch is playing, the model plays a breathing idle guard loop (boxing_idle_guard_orthodox or southpaw).
If the athlete has acute fatigue ($>40%$), the idle loop blends into the drooping guard posture developed in Section 4.
Performance & GPU Economy

Running a secondary 3D viewport while the main gym floor is loaded requires careful resource management:

SubViewport Sizing: Fixed at $700 \times 780\text{ px}$. This avoids rendering at full $1920 \times 1080$ resolution and saves roughly 60% of fragment shader fill-rate.
Main Gym Floor Culling: When this full-screen profile is active, the primary gym floor SubViewport sets render_target_update_mode = UPDATE_DISABLED. The main gym simulation continues updating its logic in _physics_process, but all 3D mesh rendering, lighting, and shadow passes for the gym floor are suspended.
Shadow Settings: The stage uses a single directional shadow map from the Key Light at $1024 \times 1024$ resolution with PCF5 filtering. The Fill and Rim lights do not cast shadows (shadow_enabled = false).
1.5 The Management Loop: Decisions Grounded on This Screen

A screen without meaningful decisions is visual fluff. In FightCamp, this screen serves as the primary workbench for three high-impact management decisions:

| MANAGEMENT ACTION WORKFLOW                                                                        |
+--------------------------+-----------------------------------+------------------------------------+
| 1. DRILL ALLOCATION      | 2. GAMEPLAN EMPHASIS              | 3. TACTICAL COUNTER-MATCHING       |
| Direct assignment of     | Set attack frequency weighting    | Side-by-side comparison with       |
| technique hours to       | for the fighter's upcoming bout   | the booked opponent's scouted      |
| specific gym stations.   | instructions.                     | tendencies.                        |
+--------------------------+-----------------------------------+------------------------------------+

1. Training Drill Allocation

The manager clicks a technique (e.g., Rear Uppercut) and uses the action panel to assign training time directly:

Slider sets weekly hours (e.g., $4.0\text{ hrs/week}$).
The system checks gym requirements and automatically routes the athlete to an available station (e.g., Uppercut Bag 1 or Coach Sparring).
If the required station is fully booked or missing, the interface warns: STATION CAPACITY EXCEEDED: UPPERCUT BAG OVERBOOKED (+2.5 hrs).
2. Tactical Gameplan Emphasis

Techniques are not just unlocked; they are prioritized for fight night:

The manager sets the technique's tactical priority: PRIMARY WEAPON, SITUATIONAL, or SUPPRESSED.
If an opponent has a weak chin and drops their left hand when exiting exchanges, the manager sets the fighter's Right Hook to Head to PRIMARY WEAPON (+35% trigger frequency).
3. Opponent Comparison Mode (Split-Screen Overlay)

Pressing C or clicking [COMPARE WITH OPPONENT] splits the right panel:

Shows the booked opponent’s scouted technique ratings side-by-side with your fighter's ratings.
Displays reach offsets directly: REACH ADVANTAGE: +2.5 INCHES (OUTSIDE JAB VIABLE).
Visualizes where your fighter's strikes match up against the opponent's defensive tendencies.
