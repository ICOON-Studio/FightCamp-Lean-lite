Role: Lead Game Designer

Scope: T1 Hall (30.0m × 18.0m), Progression to T2/T3, Equipment Slot Systems, Catalog Rationalisation, Crowd Flow, and Placement UI.

SECTION A: THE AUTHORED SLOT MODEL
1. Architectural Ruling: Freeform Grid vs. Authored Slots

Freeform tile grids are rejected. In a high-angle 3D simulation with dynamic MetaHuman pathfinding, freeform placement leads to broken navmeshes, clipping fighters, blocked ring aprons, and an amateurish, unreadable mess.

The Solution: An Authored Slot System (GymSlot3D).

Every zone has a pre-determined, architecturally certified set of mounting points and floor footprints.
Each slot possesses an explicit 3D transform, navmesh boundary, clearance radius, and category filter (e.g., HEAVY_BAG_HOOK, CARDIO_STATION, STRENGTH_CELL).
When an item is purchased, it instantiates directly into an assigned slot. No invisible abstract math: if it is simulated, it exists physically on the floor.
2. The Visual Representation of Empty Slots

An empty slot must never look like an untextured grey box or a broken CAD wireframe. The gym must look complete and functional even on day one:

Ceiling & Wall Bag Lines (HEAVY_BAG_HOOK):
Empty State: A high-detail, powder-coated steel eye-bolt welded to the overhead I-beam with a heavy industrial swivel shackle hanging downward. No dangling empty chains (which look messy). Just a clean, structural hook awaiting equipment.
Floor Stations (Cardio, Strength, Stretch, Footwork):
Empty State: Seamless rubber or maple flooring marked only by subtle, clean station demarcations—a thin, recessed brass floor trim or a semi-gloss painted floor number (e.g., small stencil "03" in #64748B at the back-left corner of the footprint).
From the high three-quarter camera, an empty slot simply looks like clean, open, well-maintained floor space. It does not read as a missing asset.
Shop / Build Mode Overlay:
When the player opens the Facility / Equipment panel, Godot displays a screen-space projection overlay:
Empty valid slots project an unshaded cyan boundary footprint (rect or projected circle) pulsating subtly (alpha 0.25 -> 0.45 at 1.0 Hz) with an overhead floating glyph icon.
Occupied slots show a muted slate border.
Invalid slots are invisible.

3. Slot Capacity, Replacement, and Upgrades
When a Zone is Full:
The catalog UI grays out the "Purchase" button for items targeting that zone and displays an explicit management blocker: "ZONE CAPACITY REACHED (CARDIO: 4/4) — Upgrade to Commercial Facility (Tier 2) or Replace Existing Station."
Hot-Swapping / Replacement:
The player can click an occupied slot and select "REPLACE".
The existing item is liquidated (refund value = Base Cost * (Condition / 100) * 0.50), and the new item drops into the exact same slot transform.
Re-assignment Within a Zone:
Slots within the same zone share universal dimensional footprints. For example, any slot in the Cardio Zone (2.0m × 1.0m + 0.6m clearance) can house a Treadmill, an Exercise Bike, a Rowing Ergometer, or an Air Bike. The player can move items between compatible empty slots within the zone without incurring financial penalties.

Geometric Validation Proof
West Heavy Bag Beam (X: 2.9 to 10.6m, Length = 7.7m):
Real-world spacing rule: 1.8m to 2.0m between heavy bag centers to prevent clash during full extension.
Math: 7.7m / 1.8m = 4.27.
T1 Allocation: Exactly 4 hanging bags at start (spaced at X = 3.5m, 5.4m, 7.3m, 9.2m), with 1 extra expansion hook on the wall offset (5 total).
East Bag Line + Wall Beam (Length = 6.0m + 6.9m):
T1 Allocation: 3 overhead beam hooks on the north-east run; 3 wall-mounted bracket hooks on the far east wall. Starting gym leaves these empty to avoid overwhelming early operating budgets.
Cardio Zone (Width = 5.1m, Depth = 6.9m):
Station footprint: 2.0m × 1.0m per machine + 0.6m walking gap.
2 rows of 2 machines = 4 machines total.
Footprint consumes 4.6m × 2.6m, leaving a wide 1.5m perimeter walk aisle to the ring apron.
Strength Zone (Width = 12.0m, Depth = 4.8m):
Power racks and Olympic lifting platforms require 2.5m × 2.5m minimum (including barbell whip and drop zone) + 1.2m walkway.
Math: Along 12.0m, 4 large platforms consume 4 × 2.5m = 10.0m + 2.0m total clearance.
T1 Allocation: 1 Power Rack, 1 Olympic Deadlift Platform, 1 Compact Dumbbell Run, 2 empty future cells (e.g., Cable Cross / Incline Bench).
Open Boxing Floor (11.6m × 6.9m = 80.0 m²):
Deliberately kept free of fixed machinery. Holds 1 dedicated coach focus-mitt dynamic node, 1 slip rope station, and open shadow-boxing space for up to 4 unassigned boxers.

SECTION D: WHY BUY — SIMULATION MECHANICS, BOTTLENECK LOOPS & DIMINISHING RETURNS

Equipment purchases serve two coupled simulation systems: The Professional Roster (Camp Performance) and The Commercial Membership (Gym Revenue & Cashflow).

       [DAILY TIME SCHEDULE: 17:00 PEAK GENERAL MEMBER ARRIVAL]
                                  |
              +-------------------+-------------------+
              |                                       |
    [ENOUGH CARDIO STATIONS]                [CARDIO BOTTLENECK]
              |                                       |
    - Members train on time                 - Members queue at station
    - Satisfaction remains 100%             - Frustration rises (+25%/day)
    - Monthly membership retained           - Member churn (+12%/month)
    - Pro fighter training uninterrupted   - Pro fighter kicked out to wait

1. The Commercial Membership Bottleneck Loop
In FightCamp, general paying members (the 60 members in T1) provide stable monthly cash flow to pay coach salaries and gym rent.
Members follow an authentic hourly arrival curve. Peak demand occurs at 07:00–09:00 and 17:00–20:00.
The Queueing Formula: $$\text{Station Demand} = \frac{\text{Active Members at Hour } t \times \text{Cardio Preference Pct}}{\text{Operational Cardio Stations}}$$
If $\text{Station Demand} > 1.0$, a visible physical queue forms.
Members wait up to 20 in-game minutes.
If a station is not open, the member leaves without working out, causing satisfaction -= 2.5 and gym_reputation -= 0.1.
Chronic queueing over 14 days causes members to cancel contracts, directly degrading recurring revenue.
2. Pro Fighter Skill Development & Training Velocity
A single heavy bag can only train one boxer at a time.
If an assigned coach schedules a morning drill for Leo Vance (boxing_one_two_hook at a Heavy Bag), but all 4 bags are occupied by general members or stablemates, Leo suffers a Drill Interruption:
Training efficiency drops by 45%.
Camp progression stalls.
Frustration and stress increase: {boxer.stress} += 4.
Buying an extra bag guarantees station availability, allowing simultaneous drills without compromise.
3. Equipment Specialization & Injury Wear Mitigation
Standard heavy bags impart high impact shock to the metacarpals and wrists (right_arm / left_arm wear increases by 0.35% per round).
Buying advanced equipment, such as Hydro Water Bags, absorbs kinetic shock while retaining power output:
Arm wear drops from 0.35% to 0.22% per drill.
Boxers can log 30% more training rounds per week before entering dangerous wear thresholds.
4. Mathematical Diminishing Returns (Anti-Spam Rules)

To prevent players from building 10 treadmills to hyper-inflate conditioning gains:
$$\text{Attribute Gain Multiplier} = 1.0 + \sum_{k=1}^{N} \frac{\text{BaseBonus}_k}{\sqrt{k}}$$

Station 1: 100% full efficiency.
Station 2: 70.7% marginal efficiency.
Station 3: 57.7% marginal efficiency.
Station 4+: Reaches a hard ceiling for pro training gains. Further units serve exclusively as commercial queue throughput to sustain higher membership caps, while still accruing full maintenance costs.

Operational Interaction Loop
Selection:
The player selects an item in the Catalog Drawer (e.g., Hydro Water Bag).
The camera glides subtly toward the compatible zones (bag_line_west and bag_line_east).
Visual Affordance (Godot 3D Shader):
Incompatible zones fade slightly via an unshaded darkening pass (CanvasItem dark tint alpha 0.3).
Compatible empty slots light up with an animated Cyan Target Ring projected onto the floor or overhead beam.
Ghost Hover:
Moving the mouse across the floor snaps a semi-transparent ghost mesh of the equipment to the nearest valid slot within a 1.5m cursor radius.
Placement Confirmation:
Left-clicking a valid slot completes the purchase:
Deducts {item.cost} from gym funds.
Spawns the physical PBR asset via GymSlot3D.mount_item(item_id).
Automatically updates the Godot NavigationRegion3D.
Displays an authoritative mechanical audio click (industrial steel bolt sound).
Item Context Menu (Existing Equipment):
Clicking any placed equipment on the floor opens a compact in-world radial dock:
[INSPECT / CONDITION]: Shows durability (92/100), accumulated training hours, and active user.
[MAINTAIN / SERVICE]: Costs $80, restores durability to 100%, and prevents equipment breakdown.
[RELOCATE]: Picks up the item to move it to any other compatible empty slot.
[LIQUIDATE / SELL]: Immediately sells the item for scrap/resale value.
SECTION F: THE TRAPS (AVOIDING PACKED, UGLY & UNREADABLE GYMS)
1. What Makes an Expanded Gym Look Cluttered or "Asset-Flippy"
Trap 1: Placing Bags Mid-Floor on Free-Standing Tripod Racks.
Why it fails: In real gyms, free-standing bag stands are flimsy consumer products. Professional gyms hang bags exclusively from structural ceiling beams or heavy perimeter wall tracks. Mid-floor stands block line-of-sight and look cheap.
The Rule: Every bag must hang from an overhead structural beam. Keep the center floor open.
Trap 2: Ignoring Natural Movement Aisles.
Why it fails: Packing equipment wall-to-wall without 1.2m–1.5m clearance aisles makes the space look like a warehouse junkyard and causes character models to slide or clip through meshes.
The Rule: Respect permanent clearance corridors between all stations. The ring apron perimeter must remain strictly unobstructed on all four sides.
Trap 3: Inconsistent Asset Scaling and Material Finishes.
Why it fails: Mixing random 3D assets leads to mismatched proportions—a giant treadmill next to a tiny power rack—and clashing art styles (e.g., weathered, rusted benches mixed with glossy modern machines).
The Rule: Enforce unified real-world dimensions and strict material guidelines: all gym metal frames must use industrial black powder-coat (#1E293B, Roughness 0.30, Metallic 0.75).
2. What Breaks Visual Readability from the High Management Camera
Trap 4: Tall Equipment Obscuring Short Stations.
Why it fails: Placing a tall cable crossover (2.4m high) or power rack in front of a flat stretch mat blocks the player's view of fighters using the floor mat.
The Rule: Height-to-Front Layout Hierarchy:
Deepest/Tallest items (heavy bag racks, power cages, cable columns) go along the perimeter walls.
Medium-height items (treadmills, rowers, benches) sit in mid-depth bands.
Low-profile items (stretch mats, agility ladders, open canvas) remain closest to the front cutaway wall.
Trap 5: Visual Saturation Overload.
Why it fails: If every newly purchased machine introduces a unique, bright neon hue, the gym floor turns into visual noise, breaking zone readability.
The Rule: Machinery must use neutral, industrial colorways (charcoal steel, black vinyl, brushed aluminum). Zone identity comes strictly from the floor mat base color, preserving clear 1-second zone recognition from above.
