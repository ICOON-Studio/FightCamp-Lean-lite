# CLUBWEEK1_V11 - Independent Critic Review (Goose)

**Critic:** Goose (independent sports management-sim designer)  
**Date:** 2026-11-18  
**Discipline:** Boxing gym management  
**Scope:** Club week mechanic with fight weeks, pad turns, loyalty, classes

---

## Scores

**DEPTH: 7/10**

The core resource-allocation problem is strong: pad turns as a shared, scarce resource creates genuine tension between money (classes) and mastery (fight prep). The collision line and 4-week forecast are excellent proactive design—they prevent frustration before it happens. Coached mornings are now a real choice with clear differentiation (solo for focused gains, shared for squad development).

The loyalty drift model adds meaningful consequences to resource decisions, and the priority/rotation system gives players agency in managing scarcity. Fight week load switches (tick over / rest up / train through) show thoughtful variety.

**Weaknesses holding it back:**
- Cover coach ($130) feels like a panic button rather than a strategic tool—no meaningful trade-off beyond "pay money to fix SHORT"
- Day job options are somewhat binary (full/half/no sponsorship) with limited strategic depth
- Loyalty conversation options lack variety—"tell it straight" has no mechanical effect, making it feel like filler
- No way to see historical patterns or plan ahead beyond 4 weeks
- Some numbers remain ESTIMATE, creating uncertainty about balance

**GENERAL DESIGN: 8/10**

The design is clear, consistent with the game's warm gym aesthetic, and respects the player's time (quiet week = one click). The wired reference table shows thoughtful integration across menus. Hiding the tab when only one fighter exists prevents unnecessary complexity.

Status words (OK/FULL/BAG/SHORT) with color reinforcement are excellent—immediate feedback without overwhelming numbers. The collision line is a standout feature: it shows conflicts *before* you accept a bout, with actionable options. The 4-week forecast mini-blocks are a smart addition that builds anticipation.

The language is plain and consistent ("gym" = facility, "camp" = pre-bout prep). The design follows owner rules: names only in headers, no flavor text, one word per concept.

**Weaknesses:**
- Option A/B for leaving state leaves ambiguity—should be a clear player choice or a fixed rule
- Cover coach pricing and availability conditions feel arbitrary
- Day job system feels slightly disconnected from the club week's core theme
- Some twist cards (heating down, photo request) don't meaningfully connect to the club week's resource management
- ESTIMATE numbers throughout create uncertainty about whether the economy actually works

**PLAY: 6/10**

The tension of managing scarce coach time is engaging, and the collision line prevents frustration. The 4-week forecast builds anticipation—you can see trouble coming and plan around it. Loyalty adds emotional stakes: your fighters are people, not just stat blocks.

However, the week-to-week experience lacks moments of delight. There's no celebration when you successfully juggle everything, no "wow" moment when a fighter hits a breakthrough from your careful planning. The system is *functional* but not *exciting*.

Day job choices feel like chores rather than interesting decisions. Loyalty conversations might become repetitive after the third time you hear "short weeks this month: 3." The twist cards add variety but some feel generic and disconnected from the club week theme.

**Missing:** A sense of progression or mastery. When do you feel like you've *cracked* the system? What's the reward for running a tight club for 20 weeks straight?

---

## Which options are missing

1. **Historical pattern view** - No way to see which weeks tend to be SHORT or which fighters absorb the most downtime. A simple "last 8 weeks" summary would help players learn the system's rhythms.

2. **Mentorship system** - A senior fighter (Loyal 70+) could mentor a junior, reducing their loyalty drift or giving them a small mastery bonus. This would reward keeping fighters happy long-term and add emotional depth.

3. **Assistant coach development** - No way to invest in Luke or Marcus improving their pad hours over time. They're static resources rather than growing assets.

4. **Seasonal variation in class demand** - Classes always pay the same, but real gyms have busy seasons (New Year resolutions, summer body prep). This would add strategic timing to class scheduling.

5. **Fighter personality affecting SHORT week handling** - All fighters react the same way to lost turns. A "team player" might accept it gracefully; a "diva" might lose more loyalty. This would make roster composition matter.

6. **Long-term planning beyond 4 weeks** - The 4-week forecast is great, but what about nationals prep? Pro debut timeline? A "season view" showing key dates and capacity would help strategic planning.

7. **Fighter feedback on their preferences** - No way to see which fighter *wants* more pad time or which is content with less. You're guessing based on loyalty drift rather than hearing their voice.

---

## Which options are useless

1. **"Tell it straight" conversation option** - No mechanical effect. It's just words. Either give it a small loyalty bonus (+1 for honesty) or remove it.

2. **Cover coach as a panic button** - $130 to fix SHORT feels like a tax rather than a choice. Integrate it better: maybe cover coach is only available for *future* weeks, not immediate fixes, or they have lower quality (0.9× mastery rate).

3. **Some twist cards** - "Heating down" and "photo request" feel disconnected from the club week's resource management. They're generic gym problems, not specific to the coach-time scarcity theme.

4. **Work evenings toggle** - Losing an evening pad slot to avoid losing a morning feels like a lateral move with no strategic depth. It's a workaround, not a choice.

5. **Option A/B ambiguity for leaving state** - This should be a clear rule, not a player choice. Either fighters can leave (Option A) or they can't (Option B). The current design leaves it undefined.

---

## What each option changes

**Pad turns:** The core unit. 3 turns = 1 full pad hour. Selling turns as classes generates money but costs mastery. This is the central trade-off.

**Classes (full/short):** The money lever. Full class = +$117 but bag night for affected fighters. Short class = +$78 but less mastery loss. Creates tension between cash flow and fighter development.

**Cover coach:** Emergency fix for SHORT weeks. $130 restores full pad capacity. Currently feels like a panic button rather than strategic tool.

**Coached mornings (solo/shared):** Solo targets one fighter's weakest stat (×1.25). Shared targets the one ability both lag in (×1.15 each). Creates choice between focused vs squad development.

**Fight week structure:** 3 pad turns + 1 film session. Load switches (tick over / rest up / train through) adjust for fatigue and opponent familiarity.

**Gym sponsorship/day job:** Fighter stipend ($250/wk) vs lost training time. Creates tension between financial support and fighter development.

**Loyalty drift:** Moves toward 60 based on how well you manage resources. SHORT weeks, broken promises, and sponsorship decisions move it. Affects morale and corner trust.

**Collision line:** Shows booking conflicts *before* you accept a bout. Prevents frustration by making consequences visible upfront.

**4-week forecast:** Mini-blocks showing fight week count, pad need vs capacity, SHORT/BAG risk. Builds anticipation and helps planning.

**Priority/rotation system:** ⭐ toggle guarantees full pad needs; rotation cycles who absorbs downtime. Gives players agency in managing scarcity.

**Twist cards:** Random events with 2 priced choices. Add variety but some feel disconnected from the core theme.

## To reach 10/10

**Most important first:**

1. **Add a "season view" showing 12-16 weeks ahead** - Show key dates (nations, pro debut, belt shots), capacity forecasts, and historical patterns. Let players plan strategically, not just react to the next 4 weeks. This transforms the system from tactical to strategic.

2. **Make cover coach a strategic tool, not a panic button** - Available only for *future* weeks (not immediate fixes), at 0.9× mastery rate, with a 2-week cooldown. This makes it a planning tool rather than a tax.

3. **Add fighter personality affecting SHORT week handling** - "Team player" trait reduces loyalty loss from SHORT weeks by 50%. "Diva" trait doubles it. This makes roster composition matter and adds emotional depth.

4. **Remove or rework "tell it straight" conversation** - Either give it a +1 loyalty bonus for honesty or remove it entirely. Dead options waste cognitive load.

5. **Add seasonal variation in class demand** - January and September classes pay 1.2× (high demand). December and July pay 0.8× (low demand). This adds strategic timing to class scheduling and mirrors real gym economics.

6. **Add mentorship system** - A Loyal fighter (70+) can mentor a junior, reducing their loyalty drift by 2/week and giving both a +0.5 mastery bonus on shared mornings. This rewards long-term retention and adds emotional stakes.

7. **Clarify leaving state as a fixed rule** - Either fighters can leave (Option A: 2 conversations with no improvement → notice) or they can't (Option B: only morale penalty). Don't leave it ambiguous.

8. **Add a "mastered the system" celebration** - After 20 weeks with no SHORT weeks, show a special card: "The club runs like clockwork." +5 reputation, +10 loyalty for all fighters. Give players a moment to feel proud.

9. **Add fighter feedback on preferences** - A fighter at Wavering loyalty says: "I need more pad time." A fighter at Loyal says: "I'm good." This lets players hear their fighters' voices rather than guessing.

10. **Confirm all ESTIMATE numbers with probes** - Run MONEYFOUR2, LOYAL1, DAYJOB1, CHECK 1, CHECK 7. A design with confirmed numbers is stronger than one with estimates.

## 10/10 as a 1920x1080 page

The page is divided into three vertical zones:

**Left zone (30%): The week grid**
A whiteboard-style grid: 7 days × 3 evenings (Mon-Thu pad nights). Each cell shows which fighter has the turn, or "CLASS" if sold, or "BAG" if running at bag rate. Color coding: green = OK, amber = BAG, red = SHORT. Drag turns between rows to rebalance. The 4-week forecast sits above as mini-blocks.

**Middle zone (40%): Fighter rows**
One row per fighter: header name only, morale chip, loyalty band with trend arrow (↑→↘), WORK flag, shared focus card, load switch dropdown, ⭐ priority toggle, rotate flag. Below: FILM swap buttons, PROMISES OPEN list, twist card (if any), conversation card (if needed). The shared focus card shows: "Both lag in Conditioning — shared morning raises this for both."

**Right zone (30%): The deal**
Top: status summary (Asked/Need/Film/Cover). Below: coach's deal order (drag to reorder). Below that: gym sponsorship options, day job toggle, class pricing. At the bottom: collision warnings (if any) with actionable options. The big button: "Start week" or "Coach deals it — same as last week" (quiet week).

The page feels like a gym office: warm tan wall, marker notes, polaroid fighter cards, the fight date circled. No spreadsheets, no overwhelming numbers. Everything actionable, everything clear.
