## Mechanics - The Corner and Tactical Control
The most critical gameplay occurs in the corner between rounds. During the three-minute 3D boxing rounds, you observe the action at 1x, 2x, or 4x speed. In the 60-second intervals between rounds, you must analyze stamina, physical damage, and the flow of the fight to adjust your strategy. You assign tasks to the cut man, select a new tactical plan for the upcoming round, and decide what to say to your fighter to bolster their performance. Most importantly, you hold the power to throw the towel, making the difficult choice to prioritize a fighter's long-term health over a single win. These decisions visibly alter the fighter's behavior and effectiveness in the following round.

## Mechanics - The Training Lap
Training days are structured as high-speed management sequences viewed from a top-down perspective of the gym floor. You direct fighters between various stations, including heavy bags, pads, speed bags, treadmills, bikes, weights, and sparring rings. A 10-hour training day is condensed into two real-time minutes, split into morning, afternoon, and evening blocks. Each station targets specific attributes but consumes stamina. You must balance gains against exhaustion, as training while fatigued significantly increases injury risks. Movement cards allow you to select three techniques to drill each week; once a move is drilled and mastered, it becomes an automated action the fighter can perform in a real bout.

## Progression Systems - Camp Preparation and Strategy
In the four weeks leading up to a fight against a named opponent, you must execute a specialized camp. This involves selecting a camp focus, a training protocol—ranging from hard sparring to scientific recovery—and hiring sparring partners who mimic the opponent's style. You can also engage visiting coaches and conduct film studies to develop counter-strategies. The camp concludes with a two-round test spar where you must make one irreversible tactical commitment, such as doubling down on a specific weapon or fixing a defensive hole. The knowledge gained during sparring generates points that can be spent on fight night to react to the opponent's specific tendencies and weaknesses.

## Playable Characters - Fighter Attributes and Traits
Every fighter is a unique individual defined by six primary attributes: power, speed, defense, stamina, chin, and ring IQ. They belong to specific stylistic archetypes, such as out-boxers, swarmers, or counter-punchers. Beyond raw stats, fighters possess traits that influence their performance, such as 'slow starter,' 'iron will,' or 'lazy in camp.' Their physical measurements, including height, reach, and weight class, are visually represented and impact their fighting style. Fighters also have hidden potential that reveals itself over time, and a limited career lifespan. Physical wear and tear are permanent; for example, pushing a fighter through a bad cut can result in a permanent 'cut prone' scar.

## Difficulty Progression - Gym Development and Career Growth
Progression is tracked through the growth of your gym and the prestige of your fighters. You begin in a modest garage gym with two amateurs and aim to ascend to a world-renowned facility with a stable of champions. As your reputation grows, higher-quality prospects will seek out your gym. You must manage the gym's finances by balancing member dues, fight purses, and sponsorships against the costs of rent, staff wages, and equipment upgrades. You can hire specialized staff, including assistant coaches, cut men, scouts, and conditioning coaches. Each facility upgrade provides a clear, tangible benefit to your fighters' development, while background members and sparring partners keep the gym environment feeling active.

## Onboarding & Tutorial - The First Hour Experience
The game introduces players to its systems through a guided first hour that serves as a demo. Players begin by naming their gym and meeting a raw amateur fighter who possesses one distinct strength and one glaring flaw. This initial phase walks the player through a short camp for an easy amateur bout, teaching the basics of training and corner management. The difficulty then scales with a second opponent who features a dangerous weapon, forcing the player to deal with a cut and make a critical 'towel' decision. The onboarding concludes by presenting the player with a professional debut offer and their first major financial decision, setting the stage for the full career mode.
