# The week, one design (v1, S425) - joined from the systems designer's and the coach designer's answers

Picture: docs/mockups/s425/week_v1.png (Home on a Monday in camp). Boxers are named or "the boxer" (sandbox rule). No third
answer (Kalli's) existed when this was written. Nothing here is built or measured beyond the facts in the brief.

## 1. The idea in five lines
1. Every week the player spends the same scarce thing, the coach's evenings, on pads, on watching a sparring night, or on the club.
2. Pads put the plan's answers in the boxer's hands; the watched sparring night tests the plan in private and reports back.
3. What a boxer does in a public bout is SEEN: the next corner waits for it. So a line can be shown, held, backed up or made sharp.
4. A board on Home shows everything set going and the day it lands; the clock stops on Thursday only if there is news, with two changes.
5. The next fight is taken or asked for on a card that prices all of it: answers owned, lines seen, evenings clashing, how much the boxer listens.

## 2. The week as played
### Camp week (Lucy fights in 19 days, taper in 12; Danny lost last Saturday and is Flat; six new members)
- MONDAY, Home. Last week's board reads back first: landed, late, surprise with its reason. Then four decisions:
  the twist card (two priced answers); Reid's three evenings (Monday pads for Lucy's missing answer, Wednesday his eye on
  Lucy's sparring, Friday open: Lucy's back-up, Danny's pads, or the newcomers); the sparring night (partner, how hard, which
  line); Danny's offer (answer by Friday). The board now shows what he waits for: tell Wednesday, report Thursday, scale Friday.
- TUESDAY. Runs. WEDNESDAY. The tape opens one tell (a flag, no stop). The sparring happens in the evening.
- THURSDAY, the call. The clock stops because the report landed and the scale drifted 0.4 kg off its line. The report: "Step
  back won 4 of 6 against a presser. The right hand dropped after the jab four times. Faded in round 3." He has TWO changes:
  move Friday's evening, swap a morning for roadwork, change one plan arrow for the new tell, put the habit on a morning.
  He cannot do all four. That is the stop.
- FRIDAY. Danny's offer: the card says Flat (hears 85% of the corner), 2 of 4 answers by taper, the one-two already seen.
  Take the tune-up, pass, or ask for a later date (a promise, due on the board).
- SATURDAY, SUNDAY. Nothing asks. Monday's review reads the board back and names what the choices cost and bought.
- FIGHT WEEK (two weeks on): no pads in taper, so everything had to land by its start. In the corner minute the note says
  "Kovacs's corner is waiting for the Jab-Jab." Swap to the back-up if it was drilled; keep it if it is sharp; else pay.
  The Why says which lines the region has now seen. That list is on the next offer card.

### Quiet week (no bout booked for anyone)
- MONDAY. The same three evenings, no bell. He lays them: pads on a back-up or on sharpening the best line (the readiness
  line on Fights moves: "a 6-week offer today: Lucy owns 3 of 4 answers"); the newcomers' night; an open sparring night
  under Reid's eye (finds a habit of his own boxer, or trials a prospect). He asks for a fight: level and date window.
- THURSDAY. The call fires only because things landed: the answer to the ask, with its reason ("yes, 7 March" or "not yet:
  one more bout for that show"); "4 of 6 newcomers stayed, one has quick hands: invite to a trial?"; the habit the eye found.
  Two changes: start the cut early for the date, move Friday's pads to the answer the named opponent calls for.
- BY SUNDAY he can name what the week bought: a member kept, a prospect, a date, a habit named, a line nearer sharp.
  If he does nothing, Reid lays the week and it is one click, as today.

## 3. The four joined mechanics
### The coach's evenings (R55, R73, R74, R46, R80, R06; systems designer's pool, coach designer's project week and promise)
One pool, spent every week, with or without a fight. Reid works Monday, Wednesday, Friday; each evening he runs the class
and gives his hands or eyes to ONE of: pads for one boxer (or half and half), the eye on a sparring night (no eye, no
report, only the mastery), the newcomers (members in their first 90 days).
Decisions in order, each feeding the next:
1. The plan picks the cards (Playbook): an answer the boxer cannot do yet shows its price in wins in 100 and in evenings.
2. Monday, the lay. Reid's default is the nearer bell first. Every claim shows what it buys and what the others lose.
3. Thursday: one of the two changes can move an evening, after the report says what is worth drilling.
4. The boxer who goes without three weeks running "feels second"; give an evening or promise the next camp (a due line on the board).
5. Next camp the promise binds the lay; the newcomers kept become members, one becomes a prospect, a prospect a new claim.
When each option is right:
- Pads on a missing answer: right when it lands before taper and the opponent's game is scouted; wrong for an unscouted guess.
- Pads on a back-up or on sharpening: right when the best line is seen and this corner reads tape; wrong against a novice corner.
- The eye: right when a bout is booked and a line is untested, or in a quiet week to find a habit; wrong in the last week of pads.
- Newcomers: right in a quiet week or when many joined; wrong when an answer would land after taper because of it.
- Half and half: right for two single moves; wrong for a combination (a week late for both, measured).
- Both boxers whole, class off ($115 and members who came for the class): right for a one or two week overlap when cash is fine.
- A clubmate holds, or the bag (half the pad rate): right for the boxer whose bell is far; costs the clubmate's own session.
- An extra night on Reid's free evening: right under 70 tiredness; wrong at 74 (it lands over 80: gains x0.6, injury x3).
- Second coach: his ten hours belong to the 40 members he brings; an evening on pads leaves them uncovered. Right once two
  boxers camp most of the year. Hale: right from the third boxer; with two the screen says he is a bad buy.
Anticipated: the day a card turns usable, against the start of taper. Reward: a new answer opens a plan line; a kept member
and a found prospect; the readiness to say yes to a short-notice offer.

### The film on you (R17, R19, R20, R21, R23, R25, R46, R47; systems designer's seen and held lines, coach designer's sparring night)
The player studies opponents in the Tape Room; now they study the player. A line used twice in a round of a public bout
is SEEN from then on. A sparring night is private. A corner that reads tape waits for a seen line from the first bell;
an unseen line can only be read inside the bout, the round after it is used twice (the R25 rule).
Decisions in order:
1. The sparring night: partner (a clubmate, free; the other boxer in camp, free, both tire; a visitor of the opponent's
   style, $160), how hard (technical, working, hard), and the one line tested. Reid's eye turns it into Thursday's report.
2. The report: which line won and lost (a range until the opponent is scouted), a habit that showed, the round the engine faded.
3. The answer to the report (Thursday's changes): rewrite the line, drill it, drill a back-up, fix the habit.
4. Writing the plan for the bout: show a line or hold it back (it stays unseen for a bigger night).
5. The corner minute: the note names the line being waited for. Swap to the back-up, keep it, or spend the change elsewhere.
6. After: the Why lists what is now seen; History and the rival file keep it; a rematch starts with both files open.
When each option is right:
- Clubmate: right for finding the boxer's own habits. The other camper: right when both bells are over 10 days off.
  Visitor: right only when the opponent's style is known, the one partner whose report is about this fight.
- Technical: right for a tired or Flat boxer (lines only). Working: the standard. Hard: the only way to see the engine
  and the chin, right two to three weeks out against a presser; wrong inside 10 days or near 80 tiredness.
- Show the line: right when the fight is close or this corner does not read tape.
- Hold it: right in a bout already likely won with a bigger one in sight (the regional, then the national draw; the
  cheapest answer to hold costs 2 wins in 100 today, the dearest 14).
- Swap to the back-up: right only if it was drilled to usable; the player who never drilled one learns why here.
- Keep the read line: right when it is sharp or better (it keeps part of its edge: to be measured).
Anticipated: Thursday's report; the corner note in round 2; who has seen what on the next card. Reward: a habit named
before the fight instead of after; a line that reaches Signature becomes the boxer's HOUSE LINE, named on the page: every
corner has seen it and it still works, so its back-up evenings come free for something else.

### The week board and the Thursday call (R92, R93, R07, R11, R05, R03, R14 replaced; the docket and the Wednesday call, joined)
The game already knows dated things (a tell opens, a card turns usable, the scale forecast, an offer's last day). The
board on Home draws them on one week: who has Reid each evening, and under each boxer what lands on which day.
Decisions in order: Monday he sets things going and sees the landing day and its colour against taper. Thursday the
clock stops ONLY if something landed or drifted since Monday; he gets two changes, each priced. Next Monday the review
reads the board back; a surprise carries its reason. The camp's three lines (weight, stat, never overreached) are the
stake a Thursday can save: all three met is the peak camp the game already pays.
When each option is right: a change on the weight when the margin is under a kilo with days left; on a plan arrow when a
tell or the report named a weapon; on an evening when the report showed a line losing; no change at all when the board is
green (the stop still shows the news in one click). Leaving the twist card unanswered gives the coach's pick, as today.
Anticipated: every line has a day. Reward: a green board he can read as progress; each landing opens the next line.

### The next fight: the card's four prices and the ask (R37, R39, R40, R33, R52; the ask and answer, the comeback's price line)
Every offer and every ask shows four prices that come from the three mechanics above: ANSWERS OWNED BY TAPER (3 of 4),
SEEN (which lines, and whether this corner reads tape, a "?" until scouted), EVENINGS (weeks this camp overlaps another),
THE CORNER'S WORD (Fired up 105%, Steady 100%, Flat 85%, Low 70%: the game's own). Beside them what rides on it.
Decisions in order: Monday he asks (level, date window, a name if he has one); Thursday the answer lands with its reason,
a checklist not a dice roll; he takes, passes, or asks for a later date (a promise to the boxer with a due week).
After a loss the same card runs the road back: a month without a fight costs morale 6, a Flat boxer hears 85%.
When each option is right:
- Tune-up: right for a Flat or Low boxer, or to hold a line for the bigger night. Wrong for a fresh boxer when the club needs a win that counts.
- Step up: right when the board was green and the Why said the plan worked. Rematch: right once the back-up is owned.
- Pass or later: right when two camps would overlap with both boxers learning their answers; it costs the month's clock.
- Short-notice offer: right only for a boxer the quiet weeks made ready. That is what the quiet weeks are for.
Anticipated: Thursday's answer; the date. Reward: the Road's ticks (bouts, weight, rank) filled by his own asks.

## 5. Numbers and the measurements to run first
FROM THE GAME (measured or in the rules): the coach's hands land a card one week sooner; a missing answer costs 2, 4, 9 or
14 wins in 100 (40.0% with all four, 18.7% with none); sharp pays 1 point over usable; being waited for costs 6.5 points
and a swap wins nothing back today; the top line earns 64% of round 1's planned edge; read after 2 uses a round; pads 2.1,
sparring 1.5, bag 1.05 mastery an hour; overreach at 80 (gains x0.6, injury x3); visitor $160; class $350 a week ($115 an
evening); second coach $280, 40 members, one roster place; morale and the corner's word; peak camp; missed weight $300.
REAL: a pad session is 6 to 12 three-minute rounds of one man's arms (J62); English clubs train three nights; corners
study an opponent's last bouts (practice, the reason the Tape Room exists; no source read for it).
INVENTED: Thursday as the day; two changes; a seen line waited for from the first bell; the back-up punishing the wait;
sharp and Signature keeping part of a read line; who reads tape (by ring IQ); how hard sparring moves injury; newcomers
kept by a night; members lost when the class is off; second coach's uncovered members; feels second -5; promise +5 / -10.
Measure in the game before anything is built (each on the real bout, 300 bouts a cell, or 20 simulated weeks):
| Measure | The result that kills it |
|---|---|
| A drilled back-up swapped in after a read, at usable and at sharp | Wins back under 2 of the 6.5 points at every level: the read only takes away; cut it to marks on the page |
| A read line kept at sharp and at Signature | Signature keeps under 3 of 6.5: pad time still dies at usable and the pool is short only in an overlap |
| Holding the top line, by win chance (50, 65, 80) | Costs over 6 points even at 80: holding is never right; drop it, keep seen marks |
| Seen (waited for from round 1) against unseen (read from round 2) | Under 2 points apart: seen and unseen are labels; over 12: too decisive to hang on an invented rule |
| One evening of three lost to the eye or the newcomers: days a card slips | No card ever slips: the eye is free and the lay is not a choice |
| The player's sparring pick against Reid's default over 40 nights | The report differs under 1 night in 4: a chore, the coach picks |
| The Thursday stop over 20 weeks, and the best two changes each time | Fires in more than half the quiet weeks, or the same two changes are best 7 times in 10: fold it into Monday |
| A smart ask against a plain one (the S402 bot test) | No difference: the ask is a button; keep only the reasons |
| A Flat boxer (85%) in a step up against a tune-up first | Under 3 points apart: the corner's word is a label on the card |

## 6. Kill list
1. "The read only takes points away. Your way back is the thing the measurements say does not work." True today: swapped
   in, a fresh right answer won nothing back, drilled or not. The design needs a new rule (waiting for the wrong line is
   the other corner's mistake that round) whose size is invented and decisive. The first two measurements decide it. If they fail I have
   no answer: the film on you shrinks to marks and the sparring report, and the whole design is a 5 or 6.
2. "The lay is still a lookup: every claim prints its price and I pick the best, or let Reid." Partly answered: the prices
   are in things that do not add up (wins in a fight with a named stake, members, a report whose worth is unknown until
   read, tiredness, a promise), the stake is a range until the opponent is scouted, and Reid's default is the nearer bell,
   visibly not always best. Not answered: a rich club with one boxer can still let Reid lay it. For that player the week
   is the sparring night and the Thursday call, not the pool.
3. "Four systems before the first click; and a novice's first five opponents never read tape, so the best rule is never
   met." Answer: every part has the coach's default and the stop fires only on news, so the floor is today's one click.
   The first corner that reads tape is scripted from what exists: Frank Doyle's Eastside boxer, bout three or four, and
   the offer card says so. The nationals are a run of bouts where each next corner has seen the last one. If playtests
   show the first season passes without one waited line, bring Doyle forward.

## 7. Build order, and what was left out
Each step makes the week better alone.
1. The nine measurements (probes only, no screens). They also settle "sharp pays", the open step of R55.
2. The week board on Home from dates the game already has, read back in the review. No new rule.
3. The four prices on the offer card: the corner's word and answers owned by taper first (words from existing numbers).
4. The sparring night's three picks and its report; the Thursday stop with two changes.
5. The seen record: marks on Playbook and History, "they have seen" on the card (a record, no effect yet).
6. The read rule with the back-up, the hold and the sharp line; the corner note and the eye on the live panel (R25, finished).
7. The evenings pool: pads, eye, newcomers, class off, clubmate and bag; the rota on People, the coach row on Training.
8. The quiet week's claims and the readiness line; the amateur ask with Thursday's answer; the promise as a due line.
Left out, and why:
- The ledger of promises as its own list, and Standing: a promise is a due line on the board; one more number buys nothing yet.
- The talk after a loss (own it, shield, push): three invented shifts, and shield's cost is unmeasured. The price line stays.
- The project week as a menu of five: hard block and rest are already mornings; the away day ($60, R49) is parked; the rest became evening claims.
- Hands 45 "single moves only" for the second coach: it contradicts the measured "being there matters, how good hardly".
- Pulling a board line forward for money, the coach's note on late habits, the sponsor's early fight (R33), the youth
  class as a fifth claim, one corner man for two bouts on a show, rivals keeping their own files: later, once the four hold up.
