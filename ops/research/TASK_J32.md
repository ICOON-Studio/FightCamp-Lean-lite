## WHY I AM ASKING

We are about to build a clinch as an animated held position and then wire it to the fight engine,
and we have no numbers for it. Everything else in this project is anchored to a real published
figure before it is tuned, and this must be too. **A held position that lasts the wrong length of
time, occurs at the wrong rate, or ends the wrong way will look right and simulate wrong.**

I need documented numbers with their sources, not impressions. Where the literature disagrees,
give me the spread and say which sample it came from — a band is a useful answer, a single
confident number with no provenance is not.

## THE QUESTIONS, IN PRIORITY ORDER

**1. How long does a clinch last?** Median and spread, in seconds, from the moment hands engage
to the moment they separate or the fight goes to the floor. Give it per discipline where the
sources allow: MMA, Muay Thai, boxing. Boxing and Muay Thai will differ enormously and that
contrast is part of the answer.

**2. How often does it happen?** Clinches per round, or per minute of fight time, or share of
total fight time spent clinched — whichever the sources actually report. **Name the denominator
every time**, because "12% of the fight" and "12% of standing exchanges" are different claims.

**3. How does it end?** The breakdown: broken by the fighters, broken by the referee, a takedown
or throw out of it, a knockdown or stoppage from strikes inside it. Percentages with sources.

**4. What happens inside it?** Strike rate while clinched (knees, elbows, short hooks, dirty
boxing), and how that compares to the same fighters at range. If there is a number for grip
changes or position switches per clinch, I want it.

**5. How far apart are the two men?** Any documented figure for torso or hip separation in a
clinch, in metres or centimetres. This is the one I expect to be hardest to source. **If there is
no study, say NO STUDY in those words** rather than estimating — an honest gap is more useful
than a plausible number I cannot trace, and I will go and measure it from footage instead.

## WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE

- Every number carries its source: study or organisation, year, sample size, and what population
  it was drawn from. A figure from 300 UFC bouts and a figure from one amateur tournament are not
  interchangeable.
- Where you give a range, say whether it is a spread across sources or a spread within one sample.
- A short section at the end: **"what I could not find"**, listing every question above that the
  literature does not answer. That section is as valuable as the rest.
- Keep it tight. This is a reference sheet for building one animation and one engine rule, not an
  essay.

## WHAT NOT TO DO

- Do not write code and do not reference repository files — you have neither.
- Do not fill a gap with a reasonable-sounding estimate. NO STUDY is a complete answer.
- Do not give me advice on how to animate it. Numbers and sources only; the design is mine.
