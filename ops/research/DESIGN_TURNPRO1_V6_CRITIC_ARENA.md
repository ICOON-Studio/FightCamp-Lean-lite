# TURNPRO1 v6 — independent design review

SCORE depth=5 general=4

**Play: 5/10.** A first call, a calendar date and a new professional road are a satisfying payoff for years of training. Two houses could make that payoff a real decision between reliable cash and ticket upside. The proposed delays also tie preparation and the gym's cash to the debut. But the advertised win rates and uptake are targets, not demonstrated outcomes for this version. The wait path is currently almost a free escape from a costly refusal, and much of the weekly anticipation is a countdown rather than a new decision. The 7/10 design bar is not met.

**Depth: 5/10.** Risk, purse, training time, ambition, membership and amateur titles are promising interacting levers. Yet players cannot choose between houses at most forecasts, the opponent is fixed by house, and expiry dominates refusal in many states. A forecast alone does not make a four-week delay valuable: the actual ticket gain depends on member movement and the 100-seat cap. The proposed house gaps make one opponent stronger and the other even, but the guaranteed house also offers a larger purse; the ticket house needs demonstrable upside in reachable states. The existing post-debut house lean and purse growth matter too and must be compared over a career, not just one night.

**General design: 4/10.** There is a buildable foundation, but several CODE labels are wrong, some proposed consequences contradict existing code, and exact forecasts are presented as guarantees. A date conflict with an already booked amateur bout cannot explain the delay choice as offers are gated while a bout is booked. A permanent lock after three refusals is especially harsh when doing nothing bypasses it. The promised 67% and 79% are calibration observations for a particular setup, not universal predictions. The proposed fixed “she” copy conflicts with the gender-neutral sandbox; names belong only in headers, so house names in sign buttons and an opponent name in a history row also breach that rule. Promoter identity can be shown in card headers, with “Sign” inside each card.

## Options and consequences

- **Sign either available house, plain:** ends the amateur road and stipend, pays or finances licences, books a four-round debut and changes future house offers. Guaranteed purse versus ticket exposure, dates and opponent strength should be compared alongside longer-term earnings. Both cards only coexist at forecasts 46–74.
- **Later +2 / +4:** sacrifice $150 / $300 of gross purse for extra preparation, recovery and possible member growth. At maximum growth of six members *per week*, the simple ticket rule adds up to six / twelve tickets over two / four weeks, not a guaranteed four / eight; drift, cap and other changes can make it lower. No already-booked amateur bout may coexist at offer creation. Four weeks is not automatically worth paying twice for two weeks; test net gym and boxer outcomes, readiness and opportunity cost.
- **Own price:** ticket house only; reduces price to $40 and adds 15 sales, subject to 100 seats. It changes both the shortfall and ticket share. Compare total purse plus ticket share and each party's cut, not price alone. At 91 forecast tickets it gains $25 gross over plain, at 92 it breaks even, and at 100 it loses $200 gross, before rounding and gym split. These examples assume the original $1,500 terms and no later ask.
- **Costs from purse:** guarantee house only; zero cash at signing, but costs plus a $100/$200 fee reduce the gross purse before the gym/boxer split. The proposed extra five *percentage points of gym share* for three fights is not built; specify a floor, starting fight, expiry, interaction with a low-loyalty 10% share and contract changes. Finance relief may be worth it for a cash-poor gym; measure it against actual cash-flow and closure risk.
- **Stay amateur:** takes a refusal and loyalty hit, changes eligibility and can reduce future terms; it retains amateur competition. Current thresholds are absolute, not always two wins after the refusal. Show actual next requirements and morale and share consequences before pressing.
- **Let expire:** advances time without a refusal or direct morale/loyalty loss and sets a two-win wait. It is not currently a selectable action, does not guarantee the same house or full terms, and wins can take much longer than eight weeks. As proposed it is generally superior to an explicit refusal unless the player needs to clear the offer immediately; give that distinction a meaningful, honest cost or remove the duplicate decision.
- **Softer opponent / better split:** v6 cuts them, so they are not v6 options. The first could be useful to a fragile boxer only if it actually changes the opponent and future choices; current code does wire tune-up sets, despite the map's contrary claim. Split is not proven useless across both houses and forecast bands. Restore either only if measured trade-offs make it distinct; otherwise the cut reduces clutter.
- **Missing:** an explicit “review later” close action that leaves the offer live, with the real expiry stop; a comparison of cash now, gym and boxer proceeds on fight night, weekly changes and future house purse/offer bias; a link to the week/camp and weight forecast before signing; and an honest opponent win *range* plus uncertainty and scouting limits. These navigate or inform a consequential sign/ask decision, not extra cosmetic buttons. If offers are gated when booked, do not promise a delay resolves a booked-bout clash. A route/eligibility explanation should include the nine-win alternative and why an offer cannot arrive; it need not be a selectable option.

## To reach 10/10

1. **Make the return rules coherent before balancing.** Record wins and eligible houses at refusal or expiry. Decide whether “two more wins” means since that event and enforce it consistently; preserve or reset decline factors explicitly. Give expiry a distinct cost from immediate refusal, or use one clear wait action. Show exact requirements rather than an eight-week promise. Cover zero, one and two prior refusals, titles, both houses, age cutoff and save/load.
2. **Make each decision truthful end to end.** Calculate future ticket projections from actual member drift with a range, capacity and assumptions; settle from actual night values. Preview gross and net for both parties, signing cash and weekly stipend/cut changes. Recompute price warning at the current forecast; show it on the action, not only a tooltip. Forecasts must not be guaranteed income.
3. **Implement and test the proposed changes, not just labels.** Wire the new opponent gaps into booking, show `BoutRead` as a calibrated range with scouting uncertainty, and test different fighters, camp states, styles and actual booked fights. Implement the costs penalty with precise percentage-point, floor and three-fight expiry semantics; show its total estimated cost. Wire ambition using the actual recruit text and make the day-job gate and nine-win alternative explicit. These are not yet established as built.
4. **Test for real choices rather than target uptake alone.** Run seeded career cohorts across ticket bands, low/high cash, injuries, ambition, titles, day-job routes and both sexes. Compare plain and each ask including future purse growth, coach time, closure, morale and win/loss risk; report sample sizes and uncertainty. Verify no action dominates within a meaningful reachable state, and tune the houses and waits before enforcing arbitrary pick-rate targets. Run boundary tests at 45/46/74/75 and 84/85/91/92/100, including changed terms and caps.
5. **Make the milestone playable across weeks.** Add the review-later action, links to week/weight/camp, and a visible post-signing timeline with cost paid, training milestones and a post-fight comparison of forecast versus actual. Retain post-debut offer bias as a visible contract consequence. Avoid creating extra screens or interrupting the weekly flow.
6. **1920×1080 page description:** On the existing warm gym wall, put a single boxer header and countdown at the top. Two equal paper house cards occupy the centre; each house name appears only in its card header. Each card shows gross purse, risk, debut date, opponent chance range, actual-now and projected-night tickets, gym cash now and gym/boxer night proceeds, followed by one-ask controls with immediate before/after costs and a card-local Sign button. A narrow lower strip shows the boxer's ambition, route, amateur opportunity and future house bias. The footer offers Review later and one honestly costed Stay amateur action; a separate expiry control appears only if it has a genuinely different consequence. Disabled actions explain why. A side link opens the existing week/camp view; no invented opponent or person names appear in body copy. This is a proposed layout, not a claim that the current modal already does it.
