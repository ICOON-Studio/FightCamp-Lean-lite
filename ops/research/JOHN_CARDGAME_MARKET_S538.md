# John - research brief: fight-sport card games and progression, and the Expedition 33 approach (S538)

Context: we are designing a STANDALONE fighting card game (prototype): seven martial arts (boxing, kickboxing, karate, judo,
wrestling, BJJ, sambo; MMA as the meeting ground), turn-based duels with an open table (you see the opponent's punches before
you answer, one guard per turn, a stamina pool, one face-down trap), bouts of about 5 minutes, "fast and punchy", every number
shown. The owner's reference games: Breachway, Hearthstone, Clutchtime, Rune Coliseum, Starless Abyss, MMA Arena, Monster Train,
Mixed Martial Cards (demo), Kungfu Card, Ironbound.

Owner's new ask (verbatim): "include ladders and championships etc , people love to see how they progress in ladder and learn
skills for fighters , take decisions on training , check if any other similar games exist out there if we are missing something
out or we could also take a different approach similars to expedition 33".

Please research and answer, with sources (store pages, reviews, player counts / review scores where public, dev interviews):
1. EVERY game you can find that is a fighting / combat-sport game played with cards or card-like turns (boxing, MMA, wrestling,
   martial arts, kung fu), released or in development, PC or mobile. For each: the core turn, how the fighter progresses (ladder,
   rank, belts, tournaments), how skills are learned, training decisions, run structure, review score and the most common praise
   and complaint. Mark the 5 best-reviewed.
2. Card / deckbuilder games with a strong LADDER or CHAMPIONSHIP progression and fighter growth (sports or not): what makes the

3. Training-decision systems in sports and fight managers (e.g. Football Manager, boxing managers, Punch Club 1/2, Fight Manager)
   that players praise: what choice the player makes each week and what it costs.
4. Clair Obscur: Expedition 33's combat: how its turn-based + real-time reaction system works (dodge, parry, jump, gradient
   counters, timing windows, AP economy), what reviewers praised and criticised, accessibility options; and any card or
   turn-based fighting game that uses a similar "your turn is a choice, their turn is a timing test" model. Would that model fit
   a boxing/martial-arts duel? Give the case for and against.
5. What is MISSING from our design that the best of these have (a short ranked list, most important first).
Answer in plain words, a table for 1, sources linked. No code.
