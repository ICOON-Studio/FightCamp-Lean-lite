## DECISIONS
Hip travel toward target: only maximal cross has a number — 27.8±6 cm (Cheraghi 2014). Jab / lead hook / rear hook / uppercut hip cm: NO STUDY. Return after impact: NO STUDY. DOCUMENTED / NO STUDY.
Do not reuse 28 cm for every punch — that is one straight-right lab mean. INFERENCE.
Lead-foot step length (cm) for stepping jab/cross; rear-foot slide cm; hook foot travel cm: NO STUDY. Coaching jab step ~5–8 cm is SECONDARY only.
Bout mean man-to-man distance 1.23±0.16 m (Schortgen ECSS) — not split by punch type. Front-foot→target for maximal straight lab: 49.6±8 cm (Cheraghi). Per-punch bout cm: NO STUDY.
Punch-type range (bag, scaled): jabs vanish closer in; hooks remain at short scaled distance (Hristovski 2006). Absolute cm from that study: NO STUDY without arm-length conversion published.
Trunk ° at impact for straight vs hook: NO STUDY (pelvis/thorax) in the sources found. Segment % of fist velocity: Filimonov + Dinu (below). DOCUMENTED %.
Games: EA relative IK / touch tags / player scaling — no published lunge cm. Indie code cites ~0.3–0.5 m punch lunge — SECONDARY, not Fight Night/UFC docs.
Operator shortfall (0.26–0.32 m straight, 0.48–0.50 m hook) needs per-type body warp; only the cross has a measured hip budget (~28 cm) — hooks need a closer launch distance, not a bigger hip shove. INFERENCE.

Scaled “strikeability” (which punch appears at which range)

Hristovski et al. 2006, J Sports Sci Med (bag task): scaled distance D (boxer–target distance normalised by arm reach). Critical values:

Jab action patterns disappear near D ≈ 0.45; uppercuts near D ≈ 0; hooks remain at the closest distances. DOCUMENTED.
Absolute cm = D × individual arm length — paper discusses scaled D, not a single cm table for all subjects. Converting without published arm lengths = INFERENCE only → leave as scaled.

Practical read (not a measured bout table): straights are longer-range tools; hooks/uppercuts are closer-range. A long-range hook is outside the pattern Hristovski measured. INFERENCE.

Degrees at impact (straight vs hook)
Pelvis / thorax rotation degrees at impact for jab/cross vs lead/rear hook: NO STUDY in Cheraghi, Stanley, Piorkowski, Dinu (Dinu reports elbow / neck / knee ° and COM height, not trunk rotation °). Same gap as K49 for hook X-factor.

Share of the punch from legs / trunk / arm (%)

Filimonov et al. 1985 (NSCA J 7(6):65–66; Soviet cohort, rear-hand / punch force contribution — method not fully modern):

Skill	Leg push-off	Trunk rotation	Arm extension
Masters / high skill	~38.5%	~37.4%	~24.1%
Class II–III / lower	~16.5%	~45.5%	~38.0%

DOCUMENTED (historical; calculation details thin — treat as classic secondary-cited primary).

Dinu & Louis 2020 — segment contribution to hand velocity at impact (Elite):

Punch	Elbow	Shoulder	Trunk	Pelvis (linear)
Cross	39.2±35.9% (highest)	15.6±12.5%	6.7±6.8%	(small)
Hook	lower elbow	50.1±21.0% (highest)	—	Elite 3.0±4.2%
Uppercut	—	54.8±12.3% (highest)	—	—

Juniors used more shoulder on all three. DOCUMENTED.

Cross ≈ arm-extension dominant; hook/uppercut ≈ shoulder/rotation dominant; legs+trunk still matter for force (Filimonov) even when Dinu’s velocity share at the hand is mostly distal. INFERENCE.
