# Question for John: how does an amateur boxing club really get a bout for one of its boxers?

For a boxing gym management game: the player runs one small amateur club (England Boxing rules first, USA Boxing second) and
wants to ASK for a bout for a boxer instead of waiting for offers. Real-world evidence only; where nothing is measured, say so.

1. Who matches amateur bouts in England today: club-to-club contact, regional/county matchmakers, England Boxing's own
   matching tools (name them), show organisers' bout sheets? How does a coach put a boxer forward, step by step?
2. What does a request have to say (weight, age, bouts boxed, wins, club, medical and card status, date window, home or away)?
3. How often does a request come to nothing, and the common reasons (no one at that weight/experience, opponent's club says
   no, medical or sparring rule not met, too many bouts too close, show full)? Any numbers: bouts per novice per year,
   share of a club's boxers who box in a season, typical wait from "ready" to a first bout.
4. Lead times: how far ahead a club show's card is built, when bouts fall through (weigh-in, no-shows) and how late a
   replacement is found. Is taking a short-notice bout a known trade-off?
5. Can a club ask for a harder or easier opponent (a "skills" bout, a novice vs an open boxer), and who decides whether a
   match is fair (the matchmaker, the officials, a rule on bout counts or ages)?
6. The same questions in brief for USA Boxing (club shows, Golden Gloves, the registration and matching steps).
Give a number with its source wherever one exists, mark OFFICIAL / MEASURED / OPINION, and list what you could not find.
