Here are five ways to defend with no timing, no reflexes and no hidden dice. Two of them (1 and 2) create defence cards during the bout, and the fifth creates them too. My pick to build first is at the end.

## 1. The spare hand (defence cards are created)

**The rule**
1. Each slot of a lead that goes unused (a lead has up to three) creates one defence card for the opponent's next lead. Which card depends on the hand that stayed home.
2. **Lead hand unused:** you get a **Parry**. It stops one straight to the head (jab or cross). If it stops a cross, you get a free jab as the first move of your next lead.
3. **Rear hand unused:** you get a **Cover**. It stops one punch from the side, to the head or to the ribs.
4. The cards lie face up, so the attacker sees them before committing. They expire when that lead ends.
5. The cost is the punches you didn't throw. You do get the usual stamina back for throwing fewer.

**One exchange**
- **Setup:** last lead, the defender threw only a jab, so both empty slots stayed home. That gives one Parry (lead hand) and one Cover (rear hand).
- **The lead:** the attacker sees both cards and leads jab, cross, lead hook anyway.
- **Jab:** lands, because the defender is saving the Parry.
- **Cross:** parried. The defender banks a free jab.
- **Lead hook:** caught on the Cover, for a small stamina loss and no damage.
- **Result:** one jab landed out of three.

**The decision**
- **What it trades:** every punch you throw now is a hand you can't defend with next.
- **Throw the full three** when the other man is hurt, tired or low on cards.
- **Throw one and keep two home** when the opponent is about to load up, or when you're the one who's hurt.
- **The attacker's side:** seeing the defender's cards, they change their combination to aim where nothing is covering. That aiming is the attacker's half of the decision.

**How it can break**
- **The lazy strategy:** turtle. Throw one punch a lead, hold two defence cards, and win on the attacker's misses.
- **Cap 1:** the scoring counts punches landed, so a man who barely throws loses rounds he never got hit in.
- **Cap 2:** an uppercut still goes through a Cover.
- **Cap 3:** defence cards never carry over past the next lead, so they can't be stockpiled.

**The real boxing:** a hand that's punching isn't guarding. Coaches say "hands home" for exactly that reason.

## 2. Reads (defence cards are created)

**The rule.** You earn defence cards by watching what the other man does. Each card is used once and lasts until the end of your opponent's next lead.

| What happened | Card you get | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| The opponent threw the same punch in two leads running | **Read: that punch** | Slips or ducks that exact punch and gives you a counter, which becomes the first move of your next lead and can't be defended |
| Your jab landed | **Range** | The first hook or uppercut of their next lead falls short. Straights still reach |
| The opponent bit on your feint | **Frozen** | Their next lead is limited to two moves |
| You landed to the body | **Breath** | Your next block costs no stamina |

**One exchange**
- **Setup:** last time, the attacker opened with jab–cross, and the time before that too. The defender also landed a jab. So the defender holds **Read: cross** and **Range**.
- **The lead:** the attacker sees both cards and still goes jab, cross, lead hook, hoping the hook gets through.
- **Jab:** lands.
- **Cross:** slipped by the Read, and the defender banks a free counter.
- **Lead hook:** falls short on Range.
- **Result:** one landed out of three. The defender opens the next lead with a counter that can't be stopped.

**The decision**
- **For the attacker,** every lead weighs the best combination against the one that hands over a Read.
- **For the defender,** it's whether to spend turns on jabs and feints that earn cards, or on power punches that do damage.
- **When each is right:** reads pay off against a one-pattern opponent. Against a varied one, throw power instead.

**How it can break**
- **The lazy attacker:** varies the opening every time, so Reads never come.
- **Why that's fine:** forcing variety is the point, but the cap is that a varied lead is a weaker lead. The attacker loses the hand-change bonus some of the time.
- **The lazy defender:** jabs every turn to farm Range.
- **The cap:** only one Range is held at a time, and it never stops straights.

**The real boxing:** corners really say "he's dropping his hand after the jab" or "he loads the right every time". Reading habits is most of real defence.

## 3. One guard call per combination (shown targets)

**The rule**
1. The attacker lays the combination face down. What each card hits stays visible: head straight, head from the side, chin from below, ribs or stomach.
2. Seeing those targets, the defender makes one call that covers the whole combination:
   - **High guard** stops straights to the head;
   - **Tight guard** stops hooks to the head;
   - **Elbows in** stops ribs and stomach;
   - **Shell and roll** halves everything but stops nothing.
3. A punch that matches the call is blocked, for a small stamina cost to the defender. Anything else lands in full.
4. Uppercuts beat High guard and Tight guard.
5. The guard you used last can't be called twice in a row in the same round, because the attacker has learned it.

**One exchange**
- **What the defender sees:** head straight, head straight, head from the side.
- **The call:** High guard. The jab and the cross are blocked and the lead hook lands.
- **The other way:** Tight would have stopped the hook but let two straights in.
- **The defender's reasoning:** a lead hook does more damage than a jab, but the cross does more than the hook. High guard costs one punch landed, and the least dangerous one.

**The decision**
- **For the defender,** it's which punch in the combination hurts most, given their own Health and Stamina right now.
- **For the attacker,** it's building combinations that split the targets, such as head, body, head, so no single guard covers them.
- **The rule of thumb:** mixing targets is how you beat a guard, and the game rewards it.

**How it can break**
- **The lazy defender:** always calls High guard, because most punches are straights.
- **The cap:** the no-repeat rule above, plus uppercuts going through it.
- **The lazy attacker:** throws three different targets every time.
- **The cap:** changing targets breaks the hand-change bonus, or costs extra stamina, because it's awkward.

**The real boxing:** you don't see the punch coming, you read the shoulders and hips and guess where it's going. Real fighters set up the body to open the head.

## 4. The cut-in (defending by punching first)

**The rule**
1. Once per opponent's lead, the defender may interrupt it by playing one of their own face-up punches before a chosen incoming punch.
2. The interruption follows fixed rules:
   - a **straight** beats a **hook or overhand** (it takes the shorter, inside line);
   - a **hook** beats a man who stepped in with an **uppercut**;
   - **nothing** cuts in on a jab.
3. If the cut-in wins, it lands and the rest of the attacker's combination is cancelled.
4. The card used comes out of the defender's next lead and costs double stamina.
5. If the rules say it loses, it can't be played, and that's shown before you choose.

**One exchange**
- **The lead:** jab, cross, lead hook.
- **The jab and the cross:** both land.
- **The hook:** the defender cuts in with their face-up cross, the straight inside the wide hook. It lands and the hook is cancelled.
- **The cost:** the defender's next lead has two slots instead of three, and they paid double stamina for the cross.

**The decision**
- **For the defender,** it's whether to spend part of their own attack to save damage now. They also pick where in the combination to cut in.
- **When it's right:** cut in early against a big three-punch combination, or not at all against a single jab.
- **For the attacker,** it's whether to end on a hook or overhand, which can be cut, or keep combinations straight. Straight combinations are safer and do less damage.

**How it can break**
- **The lazy defender:** cuts in every lead.
- **The cap:** the double stamina cost and the lost slot, plus only one cut-in per lead.
- **The lazy attacker:** throws only straights so nothing can be cut.
- **The cap:** straights can be parried in the other mechanics, and they do less damage.

**The real boxing:** "beat him to the punch". The straight inside the wide hook is the oldest counter in the sport.

## 5. Ground and the ropes (defence cards are created)

**The rule**
1. Each boxer has a ground track of four steps between the centre of the ring and the ropes.
2. The defender may give up one step to make one punch fall short. There's no stamina cost, but the attacker gains the centre.
3. At the ropes, stepping back is impossible. Every punch that lands there does a little more damage.
4. Two conditions create defence cards:
   - **Pinned on the ropes:** you get a **Clinch** card. It stops the rest of the combination and resets both boxers one step towards the middle, but it costs the round's score (points against).
   - **Your jab landed:** you get a **Pivot** card. It moves you back to the centre without giving up a step.
5. Both are used once and expire at the end of the round.

**One exchange**
- **Setup:** the defender is two steps from the ropes.
- **Jab:** lands.
- **Cross:** the defender steps back, one step from the ropes, and the cross falls short.
- **Lead hook:** lands, because the defender won't step back again and get pinned.
- **Next lead:** the defender jabs, it lands, and they earn a Pivot to get back to the middle.

**The decision**
- **What it trades:** space against damage. Every step you give up is a punch saved now and less room later.
- **Step back** early in a round, when you have room.
- **Take the punch or clinch** late, or when you're already near the ropes.
- **The attacker's side:** for the attacker, it's whether to push forward to pin you, or score from range.

**How it can break**
- **The lazy defender:** steps back every punch, then clinches.
- **The cap:** only one Clinch per round, and each costs the round's score.
- **The lazy attacker:** charges in every lead.
- **The cap:** someone moving forward walks into the cut-in from mechanic 4, so it's worth pairing the two.

**The real boxing:** "keep off the ropes", "cut off the ring" and the clinch to survive. These are the most common corner instructions I found in the research.

## Which one I'd build first: the spare hand (1)

1. **It fits what you already have.** It uses the two decks you already have, with no new deck, bar or timer. It's one rule: an empty slot in your lead becomes a guard card for your hand.
2. **It merges attack and defence.** Every lead you build is also your next guard, so the fight stays one decision a turn and stays inside five minutes.
3. **Everything is visible.** The attacker sees the defence before committing, which matches "every result shown before he commits".
4. **The pictures are simple.** A Parry, a Cover or a clean landing is all it needs.
5. **It leaves room to grow.** Reads (2) and the ground track (5) can be added later without changing it.

If it plays flat, my second choice is mechanic 2, the Reads.

## Games to study
These come from what I know, not a live check today, so check the details before copying any of them.
- **Yomi** (Sirlin Games, 2011). A fighting card game where attacks, throws, blocks and dodges beat each other in a fixed circle, with combos built from linked cards. The closest match to mechanic 4.
- **Exceed** and **BattleCON** (Level 99 Games). Card fighting games with a range track and speed rules. Moving back makes attacks miss, which is close to mechanic 5.
- **Slay the Spire** (Mega Crit, 2019). Enemies show their next attack in advance, and block comes from cards you choose to play instead of attacking. That's the same give-and-take as mechanic 1.
- **Into the Breach** (Subset Games, 2018). Every enemy attack is shown before you act, and defence is about moving and positioning. A good model for "every result shown before you commit".

One caveat on the build-first pick: if turtling turns out to win often in play, the rule that rounds are scored on punches landed is the safeguard. If that isn't strong enough, give the defender a stamina cost for each slot left unused.
