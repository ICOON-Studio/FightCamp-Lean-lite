# BJJ (no-gi focus): real published numbers for calibration
Compiled 2026-11-07 from web research. Every number below is paraphrased, with a solidity note:
**strong** = official rulebook text, or a peer-reviewed study / dataset of 1,000+ matches;
**medium** = a federation or stats-site tally of one or more full events (100+ matches);
**weak** = a small sample, a self-reported database, a blog, or inconsistent counts;
**coach lore** = nothing measured.
Many pages (BJJ Heroes, jits.gg, theseus, ResearchGate) blocked direct reading. Where a number came only from a
search-engine summary of the page, that is noted as "(via search summary)".

### By belt (adult)
- **No published adult white-to-black breakdown was found** (no IBJJF report, no paper, no large dataset).
- Best proxy is youth: jits.gg, 59,000+ decided youth/national matches: white 48.0%, grey 57.9%, orange 63.3% (kids' belt

  https://jits.gg/guides/submission-rate-guide (2026) (via search summary). **Weak for adults** (youth, one vendor).
- What the data does show: local and regional events (all belts mixed, short matches) finish ~50-63% by submission, while
  elite black belt gi majors finish ~31-43% and elite no-gi black belt ~39-56%. The level gap between the two athletes
  seems to drive submissions more than the belt itself does (section 3, Nogi Ratings table).
- Pattern in every elite source: **women's black belt divisions finish more often than men's** (no-gi: 54-56% vs 39-42%).

## 2. Advantages, referee decisions, penalties, stalling

### Rule text (IBJJF Rules Book, version 5.2, January 2021; the PDF read was dated 2021, hosted as "MAR2022")
Source: IBJJF Rules Book, https://jjbqc.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/EN_IBJJF_RulesBook_MAR2022.pdf (2021). **Strong** for that
version. I found no newer official text; check ibjjf.com for a later version.
- How a match is won, in order: submission / stoppage / DQ / loss of consciousness → **more points** → if level, **more
  advantages** → if level, **fewer penalties** → if level on all three, **referee decision**.
- What the referee judges: which athlete was more offensive during the match and came closer to a scoring position or a
  submission.
- Points: takedown 2, sweep 2, knee on belly 2, guard pass 3, mount 4, back mount/back control 4; a position must be held 3 s.
- Serious-foul / stalling penalty ladder: **1st = marked only; 2nd = an advantage to the opponent; 3rd = 2 points to the
  opponent; 4th = DQ.** Stalling and serious-foul penalties add up on the same count. Both athletes on 3 stalling
  penalties = restart standing. Fleeing the area to escape a submission = penalty + 2 points to the opponent.
- Stalling (lack of combativeness): one athlete clearly not trying to improve position, or stopping the opponent from
  doing so; referee counts about 20 s then calls it. Not stalling when defending from mount, back, side control or north-south,
  or when holding mount/back properly. Examples: holding side control without trying to advance; holding a closed guard
  without trying to pass while stopping the guard player; clinching from closed guard with no attack. Double guard pull =
  20 s count, then a penalty to both.
- Conflicting secondary guides (jits.gg, cnxbjj, elitesports) describe "1st penalty = advantage" or "2 penalties = 1
  advantage". The official 2021 text above is the version to use unless a newer rulebook says otherwise.

### How often
- Referee decisions at IBJJF Worlds, adult black belt: **7%** (29 of 413 men's matches over two years) and **7.2%** of 800+
  matches in the follow-up study. BJJ Heroes, "BJJ Analysis: Deciding Factors in Winning a Referee Decision" and
  "...Decision 2.0", https://www.bjjheroes.com/editorial/bjj-study-deciding-factors-in-winning-a-referee-decision-2-0 (~2019-2021)
  (via search summary). **Medium.**
- In those decisions: the athlete with a previous world title won **82%** (only 23 matches, **weak**); the athlete with more
  takedown attempts won **85%**, more submission attempts **58%**; top position and trying to improve it was the clearest
  pattern; pulling guard had little effect. Same source.
- Final scores, 2019 IBJJF Worlds (gi) black belt quarter-finals to finals, 87 matches read off the scoreboard by computer vision:
  **24% ended 0-0 on points**, 45% ended 0-0 or 2-0, 8% ended 2-2, 5% ended 4-4; **~32% were level on points** (decided by
  submission, advantages, penalties or decision); ~25% of matches had a 1-0 advantage score; ~17% were 0-0 on points AND
  advantages (penalties or a decision decided them). BJJ Data by Riggs, "BJJ Scoreboard Analysis with Computer Vision",
  https://bjjdatabyriggs.blogspot.com/2020/06/bjj-scoreboard-analysis-with-computer.html (2020). **Medium** (one event, elite, gi).
- 2012 Worlds (gi): 48% of matches decided on points, two-thirds of those by fewer than 5 points. BishopBJJ, "World Jiu-Jitsu
  case study 2.1: points and submissions", https://bishopbjj.pages.dev/posts/world-jiu-jitsu-case-study-2-1-points-and-submissions (2012). **Weak** (match count not given).
- No-gi: IBJJF No-Gi Worlds 2025 black belt, only 3 of 125 recorded matches went to advantages (2.4%) (DigitSu, **weak**).
  ADCC (no advantages; negatives + referee decision) 2022: about 23% of matches were neither a submission nor a points win.
- **Usable calibration (my reading of the above):** elite gi black belt ≈ 35-40% submission, ≈ 40-45% points,
  ≈ 10-15% advantages/penalties, ≈ 7% referee decision. Elite no-gi IBJJF black belt ≈ 45% submission, ≈ 40% points,
  ≈ 3-8% advantages, the rest penalties/decision/DQ.

## 3. Belt gap, favourites, upsets

- **No published win rate for a higher belt against a lower belt was found** (no study, no federation data, no measured
  coach number). **Coach lore only.**
- Federations' own rule is a proxy: IBJJF puts an athlete with a judo black belt or wrestling experience at **blue belt or
  higher** (IBJJF Rules Book 2021, art. 5.5), and Grappling Industries makes experienced grapplers from other sports enter the
  blue/intermediate level or higher (https://grapplingindustries.com/rules/). Both treat outside grappling as at least a one-belt jump.
- **Rating-gap win rates (best proxy for a skill gap)**: Nogi Ratings, no-gi Elo with margin of victory, ~60,000 matches 1998-2026.
  - Established athletes (3,912 matches): the higher-rated athlete wins **65.7%**, draws 6.7%, upsets 27.5%.
    Gap 0 → 50.6% · 50 → 61% · 100 → 66% · 150 → 75% · 200 → 76% · 250 → 80% · 300 → 85% · 350 → 91% · 400 → 90%.
    https://nogiratings.com/studies/accuracy (2026). **Medium-strong.**
  - Larger set (7,189 matches, athletes with 20+ matches, draws excluded): gap 0-24 → 49%, 25-49 → 54%, 50-74 → 60%, 75-99 → 64%,
    100-199 → 75%, 200-299 → 84%, 300-399 → 90%, 400-499 → 92%, 500+ → ~93%. https://nogiratings.com/studies (2026). **Medium-strong.**
  - Submission rate rises with the gap (favourite submits / favourite gets submitted): gap 0-49 → 22% / 33% (18,479 matches);
    100-149 → 41% / 15%; 200-249 → 53% / 9%; 300-349 → 61% / 4%; 400-449 → 69% / 3%; 500+ → 76% / 1% (458).
    https://nogiratings.com/studies/submissions (2026). **Medium-strong.** So a mismatch ends by submission ~75% of the time;
    an even match ~55%. Upset wins come by submission about as often as by points.
  - Favourite wins by method (established set): favourite by submission 1,308, by points 812, by decision 452; underdog by
    submission 419, points 372, decision 285.
- Older small study: an Elo model for IBJJF black belts predicted 72% of results (83% of 24 first-round Europeans 2020 matches).
  BJJ Data by Riggs, "Elo Rating for BJJ" + "IBJJF Europeans Update", http://bjjdatabyriggs.blogspot.com/ (2020). **Weak.**
- Betting markets: lines are published (FloGrappling ADCC 2017/2022/2024, WNO), but **no published favourite win rate** was found.
- In-match favourite signals (gi, elite): the first athlete to score points won 65 of 73 matches (**89%**) at the 2018 Pans
  (115 black belt matches), 6 of the 13 comebacks by submission; the first to score an advantage won 31 of 42 (74%). BJJ Heroes,
  "IBJJF Pans Stats: The Importance of Being First", https://www.bjjheroes.com/editorial/ibjjf-pans-x-uaejjf-grand-slam-stats-the-importance-of-being-first
  (2018) (via search summary). **Medium.** 2019 Worlds: first scorer won 75%, last scorer 87% (Riggs, above). 2012 Worlds:
  73% of submissions were by the athlete who scored first, 4% by the athlete losing on points (BishopBJJ, **weak**).

## 4. Most common submissions in no-gi; leg locks at brown/black

- IBJJF No-Gi Worlds, adult black belt:
  - 2023 (98 subs): heel hook 23, RNC 21, armbar 15, kata-gatame 7, straight ankle lock 6 → heel hook ~23%, RNC ~21%, armbar ~15%.
  - 2024: RNC 25, heel hook 20, armbar 10, toe hold 7, straight ankle lock 6, guillotine 6.
  - 2025 (118 subs): RNC 26, inside heel hook 20, straight ankle lock 14, armbar 10 → RNC 22%, heel hook 17%, SAL 12%, armbar 8%;
    leg locks together (HH + SAL + toe hold) ≈ 30-35% of black belt finishes.
  - 2022 women brown/black: RNC 14, armbar 9, inside heel hook 7.
  Sources: BJJ Heroes results pages 2023/2024 (via search summary), DigitSu 2025, FloGrappling 2022 (as in section 1). **Medium.**
- Older IBJJF No-Gi Worlds men's black belt (2007 to ~2015, before heel hooks were legal there): RNC 28%, armbar 17% of subs.
  (Gi Worlds same years: back choke 23%, armbar 22%.) FloGrappling 2015. **Medium.**
- ADCC Worlds (heel hooks legal at all stages): 2015 RNC 8 of 33 (24%); 2019 RNC 8, inside heel hook 6; 2022 RNC 9, armbar 7,
  heel hook 6 of 40; 2024 RNC 15, armbar 4. DigitSu event pages (2019/2022/2024), FloGrappling 2015. **Medium.** ADCC Trials
  (2018 Europe, 2023 East Coast): heel hook the most common finish (FloGrappling "Stats Prove Heel Hooks Dominate At ADCC Trials", 2018). **Medium.**
- For comparison, gi elite: back chokes 45% of subs at 2023 Worlds (21 of 47), ~50% at Pans/Europeans 2023, 35% at the Brasileiro;
  armbar ~21%. IBJJF reports 2023. **Medium.**
- Lower belts (gi, white + blue, 140 UK matches, 173 sub attempts): attempts armbar 34%, triangle 21%, cross collar 12%,
  kimura 8%, rear choke 7%; success rear choke 75%, triangle 22%; ~75% of attempts from bottom guard; white belts attempted
  only 8 submission types, blue belts 9; omoplata appears at blue, not white. Williams J, Callaway A, Gara M, Tattersall A,
  "Technique utilisation and efficiency in competitive BJJ matches at white and blue belts", Int J Perform Anal Sport 19 (2019);
  preprint https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/32250/ . **Medium** (peer reviewed, small, gi).
- Leg locks by belt (rule): IBJJF made heel hooks and knee reaping legal in **adult no-gi brown and black belt** from 2021;
  still illegal for white/blue/purple and masters (and in gi). FloGrappling, "IBJJF Confirm Heel Hooks For Black & Brown
  Belts In 2021", https://www.flograppling.com/articles/6797682 (2020); rulebook 2021 table. **Strong.** (The rulebook's legal-techniques table by belt also limits
  knee bars, toe holds and slicers for lower belts; check the exact columns before encoding per-belt move lists.)
- Heel hook winners in sub-only no-gi often had no positional control before the finish (upper-body subs correlate with
  dominant position, r = 0.50). Spanias C, Kirk C, Øvretveit K, "Position before submission? Techniques and tactics in
  competitive no-gi BJJ", Rev Artes Marciales Asiáticas 17(2):130-139 (2022), https://doi.org/10.18002/rama.v17i2.7410 . **Medium** (52 athletes).
- Trend claim (heel hook 2% of no-gi finishes in 2002 → 24% in 2021-25, overtaking RNC in 2022): sports.yrciou.net "BJJ Trends
  by the Numbers" (2026). **Weak** - inconsistent figures, unclear method; use only as a direction, which the IBJJF/ADCC
  counts above do support.

## 5. Match lengths (IBJJF adult)

- IBJJF Rules Book v5.2 (Jan 2021), duration table: adult **white 5, blue 6, purple 7, brown 8, black 10 min**. Masters 1-2:
  white/blue 5, purple/brown/black 6; Master 3+ 5 min; juvenile 5 min. The table is one table for gi and no-gi. **Strong**
  (confirm against the newest rulebook; no change found).
- Other organisations: beginner/intermediate/advanced no-gi often 5/6/8 min (third-party rules guide, rollbook, **weak**).
- ADCC: 10 min regulation, up to two 5-min overtimes; ADCC 2019 matches lasted **8.9 ± 3.0 min** on average (shortest 1:55,
  longest 19:30). Lamas et al. 2023 (below). **Strong.**
- Day load: 4-6 matches a day in an IBJJF bracket (Andreato et al. 2017 review). **Medium.**

## 6. Physical and athletic predictors

- Systematic review, 58 studies, 1,496 subjects: Andreato LV et al., "Physical and physiological profiles of BJJ athletes: a
  systematic review", Sports Med Open 3:9 (2017), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5306420/ . **Strong.**
  - **Do NOT separate levels:** VO2max (men 42-52 mL/kg/min; aerobic power similar at all levels), body fat (men 5-20%,
    ~12% typical; one study elite 11.2% vs 14.8%), isometric handgrip (48-57 kgf; not clearly different).
  - **Do separate levels:** grip endurance on the gi (static hang 54-62 s competitors / 38-41 s state level / 28-36 s
    beginners; gi chin-ups 15-18 / 8-17 / 7-10 reps), relative bench press 1RM (1.46 vs 1.36 kg/kg high vs state;
    brown/black 1.48 vs intermediate 1.32), countermovement jump (expert 34.2 vs novice 29.7 cm), sit-and-reach
    (40 vs 32 cm elite vs non-elite). Squat 1RM ~1.20 kg/kg did not separate levels.
- Elite vs non-elite gi grip tests (20 men): hang 56 ± 10 s vs 37 ± 11 s; gi pull-ups 15 ± 4 vs 8 ± 3 reps.
  "Reliability in kimono grip strength tests and comparison between elite and non-elite BJJ players"
  (https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4026725). **Weak-medium** (n = 20).
- Meta-analysis 2024 (Retos): elite vs non-elite effect size strength 1.01, power 0.62 (both p < 0.0001); aerobic not separating.
  https://doaj.org/article/37f57e7021094b6bbe6cdff374b5659a (2024) (via search summary). **Medium.**
- Øvretveit K, "Anthropometric and Physiological Characteristics of BJJ Athletes", J Strength Cond Res (2018), 42 Norwegian men:
  VO2max 50.6 ± 4.6; physical traits were largely **independent of rank**, experience and training volume. **Medium.**
- No-gi pace (Spanias 2022, sub-only events): high- to low-intensity ratio 1:2 for regional athletes vs **1:1 for
  international** athletes; standing:ground time 1:2 at both levels. Gi: effort:pause ~6:1 (cited in Lamas 2023). **Medium.**
- **Weight** (no-gi absolutes, ADCC Opens and above, 2022+, 1,017 cross-weight matches): heavier athlete wins **60.5%**
  (one class ~11 kg: 61%; two classes: 59%; three classes ~33 kg: 69%), about 5 points more than the rating gap alone predicts.
  Nogi Ratings, https://nogiratings.com/studies/weight (2026). **Medium.**
- **Age** (no-gi, 63,215 matches, 2000-2026): peak ~28-31; win rate vs expectation: 25-29 +1.9 pts, 30-34 +0.8, 35-39 -0.5,
  40-44 **-4.8**, 45-49 **-8.3**; clear drop after ~38. Older + heavier vs younger + lighter in absolutes: older-heavy 60% (144 matches).
  Nogi Ratings, https://nogiratings.com/studies/age (2026). **Medium.**

## 7. Scoring per match, takedowns, guard pulls

- **ADCC 2019 (no-gi elite), all 93 matches, 90 men**, Bayesian action counts (per athlete per match, 10-min standard):
  submission attempts **1.03**, takedown attempts 0.73, back-take attempts 0.61, sweep attempts 0.56, pass attempts 0.43;
  pull guard **0.66** (the most frequent completed action); completed takedowns, passes, sweeps, back takes and submissions
  each only **0.17-0.25**. Likely next step: back take → submission attempt/finish 45% directly; a back take ends in that
  athlete's submission **61%** of the time vs 6% being submitted; most other actions lead to a reset 64-73% of the time and to
  the athlete's own submission only 14-25%. When points were scored, the final margin was 1 to 5 points. Lamas L, Heiner M et
  al., "No-gi Brazilian jiu-jitsu: A Markovian analysis of elite-level combat dynamics", Int J Sports Sci & Coaching (2023),
  https://doi.org/10.1177/17479541231210979 . **Strong** (peer reviewed; one event, men only).
- IBJJF gi 2019 Worlds black belt (87 matches): 24% ended 0-0 on points; 45% ended 0-0 or 2-0 (so **in about half of elite
  matches at least one athlete scores zero points**; my reading, as 2-0 and 0-0 both mean a scoreless side). Riggs 2020. **Medium.**
- Guard pulls (gi, elite): 99 of 115 black belt matches at the 2018 Pans had at least one guard pull (16 double pulls); the
  puller's record 47-36 (57%). BJJ Heroes 2018 (via search summary). **Medium.** 2022 gi Worlds black belt: 227 completed guard
  pulls vs 28 takedowns (~8:1). bjjmore.com "10 BJJ statistics" (2026) (via search summary). **Weak.** 27% of takedown points at
  Worlds/Pans 2016-19 came from punishing a failed guard pull (BJJ Heroes "Takedowns and Throws in Jiu-Jitsu", 830 matches). **Medium.**
- 2012 Worlds: first points went to the guard puller (via a sweep) 46%, to the top athlete (pass/throw) 36%, after a double
  pull 18%; first points came inside minute 1 in 34% of matches. BishopBJJ 2012. **Weak.**
- White/blue gi (Williams 2019, 140 matches): 247 takedown attempts; guard pull = 50% of attempts (white 46%, blue 53%) with
  94% success; single-leg attempts blue 18% vs white 7%; double leg ended in side control 31% of the time, but in the
  opponent's guard 28%; 107 guard passes, 56 sweeps over 140 matches. **Medium.**
- **Not found:** guard-pull share or average points per match for IBJJF no-gi specifically. Best proxy: ADCC 2019
  (0.66 pulls per athlete per match → most matches feature a pull) and the IBJJF gi figures above.
