# D41_FRESH_EYES_THE_REAL_PROPS

---

### 1. First impression & what reads wrong
It *does* read as a small, gritty neighborhood gym—great vibe from "Back-Room Club" and the compact layout. **First thing that jumps wrong**: the ring is too visually dominant and almost fills the space; real small gyms tuck the ring into a corner, not center-stage. Also the floor zones are sharp block colors—real gyms have worn, faded mats, not clean solid red/green/blue rectangles.

### 2. Scale checks
- ✅ Heavy bag, speed bag, fighters: good proportion
- ⚠️ **Dumbbell rack**: looks slightly narrow/shallow, as if it holds only 4–5 weights per side—real compact racks are denser and a touch wider
- ⚠️ **Stationary bike**: seat + handlebars feel low relative to fighter height; frame is a bit slender
- ✅ Locker, grappling dummy: spot on

### 3. Placement oddities
- **Ring entrance**: no walkway/clear space—fighters step directly from mat to ring apron; real gyms leave 1–1.5m open around ring sides
- **Dumbbell rack**: too close to the heavy bag station; swinging a bag would hit someone lifting there
- **Cardio bike**: parked right where foot traffic passes to the ring; high-traffic bottleneck
- No wall mounting for bags—they hang from thin air; bags should be against walls or sturdy posts

### 4. The ONE missing thing
**Walls lined with mirrors + a worn, taped-up, scuffed floor surface.**

Mirrors define every boxing gym—they go on the wall behind the bags and along one side of the ring. The floor is the biggest giveaway: replace clean blocks with patched, taped, faded mats and it instantly feels lived-in and real.
