G91 - FROM FIGHTER TO COACH

FightCamp research, 2026-09-24. No code. Existing staff fields (G90): technical_teaching, tactical_rating, youth_development, capacity. Real-world coach rank is pupils' year, not a hidden Elo.

1. The path

Share who coach — no census. Athletic commissions, USA Boxing, England Boxing, the BBB of C, and UFC Athlete Health do not publish “% of retired pros now coaching.” PFA-style union transition reports do not exist for boxing or MMA. California’s boxer pension pays ~6% of eligible claims and averages ~$17,000 lump sum; it does not track occupation.⁠LA Times

What exists is qualitative and small-n:

MMA Junkie 2014 oral history of a retiring generation: gym owner is “by far the most common occupation” among that cohort. John McCarthy: coaches told them the next step was opening a gym. Rivera: “Quarterbacks become coaches. Fighters become trainers.” Same piece: a UFC name does not guarantee a gym; the real competitor is “John’s Taekwondo down the street.”⁠Mmajunkie.usatoday
Fighters Only: coaching / gym ownership is “the most obvious career path” from local schools to Elevation / Easton (Nate Marquardt).⁠Fightersonly
Polish pro MMA survey (n=88) + 15 interviews (2025): dual careers and planned retirement, no published % who coach.⁠Tandfonline
Indigenous Australian elite boxers (n=14): post-sport jobs clustered on physical capital — personal trainers, youth workers, labour; two unemployed. Not a population rate.⁠Pdfs.semanticscholar
Stellenbosch Life After Sport (mixed sports, 580 respondents): of 282 who wanted a sports-related career, 152 named coaching (then officiating 74, admin 56). Not combat-specific.⁠Sport.sun
Individual-sport retirees plan less and leave with less money than team-sport athletes. Boxing/MMA sit on the worse side of that split.⁠Mdpi

Engine read: treat “goes into coaching” as common-but-not-default among named retirees, rare among the invisible four-round undercard. Invent a band; do not fake a commission statistic.

Certification (amateur / club vs pro corner are different worlds).

USA Boxing pathway: Green (free online, 100% quiz) → Bronze ($100 clinic) → Silver ($150, 6 months after Bronze; recertify every 3 years) → Gold ($1,000, application, max 8 per clinic, 1 year after Silver, Colorado Springs).⁠Usaboxingeducation
England Boxing: L1 Assistant (17+, four days; licence needs DBS, safeguarding, first aid, Tier 3 membership; lasts 3 years) → L2 Full Coach (L1 held ≥1 year; from Jun 2025 every competing club must list a licensed L2 as Head Coach) → L3 Senior (23+, L2 held ≥5 years, high-performance year).⁠Englandboxing
BJJ: culture, not a state licence. Black belt is the usual bar to run an academy. IBJJF: head professor does not need the paper certificate, but must be a non-provisional black belt; promoting someone to black requires a professor with ≥2 certified degrees. Black belt ≠ teaching skill (Gracie ICP exists because of that gap).⁠Help.ibjjf
Muay Thai kru: traditional title from a teacher, not a ministry exam. Kru Muaythai Association (Ministry of Culture, 2003): Khan 11 Assistant Kru / Khan 12 Teacher, ~10-day Bangkok courses, ≥2 years training for assistant. Parallel to, not a replacement for, gym lineage.⁠Krumuaythai
Pro boxing / MMA corner: athletic commissions license seconds, not “head coaches.” No national coaching diploma is required to take 10% of a purse. That is why Roach, Dundee, Jackson could exist without L3.
2. Does a good fighter make a good coach?

Short answer: no reliable link. Playing pedigree is a door-opener, not a skill.

Combat evidence (cases, not a regression)

Hall of Fame trainers who were not notable pros:

Cus D’Amato — one amateur fight, lost; peek-a-boo + Patterson / Torres / Tyson.⁠Boxrec
Ray Arcel, Emanuel Steward, Angelo Dundee, Teddy Atlas — same pattern (Boxing Social).
Freddie Roach — 40–13 lightweight, never a world title; 40 world champions as a trainer; seven BWAA Trainer of the Year. Apprenticed under Futch.⁠Wikipedia
Firas Zahabi — amateur Muay Thai / grappling titles, no notable pro MMA run; GSP’s lead coach post-Serra; owns Tristar. Started teaching as a blue belt when the owner got busy.⁠Wikipedia
Greg Jackson — invented Gaidojutsu in 1992, gym to MMA in 2000; not a notable pro fighter. Jones, Holm, Evans, Condit. World MMA Awards coach 2009–11. Partner Mike Winkeljohn was a kickboxing champ (25–7–2) — the striking half of the brand.⁠Wikipedia

Britain snapshot (Boxing Social, 2019): 12 of BoxRec’s then-top-20 British P4P fighters were trained by people who never boxed professionally (Campbell, BJS, Khan, Taylor, Callum Smith, Fury…). Gary Logan (33–8–1 as a fighter): “It doesn’t really hold the argument that you need to have boxed professionally to be a pro coach.”⁠Boxing-social

Champions who did become useful coaches exist (Khabib, Serra, Couture, Faber, Mike Brown, later-career GSP as a visitor). They are visible because the name is the story. Failures are quieter: name-brand gyms that cannot teach, or champions who cannot explain what they did by feel.

Transfer from football / basketball (the only quantified literature)
IZA DP 15024 (football HCs who were ex-pros): prior assistant or youth-coach time predicts HC success; playing career does not. Too long in the wrong staff job turns negative.⁠Docs.iza
Cannon Stats / similar PL scrapes: ~70% of current top-league coaches have zero senior caps; ~40% never played a top-5 league. Elite-player → elite-coach is the exception (Zidane, Alonso), not the base rate.⁠Cannonstats
Schleu et al., Journal of Management Scientific Reports: 329 NBA head coaches, 1,800+ seasons — weak link from playing achievement to coaching results; the gap does not widen with tenure.⁠Sportico
LinkedIn “Boots Off, Blazers On”: legendary-player managers score worse on average than the field; slightly more F-tier disasters.⁠LinkedIn

How far it transfers to combat: far. Combat coaching is explanation, camp design, pad/clinch grammar, and corner decisions. Football’s finding (apprenticeship beats pedigree) matches Roach-under-Futch and Zahabi-teaching-as-blue-belt.

3. Money and pull

Purse cut (pro corner). Industry handshake, not a commission cap:

Head trainer on a full camp: 10% of purse is the phrase everyone uses. Range 10–20% if the trainer also manages. Corner-only / late hire: ~5% or a flat fee. Cutman ~2%. Manager 15–33% off the top (often contracted; trainer usually is not).⁠Financial-advisors-for-athletes
Roach / Steward / Goossen (LA Times 2005): no contract, handshake, fighters skip town, big purses get capped or flattened (“If the purse is $8 million, you are not going to get 10%”).⁠LA Times
Derrick James note: 10% on a $500 four-rounder is $50 for six weeks; the same % on seven figures is the fight. Long-time trainers get squeezed when the horse finally pays.⁠Snac

Session / salary. No combat-wide wage survey. Anchors only:

Wild Card (Roach): $5/day or $50/month for decades; gym “breaks even” on walk-ins, not on the 10%. After Pacquiao–De La Hoya he signed 300+ new members in a few months.⁠LA Times
Modern boutique (BOXR Miami, ex-amateur champ owner): ~4,000 members, $220/month base. Name + facility, not a 10% corner.⁠Forbes
US “boxing coach” job-board band often quoted ~$35k–$80k W-2. That is school/rec, not a Pacquiao cut. NOT FOUND as a combat-pro mean.

Name-value pull. Evidenced as a spike, not a permanent multiplier. Wild Card’s 300 sign-ups is the only hard figure found. Counter-evidence is McCarthy: name fills the first month; John’s TKD wins the third if the classes are bad. Forrest Griffin (post-UFC ops): fighters know the A-side of a gym (how to teach a combo) and not the B-side (how to get bodies in the door).

Engine read: name → short membership/recruit spike + higher ask on the 10%. Teaching stats → retention and pupil results. Do not let reputation write technical_teaching.
