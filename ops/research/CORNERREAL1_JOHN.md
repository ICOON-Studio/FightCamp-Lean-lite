# CORNERREAL1 - John - what a boxing corner really tells its boxer between rounds (research, sources with links)

We are a boxing gym management game. Between rounds the player's corner gives the boxer one instruction. We need the
real-world baseline: what a competent amateur or professional corner actually does in the 60 seconds, so that the
game's own coach (when the player does not give the instruction himself) behaves like a real one.

Answer each with sources (coaching manuals, federation coach education, interviews with named trainers, studies):
1. How many instructions does a good corner give in one break? (the "one or two things" rule: who says it, where)
2. What decides the instruction: the opponent's habit seen in the last round, the boxer's own tiredness, the score,
   damage? Give the order of priority real cornermen describe, with quotes.
3. Typical instructions against each opponent habit, as real trainers word them: a pressure fighter who walks in; a
   mover who boxes at range; a counter-puncher who waits; a man who loads up one big shot; a body puncher; a slow
   starter; a switch-hitter. One or two lines each, with who said it.
4. When does a corner tell a tired boxer to rest for a round (box and move, hold, "take the round off") and what does
   it cost on the cards? Any numbers on work rate dropping after a rest instruction.
5. How often is the corner's read of the opponent WRONG, or the boxer does not carry the instruction out? Any study
   or coach estimate of compliance (how much of the instruction is done in the next round).
6. Does the quality of the cornerman measurably change results? Any numbers (studies on coaching in combat sports,
   corner changes mid-career, judges' or analysts' estimates).

Give real-world numbers only, each with its source. Where no number exists, say so plainly; do not average or guess.
End with a table: opponent habit | the instruction | source.
