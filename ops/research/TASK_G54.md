# TASK G54 — GROK — WHAT IS DEFENCE, MEASURABLY?

Research only: no code, no module, no test, no repository access. Answer as
plain chat text. Expert tier.

## Why this exists

The operator has asked for defensive mechanics — dodge, kite, sidestep. Before
a line is written we need to know **which of those a real dataset can see**,
because we have been here twice already and both times the answer changed the
stage:

- **G50 refused "pressure" outright.** It is a narrator's word; no commission or
  stats provider measures it, "octagon control" is a broadcast phrase and not a
  column, and over ~7,300 scored rounds movement pressure added essentially
  nothing once punch features were controlled for. **Kiting is the mirror image
  of pressure.** If it is the same unmeasurable thing wearing the other hat, say
  so and this half of the stage becomes explicitly AUTHORED game feel rather
  than something we pretend is validated.
- **G53 replaced a guessed constant with a measured one and reversed a finding
  we were about to publish.** Asking first is cheaper than shipping a guess.

Our engine today: the defender has **no agency at all**. A blow is thrown, and a
defence roll downgrades it one rung on our wear ladder — clean becomes
"absorbed", glancing becomes "slipped". The defender never chooses anything. Our
footwork picks CLOSE / HOLD / RETREAT / CIRCLE from his own preferred range,
his own stamina and whether his back is to the ropes, and **it never once reads
what the other man is doing.**

## The questions

1. **IS DEFENCE COUNTED AT ALL?** Does any official system — UFC Stats /
   FightMetric, CompuBox, an Olympic boxing scoring system, a commission —
   record a defensive ACTION? A slip, a block, a parry, a shoulder roll, a
   check? Or is the only thing recorded what happened to the incoming strike?
   **Name the columns that actually exist.**

2. **"STRIKING DEFENCE %" — IS IT A SKILL OR AN ARTEFACT?** UFC Stats publishes
   it as one minus the opponent's significant-strike accuracy. Is there evidence
   it is a stable property of a FIGHTER, or is it mostly a property of who he
   fought and what they threw? If it is the latter, say so plainly — we would be
   modelling an artefact.

3. **CAN A SLIP BE TOLD FROM A BLOCK?** G51 already established that a miss is
   ONE BUCKET in both official systems. Is that still true in any research-grade
   notational dataset — does anyone code avoided-versus-blocked-versus-parried —
   or is the distinction purely a coaching one? Boxing and MMA separately.

4. **FOOTWORK: IS MOVEMENT MEASURED?** Distance covered in a round, direction
   changes, time on the back foot, time circling versus squared up. Is there a
   tracking dataset, a time-motion study, or a broadcast tracking product that
   publishes any of it? **This is the question most likely to be a G50 repeat —
   if the honest answer is that the only movement figures published are the ones
   G50 already found worthless for scoring, that IS the answer.** But be
   precise: G50 asked whether movement predicts SCORE. This asks whether
   movement is measured at all, which is a different question, and a quantity
   can be well measured and still not predict a score.

5. **DOES DEFENCE COST ANYTHING, MEASURABLY?** Is there a published energetic
   cost to slipping, blocking or moving — anything that would make a defensive
   fighter tire differently from an aggressive one? Our engine already spends
   stamina on movement and on throwing.

6. **STYLE AND OUTCOME.** Is there a measured relationship between a defensive
   style (out-boxer, back-foot, high-guard) and winning, or between it and
   fight length? Distinguish research from received wisdom — "styles make
   fights" is a saying, not a finding.

7. **THE ONE CHECK.** If we build a defensive read and want to grade it against
   ONE published quantity, which is it? Name the quantity, its source, its
   denominator, and the band we should expect to land in. **If no such quantity
   exists, say that** — we would far rather know the number does not exist than
   have one approximated for us.
