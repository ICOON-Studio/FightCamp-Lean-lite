# The playbook as the steering wheel - an outside proposal the owner brought in (S425)

The owner pasted this (written by another AI for "a boxing management game, AI vs AI") and asked "what do you think of
this". Condensed faithfully below, then Claude's read against what FightCamp already has and has measured.

## The proposal
1. FIGHT PLAN DASHBOARD: three sliders set before the fight and between rounds, which interact: distance (back-pedal /
   hold centre / cut off the ring), pace (counter / rhythm / high volume, volume drains stamina), target (head / balanced /
   body).
2. CONDITIONAL RULES ("if-then"): the player writes rules the boxer runs when a trigger happens. Examples: opponent's
   stamina under 40% -> swarm the body; own torso hurt over 60% -> high guard and clinch; opponent throws 3+ jabs in a
   round -> slip right more and answer with the rear cross; rocked -> keep distance, double jab, clinch when close;
   opponent's stamina under half -> combinations go from 2 punches to 4.
3. THE CORNER: between rounds the coach shows 3 key stats and the player changes the rules; in the round, a limited
   "corner focus" lets him shout a call a few times that overrides the plan for 15 seconds.
4. FORGED IN THE GYM: sparring sessions against stand-ins for the next opponent; after each, a punch map and a stamina
   chart that send him back to the playbook; a boxer's ring IQ caps how many rules he can hold (2 for a novice, 6 for a
   veteran).
5. SEEN WORKING: when a rule fires, a plate on screen says so ("TRAP LANDED: PULL-COUNTER"); the cornerman's voice
   follows the plan.

## Claude's read
ALREADY IN THE GAME, in some form: a plan of answers to situations (worth about 21 wins in 100: 18.7% with no plan, 40.0%
with a good one); a corner call every 30 seconds; the corner minute with one change; pace and aim on the corner bar;
sparring nights as tests of the plan; a hired partner who boxes like the opponent; the NOW! plate in the live fight.

WORTH TAKING (new, and it answers what our critics keep finding):
- Rules on the boxer's STATE (tired, hurt, ahead or behind on the cards, the round), beside today's answers to the
  opponent's situations. One more kind of line on the same plan, not a second system.
- A rule must be DRILLED to fire: pad nights and sparring raise how reliably it fires, from usable to sharp. This gives
  pad time a job all camp (measured hole: sharper than usable is worth 1 point today) and makes the coach's evenings
  matter.
- Rule places grow with ring IQ and the coach: the clearest REWARD for playing on that opens new choices (the critics'
  weakest score, 4 of 10).
- The plate when a rule fires, and The Why naming which rule fired and which failed: the player sees his week's work
  land. Anticipation with a pay-off.

HANDLE WITH CARE:
- Three sliders tend to have one best setting per opponent: "read the bigger number", the exact fault our critics kill.
  Keep them only if each setting has a case and a visible price (we already have pace and aim; do not add a third dial
  without measuring).
- Rules written once and never touched make the fight play itself (the known weakness of gambit systems). The answer is
  scarcity and wear: few places, rules that must be kept drilled, and opponents who study the rules you used (the film
  on you).
- "Clones of the next opponent" in sparring is not boxing: we have scouting with honest ranges and a hired partner in
  her style.
- Every number in it (+40% slip, 15 seconds, 2 to 6 rules) is invented: measure on 300 bouts before any is promised.
- A "corner focus" resource duplicates the shout clock we have.

## A corner-minute screen the owner brought in (docs/mockups/s425/ref_corner_from_owner.png)
Three columns: the last round (exchanges won, clean punches, energy at the bell for both), "choose two corner
adjustments" (a range plan, a body counter with punch-by-punch aim on a body figure, recover for 1 corner point: water or
ice), and "your read on the opponent" (if she does this -> Lucy answers like this, with the trigger chips "hook after
jab / opponent tires / Lucy hurt"). A 0:48 clock to the bell.
TAKE: the trigger -> answer picture with numbered punches on the body figure (the clearest way to show a plan line);
corner jobs paid from a small point budget; the read backed by counts (seen 3 times, 2 landed, 1 opening).
CHANGE: it is full of sentences (his own rule: a word, an icon, a number, a meter, a clock; the rest in tooltips); the
"Coach's call" names the right answer, which turns "choose two" into one click; an answer must be greyed out when it is
not drilled (measured: an answer she cannot do costs 2 to 14 wins in 100); small type.
