# Ideas wanted: Ring Switches, the order version - how would you balance it?

You are a game designer who knows boxing. We want your own ideas, ranked, each specific enough to build. This is not a
review of a proposal: below is the game as built today, what we measured, and what the owner asked for. Plain words, no
jargon, no reflex or timing mechanics (the owner rejected those).

The owner, after playing: "vs the jabber you win very easy , all your rear hands land easy , need better balance , i want
this slice to be perfectly balance to move on (always have in scope other arts that will be added and knees and elbows)".

## The game as built (boxing only today)
- One man against one man. Three rounds of six beats. Each beat BOTH MEN SET SWITCHES AT ONCE, then the beat is shown.
- LEAD HAND: Jab, Lead hook, Lead uppercut, Body hook, Feint, Push off - or hold it HIGH or LOW.
  REAR HAND: Cross, Overhand, Rear uppercut, Body cross - or hold it HIGH or LOW.
  FEET: step in, hold, back off. Or CLINCH (no punch lands, stamina back, not twice running).
- THE ORDER: a beat is three moments. The order you click your switches is the order you do them: your first action
  meets his first, your second his second. Feet take a moment too (step in and THEN hook; jab and THEN back off).
- A hand left off defends, by where it is held: lead high stops the jab and cross, lead low stops body punches, rear
  high stops the lead hook and overhand, rear low stops uppercuts. A hand that punches is out of its guard only in the
  moment it punches; before and after it blocks.
- Ranges Long, Mid, Inside. Jab: Long and Mid. Cross, body cross: Mid only. Lead hook, overhand, body hook: Mid and
  Inside. Uppercuts: Inside only. Every round starts at Long.
- In one moment the fast punch lands first (jab; then cross, hooks, body punches; then overhand, uppercuts). A big
  punch that lands stops his slower punch of that moment. After his punch misses or is blocked, your later punches that
  beat are counters (+1 damage).
- Numbers: health 22; damage jab 1, body punches 2 (and they shorten his stamina bar by 1), cross and lead hook 3,
  overhand and uppercuts 4. Stamina 8: jab 1, cross / hooks / body 2, overhand / uppercuts 3, a step 1; 1 comes back a
  beat, +1 for each hand held. At 0 health he is down (he gets up on 9, then 7; the third knockdown stops it). No
  stoppage: rounds on points (big punch landed 2, small 1, knockdown 3).
- Backing off at Long gives ground: two squares, then he is on the ropes and cannot back off until he steps in.
- A jab that lands keeps him out: he cannot step in next beat (a trial rule).

## What we measured
We now solve each beat as the guessing game it is (both men choose at once, so there is no best plan, only a best mix
of plans) and let two equal men who both play that mix box 56 bouts. This shows what the RULES are worth when nobody
makes a mistake.
- 75% of beats are fought at Long, 19% at Mid, 6% Inside.
- The jab is thrown in half of all beats; "jab, then back off" is 70% of best play at Long (it pays for itself: jab 1 +
  step 1, and 2 stamina come back). Back off is in 40% of all beats.
- Crosses and body crosses miss 85% of the time (thrown as he backs out of reach). No knockdowns in 56 bouts, health
  taken 12 of 22 a bout, a third of bouts are draws.
- The lead uppercut is never worth choosing; the lead hook in 1% of beats; the clinch is 25% of best play at Mid and
  Inside whenever the bout gets there.
- With the trial rule "a landed jab keeps him out" switched OFF: Long 57%, Mid 30%, Inside 13%; health taken 15; still
  no stoppages; back off in 45% of beats; body cross still misses 73%.
- Real boxing, for scale (our own research): the jab is 40-44% of punches thrown, 27-32% of punches land (CompuBox);
  stepping back is the winners' most used defence (23% of defensive actions, 38% with the step-back counter; IBA
  women's finals 2023); about 47% of modern pro bouts end by KO or TKO.

## What we want from you
1. YOUR RANKED LIST of rule or number changes (at most 6) so that a bout between two good players is fought at all three
   ranges, every punch has its moment, backing off is a choice with a price and not a habit, and bouts end both ways
   (some stoppages, few draws). For each: the rule in one sentence a player can read, the real-boxing reason, and what
   you expect it to change. Say which you would try FIRST.
2. THE WEAK SWITCHES: what would you do with the lead uppercut and the lead hook so each is the right click somewhere?
3. THE CLINCH: at a quarter of best play at close range it is a habit. What is its price?
4. OTHER ARTS LATER: knees, elbows, kicks and takedowns will be added to this same board (switches per limb, three
   moments, ranges, lines a held limb covers). What should we decide NOW so that boxing balanced today does not have to
   be re-balanced when they come - which ranges, which lines, which limb covers what?
5. One sentence: what would make you stop playing this after ten bouts?

## What would a human do?
A boxing coach would say: the out-boxer may jab and move, and the answer is cutting off the ring; inside, the short
punches and the clinch decide. A designer would say: every switch must be the right click somewhere.
