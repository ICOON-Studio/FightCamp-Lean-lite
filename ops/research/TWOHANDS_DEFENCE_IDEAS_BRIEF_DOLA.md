🥊 Five Defence Mechanics — No Timing, No Reflexes
All fit your existing turn/card structure, no hidden dice, and at least two generate defence cards dynamically.
Mechanic 1 — Stance & Position (Passive → Active Choices)
THE RULE
You always have 3 stance options: High Guard, Low Guard, Out of Range. Switching costs 1 Stamina, done once at the start of your turn or when it's your turn to defend.
Your current stance automatically blocks/weakens matching targets: High Guard = head/face shots; Low Guard = body/ribs; Out of Range = only straight punches miss completely.
Any shot your stance doesn't cover — you may spend 1 Stamina to turn a matching available hand into a Parry/Slip card for this one punch only.
You start each round with 2 Parry/Slip slots — no deck, no random draw.
EXCHANGE: Opponent leads Jab → Cross → Lead Hook
Your setup: You start in High Guard, 5 Stamina.
Jab (head straight): High Guard blocks it clean — no cost, no action.
Cross (head straight): High Guard blocks again — easy.
Lead Hook (head side): High Guard doesn't cover side head. You spend 1 Stamina, use your idle lead hand → generate Slip (side) card. Slip works — hook misses, you keep your stance.
THE DECISION
Switch stance early to cover what your opponent usually throws — but if they mix levels, you're exposed. Save Stamina for reactive slips/parries only on the hardest or most damaging shots.
Choose Stance when opponent is predictable; Spend Stamina to Slip when they mix levels or throw something dangerous.
HOW IT CAN BREAK
Lazy: Sit in one stance forever, only parrying exceptions.
Cap: Stance only reduces damage by 70% if the punch is overpowering (2+ combo moves behind it) — perfect blocking everything forever doesn't work; you still need slips.
REAL BOXING
"The guard is your first wall — footwork and stance position stop most punches before you ever need to move."
Mechanic 2 — Reaction Cards (Generated by What YOU Do — ✅ Dynamic Defence Cards)
THE RULE
Defence cards do NOT live in your deck. You earn/generate them during your own turns based on what you do:
✅ Feint → generates 1 Slip card next round
✅ Hold a hand back without attacking → generates 1 Block card with that hand next round
✅ Step back (end your turn early, lose 1 potential combo move) → generates 1 Step Back card next round
Max 2 defence cards per hand at once; they expire at the end of the round — don't hoard them.
When a punch comes in, play one matching card from what you've earned this round. Cost: 0 Stamina — you already paid by giving up offence to get it.
EXCHANGE: Opponent leads Jab → Cross → Lead Hook
Your setup: On your previous turn, you feinted once and held your rear hand back. Now you hold: Slip + Rear Hand Block.
Jab (lead hand, head straight): Play Slip — you shift your head, jab misses.
Cross (rear hand, head straight): Play Rear Hand Block — forearm catches it, no damage, arm recoils slightly.
Lead Hook (side head): No card left — you take it on the gloves (light damage) or eat the shot (full damage). You chose defence on the first two, not the third.
THE DECISION
Every defence card costs offence now for safety later. Feinting or holding back means fewer punches you throw — but more options when it's your opponent's turn.
Hold back when you expect a hard counter; go all-out only when you want to finish the round fast and accept taking some return fire.
HOW IT CAN BREAK
Lazy: Feint every single turn to max out defence cards, never attack.
Cap: Defence cards from feints/holding back cap at 2 per round total — you cannot stockpile a full hand of free defences and never punch.
REAL BOXING
"You don't get to defend well by accident — you create your openings and your safety by what you choose not to throw."
Mechanic 3 — Read & Respond (Predict → Match)
THE RULE
When your opponent starts their combination, you see all three moves in order immediately — full information, no guessing.
You pick ONE defensive pattern for the whole sequence:
Cover Up: Reduce all damage by 50%; costs nothing — safe but passive.
Pick One: Choose exactly ONE punch in their sequence to fully avoid — the rest hit. Costs 1 Stamina.
Pick Two: Fully avoid two punches — the third hits clean. Costs 2 Stamina.
You pick once before the exchange starts — you don't decide punch-by-punch.
EXCHANGE: Opponent leads Jab → Cross → Lead Hook
Your setup: You see all three clearly. You have 3 Stamina left.
You choose Pick Two — avoid Jab and Cross, take the Hook. Cost = 2 Stamina.
Jab: Avoided — you lean back just enough.
Cross: Avoided — slight shoulder turn, it grazes past.
Lead Hook: You didn't pick this one — it lands on your cheek. Damage happens, but you saved yourself from the first two.
THE DECISION
How good is their last punch vs their first two? Is their third a power shot you have to eat, or their opening jabs that don't hurt much?
Cover Up when low on Stamina or their shots are all weak; Pick Two only when their combo is deadly and you have the energy.
HOW IT CAN BREAK
Lazy: Always Pick Two — dodge the two hardest shots every time.
Cap: Stamina cost means you can't also throw hard on your turn — every dodge now is less power/volume when you lead. It's a trade-off, not a cheat code.
REAL BOXING
"Read the combination — know which shots you can roll with and which ones you have to stop. You can't catch everything."
Mechanic 4 — Commitment Gauge (Defence from What YOU Gave Ground — ✅ Dynamic Defence Cards)
THE RULE
You have a Commitment Gauge that fills when you don't push forward:
End your turn without using all 3 possible moves → +1 Charge
Don't attack for a full turn → +2 Charges
Every charge = 1 Defence Token that converts into a card on demand:
1 Charge → Block (head or body, your choice when used)
2 Charges → Slip/Counter — avoid AND land a quick counter-punch
Max 3 Charges stored. Using a defence card resets that charge. No other cost — you paid ground instead of Stamina.
EXCHANGE: Opponent leads Jab → Cross → Lead Hook
Your setup: You ended your last turn after 1 light punch instead of 3 — you have 2 Charges available.
Jab: Spend 1 Charge → generate Head Block instantly — catches it clean. Charges left = 1.
Cross: Spend 1 Charge → generate Head Block again — takes it on the glove. Charges left = 0.
Lead Hook: No charges left — you can't slip, can't block perfectly. You take the hook to the shoulder (partial damage) — you gave ground to stop the first two, and that ran out.
THE DECISION
Push forward = you threaten them but have NO extra defences. Hold back/shorten your combos = you build a safety buffer that only activates when you need it.
How far do you let them come before you have the safety to stop their best shots?
HOW IT CAN BREAK
Lazy: Never throw more than one punch ever — just farm charges and block everything.
Cap: If you end three consecutive turns without throwing a meaningful attack, your opponent gains a Pressure Bonus — their shots do +30% damage and break through 1 block per round. You must threaten them occasionally.
REAL BOXING
"Give ground to set your feet — don't crowd yourself trying to hit and defend at the same time."
Mechanic 5 — Hand Assignment (Your Hands = Your Tools)
THE RULE
You have two hands — each can be assigned to ONE job at the start of the opponent's turn:
🛡️ Defend Head — blocks head shots from that side
🛡️ Defend Body — blocks body shots from that side
👊 Ready to Parry — if a punch comes down that hand's line, you avoid it and get a free counter-punch on your next turn
⚡ Leave Open — that hand does nothing; you take whatever comes
Assignments cost nothing to set — but once the opponent starts throwing, your hands are committed where you put them.
EXCHANGE: Opponent leads Jab → Cross → Lead Hook
Your setup: You assign: Lead Hand = Defend Head, Rear Hand = Ready to Parry.
Jab (lead hand, head): Your lead hand is there — blocks it clean.
Cross (rear hand, head): Your rear hand was set to Parry — you catch the timing perfectly, cross misses, you note: Counter available next turn.
Lead Hook (side head): Neither hand covers this angle — you take it on the gloves (reduced damage) because you chose coverage over wide movement.
THE DECISION
Which hand do they use most? Head or body? Do you want to risk a parry for that counter — or just be safe?
Double-cover where they're strongest; parry where you want to punish them for repeating the same shot.
HOW IT CAN BREAK
Lazy: Always put both hands on Head — everyone throws jabs and crosses, right?
Cap: Body punches exist and hurt longer (permanent stamina loss that round) — ignoring body coverage means even if you never get knocked out, you'll gas out in round 3.
REAL BOXING
"Your hands are your shield — position them where the punches are coming, and leave openings only where you're willing to pay the price."
🏆 Which ONE I'd Build First & Why
Mechanic 2 — Reaction Cards
It matches exactly what your owner hinted at — defence comes from your own choices, not a separate deck or reflex test. It creates the cleanest boxing logic: every defensive option costs you offence first, which naturally creates back-and-forth rhythm — aggressive players get hit more; cautious players get fewer counters. It's also the easiest to teach: "Feint → Slip, Hold Hand → Block, Step Back → Step Back." No new systems to learn on top of your existing punch cards — defence is just the consequence of what you choose not to do. It also stays true to your design goals: fast, predictable, every result visible before you commit.
