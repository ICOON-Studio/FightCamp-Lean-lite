# Three coaching moments · design review, round two
SCORE depth=8 general=7 play=7

**Depth — 8/10.** The best decision is at the bell. If a drilled exit got your boxer off the ropes twice but the last exchange was lost, keeping that line can be better than swapping it. If the exit never got off, the same scorecard might lead to a different next round. Booking also has a real price: the date that fits the goal can be the week two boxers need the same film night. Signing the opponent can cost your *current* boxer loyalty as well as the gym a place. Two weak spots: declining a forbidden bout isn't a trade, and a free trial that extends the deadline can beat signing almost every time.

**General design — 7/10.** One read on both sides of the Open button; one place to make a call; one boxer in the world after a signing. These are good rules. The read belongs to a gym, not a betting desk. But a move allowed on a plan is not necessarily ready on fight night, a won exchange isn't proof of an escape, and the day written on the visit is not quite the last day the current code lets you sign. Those differences matter more than the colour of the arrows. Meets the 7/7 design bar, not a build or balance sign-off.

**Play — 7/10.** Hearing the opponent describe the very exchange you watched is the reward. It should still be worth opening the result after a loss, a skip or a quiet month. At 40 reputation the proposal gives 12% **after** all the gates: about one ask per eight qualifying wins on average, not per eight bouts. A month with none tells us almost nothing about the roll. The reliable reason to come back on Monday should be to see whether the exit you chose to drill is any nearer to working. Two rounds of trails and a running grade for the coach won't supply that reason.

## What the options buy — and what is missing

| Moment | Keep or add | What changes; when not to take it |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed amateur offer | Accept / Pass; open the week and return to this offer | Accept books one date, not a win or a place. Pass gives up the date; show the actual loyalty price (normally −3 when fit and free; zero for a mismatch). Looking at the week changes nothing until a session is edited. Show the other camp's film clash here, not just the weight estimate. |
| Later date | After passing, ask at the next legal show | A live legal offer closes the amateur ask. Passing first gives up a certain opponent for an uncertain answer. Preview that cost; don't sell “+7 days” as a postponement button. First bout still comes from the sheet. |
| Pro offer / debut | On a fixed offer, compare payout **to the gym**, place, rest and duck cost; keep the paid scout. At debut, keep the one house ask, sign or stay amateur | Scouting spends $250 for information, not better odds in the ring. A tune-up can pay the bill but do less for the road. At debut, a later date can cost purse money; show the actual house answer. No fixed opponent, no matchup grade. No amateur scout before acceptance. |
| Live exchange | See last settled clue / clean view; use the existing shout bar or say nothing | The view changes no result. A heard tie-up can recover energy but risks shots in the clinch and uses a shout. Silence adds no shout; the standing call can still apply. Neither replays the last exchange. |
| Bell | Keep / replace / add if there is room | Keep spends no change. Replacing trades away the old answer **at that place**; adding covers an uncovered place actually visited, within the line limit. Don't bury other legal calls, recovery or medical decisions behind the suggested exit. |
| Result, with or without a visit | Open the deciding exchange; **work on this step** in the existing study slot | Reading changes nothing. Confirming the drill replaces the one step being studied; pads are needed for progress. Show what is displaced. If already usable and no further gain is supported, link to the plan instead. Don't auto-edit next week. |
| Reception | Sign with one focus / trial / pass / leave it open | Sign consumes a shared place and the shown stipend; preview same-class loyalty before committing. Focus changes mastery, a habit, or potential; the first goal is assigned. Trial should buy one missing fact at a dated appointment, not a free extension on demand. Pass keeps cash and place but ends this visit and starts the proposed 120-day wait. Leaving keeps no place or promise past the date. |

**Cut or fold away:** don't put “brief/full” beside an expander that already reveals the same reasons. Keep the odds band; replace a second three-mark confidence score with what is actually known or missing. Drop a single “correct out of 15” across weight, goal and rank: an easy weight prediction shouldn't make bad rank advice look good. Keep the individual forecast beside its result instead. Put earlier attempts in a round-labelled strip on request, not as paths across today's ring. No buy, recruit-from-the-crowd, reroll, or automatic return button.

## To reach 10/10

1. **Make one exchange the witness for all three moments.** At selection save the requested line, selected move and why they differ. At settlement append the actual call, whether heard, fit, start/end card, fired/held/didn't fire, win/loss, round and exchange. Define pressure for this visit as starting on your ropes; count a planned escape only when the fitted line fired, the exchange was won and the end card is off the ropes. Count repeats by line ID on distinct exchanges. For a call require a heard, fitted call and a recorded escape; say “after the call”, not “because of the call”. Keep up to two matching exchange references and one Why clue. Verify watched and skipped records with the same inputs and seed; overlay off must produce the same result. On old saves say “no recorded clue”.
2. **Make the read change a booking, not just describe it.** Use the booking rule first. Then compare this date's rest, scale estimate and shared film nights against the next *available* show under the same week assumptions. Label the other date “not offered”. Use actual goal deadline, method-specific rank scenario and take-home share; show “unknown” where inputs are missing. Snapshot the assumptions on Accept. At result pair forecast with actual and explain changes (different opponent, changed week, different decision). Test blocked novice, legal but short rest, higher rank with no place gained, two overlapping camps, pro purse versus progress and debut terms without an opponent.
3. **Move the boxer, not the name.** Give a regional boxer a persistent ID on offer, result, visit and future fixtures. At sign, re-read today's world record, bouts, medical restriction, place count across both sports and expiry. In one operation move the boxer to the roster, retire the active rival row and invalidate outstanding offers for that rival; retain past results. Show the actual loyalty and weekly cost before committing. Duplicate press, save/load, a filled last place, a second bout while visiting, or an unlinked old visitor must never create an extra boxer. Don't roll an ask for an unresolvable rival.
4. **Give the trial a price in time, not a made-up fee.** Offer the next free Monday once if it falls within the visit and the boxer is medically cleared; use a non-contact assessment if rest forbids sparring. Spend that one appointment (another trial cannot use it); reveal one previously ranged ability. If nothing can be learned, omit Trial. Sign now starts this week's focus; waiting starts nothing and holds no place. After trial give one day to decide. Check expiry after the clock advances **and** on sign. Test sign on the final day, rejected sign the day after, a non-blocking warning before a jump across it, and no new ask from that rival until 120 days after pass/expiry.
5. **Put the decision at the next opening.** Show the last settled place and move, with words for held, unavailable, attempted/lost and attempted/won. If the next call is open, highlight a legal choice in the existing bar with its signed energy change; always leave silence. At the bell show the specific line surrendered or slot used. On Why link to the one study slot and preview what leaves it. Test a retreating winner, an ignored shout, hurt *and* tired, a skipped bout, and a successful call followed by no escape. The last case earns no escape quote.
6. **Measure the quiet weeks.** Replay a month to check dates, weekly costs and the no-visitor journey; then run many seeded seasons and count amateur bouts → eligible wins → free reception/place → visits → decisions. Compare a weak rematch loop with challenging bouts at equal reputation. Repeated qualifying wins still buy extra rolls even with a capped per-bout chance; if rematches dominate, start the per-rival wait on a qualifying roll, not just a pass. Play the same month as a cash-poor gym and as a solvent gym with one place left. Ask which offered bout, call and signing each would turn down, and why, before tuning the numbers.
