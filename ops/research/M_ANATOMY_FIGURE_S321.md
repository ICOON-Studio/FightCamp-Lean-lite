# Maria - art direction: the boxer's anatomy figure on the fight side panel (S321)

FightCamp is a PC boxing gym-management sim (Steam, $19.99). During a 3D fight night, each boxer has a figure on her own
screen edge (about 260 px wide x 520 px tall at 1080p) showing where punches land. The operator now wants it to look like a
high-quality, movie-like realistic human: muscles (and maybe skeleton) visible, blood that RUNS from an open cut, a swelling
that grows, and between rounds the player taps the figure to choose where the cutman works (close a cut, enswell an eye)
and where the second puts ice (neck = calms her, or a swelling). Earlier attempts were judged "not good enough" by him:
your own M44 pick (flat sports-science muscle map) and Kalli's K3 anatomy photo figure.
His quality reference: a premium female ecorche anatomy model (realistic skinless muscle figure, film-quality render).

Answer in full:
1. Which LOOK reads best at 260x520 px over a dark 3D fight broadcast, and still feels film-quality? Compare at least:
   a) half skin / half ecorche, b) full ecorche with her own face kept, c) TV-broadcast x-ray (glass skin, glowing muscles +
   skeleton), d) your M44 flat muscle map done at a higher finish, e) anything better you know from real broadcasts/games.
   Rank them and say why, with what fails at small size.
2. How should running blood, swelling, ice and the hit glow be drawn on that look so they read in 1 second at 1x speed?
   (colour, motion speed, size, what NOT to animate).
3. Each boxer has a different build and skin tone (women and men, flyweight to heavyweight). How do we keep the figure hers?
4. Production: we cannot sculpt. Options: buy a royalty-free ecorche model ($35-90) and render it per build, generate
   images, or draw 2D. Which gives the best quality per hour, and what exact render setup (camera, lens, light, background)?
Give your recommendation first, then the reasons. Real references welcome (name the broadcast/app/game, never copy).
