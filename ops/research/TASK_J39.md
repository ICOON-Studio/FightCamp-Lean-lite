## The question

**When a real instructor demonstrates a grappling technique WITHOUT a partner,
what do they actually do, and which techniques do they refuse to show that way?**

This is a documented practice, not a hypothetical - solo drilling is a whole
genre, and it exploded as a published form during the 2020-21 gym closures.

1. **THE CONVENTIONS.** What are the established solo-drill conventions, by
   name, with sources? Shrimping / hip escape, bridging, technical stand-up,
   granby roll, sprawls, penetration-step shots, breakfalls, solo guard
   retention drills. For each: is it a recognised solo drill in its own right,
   or a paired technique being mimed?

2. **WHAT SOLO DRILLING IS DOCUMENTED TO TEACH, AND WHAT IT IS NOT.** Is there
   any study or coaching literature on what a learner takes from a solo
   demonstration versus a paired one? A number here would be gold; **NOT FOUND
   is a real and valuable answer** and J37's NOT FOUND is exactly why this
   project does not gate on invented constants.

3. **THE REFUSAL LIST.** Which grappling techniques do published instructors
   NOT demonstrate solo, and do any of them say why? An armbar from guard, a
   kimura, a collar choke - the technique IS the contact. Name the categories
   and give the sources.

4. **THE CAMERA.** When solo drills ARE filmed for instruction, what is the
   convention - angle, distance, tempo, whether the implied partner is marked
   by a prop, a floor mark, or nothing? We need this to write generation
   prompts and we would rather copy a practice than invent one.

5. **THE ONE LINE.** Of the four grappling arts in the catalogue - judo,
   BJJ, wrestling, sambo - **rank them by how much of their syllabus survives a
   solo demonstration**, and say what the ranking is based on.

## THE TRAP

**Do not rank by what is easy to film.** Rank by what a practitioner would
recognise as the named technique. A solo armbar might film beautifully and
teach nothing.

**And do not answer from first principles.** Anyone can reason that an armbar
needs a partner. What is worth your pass is what instructors, federations and
coaching literature ACTUALLY DO - with names, sources and, where they exist,
numbers.
