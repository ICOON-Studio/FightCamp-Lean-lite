2. FULL INTEGRATION SOLUTION FOR APPROVED STATS

We are adding zero redundant core simulation attributes. The existing six boxing simulation attributes (power, technique, speed, defense, conditioning, fight_iq) remain the authoritative foundation.

To capture the physiological realities exposed by "HP Recovery" and "SP Recovery" without adopting arcade tropes, we introduce two derived physiological metrics:

aerobic_recovery (Player-facing profile readout: RECOVERY RATE)
tissue_recovery (Player-facing medical readout: CUT & SWELL RESISTANCE)
EXISTING ENGINE FOUNDATION:
  Sim Attributes (6):  [power] [technique] [speed] [defense] [conditioning] [fight_iq]
                                │                           │
                                ▼                           ▼
DERIVED READOUT ADDITIONS:  [tissue_recovery]         [aerobic_recovery]
                            "CUT & SWELL RESISTANCE"  "RECOVERY RATE"

STAT A: aerobic_recovery (Player-Facing: "RECOVERY RATE")
a. Data Definition & Generation
Internal Key: aerobic_recovery (float, 0.0 to 100.0).
Player Readout: RECOVERY RATE (Integer display 0–100 with descriptive bandings).
Real Boxing Meaning:
20 (Compromised / Poor Parasympathetic Tone): A fighter whose heart rate stays elevated in the red zone ($>85%$ HRmax) throughout the 60-second corner rest. They recover only 12–16 BPM between rounds, entering late rounds exhausted.
50 (Club Standard): Standard professional baseline. Recovers 22–26 BPM during the 60-second break when seated, allowing them to sustain a steady work rate through 6–8 rounds.
80 (Elite Aerobic Engine): Exceptional parasympathetic reactivation. Heart rate drops 34–40 BPM within 45 seconds of sitting down. They clear lactic acid rapidly and can maintain high punch volumes across 12 full rounds.
Generation Formula: $$\text{aerobic_recovery} = (\text{conditioning} \times 0.65) + (\text{breathing_mastery_bonus}) + \text{Gaussian}(\mu=0, \sigma=4.0)$$
Weight Class Bias: Heavyweights skew $-12$ points lower on average; Flyweights/Bantams skew $+14$ points higher due to cardiac output and body mass scaling.
Technique Interlock: Having boxing_breathing_rhythm at Stage 3 or 4 grants a permanent $+6.0$ to $+10.0$ bonus to this metric.
b. Fight Engine Integration
What It Changes: During the 60-second corner rest interval, heart rate drops according to this recovery curve: $$\Delta \text{BPM}_{\text{rest}} = 14.0 + \left(\frac{\text{aerobic_recovery}}{100.0} \times 24.0\right) + (\text{Coach Cutman/Cornerman Skill} \times 0.08)$$
At aerobic_recovery = 20: Fighter recovers $\approx 18.8\text{ BPM}$ per corner interval.
At aerobic_recovery = 80: Fighter recovers $\approx 33.2\text{ BPM}$ per corner interval.
In-Round Lactic Buffer: When a fighter breaks from exchanges ($>2.5\text{m}$ distance, no punches thrown for 3 seconds), heart rate decay speed scales directly with this stat: $$\text{BPM Decay (Active Rest)} = 0.15 + \left(\frac{\text{aerobic_recovery}}{100.0} \times 0.35\right)\text{ BPM/sec}$$
What It Must NOT Change: It must never modify maximum punch velocity (speed), base punch impact force (power), or flash knockdown resistance (chin). It strictly governs cardiac recovery and energy replenishment.
c. Training & Progression
Primary Gym Stations: Roadwork, Rowing Ergometer, Air Bike, High-Intensity Sparring intervals.
Progression Rate: Scales slower than raw muscle adaptation: $+0.15$ to $+0.35$ points per 7-day training block with an aerobic focus.
Ceiling & Detraining: Bounded by the fighter's genetic conditioning cap. If aerobic conditioning is neglected for more than 21 days, aerobic_recovery decays by $-0.4$ points per week down to a baseline floor of $\text{conditioning} \times 0.50$.
d. Aging & Injury Impact
Aging Curve: Peaks early between ages 22–26. Beginning at age 29, it declines by $-0.8$ points per year. By age 34, this decay accelerates to $-1.8$ points per year, forcing older fighters to rely on ring craft, pace control, and counter-punching rather than sustained output.
Injuries: Chronic rib fractures or pulmonary illnesses apply a temporary $-15%$ to $-25%$ penalty to recovery rates for 6 to 12 weeks.
e. Scouting & Fog of War
Unscouted / Unknown Opponent: Displayed as a wide estimated range based on fight film (e.g., RECOVERY: 40–65).
Scouted (Level 2 Analyst): Displayed as a narrowed 10-point tier (e.g., RECOVERY: 68–78).
Fully Scouted (Opponent Sparring Footage Audited): Exact value revealed with historical heart-rate recovery telemetry from their previous 3 bouts.
STAT B: tissue_recovery (Player-Facing: "CUT & SWELL RESISTANCE")
a. Data Definition & Generation
Internal Key: tissue_recovery (float, 0.0 to 100.0).
Player Readout: CUT & SWELL RESISTANCE (Integer display 0–100 with medical status tags).
Real Boxing Meaning:
20 (Vulnerable / Heavy Scar Tissue): Thin, brittle skin and prominent orbital bone structure. Prone to opening deep cuts from glancing jabs; hematomas swell rapidly and threaten doctor stoppages.
50 (Resilient / Pro Standard): Average professional durability. Absorbs standard head contact without abnormal swelling; cuts occur primarily from clean, high-impact clashes or flush power shots.
80 (Pristine / Tough Facial Tissue): Dense skin and resilient facial structure. Resists cuts from minor friction or glancing punches; swelling remains localized and responds quickly to adrenaline and cold compress treatment.
Generation Formula: $$\text{tissue_recovery} = 100.0 - (\text{Career Rounds} \times 0.15) - (\text{Severe Facial Cuts Count} \times 4.5) + \text{Gaussian}(0, 5.0)$$
Young debut fighters start high ($75–92$).
Veterans with 35+ professional bouts who have been involved in ring wars will naturally drop toward $25–45$ due to accumulated scar tissue.
b. Fight Engine Integration
What It Changes:
Laceration Check Threshold: When a clean head punch lands (head_neck region), the probability of opening a cut is calculated as: $$P(\text{cut}) = \text{BaseTechniqueCutChance} \times \left(1.0 - \frac{\text{tissue_recovery}}{120.0}\right)$$
Corner Cutman Efficiency: During the 60-second break, the corner team's work with the Enswell compress, adrenaline chloride ($1:1000$), and thrombin scales directly against this attribute: $$\text{Swelling Reduction} = \text{CutmanSkill} \times \left(0.40 + \frac{\text{tissue_recovery}}{100.0} \times 0.60\right)$$
What It Must NOT Change: It must never absorb kinetic concussion damage, prevent knockdowns, or improve punch evasion. It governs purely external soft-tissue durability and ringside medical interventions.
c. Training & Progression
Cannot Be Trained Upward: You cannot improve facial skin durability through heavy bag work.
Management & Mitigation: Managed through preventative medical care:
Allocating training budget to high-grade protective headgear during hard sparring preserves skin integrity.
Allowing facial lacerations to heal completely before scheduling the next bout (minimum 6–8 weeks post-cut) prevents the formation of fragile scar tissue.
d. Aging & Injury Impact
Permanent Degradation: Every severe laceration suffered in a fight applies a permanent $-2.0$ to $-4.0$ reduction to tissue_recovery.
Scar Tissue Accumulation: By the end of a long, punishing career, a fighter's resistance naturally drops into the $20–35$ range. This makes veteran fighters vulnerable to technical stoppages on cuts, even when their tactical skills and power remain elite.
e. Scouting & Fog of War
Visual Inspection (Tale of the Tape): Visible scar tissue around the eyebrows and cheekbones is immediately recognizable to scouts:
Pristine (80–100): "No visible facial wear; skin clean."
Moderate (45–79): "Mild scar tissue over right orbital ridge."
Compromised (0–44): "Extensive scar tissue around both eyes; high laceration risk."

Why Copying the Competitor's Panel Literally Harms a Deep Sim
ARCADE PANEL APPROACH (COMPETITOR):
  - 9 arbitrary stats shown side-by-side.
  - "Health" and "HP Recovery" turn fighters into fantasy RPG health pools.
  - "Attack Speed" and "Strength" duplicate "Speed" and "Power", muddying the numbers.
  - Tactical decisions collapse into simple stat-checking: pick whoever has higher total numbers.

DEEP-SIM ARCHITECTURE (FIGHTCAMP):
  - Every stat represents a clear, functional physiological or technical variable.
  - Punch accuracy emerges naturally from technique, footwork, and opponent positioning.
  - Damage is tracked across six distinct anatomical regions, creating realistic medical risks.
  - Preserves the tactical depth expected of an authentic, high-end sports management simulation.
