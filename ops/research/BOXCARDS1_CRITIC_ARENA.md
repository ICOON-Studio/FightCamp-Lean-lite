# BOXCARDS1: independent code-checked critic

## Verdict

**Do not build the page yet.** REAL BOUT **5/10**: combinations, body work and a count are legible, but the timing of defence, counters, scoring and exhaustion repeatedly misdescribe boxing. DEPTH **6/10**: interesting order/answer/hand trade-offs, but a fixed opponent, simplistic scoring and an unvalidated lookahead do not prove skill beats draw. PLAY **6/10**: 32.2 *instrumented* decisions for both players is encouraging, but no human timed it, a leader must enumerate ordered triples and anticipate hidden answers, and 5 minutes is unproven. The keep bar is **8 depth and 8 play: fail both**. The owner should not have to accept a simulator's bar as a real-boxing fact.

Method: I printed and read seeds 1–10 (`walk=1`) before looking at the aggregate output. I then read `rules.mjs` line by line, `bot.mjs`, probe, both prior papers, and reran the tests and probes. No human games were played. References below are repository line numbers, not assertions about a proposed UI.

Other rules not stated or not stated fully: a discard pile is reshuffled on deck exhaustion (`36`); all committed cards are spent and **all punches counted thrown before** clinch, step, knockdown or counter ends them (`37,58,77–88`); an empty lead recovers an *extra* 2 wind and clears the opponent's held status, but does not clear ropes or consume loaded slip (`7,59–60,74`); a slip bonus is lost on a missed first punch (`83–84`); blocked head damage builds daze but cannot cause a knockdown (`68–71`); a jab can take daze beyond 7 but cannot knock down (`13,71`); a counter itself can knock its attacker down (`80`); at bell held, ropes, skip, ready, thrown and hurt clear, unused hand is discarded (`105`). There is no validation in `resolve`/`bout` that a pilot's volley is in hand, within wind or length, or that an answer is legal; `spend` checks card possession only (`37,56–63,124–126`). `volleys` excludes bare/terminal feints (`bot.mjs:15–24`), but engine does not. Enforce legality and hidden state before human/other-bot testing.

The page's claim that “one defence cannot cover three punches” is false literally: block covers all eligible heads, step all non-jabs, clinch all after the first (`rules.mjs:77–88`). Page says all cards already played are visible; reshuffle erases `pile` without a separate history, although a UI could retain events. “All wind at the bell” means full **current cap**, not initial six. The 9.3% answer “none” is of nonempty leads; empty leads are a different denominator. Each reported card-used percentage is pooled across either boxer and “held sometime in bout,” not actually **legal** when used; a counter uses cross/hook but is tracked separately (`probe.mjs:32–42,50,64`). The 22–45 bar in the scope is per player, but this probe explicitly counts both (`probe.mjs:33`); calling that bar passed changes its meaning. Combination pass prints `0.9`, rounded fraction, rather than 90%; the under-10 test actually accepts exactly 10 (`56,66`). The page's 79% at line 6 is not reproduced: see sweep.

## Reproduction and bot blindness

`node proto/boxcards/probe.mjs`: exactly 305 decisions + 23 decisions on punches + 5 draws = **333/400, 83.25%**, 61 stoppages, 6 KO; first lead **54.625%** including half-draw, combination **1.74/bout**, 32.2 decisions, 9.3% empty; 0.60 knockdowns, 36.8% of bouts; cap 5.12. Answer/opening/usage table rounds to the page values. Combination **90%** confirmed by probe. `node proto/boxcards/probe.mjs pilots=reader,slugger`: reader seat 0 **91.625%**, including half a draw; 155/400 cards (38.75%). This tests a very weak slugger (largest damage sum and block-first), not human skill. `node --test proto/boxcards/boxcards.test.mjs`: **12/12 pass**.

`worth` (`bot.mjs:8–13`) sees immediate differential points, daze, cap, next-recovered wind, sign of thrown count, knockdown deductions, ready, and fixed ±40 on stoppage. It does **not** value actual round/card state, remaining exchanges, round or match lead, held/ropes, skip, card opportunity cost, future hand composition, or an actual future sequence. Its busy term is the sign even when points are unequal. `volleyValue` (`40–46`) invents an opponent hand of one of every type, estimates individual holds from unseen counts, sorts imagined answers and multiplies marginal absence probabilities as if independent. Multiple slip targets reuse the same marginal event, so they are incorrectly treated as independent chances; cross/hook and other answers compete for the same finite hand, also correlated. Counts are not always `DECK − pile` after reshuffle while cards are in hand. Reader is not an equilibrium, cannot see two exchanges ahead and its belief is approximate, even though raw `S` is visible to a dishonest pilot.

## Adversarial pilots

Scratch `/tmp/boxpilot.mjs` imported engine and bot, never changed repo. Each row is **400 bouts in each seat**, same seeds `77000001+7919*i`, first `i%2`; entries are wins plus half draws /400, so the two seats need not coincide. All pilots use reader's legal `bestAnswer` unless stated; they never read the opposing hand. Fixed priorities take available cards in listed order, stop at wind and 3; no substitutions except stated. The sample is deterministic, not a confidence guarantee (at 50%, 400 bouts is roughly ±4.9 percentage points per seat).

I also tried bonus 0.2, 0.4, 0.7 and 2.0. None establishes a >60% non-reading exploit. This is a limited search, not proof there is no dominant strategy; “skill beats luck” is also unproven. The busier-man tiebreak counts punches that never actually happen (`rules.mjs:58` before `77–88`): e.g. three committed cards clinched after the first are credited as three thrown. That **is a broken judge input**, even though my passive pilot did not win. A player who passes can also counter, so “never lead” is not literally never fight.

Combo percentages here are approximate because the probe prints the raw fraction rounded to one decimal in the PASS line, not a precise percentage. The most fragile observed bars are decisions-to-cards, first lead and empties; even a *bot weight* crosses first-lead bar. Moving kdAt from 7 to 6 yielded **77%, not page's 79%**, and 55.3% KD bouts, not merely “half.” `kdAt=5` passes all numerical bars while 75.3% of bouts have a knockdown; this is exactly why passing the old bar is not a design win. **83% decisions is not demonstrated to be a fault in a three-round amateur game.** USA Boxing's Paris results list many 3-round Olympic decisions, including split decisions and a controlled 5–0 win [USA Boxing, Paris 2024](https://www.usaboxing.org/2024-paris-olympic-games); the [complete results compilation](https://www.badlefthook.com/2024/7/27/24206890/olympics-2024-boxing-results-updated-daily) also shows numerous decisions. These examples do not establish a population percentage, nor validate 83% for all weights, sex, or competition levels. Scope's 40–70 was an uncalibrated game bar from a different 12-round framing; collect a defined comparable tournament sample including RSC/KO/withdrawals before setting a new one. Do not tune toward 70% by manufacture.

## Six walked bouts: coach's objection log

Notation `seed Rround.exchange`, count included as an exchange even though walk suppresses a lead line. These are **all instances in these six walks of the mechanical effects I reject**, not a claim that a real boxer could never throw any of the named combinations. I did not mark an ordinary missed punch or clinch merely for being possible. Full logs reproducible with `node proto/boxcards/probe.mjs walk=1 seed=N` for N = 1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 10.

- **One block automatically covers an entire head combination:** 1 R1.2, R1.6; 2 R1.2, R2.3, R2.6, R3.5; 3 R1.3–4, R2.3–4, R3.2; 4 R1.4, R1.6, R3.1, R3.3; 7 R1.6, R2.1, R2.6, R3.1; 10 R1.4, R1.6, R3.4. A boxer adjusts guard, parries, slips, or gets split/opened as punches change; a single guard is not a blanket for every angle. Body and uppercut exceptions help but are categorical, not placement or timing. In 1 R1.6 the slip-loaded jab gets through for 1 daze and still scores zero; a visibly clean scoring blow is not automatically null just because a block was declared. 7 R2.6 and 3 R2.3 show the opposite automatic uppercut exception.
- **One step makes every committed non-jab fall short regardless of sequence or pursuit:** 1 R1.1, R1.3, R2.2, R2.4, R3.2; 2 R1.3, R2.1–2, R3.1, R3.4; 3 R1.5, R2.1, R2.5, R3.1; 4 R1.3, R3.2, R3.5; 7 R1.1, R1.4, R2.4–5; 10 R1.2, R2.2, R3.2–3. The attacker can follow, cut off, or stop throwing; especially 2 R3.4 and 3 R2.5 a later jab lands while earlier shots magically miss at the same fixed range. The rule doesn't model moving feet, distance, or re-entry.
- **Clinch always waits for the first card and then cancels even punches already credited as thrown:** 1 R2.1; 2 R3.2, R3.6; 3 R1.1, R3.5; 4 R1.1–2, R3.6; 7 R1.2, R2.3; 10 R1.1, R1.5, R2.1, R3.1. In real life an entry can smother a shot *before* impact or fail while trying; after a jab lands a tie is plausible, but invisible unthrown follow-ups do not count as thrown. Feint-first clinch as an instantaneous grab from unspecified distance (2 R3.2, 3 R3.5, 10 R1.1/R2.1/R3.1) also needs a range condition.
- **Counter trades as a compulsory clean hit after taking the opening shot, with no chance to slip/parry it, and kills the whole continuation:** 1 R2.5; 2 R1.1, R2.4; 3 R3.3; 4 R2.1–2; 7 R1.3; 10 R2.4. A genuine counter may land while avoiding or interrupting the lead, or trade; neither result should be guaranteed by the card name. 7 R1.3 is possible as a trade, not as the only counter outcome.
- **Preselecting a later punch to slip after eating the rest, then banking a +2 “counter” until next own lead:** 1 R1.4–5, R2.3, R2.6, R3.1, R3.4, R3.6; 2 R1.5–6; 3 R1.2, R2.6, R3.6; 4 R1.5, R2.3, R3.4; 7 R1.5, R3.2; 10 R1.3, R3.5–6. Slipping a particular shot is plausible, but the printed sequence guarantees foreknowledge of the entire face-up combination, and no immediate return even when a counter opening exists. The bonus can also be lost on a miss, not necessarily a hit. 7 R3.2 is recognisable as a slip followed by being caught by the next cross, but B had no option to change defence mid-combination.
- **A planned punch cancelled by clinch/counter/knockdown is nevertheless credited as thrown for the tiebreak:** every multi-punch clinch/counter instance above, plus 7 R3.2 and R3.4 (the walk prints R3.3 as count). In 7 R1.6 the round goes to A on committed volume despite blocked work; the report calls this “busier,” but not all committed follow-ups in earlier clinches were thrown. Actual judges see punches thrown/landed and quality, not a private intention to throw.
- **Empty hands / alternating turns create bouts of inactivity and prevent a second spontaneous action:** 1 R1.6, R3.3, R3.5; 2 R2.5, R3.3; 3 R1.6, R3.4; 4 R2.4–6; 10 R2.3, R2.5–6, R3.3. A tactical lull is plausible; three mandatory action slots with nobody punching (4 R2.4–6, 10 R2.5–6) and no card cycling is a deck artefact, not a three-minute round. Seed 7 R3.3 count followed by R3.4 another immediate knockdown is a plausible finish but lacks a real count duration or referee assessment.

The corresponding ten logs were read end-to-end; the selected six are analysed above. Counts are fixed slots, not real clock seconds. This is intentionally a mechanical critique, not a claim that individual slips, blocks, trades, or lulls are impossible in boxing.

## Check against all fourteen things in CARDBOX1 part 2

1. **Range:** only temporary step/ropes flag; no long/inside/corner, advance, cut or spin. 2. **Jab:** scores and has reach; no blind/measure/setup beyond counter immunity. 3. **Lead/counter:** represented, but counter is forced trade and cannot answer jab. 4. **Combination:** order and multi-land exist, but one answer may blanket a volley. 5. **Head/body:** both exist; body cannot itself cause knockdown, no fatigue from accumulated normal work across bells. 6. **Defence kinds:** block/slip/step/clinch; no roll/parry, true slip-and-counter or guard target. 7. **Feint:** visible and unslippable follow-up, no believable disguised bite/call. 8. **Ropes/corner:** only no-repeat step; no pressure/cutting ring or physical exit. 9. **Tiredness:** within-round wind and body-cap carry; no accumulating pace debt or second-wind choice. 10. **Hurt/count:** down/skip/locked daze exist; no count decision or effective safe clinch guarantee. 11. **Bell:** six exchanges, no clock/time-to-bell decision beyond slot count. 12. **Corner:** fresh hand only, no plan choice. 13. **Judges:** one deterministic scorecard, no three independent judges, quality/aggression/generalship or realistic tie-break discretion; late round steals possible via points. 14. **Referee:** thresholds exist; no standing-barrage stoppage, referee judgement, holding warning or corner towel. The earlier paper's named pro bouts and its proposed 12-round ideas are not ground truth for Olympic three-round rules.

## Five questions, two lines each

1. **Read as boxing?** Broad arc yes, every exchange no. 7 R3.4 is falsely narrated as a clinch on the page; actual answer is none. 7 R1.6 rewards phantom busyness and blanket blocking.
   A boxer can adapt mid-combination, follow a retreat, or tie up before a shot; the script makes these absolute.
2. **Clinch lets first shot through?** Wrong as an unconditional rule, particularly for the hurt boxer; an actual tie may pre-empt, smother, or fail.
   Make the safe entry conditional on range/position and costly, with a capped holding/referee loop; test rather than promising safety for free.
3. **83% cards?** Plausible for a short amateur bout; 40–70 is not evidence-based for this population.
   Obtain comparable full tournament distributions; KD rate and credible action matter more than forcing an arbitrary decision quota.
4. **Best volley/answer?** I found no blind fixed strategy >60% against reader; tested heuristics are not exhaustive.
   Defence depends strongly on order, hand and position, but omniscient whole-volley answers, step and unthrown-volume scoring invite gaming.
5. **Corner?** Choose a public plan: recover, move, body or pressure, each with a cost and one-round effect.
   The hurt man chooses survival versus finish; plans must not erase damage or guarantee a dominant loop.

## TO REACH 10/10

1. **Fix information and legality before tuning.** Pass pilots a redacted view (own hand, public discards, visible volley and bars); validate ≤3 cards, wind, ownership and `answers` membership at engine boundary. Preserve public play history through reshuffle. Expected probe: illegal/hidden-read attempts **0/10,000**, mirror outcomes unchanged when using compliant reader. **Untested proposal**; add adversarial legality tests. This closes the information-cheating loop, not a balance fix.
2. **Fix scoring input and sequencing.** Increment `thrown` inside resolution only when a punch is actually attempted, including those that miss; never count smothered or cancelled cards. Keep a separate committed-card count for wind/discards. Tests: jab–cross–hook into clinch counts **1**, into step **3**, knockdown on first **1**; expected probe records zero phantom throws. **Untested**, no inferred win-rate. Loop: extra attempts → busy tiebreak → players throw wastefully; cap by paying wind and scoring clean points first. Do not assume the 0% never-lead pilot proves judges are good.
3. **Make defence a timed choice, not clairvoyance.** Prototype an opener revealed, defender chooses answer, then leader may commit up to two affordable follow-ups, with defender able to block one zone or step once; track long/punching/inside and allow a paid advancing follow-up to chase step. Block applies to one selected head punch, uppercut is not categorically unblockable; clinch pre-empts only at inside range, costs both 1, cannot repeat without an intervening real punch, third hold in a round loses 1 judge point after warning. Expected target: combos in **>50%** of bouts, first lead **45–55%**, no fixed blind pilot **>60%** from both seats; no claim these will hold. **Untested multi-rule design**, needs a scratch implementation and honest, trained pilots before shipping. Loop: stepping creates long range → favours jab → more steps; cap one step until actual advance/lead and 3 wind recovery. Clinching → survival → more clinching; warning/point cap and inside entry create a cost, but can also punish legitimate defence.
4. **Do not force stoppages to satisfy the obsolete bar.** Keep kdAt=7 as baseline. Tested alternatives: kdAt=6 yields **77.0% cards, 55.3% KD bouts, first 55.4%, 8.9% empty**; kdAt=5 yields **61.0%, 75.3%, 51.9%, 8.7%**, respectively. The latter numerically passes 6/6 but produces too many knockdown bouts for this critic without external calibration; reject it. Down → no fade → down again is limited by stoppage thresholds, not eliminated. Obtain real amateur cohort before choosing target, then retest with `kdTo` consistent with the threshold.
5. **Prevent stuck hands and price corner choices.** At each bell offer one face-up plan for next round: Recover (+1 wind once, no cap healing), Pressure (+1 wind cost on first lead, +1 wind to opponent on first legal clinch), or Move (first step costs 0 but no counter that exchange); retain default hand refresh. Also allow once per round discard one non-punch on an empty lead, then draw at next exchange. Targets, **untested**: empty leads <10%, each plan chosen ≥10% by competent pilots, no plan >60% versus alternatives from both seats. Feedback: free step → no damage → free survival; once-per-round limits it. Discard cycling → better hands → fewer empties; once per round and opportunity cost cap it. Validate with real hand-play; do not assert these numbers as measured.
6. **Replace misleading gates and run real playtests.** Instrument decisions separately per player, actual legal opportunities per card and actually attempted punches; report exact combo rate, seat/first splits with intervals, independent held-out seeds, and early-stoppage exposure. Current measured baseline is **32.2 both-player decisions**, **9.3% empties**, **54.6% first**; no measured human time. Target 20 hands with a friend, then ≥20 timed bouts, median ≤5 minutes, no >60% blind exploit in both seats on held-out seeds, and a revised amateur decision range backed by a defined sample. Expected metric for the instrument fix is **100% agreement with event-log recount**; untested. Include stronger multi-exchange and score-aware pilots; the present 91.6% merely proves reader beats slugger.
