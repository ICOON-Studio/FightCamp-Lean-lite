# Claude v John - A DRAW 28-28 (Claude 10-9, 10-9, John 10-8 in round 3)

## 1. Why it was a draw - the two beats that decided it
- Round 3, beat 1: John stepped in, feinted and crossed while I stood still with both hands high to bank stamina. The feint made my high guard useless: 3 health, 2 points, and it left me at 1 health. Standing still with a full guard against a feint is a dead plan.
- Round 3, beat 6: I needed 2 points to stop a 10-8 and threw a lone body jab. It landed (1 point), but his step-in, feint, cross put me down (2 + 3 points). A 10-8 round then wiped out the two rounds I had won. My own counting was wrong (I thought 1 point was enough); the rule was clear, I misread the margin.

## 2. What I could read and what I could not
- Readable: at Long he almost always threw a single lead (jab or body jab) with the rear hand high. So his body was open after he jabbed, and my step-in + body cross landed three times (R1B2, R2B1, R2B5 - the last one a knockdown). He also went body-jab twice in a row (R3B2, R3B3), and the back-off pull-counter body jab (R3B5) punished his jab for a knockdown.
- Not readable: when he would back off. Twice (R2B3, R3B4) he backed off just as I threw my finisher, my body cross fell short, and once his jab came back as a pull counter for 3. The back off is the one choice that cannot be seen coming and beats any punch thrown at Mid.

## 3. More fun than a scripted opponent?
Yes. John adapted: after I won two rounds with body work, he switched to step-in, feint, cross, and he guessed my "rest" beats. The beats where I had to guess whether he would back off felt like real boxing - the feint-in-the-other-man's-head moments.

## 4. Too strong / never wanted
- Too strong: FEINT then a big punch. It lands whatever the guard, for 1 extra stamina. It decided round 3 twice for him, and it won R1B4 and R3B3 for me. Repeating it was never punished (John used it in R3B1 and again in R3B6 and both landed).
- Never wanted: the lead uppercut or push after round 1; and holding both hands high, which only protects against what a feint makes uncoverable.

1. Make the feint readable or beatable: a held hand that does not move (or a back off) should beat a feint, or the feint costs the feinter 1 guard if he is not covered. Tied to R3B1 and R3B6, where a feint-cross beat a full guard with nothing I could do.
2. Show the round's 10-8 line in the show screen ("you need 2 points to keep it 10-9"). Tied to R3B6, where a 10-8 swing threw away two won rounds and the margin was easy to miscount; a 10-8 that undoes two rounds also feels too big for one beat.
3. Make the back off cost something visible at Mid: it beat my finisher twice for 1 stamina (R2B3, R3B4) and once gave him a pull counter for 3. Let a step in that meets a back off give the stepper a small edge (the gap closes AND his next punch +1), so chasing a hurt man pays.
