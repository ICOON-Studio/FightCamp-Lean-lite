# Claude v Kalli - Claude WINS, the referee stops it (round 3, beat 2; cards were 10-8, 10-8)

## 1. Why I won - the two beats that decided it
- Round 2, beat 2: Kalli had learned to jab and back off in moment 2, which made my step-in-feint-cross fall short the beat before. I moved the step in to moment 2 so it met his back off, threw the body jab first and the body cross last: 1 + 2 = his 3 health, first knockdown, and it put him on the ropes.
- Round 3, beat 2: with him down twice on 4 health, I stepped in FIRST, then feinted, then threw the overhand. His back off met my step in and I "cut him off" (Mid to Inside); the feinted overhand could not be covered: 4 health, third knockdown, stopped.

## 2. What I could read and what I could not
- Readable: Kalli kept the lead hand high at Long and threw one jab with the rear high, so the body was open every time - body cross landed in R1B1, R1B4, R2B2, R3B1. When he crossed, his lead went low, so my fast lead hook beat his cross to the punch (R1B2).
- Readable after one miss: his back off came in moment 2 (R2B1). Once seen, the answer (step in in the same moment) was clear.
- Not readable: the clinch in R2B5 that ate my uppercut, and the step-in that blocked my hook in R2B4.

## 3. More fun than a scripted opponent?
Yes, but less than v John. Kalli adapted once (the moment-2 back off) and then repeated his guard (lead high at Long) through all three rounds; repeating my body-jab / step-in / body-cross was never punished - it landed three times in a row.

## 4. Too strong / never wanted
- Too strong: the body cross behind a step in. A man who jabs keeps his lead high in the other moments, and the rear hand can never cover the body, so the body line is open on almost every beat; it also shortens his stamina bar for good (Kalli ended at 4 of 8).
- Never wanted: the push. Also the lead uppercut was eaten by a clinch.

## 6. To reach 10/10
1. Let the rear hand cover the body too (rear low = body and under, or a "tuck" that covers the body with either elbow). Tied to R3B1: the body cross landed through a full high guard for the fourth time; one line that only one hand can ever close is a hole.
2. Say in the show screen what each held hand blocks this beat AND what is open ("open: body, under"). Tied to R1B1/R2B2, where I won by spotting the open line from his last beat; the player who does the bookkeeping wins, not the one who reads the man.
3. Make the third knockdown harder to reach once a man is hurt - e.g. a downed man's guard comes back at full and he gets one free back off. Tied to R3B2: the bout ended six beats after the second knockdown with a play (step in, feint, overhand) he had no answer to at all.
