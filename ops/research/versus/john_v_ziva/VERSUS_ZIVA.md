# John v Ziva - VERDICT (Ziva wins 29-28: 10-9, 10-9, 9-10)

**1. Decided by two beats.** R2 beat 5: with John on 3 health I stepped in, feinted, crossed -
uncoverable, -3, and he went down. That knockdown was 5 points (2 + 3) and made R2 8-4; without it R2 sat
at 3-3 and he had the round. R1 beat 6: my feint-cross landed for -4 while BOTH his punches - lead hook and
the cross behind it - died on my guard (lead high, rear high). That took R1 10-9, and since R3 went to him,
that one point was the bout.

**2. Readable, and not.** His tell was behavioural: he guards whatever line last landed on him. Twice my
body jab landed and the next beat his lead sat LOW, handing me the jab; in round 3 he held it low for three
beats and I took three points with the jab alone. Also readable: at Long his kit is only the two pokes,
thrown at moment 1 with the rear home, and his pull counter (R2 b4: back off then body jab for 3 damage) is
a set piece. What I could NOT read is the moment: his feint-cross cannot be covered at all - it beat my
both-hands-home guard in R2 beat 6 for -3 and the round's margin - and the same mechanic won me R2 beat 5.
On a beat where one man feints, the guard game stops existing.

**3. Agent versus script.** Better, for one reason: he LEARNED inside the bout - adopting the lead-low
guard after my first body jab, then using it so hard it cost him three points in round 3, pulling a counter
off my jab at R2 b4, and switching to the feint-cross for R2's last beat once the round was lost. A
script's habits are fixed, so a read repeats forever; here my read (he guards the last line that hurt him)
worked only until he noticed it.

**4. Too strong, never wanted.** Too strong: the feint, which makes the next punch uncoverable, so
feint-cross ignores every guard (his R2 b6, my R2 b5); only a faster big punch in that same moment answers
it. Never wanted: the push off - Inside-only, and I never found a reason to press it once.

**5. Scores.** FUN 7, DEPTH 8, CLARITY 8 (every beat explained; the moment is fog), VARIETY 7.

**6. TO REACH 10/10.** (a) Stop the knockdown being a heal: R3 beat 2, floored at 1 health, I stood up on
6 - more than before the beat - so losing it paid me. (b) Price the feint, or let a guard answer it: R2 beat
6, his uncoverable cross beat a guard with both my hands home. (c) Make Long worth playing: R3 beats 3-6
were one-damage pokes decided only by which hand the other man had parked; one 2-damage punch that reaches
Long, or start rounds at Mid.

VERDICT END
