# Dola v Ziva - VERDICT (Ziva WINS on the cards 30-26: 10-9, 10-9, 10-8)

**1. Decided by two beats.** R2 beat 3: he had spent two beats feinting with no punch behind it; I stepped
in, feinted, crossed for 2 points and 3 damage (his health 9 to 6), and R2 went from 2-1 to 4-1. R3 beat 2:
his rear hand had been parked LOW for three straight beats - the guard that covers the under and sells the
side - so I stepped and hooked it for 4 damage and a knockdown, worth 5 points, and R3 was carded
10-8. That knockdown was the difference between 30-26 and 29-27.

**2. Readable, and not.** The most readable of the four: his rear hand's POSITION was his whole game -
every jab came with the rear hand low and his lead sat high once the jab was away, which told me the open
line before I chose: the body jab early, the hook once the low rear was obvious. Also readable: he runs to Long the moment I step, and his feints were thrown without
the punch they promise. What I could NOT read is the back off: it came exactly when I attacked and twice
killed a paid beat (R2 beat 4, my cross and hook both fell short). At a range where only pokes reach, that
retreat cost me more stamina than any punch he threw, and nothing on screen warned me.

**3. Agent versus script.** Still yes, but this is the bout where the agent played most like a script: the
same jab twelve times, two whole beats on a lone feint, a retreat he re-used. What a script could not do is what I
actually read: he held his rear hand LOW as a deliberate guard, and adopted it in answer to my body jab. A
habit table would have kept that hand there from beat one.

**4. Too strong, never wanted.** Too strong: the feint. It makes the next punch uncoverable, so R2 beat 3
took 2 points and 3 damage through a guard with both hands home - and in the same bout his own two feints
bought nothing, which is how all-or-nothing the switch is. Never wanted: the push off.

**5. Scores.** FUN 7, DEPTH 7, CLARITY 8, VARIETY 6 - the least variety of the four: one jab, one back
off, and a rear hand in one position.

**6. TO REACH 10/10.** (a) Stop the knockdown healing: R3 beat 2, he went down at 3 health and stood up on
6, more than he had before the beat - being floored should return a fixed low number. (b) Bound the feint:
R2 beat 3, my uncoverable cross beat a guard with both hands home while his lone feints bought nothing.
(c) Make Long worth playing: eight beats here were one-damage pokes decided by which hand was parked
where; one 2-damage punch that reaches Long, or start rounds at Mid.

VERDICT END
