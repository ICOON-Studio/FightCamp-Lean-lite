# Dola on Dola v Claude (Claude WINS - the referee stops it)

VERDICT
(1) Why I lost — the two deciding beats
Beat R2B1: I threw a jab into Claude's feint-step-overhand sequence — my jab blocked, his feint stripped my guard, his overhand landed for -5 HP and the first knockdown. Beat R3B1: I held lead-low expecting a body jab, Claude stepped-in → feint → overhand again — his feint negated my guard, overhand finished me. Both times I played the punch or guard I expected, not the sequence he actually ran.
(2) What I read vs missed
Read clearly: Claude loved feint→cross/overhand, body shots when lead-high, step-in from Long. Missed completely: his three-beat timing — step-in (1), feint (2), power punch (3) — and that the feint makes my next punch unblockable, not his. I kept throwing into the feint window instead of guarding through it.
(3) Agent vs scripted opponent
More fun — absolutely. Scripted opponents you learn and exploit; Claude adapted — when I guarded body, he threw overhand; when I stayed Long, he stepped in. Felt like a real thinker, not a pattern-memory puzzle. Every beat felt alive, never just "waiting for the loop".
(4) Strong plan & unwanted switch
Too strong: Claude's 3-beat step→feint→overhand — consistently scored big with zero counter from me. Unwanted switch: repeatedly choosing between "guard and get hit anyway" or "punch and get countered" — no good answer when the feint mechanic is that dominant.
(5) Ratings
FUN: 9/10 | DEPTH: 8/10 | CLARITY: 7/10 | VARIETY: 8/10
(6) Three changes to reach 10/10
Fix feint wording/effect clarity — R1B2: I thought feint protected my next punch, not exposed it. The description needs to say: "Your NEXT punch cannot be covered BY HIM — but HIS next punch ignores YOUR guard." Cost me 2 rounds.
Break 3-beat sequences mid-chain — R2B1/R3B1: Once an opponent commits to step-in→feint, let an early feet=in interrupt or shorten the sequence. No option to stop the combo once started feels unfair.
Show punch speed visually — I couldn't tell jab vs overhand speed difference until it landed. If speed/range was clearer, I'd have known my jab would always lose to his later overhand.
