# Claude v Dola - Claude WINS, the referee stops it (round 3, beat 1; cards were 10-9, 10-8)

## 1. Why I won - the two beats that decided it
- Round 2, beat 1: step in, feint, overhand. Dola's jab in moment 1 was blocked by my lead (still up, its feint came in moment 2), so the overhand landed as a counter for 5: Dola from 6 to 1. The same play in R2B4 did exactly the same thing (5 again, 6 to 1).
- Round 3, beat 1: the same step-in-feint-overhand for the third time ended it (4 health, third knockdown). It was never punished.

## 2. What I could read and what I could not
- Readable: Dola threw ONE lead punch in moment 1 almost every beat with the rear held high, and opened at Long with a jab. So my lead held high in moment 1 blocked it and made my later punches counters. When Dola was hurt (1 health) he backed off in moment 1 - a fast body jab still reaches Long and dropped him (R2B2).
- Not readable: Dola's guard changed every beat (lead high / low), so a body cross was blocked once (R1B3) and his cross landed through my low lead the same beat. Whenever I had to pick a line, it was a coin flip; whenever I feinted, it was not.

## 3. More fun than a scripted opponent?
Partly. Dola mixed punches (jab, body hook, lead hook, cross) like a person, but never punished a repeated plan: I threw step-in-feint-overhand three times and feint-cross twice and every one landed. A scripted opponent that "reads habits" would have answered it; this felt less tense than v John.

## 4. Too strong / never wanted
- Too strong: STEP IN + FEINT + OVERHAND (5 stamina). The step in cuts off a back off, the lead stays up in moment 1 to block his jab and turn the overhand into a counter, and the feint makes the overhand uncoverable: 4-5 health and 2 points every time, from Long. It produced all three decisive beats.
- Never wanted: the lead uppercut, the push and the clinch - Inside never came up, because the bout lived at Long and Mid.

## 6. To reach 10/10
1. Give the feint a real answer: a man who throws in the feint's moment (or holds both hands and does not bite) should make the feint fail and leave the feinter's next punch blocked or countered. Tied to R2B1/R2B4/R3B1: the same feint-overhand landed three times out of three.
2. A held lead that blocks in moment 1 and THEN feints is two jobs for one hand; make the feinting hand drop its guard from the start of the beat. Tied to R2B1 and R2B4, where his jab was blocked by the hand that was about to feint, and that block turned my overhand into a 5.
3. Put the play at Inside to work: uppercuts, push and clinch were never worth it because the bout stayed at Long and Mid; give Inside a reason (e.g. a body cross at Mid costs more, or an overhand cannot reach Mid). Tied to the whole bout: in 13 beats we were Inside zero times.
