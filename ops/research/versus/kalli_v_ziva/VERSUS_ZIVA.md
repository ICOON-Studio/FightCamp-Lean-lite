cross landed as a counter and she went down: 5 points that turned a 2-2 round into 7-2 and won the bout. R3 beat 6: 8-6 up with one beat left, I held BOTH hands instead of chasing a point and her
hook - the punch that had beaten me twice - died on my rear glove. 0-0 won the fight; greed risked 8-8.

**2. Readable, and not.** Readable: her lead hand's posture was the whole read - held low was her body
guard, which also killed her own jab, so my straight was free; high, or out punching, invited my body jab.
She attacked at moment 1 with one fast punch and covered the other line; under pressure at Inside she
clinched twice; behind on points she backed off to Long. She adapted mid-bout:
blocking my body jab the beat after it landed, then going high once I switched to the straight. NOT
readable: the moment. Her hook is fast and my cross medium, so twice (R1 b6, R3 b5) her single punch landed
first and stopped my bigger one with no warning - the read that won R3 b3 was a coin flip at R3 b5. Nor
could I guess her guard: jab-versus-body-jab was a guess every long beat, and I lost it four times.

**3. Agent versus script.** Yes, much more fun. In R3 b4 she found a new line mid-fight - body jab then body
cross into my high lead, for 2 points, 3 damage and a bar point off me for the bout. The feint-cross engine
only kept working because she forced me to re-aim it: at Long with a step, at Inside without. A script would
have repeated its opening jab.

**4. Too strong, never wanted.** The feint makes the next punch uncoverable, so feint-cross beat every guard
for 10 points and a knockdown; its only answer was her fastest punch at that same moment. The switch I never
wanted is the push off: thrown once in 36 beats, only to kill her clinch.

**5. Scores.** FUN 8, DEPTH 8, CLARITY 8 (every beat explained, but the moment is fog and the counter text
named the wrong man), VARIETY 7.
