# John on John v Dola (Dola WINS on the cards 29-28)

**(1) Why I lost.** Dola won 29–28 on the cards, and two beats decided it.
- **R2 B1:** I stepped in with my lead held low, guessing a body cross. Dola threw jab then cross down the straight line instead, both landed, and I lost 4 health. That left me on 8, then 2 a beat later, and I spent the rest of round 2 hiding at Long, which cost the round.
- **R3 B6:** I needed a knockdown with him on 6. The lead hook landed for 3 and beat his body cross to the punch, but my cross ran into his lead held high. One more line open and I'd have won.

**(2) What I could read, and what I couldn't.**
- **What I could read:** Dola threw one punch a beat from R1 B3 on, and his body cross always came with his lead held high. My body crosses into that line landed at R1 B4 and R1 B6. At Long he only had the jab, and my lead held high blocked it three times (R2 B4, R2 B6, R3 B3).
- **What I couldn't read:** whether the next punch went to the straight line or the body. He swapped between the cross and the body cross with no pattern I could find. Every guess on my lead hand was a coin flip, and in R1 B3, B5 and B6 his body cross landed while my lead was held high.

**(3) Was another agent more fun?** Yes, more than the Kalli bout. Dola mixed the body and the head well enough that I had to change plans. Being on 1 health and still winning round 3 with a knockdown felt like a real comeback. A script wouldn't have stepped in with a cross at R3 B4 to finish me.

**(4) Too strong, and never wanted.**
- **Too strong:** the body cross with the lead held high. It's 2 stamina, it lands whenever the other man guards the straight line, and it shortens his stamina bar for good. Both of us leaned on it all bout, and both our bars ended at 4.
- **Never wanted:** the rear hand held low. Nobody threw an uppercut at Mid or Long, so it blocked nothing for six beats.

**(5) Scores.**
- **FUN 8:** a one-point loss with a comeback round.
- **DEPTH 7:** the guess between the straight line and the body is real, but it's mostly a coin flip.
- **CLARITY 8:** every beat explained itself.
- **VARIETY 6:** about 80% of the punches thrown were the jab, cross and body cross.

**(6) TO REACH 10/10.**
1. **Let the lead hand guard both the straight line and the body, for a price (R1 B3, B5, B6):** add a "lead mid" guard that covers both lines but turns each blocked punch into a half-block that still costs 1 health. That gives the defender a safe pick between the two, so the body cross stops being free.
2. **Make holding at Long cost something when behind (R2 B3–B6):** if you give no punch and no step for two beats running, the judges' round tips to the other man. Then hiding on low health stops being free, and the man who is ahead has to come and take it.
3. **Add the comeback punch that's planned (R3 B6):** a one-shot all-in rear hand open only when you're down on the cards, which can't be blocked by the lead held high but costs all your stamina. My cross at R3 B6 is exactly the moment it was missing.
