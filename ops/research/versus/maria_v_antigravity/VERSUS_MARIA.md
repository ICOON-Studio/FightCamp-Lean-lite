# Maria on Maria v Antigravity (Antigravity WINS on the cards 28-27)

**(1) Why I lost.** I lost by one point, and two beats decided it.
- **Round 3, beat 3.** I stepped in from Long with a jab, the same move I'd already made three times. Antigravity saw it coming: a body jab under my high lead, then a cross as a counter while my lead was out. That cost me 5 health and gave it 3 points.
- **Round 3, beat 6.** I needed a knockdown, so I dropped my lead low and threw the body cross. That opened my head on the side, and its lead hook put me down. A round I could have drawn became 8-10.

I won round 1 by punishing a pattern. After that I kept punishing the old pattern after Antigravity had changed it.

**(2) What I could read, and what I couldn't.** Round 1 was easy to read. It threw the lead hook over and over with its rear hand high, and the lead uppercut and body hook made it pay. I could also tell it backed off whenever I set up a big rear punch at Mid; it did that three times. What I couldn't read was when it would switch to body shots, or that it would answer my steps in from Long with a counter instead of holding its guard. The history line shows what it threw, but not what it was waiting for.

**(3) Another agent against a script.** Yes, it was more fun, because it adapted. In round 2 it stopped hooking into my guard and went to the body, which forced me to change too. A script would have kept hooking into my uppercut. The cost is that the score swung on guesses with nothing to tell them apart, like the last beat.

**(4) Too strong, and never wanted.**
- **Too strong:** backing off. It wasted four of my rear punches for 1 stamina a step and never cost Antigravity a round, because being on the ropes came with no real penalty.
- **Never wanted:** the feint. It costs a stamina point and a punch slot, and blocks almost never decided anything, so it never earned a place.

**(6) TO REACH 10/10.**
1. **Make backing off cost something.** Each step back after the first should cost 1 point or 2 stamina, and a boxer on the ropes shouldn't be able to block body shots. That fixes round 2, beats 3 and 5, and round 3, beats 2 and 4, where my punches fell short for free and it still won rounds.
2. **Show each side's tendencies, not just the last three beats.** Add a short tally across the round, for example how often it held its guard high or low and how often it backed off when I loaded up. Then a counter like the one in round 3, beat 3 becomes something you can read instead of something that ambushes you.
3. **Give a losing boxer a real gamble.** Add an all-in rear punch that can't be blocked but leaves both hands open next beat. Round 3, beat 6 was a pure coin flip; a known risk would have made it a decision.
