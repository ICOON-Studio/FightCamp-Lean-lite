# Maria on John v Maria (John WINS on the cards 30-28)

**(1) Why I lost.** I lost on the cards, 30-28. The feint followed by a cross beat me four times, and two beats decided it.
- **Round 1, beat 5.** I'd already seen the feint and cross once, and I still threw a hook into John's high rear hand. Its cross landed as a counter for 4 health and 2 points, which turned a round I was leading into a 10-10 draw.
- **Round 3, beat 6.** The points were level at 4-4, and I'd backed off once in round 3, at beat 4, to save my last 2 health. The tie-break gave John the round for backing off less. Without that one step back, the round would have been even.

**(2) What I could read, and what I couldn't.** John's routine was easy to see. At Long it opened every round with a body jab, then waited with its lead hand low, then threw the feint and cross once we got close. Backing off out of reach of that cross worked twice (round 2, beat 5, and round 3, beat 4). What I couldn't read was which beat at Mid it would pick for the feint and which for a plain jab. The feint can't be blocked, so the only answers were to leave or to hit first, and both cost me something.

**(3) Another agent against a script.** Partly. John was disciplined and kept its health above mine, which made for a tense finish. But it barely changed its plan, so the bout felt close to a script that happened to be strong. The body jab trades (round 1, beat 1; round 2, beat 1; round 3, beats 1 and 5) were identical and cancelled out.

**(4) Too strong, and never wanted.**
- **Too strong:** the feint followed by a cross. For 3 stamina it guarantees a 3-health, 2-point punch that can't be blocked, and the only counter is the 1-health jab it lets through.
- **Never wanted:** the overhand. It's slow and costs 3 stamina, and the feint and cross does its job better.

**(6) TO REACH 10/10.**
1. **Give the feint a counter.** If the other boxer throws a big punch in the same moment as the feint, it should land and cancel the feint's free punch. Holding both hands should also halve the damage of a punch the feint opened up. That fixes round 1, beat 5 and round 3, beat 3, where I had no answer except getting hit.
2. **Change the backing-off tie-break.** Count ground given net of steps back in, or reward the landed counter, so one smart retreat doesn't decide a round. Round 3, beat 6 was lost on a footnote, not a fight.
3. **Make body jab trades worth something.** When both boxers throw the same small punch, the one who held a hand back should win it. Four identical beats this bout produced nothing.
