# A week worth playing - the coach designer's answer (S425)

Lens: what a head coach really decides between Monday and Sunday with three boxers at three points, and what he waits on.
Boxers are named by role, never by sex: the CAMPER (bout in 5 weeks, in camp), the RETURNER (lost last Saturday), the JUNIOR
(youth section, 15, no bout yet). Every number is marked REAL, GAME (the game's own) or INVENTED. R-numbers are the pool's.

## What I found in the pool
The week is already rich in parts: R01/R02 (mornings, the coach's week), R05 twist, R06 push-back, R20 test-it nights, R23 habits,
R03 camp lines, R55 pad hands, R39 offers that pull four ways. They do not talk to each other. A real coach's week is one
chain: a thing set going on Monday, a sign of it midweek, a choice that answers the sign, a result he sees at the weekend.
Every mechanic below is a link in that chain, and uses numbers the game already measured (morale to corner trust 0.7 to 1.05;
a loss -12, a win +8, a month without a fight -6; sparring 1.5 mastery an hour; overreach at 80; a stoppage = 30 days out;
a missed weigh-in $300; outside sparring partner $160; one pad week = up to 14 wins in 100).

## 1. THE WEEK AS PLAYED
MONDAY (Home, the Monday page; the clock has stopped by itself). He reads, in this order: the review with its causes (R07, R11);
the twist card (R05); the LEDGER line "due this week" (mechanic 5); the BOARD: a strip Mon to Sun with what lands when
(mechanic 2): "Wed scale (Camper), Thu sparring report, Fri offer expires (Returner), Sat Junior's first spar".
- Camper, week 2 of a 5-week camp: he lays Reid's evenings (R55), picks Thursday's sparring partner and what to test (mechanic 1).
- Returner: the talk after the loss (mechanic 3). An offer is on the table: a tune-up in 5 weeks, answer by Friday. The card
  says the price of rushing: "Flat: takes in 85% of the corner's word".
- Junior: nothing is due; he sets a small bet (the first spar is Saturday, against a clubmate of the same weight).
TUESDAY: one click, nothing stops. A quiet day is a feature: the week has rhythm because not every day asks.
WEDNESDAY (the Midweek Call stops the clock, but only because a thread drifted): the Camper's scale is 0.6 kg behind the
line, and the Returner's next opponent's last bout (a rival result) has landed as one new tell. He has TWO changes to spend
(mechanic 2): hold the cut and swap Thursday's morning for a run (costs Thursday's freshness, so the sparring report is weaker),
or leave the weight and change one plan arrow for the tell. He cannot do everything: that is the stop.
THURSDAY NIGHT: the sparring is done in the background (mechanic 1). Nothing to click.
FRIDAY: the report lands: "Visitor read your 1-1 in round 3; right dropped after the jab four times; held pace to round 4."
He answers the offer (take the tune-up, or ask later: it costs nothing now but binds a promise), writes a second answer
beside the read line, and sets the Returner's next sparring easy.
SATURDAY: Junior's spar is a small event (he may corner it, or send it with the senior). If there is a club show with nobody
of his on it, it is a night for the members and the dues. SUNDAY: nothing; the week closes into Monday's review: the moves
that became usable, the weight on the line, the ledger rows kept or broken, the one cause for each swing.
What he waited for: the scale, the report, the offer's date, the rival's tape, the junior's Saturday. What he decided: lays,
partner, intensity, two midweek fixes, the talk, the offer, one plan arrow. Nothing was upkeep: every click had a price.

QUIET WEEK, no bout booked for anyone (the hardest): the Board has no scale and no bell. What replaces them is the PROJECT
WEEK (mechanic 4): on Monday he picks the one thing the club does this week (hard block, technique week, away spar, open
mat, rest). The Wednesday stop does not fire. The reward is Monday's "Project results" row and the READINESS LINE on Fights:
"If a 6-week offer came today the Camper would own 3 of 4 answers, the Returner 1 of 4." Anticipation in a quiet week is the
next offer: he prepares for a fight he cannot see yet. If he does nothing the coach's week runs and one click ends the week.

## 2. THE FIVE MECHANICS

### Mechanic 1 - THE SPARRING NIGHT (joins R20 test-it, R46 sparring supply, R23 habits, R21 test accuracy, R04/R10 risk, R25 variety)
Today sparring nights run the plan and give mastery; nobody chooses a partner, an intensity or what to learn from it.
Decisions, in order:
1. WHAT TO TEST (Fighter > Week, the sparring evening): one plan line. Right: the line the opponent's found tell targets
   (scouted, so the test is accurate, R21). Wrong: any line when the opponent is unscouted, a coach would call that sparring, not
   a test: then pick intensity "Technical" and learn.
2. THE PARTNER (price shown): the other camper (free; both tire; right when both are in camp, fatigue under 60, bells more than
   10 days off; wrong when one is a week from the bell: the injury lands on a fight). A club senior (free; lighter; right for
   the Returner's confidence). The Junior (free; right for the Junior's growth and for a Returner who needs to win a round,
   wrong if the Returner is Low: losing a round to a child costs morale). A visitor matching the opponent's stance or style
   ($160 GAME; right in PEAK when the opponent is a presser or southpaw the boxer never met). Bag work (free fallback, 1.05 an hour
   against 1.5 GAME; right when nobody is fit to spar).
3. INTENSITY: Technical (almost no injury risk, half the test; right for the Returner and any boxer above 70 fatigue), Working
   (standard), War (the whole test; the only way to see "fades late" and the chin; right two to three weeks out for a boxer who must
   go the distance against a presser; wrong inside 10 days and with fatigue near 80, where gains are x0.6 and injury x3 GAME).
Friday's report (words, from the Why's language): a line that was READ (R25 in miniature: a second trained answer is worth
having, so variety has a reason), a habit that showed (feeds a morning or the corner fix, R23), the engine's round. Feeds: next
Monday's plan arrow, the Wednesday call, Fight prep's "what your plan should answer".
Anticipation: Friday's report. Reward: a tell he did not know his own boxer had, named before the fight, not after (the Why's job).
Menus: Fighter > Week (the evening card with partner and intensity), Fighter > Playbook (the tested line gets a mark "read in
spar"), Home Board (Thursday), the week review (the report row), the Why (a sentence: "you saw this in sparring, you changed
it / you did not").
Reuses: SparNights, PlanTest, HabitFix, outside partner, overreach, injury roll. NEW: partner and intensity pick (small), the
report (medium: needs a line read-rule from R25's PlanRead).
Numbers: $160, 1.5 an hour, overreach 80, injury 1.3% a week: GAME. Injury x for intensity (Technical 0.3, Working 1, War 2.5):
INVENTED. Test accuracy by scouting: GAME direction (R21), size to measure. Hands: a visitor held in sparring as in real clubs:
REAL (practice, no source read).
Kill list: (a) "War is always best." Answer: it costs fatigue that lands on Saturday's weigh-in and the Wednesday budget; the
cases above give Technical a real owner (the Returner, an overreached boxer). (b) "A chore, the coach auto-picks." Answer: the
auto-pick is Working with a club mate, and the report shows exactly what it missed; if the report never differs it is a chore.
Measure that before building.

### Mechanic 2 - THE WEDNESDAY CALL (joins R05 twist, R14 tease, R03 camp lines, R93 stops, R19 tape, R41 camp risk)
A thread is a thing set going with a forecast and a landing day: the weight, the sparring report, an offer's date, a rival's
bout, a low mood, the twist's second half. Monday draws them as a strip. CONTINUE stops Wednesday only if a thread drifted
outside its band; otherwise it runs on ("quiet week = one click" stays true). At the stop he has TWO changes (as the corner has
two fixes), each priced:
- Weight behind the line: hold the cut and swap a morning for roadwork (right when the margin is under 1 kg: costs Thursday
  freshness); cut 500 kcal for two days (right with 2 days left and a stable boxer: costs the Friday morning's gain); pull
  out (right only when 2 kg over with 3 days left: the club pays $300 GAME, but a missed scale also costs the bout).
- A rival result landed (a tell opens): change one plan arrow now (free; right when the tell is a weapon, wrong when it was one
  bad night: the tape is one bout); wait for the show's tape (nothing lands in time; right never when the bell is 10 days off).
- Mood low or a partner ill: see mechanics 1 and 3.
Anticipation: Monday's strip names the day things land; he plans around them (a hard Thursday morning kills Thursday's spar).
Reward: Peak Camp (R03), the camp lines all met, becomes the visible stake of Wednesday: the scale, the stat and the
overreach line each have a chip that Wednesday can save.
Menus: Home (the Board strip on Monday, the stop on Wednesday), Fighter > Week (the thread chip on the day column), Fight
prep (the camp lines with this week's drift), the corner minute is not touched.
Reuses: weight forecast, camp lines, WeekTwist, the plan arrow, Needs stop reasons. NEW: the drift rule and the Wednesday stop
(medium); the Board strip is a drawing of what already exists (small).
Numbers: a day-to-day weight drift of about 0.5 kg: REAL (practice; boxers' scale weight moves by half a kilo a day), size
INVENTED. Two changes: INVENTED, same shape as the corner's two fixes GAME. Stop only on drift: INVENTED.
Kill list: (a) "A forced stop every week is a chore." Answer: it fires only on drift; a week on the line never stops. Measure
how often over 20 weeks; above 1 in 2 is a chore, tune the drift. (b) "Two changes, always the same two." Answer: only if
threads are always the same; the thread list varies with the camp phase (taper has no pads, base has no scale). If it does
not vary in play, this mechanic is only the weight, and I would drop it.

### Mechanic 3 - THE COMEBACK (joins R30 Why, R31 next change, R06 push-back, R27 trust, R39 four pulls, R37 ask, R52 goal, R10)
A loss starts a three-week arc with a clock, because the game's own numbers pull against each other: a loss -12 morale, a
month without a fight -6, a stoppage is 30 days out, a Flat boxer takes in 85% of the corner's word, a Low one 70%.
Decisions, in order:
1. THE TALK (Monday after, and the Why's NEXT line): "Own it" (the plan's failed line named; right with high coachability: trust
   up; wrong with high temper: flares), "Shield" (the draw, the judges; right for a first loss and a low-heart junior: morale
   falls -8 not -12; wrong when the Why showed a real fault: the fix is skipped and the next loss repeats it), "Push" (fire up:
   holds the morale, adds fatigue; right for a settled veteran, wrong for a Low boxer, who ignores it).
2. THE GAP: back Monday, or time off. Time off is right after a stoppage (30 days anyway) and when fatigue is high; back Monday is
   right when the stoppage was a cut and the boxer is Fired up. The month without a fight (-6) starts counting.
3. THE REBUILD: a soft sparring week (mechanic 1) with a senior and a Technical round: confidence back, nothing learned about the
   plan. Or a hard one: the plan learned, the risk taken on a boxer who may be Flat.
4. THE NEXT FIGHT (Fights, the offer card): the price line is new and plain, from existing numbers: "Flat: takes in 85% of
   the corner's word. A tune-up win lifts to Steady (+8)." Tune-up (R39): right when the boxer is Flat or Low. The rematch
   or step-up: right only when Steady or better, or when the entry closes (the nationals). Asking later (R37): right when a hurt
   boxer has weeks left; it costs the month's -6.
5. AFTER A WIN: the comeback is named in History ("first win after a stoppage") and the goal talk offers a Comeback goal (R52).
Anticipation: the Friday the offer expires and the first bout back. Reward: a comeback win is worth more than a plain win
(+12 not +8 morale: INVENTED), shows as a mark on History, and the boxer who is brought back well keeps coachability
(Loyalty rises: feeds R58, because a boxer who was dropped after a loss listens to a bigger gym).
Menus: Fights (the offer card price line), Fighter > Overview (the talk and the arc as a three-step strip), Home (the Returner's
row: a chip "Flat, back in 2 weeks"), the Why (the talk is the Why's last line), Week (the soft week).
Reuses: Morale and CornerTrust (all numbers), FightWhy, offers and Matchmaking, FighterGoal, injury. NEW: the talk choice
(small), the price line on the offer (small), the arc strip and the comeback mark (medium).
Numbers: -12, +8, -6, 30 days, 0.85 and 0.7: GAME. Talk shifts (-8 Shield, +/-3 around Push) and the +12 comeback: INVENTED;
anchor in real coaching: the first call after a loss decides whether a boxer trusts the corner: REAL (practice, no source read).
Kill list: (a) "Tune-up is always right, the price line is a lookup." Answer: only until the entry for the nationals closes, a
sponsor date lands, or the boxer is near 30 days without a fight; the list of fights has to be built so a rematch is sometimes
the only fight in the window (the bot test of R37 should confirm). (b) "Shield dominates Own it." Answer: Shield skips the Why's
fix, so a repeated loss costs 10 points of win chance in the next bout: measure it before approving.

### Mechanic 4 - THE PROJECT WEEK (joins R09 test day, R23 habits, R49 away shows, R80 intake, R74 90-day cliff, R46, R71, R55)
A quiet week is the base phase: no bell, no scale. Today the coach's week runs and the player clicks. New: one project a week,
named on Monday, result on Monday:
- HARD BLOCK: Hard mornings (x1.3 gains GAME) three days in a row. Right: no bout inside 8 weeks, fatigue under 50. Wrong: the
  Returner (Flat) or any boxer over 70 fatigue.
- TECHNIQUE WEEK: four pad nights, no sparring, the coach's hands go to the boxer whose next fight needs a card (R55's scarce
  hands now have a use in a quiet week). Right: a card the next offer is likely to need. Wrong: when a member drift is under way.
- AWAY SPAR DAY (Saturday at another club): every boxer sparring strangers; one tell on a named rival (the file opens, R19);
  costs $60 and a night's fatigue; the Junior may be noticed by the host (R58 seed). Right: a named rival is the likely next
  opponent; wrong with an overreached or injured boxer.
- OPEN MAT NIGHT: the pad evening goes to the members (R80, R74): more members through the door, a junior intake. Right when
  runway is under 8 weeks or members drift; wrong in camp, because it takes pad hands.
- REST WEEK: morale +4 GAME, fatigue off. Right: the Returner, the overreached, or before a block.
Anticipation: Monday's "Project results" and the READINESS LINE on Fights (how many of the next offer's answers each boxer owns).
Reward: readiness shows he can say yes to a short offer; and the base he built is the camp he starts from (weight, cards
usable), so the quiet weeks pay out as an easier camp, as in a real season.
Menus: Home (Monday, "pick this week's project", one click to take the coach's), Fighter > Week (a tag on the week), Fights (the
readiness line), the week review (Project results), Club > People/Money (open mat's members).
Reuses: WeekPhase, HabitFix, outside partner, Membership, TestDay, tape file. NEW: the project pick and its results (medium);
the away day and the readiness line (medium).
Numbers: x1.3 hard, +4 rest, 70 and 50 fatigue lines: GAME. $60 away day, the open-mat member gain, the tell odds: INVENTED. Real
clubs spar away and hold open nights: REAL (practice, no source read).
Kill list: (a) "A menu of bonuses." Answer: each has a wrong case and a cost in the same scarce thing (pad hands, fatigue, money),
so one week's pick is another week's loss; if the playtest shows one pick winning every base week, cut it. (b) "A quiet week
still needs a click." Answer: the default is the coach's pick, so the quiet week stays one click for a player who does not care.

### Mechanic 5 - THE LEDGER OF PROMISES (joins R55 "next camp is yours", R58 promise, R06 push-back, R33 sponsor, R52 goal, R68)
A coach's currency is his word. There is no new menu to make a promise: it is an option inside other decisions, always the cheap
one now. (a) the pad clash: "next camp is yours"; (b) a push-back ask: "next show" instead of overruling; (c) a sponsor wants a
fight early: "after the show"; (d) a bigger gym's offer: promise a title shot by a date; (e) the Junior asks to fight: "at 16".
Each lands as a row on Home: who, what, due week, what it binds. In the matching lay or offer the promised boxer carries a
chain mark: "Promised: first call on Reid next camp". Breaking is allowed and priced (-10 morale, Loyalty down, a bigger gym's
offer lands easier, GAME shape from R55/R58).
Decisions: make it (right when the due date is far, the fixtures are visible and the boxer is loyal: "later" is believed);
break an old one (right when keeping it costs a bout or an injury); overrule instead of promising (R06; right when the ask is
harmful). The collision is the stake: two promises on the same camp mean one is broken; the Ledger shows a capacity line for the
next 8 weeks ("you owe Reid's evenings twice"). Anticipation: the due date. Reward: a kept promise builds Standing with that
boxer: "later" is accepted without a morale hit, so a coach who keeps his word gets cheaper clashes: it opens decisions, not
numbers; and a loyal boxer refuses a rival offer.
Menus: Home (the Ledger, below the needs), Fighter > Overview (promises to her), Fights (the chain mark on offers),
Club > People (the lay), Fight prep (the due), the Why/review (kept or broken).
Reuses: PushBack, FighterGoal, CornerTrust morale, R55's lay, R58's offer when it exists. NEW: the Ledger (medium); Standing
(small; one number per boxer).
Numbers: -10 broken and +5 kept: INVENTED (R55). Standing: INVENTED. Capacity from the fixtures: GAME.
Kill list: (a) "Always promise, it is free." Answer: only until the capacity line shows a second promise on the same camp; the
price is collision, not money. (b) "Nothing makes you keep it." Answer: the lay screen marks the promised first; breaking is one
click but it appears on the Why and in Standing. If playtests show the player never has two promises at once, this mechanic is
a morale line and I would cut it.

## 3. How they join
Mechanic 1's report feeds mechanic 2's Wednesday and mechanic 3's rebuild; mechanic 3's offer price feeds the Friday answer and
may create a promise (mechanic 5); the promise binds the next Monday's lay (R55); a quiet week (mechanic 4) is where the
arc's soft sparring and the readiness for the next offer live. One chain, seven days, one loop week to week.

## 4. THE ORDER TO BUILD (smallest first; each step already improves the week)
1. The price line on the offer card (mechanic 3, step 4): morale to corner trust, wording only. Smallest; makes Fights smarter now.
2. The talk after a loss (mechanic 3, steps 1 to 2): one choice on the Why.
3. The Board strip on Monday (mechanic 2, only the drawing): anticipation without new rules.
4. Partner and intensity on sparring (mechanic 1) and its Friday report: needs R25's read rule.
5. The Wednesday stop with the weight drift (mechanic 2).
6. The Project week and the readiness line (mechanic 4).
7. The Ledger with R55's promise (mechanic 5), because it needs R55's lay first.
Measure before each from the harness: the price line (does a tune-up still win every base case), the Shield cost, how often the
Wednesday stop fires, whether War ever loses.

## 5. SCORES AS A HARD CRITIC (depth 0 to 10) AND WHAT WOULD TAKE EACH TO 10
| Mechanic | Depth | To reach 10 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 The Sparring Night | 6 | Partners with their own week (a partner is tired too, remembers a bad night); the report tied to a measured test accuracy; a worked case where War is the only right answer |
| 2 The Wednesday Call | 5 | Threads that differ every week by phase; a drift that has real causes (the twist, the load) not noise; the call reachable on Fighter > Week |
| 3 The Comeback | 6 | A rematch the only fight in the window by design; Standing and Loyalty visible; a measured cost for Shield; a career-long record of how losses were handled |
| 4 The Project Week | 5 | One project that competes with another for the same pad hands; the block remembered across weeks; the away day tied to the Ask for a fight (R37) |
| 5 The Ledger of Promises | 5 | Collisions in play data (two promises on one camp); Standing that opens a concrete option; the bigger gym's offer (R58) built so the promise has a taker |
I would not call any of them 7 yet: none is measured, and the three that carry the most invention (2, 4, 5) need a playtest to
find out if a second choice is ever right. I would call 1 and 3 closest, because they stand on the game's own numbers.
