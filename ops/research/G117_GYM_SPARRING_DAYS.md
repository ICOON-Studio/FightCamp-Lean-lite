# G117_GYM_SPARRING_DAYS (Grok, collected by Claude S85, 2026-09-26)

## What would a human do?
The head coach assigns pairs; fighters do not pick. Size first, then experience and style. Intensity is agreed before
the bell; the coach watches the first exchanges and stops the round if power climbs, a fighter is overwhelmed or hurt.
Hard work is rare, reserved for camp or a visiting gym, and still structured. Gym wars are a failure of control.

## 1. Intensity (SOURCED Tony Jeffries; boxingnewsonline 2020; seventhflow 2026; sigmanutrition 2017)
| Name | Power / pace | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Flow / play | ~10-30%, continuous | rhythm, creativity |
| Technical / light | ~30-60%, not trying to finish | daily skill work (most sparring, camp included) |
| Medium | ~60-80% pace, controlled | intermediate pressure |
| Hard / fight-camp | agreed fight pace, still structured | camp, visitors, dress rehearsal |
| Gym war | uncontrolled, ego | coaches reject |
Ingle model: open (hard) sparring only 3-4 weeks before a fight. 16 oz gloves are the gym default.

## 2. Volume
Amateur 1-2 sessions/week, 3-6 rounds, rarely hard (Delphi panel of 30 boxing coaches 2023, SOURCED). Pro between
camps 1-2 x 4-8 technical rounds. Pro in camp 2-4 mixed, building toward fight length +1; MMA/MT often only 3-4 hard
sessions in a 6-week camp. Hard contact stops 7-14 days out; taper 40-60% (SOURCED).

## 3. Matching
Same class preferred, +/-1 class common; never two first-timers; novice with a veteran who controls power; the
bigger/better-partner rule is used on purpose (SOURCED). A coach refuses: a big weight mismatch, two raw beginners, a
known gym-war partner, unagreed intensity. Mixed-sex pairing is common in small MMA rooms by consent, power scaled to
mass (SOURCED mmafighting 2022, apexmma 2023). Game note: FightCamp's standing rule "females never fight males" is for
BOUTS; whether it also governs gym sparring is an operator product ruling.

## 4. Outcomes
Amateur boxing training ~1.3 injuries / 1000 h; MMA training ~1.4 / 1000 h; training injuries are upper-limb sprains and
contusions, competition injuries are head/cuts (SOURCED JSAMS meta, Zazryn, MMA 2026). ~1 reportable injury per ~2,600
amateur rounds (PROXY). ~18 low-magnitude head impacts per athlete per session (SOURCED PMC8664317). Most light rounds
end on the bell (JUDGEMENT).

## 5. What sparring builds
Timing, distance, rhythm, reading, composure after being touched - the live opponent is the missing variable; pad work
and sparring both ~70% VO2peak (SOURCED 2010). No RCT isolates sparring vs bag on ring IQ (JUDGEMENT).

## 6. Rivalries
Same-gym pairings heat up; useful while technical, a problem with ego, retaliation or accumulated damage; the coach
separates when trust breaks (SOURCED mmafighting 2011, rossboxing 2016). Frequency not measured (PROXY).

## 7. First-release rules (JUDGEMENT)
- Bookings: 1 spar/week off camp, 2 in camp; Hard max 1/week, never inside 10 days of a booked bout; same pair max 2/week.
- Intensity: Technical (default), Light, Hard - no gym-war option.
- Rounds: Technical/Light 3-6 x 3 min; Hard 4-8, capped at fight length +1; the coach can cut a round.
- Gates: refuse > 1 class (~10-12%) weight gap, two novices, anyone on medical hold.
- Per round (loser; winner ~40% of it): Technical 2% bruise / 0.2% cut; Light 5% / 0.5%; Hard 12% / 2%. Early stop on a
  cut, a concussion flag, or a bruise on an already damaged region.
- Edge: winner a small visible form/timing tick for 3-7 days; loser no skill loss beyond the bruise.
- Rivalry: same pair 3+ spars in 21 days with a one-sided (2+ wins same fighter) or 2-Hard pattern -> small aggression/
  composure bump when both are in the gym and a higher Hard bruise chance; the coach can break the pair after a gym-war
  flag.
