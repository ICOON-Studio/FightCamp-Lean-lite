# G45 — SEVEN PERCENT OF A FIGHT

Grok, Expert, 2026-09-10. Driven in the in-app browser by Claude; 106 sources.
Research only — no code, no module. Spliced verbatim by Claude, who wrote this
header and the HANDOFF block and changed nothing else.

Commissioned the day R97 landed, because the adapter's measured drop rate turned
out to be far larger than G42 assumed when it licensed dropping: **896 impacts
emitted, 66 shown, 830 dropped — 92.6%.**

**The call: the screen must show a live, complete punch count computed from all
896, and spend the corner minute on the round board and the card.**

---

The measurement is in. In a seeded three-round bout the engine emitted 896
impacts; the 1.4-second beat presented 66; 830 were dropped — 92.6%. Downstream
reads (scorecards, damage, per-minute rates, stoppage) are computed from the 896.
The view is an honest sample of the same fight, not a different fight. The
unsolved surface is how to let a player watch 7% without being taught the wrong
density.

## §1 — How shipped products tell you they dropped events

**Madden Super Sim / GameCast.** When the 3D drops out, a GameCast-style text
ticker replaces it: one line per play, advancing at about one play every 1–2
seconds on Normal, two or three per second on Fast. Slow mode puts you back on
the broadcast camera and plays the snaps. The ticker is complete at the play
grain — every simulated snap gets a line — and is calibrated to a ~120–130 play
game regardless of quarter length or ticker speed. It does not claim completeness
at the physics grain. The box score is generated by the same Super Sim tables as
the ticker, so **the text layer and the card are the same object.** It does not
label missing micro-events, because the unit of record is the play, not the
block. Authenticity complaints land on Super Sim totals versus real NFL or versus
played-out snaps (rushing yards, low interceptions, ugly long-run distributions),
not on "I wasn't shown that screen." The 3D and the ticker are different
densities of one ledger. **They are not two ledgers.**

**Football Manager (text / 2D / 3D).** FM is the cleanest shipped answer to three
densities of one match. Highlight Mode is an explicit control: Full Match,
Extended, Key, Commentary only. The manual for the 3D "full" view notes it is not
actually ninety minutes. Commentary continues through dead time; shots, fouls,
and cards the 3D does not animate still increment the stats panel and still
appear as text. Between highlights, FM shows stats, staff notes, and — from FM26
— the old 2D pitch running the whole match at speed so the unshown minutes are
not a black gap. **The 3D clip is named a highlight. The stats panel is named the
match.** Players do complain that results feel "FMed" against xG and shot counts;
that is engine-versus-expectation, not highlight-versus-card. A highlight that
contradicts the stats panel is treated as a bug (clips playing under the stats
overlay, highlights failing to appear), not as a design.

**Out of the Park.** Play-by-play text is the primary surface. Pitch-by-pitch or
one-pitch mode, short or verbose text, ticker or line delay: the words are the
game. The 3D and the ball-flight scribble are optional costumes. OOTP's own
options warn that full commentary will go out of sync with the animation. Markus
Heinsohn has said the 3D and the text "don't match up 100% because it's really,
really tough for us to generate text and also have the 3D look at the text."
Community doctrine is blunt: the text box is what happened; the 3D is a
sometimes-poor attempt to visualize it. **That is a shipped admission that
picture and card can diverge, with the card winning.**

**Broadcast boxing, and CompuBox.** A televised fight shows every punch and still
runs a counter, because a viewer cannot count. CompuBox, on-air since the 1985
Mancini–Bramble rematch, is two ringside operators, one per fighter, each with
four keys: jab thrown/landed, power thrown/landed (a body key came later). It
does not score damage, ring generalship, or clean punching as a judge does. It
tallies volume. Graphics show live running totals and, between rounds, the
standard board: thrown / landed / percent, split jab versus power, plus-minus for
the round and the fight. Announcers cite the live number when a surge is obvious;
the full board is a between-rounds staple on HBO, ESPN, and anyone who licensed
the feed. Showtime spent long stretches without it.

**How it is framed matters more than the keys.** CompuBox's own line is that the
numbers "in no way, shape or form, determine a winner." Bob Canobbio: a
"statistical barometer of a fighter's activity," "quantitative, not qualitative,"
historically a "2% margin of error." Broadcasters still treat the board as
scripture; Jim Lampley would read it as if it settled a round. The numbers are
contested every time they disagree with a card. Thomas Hauser re-counted
Pacquiao–Bradley I round 7 off tape and got Bradley 16–12 landed against
CompuBox's 27–11 Pacquiao. Lomachenko's father counted 150 landed on Vasiliy
against a CompuBox figure thirty higher. Pacquiao–Horn and Conceição–Foster
produced promoter-level attacks (Arum: remote operators "weren't even at the
fight. That's a fraud"). Recounters of Beterbiev–Bivol round 1 have published
double-digit gaps on a single fighter in a single round. **The lesson for an
in-game counter is not "don't show one." It is: claim volume, not quality; do not
let the counter pose as the judge; expect the player to argue with it.**

HBO's 2009 **PunchZone** sat on top of CompuBox: two extra operators tagged
landing zone, and between rounds a 3D body lit seven zones (three head, three
body, one low) from yellow to orange to red as punches accumulated. **That is a
density signal that does not apologize.** It says: more landed here than you can
remember.

**Explicit "there was more than we showed you."** Named, shipped: FM's Highlight
Mode labels; Madden's "Play Key Moments" / "Play the Moment"; every YouTube
package titled "Fight Highlights"; the football half-time stats board over a
highlights broadcast; PunchZone's between-rounds body map. **Not found, after
looking:** a live combat-game view that draws an ellipsis, a "12 more in that
minute" meter, or a per-beat caption that the punch on screen is a sample. If
that control exists in a shipped title, it is not in the public record this paper
could reach.

## §2 — The honest-summary problem

The beat already knows, per window: how many impacts occurred, how many were
dropped, which one was chosen and why. The temptation is to print that.

**A live density read ("12 exchanges that minute").** *For:* it names the gap
instead of hoping the player infers it; CompuBox exists because the eye cannot
count; a manager-sim player already accepts "I watched Key Highlights and the
shot count is 19." *Against:* it turns the watch into an audit; "12 exchanges" is
shop talk, not a feeling; it advertises absence on the one surface that is
supposed to feel like a fight; a number that updates every 1.4 seconds will be
read as a loading bar. **Pick: do not put a drop-count on the live beat.** The
live beat shows a punch. Put the complete totals next to it. Put the density
sentence in the corner, where broadcasts already put it.

**Label the chosen punch REPRESENTATIVE.** Closest shipped precedent is a mode
label, not a punch label: FM "Key highlights," Madden "Play the Moment." No
shipped boxing or management UI stamps an individual animated strike "this stands
for twelve." **Labelling the punch is an apology. Labelling the mode once —
Highlights, not Documentary — is enough.** After that the punch is just the
punch.

**A live running count computed from all 896.** This is the CompuBox seat. It is
complete and honest. Sitting it beside a thin animation does advertise the
discrepancy when the count jumps fourteen and the view showed one. **That
advertisement is the point**, and it is how television already works: the camera
showed the exchange, the bug graphic says 28–19. Reconcile, don't hide. The trap
is the other design: increment the counter only on the 66. That makes picture and
card agree by lying. **The counter must be the 896 or it must not exist.**

**The knockdown after two visible hits.** This is a real risk, not only a
designer's worry, but the named complaints come from games that claimed the
picture *was* the card. Undisputed and the late Fight Night arguments are
hit-registration arguments: a punch that visibly landed did not count, or a punch
that visibly missed did. Players call that broken because those games sold the
animation as the official event. Sims that separate picture from card get a
different complaint. OOTP players rage at 3D fielders who "eat the ball" while
the text says the runner is out — and then they trust the text. FM players say
they were FMed when twenty shots become a 0–1. They do not say the goal was fake;
they say they didn't see the other shot. **A knockdown that the engine earned on
accumulated wear, while the view showed two punches, will read as scripted unless
the surface has already been teaching accumulation:** the live count climbing,
the body starting to look like the damage model, the corner saying he took
twenty-eight. Broadcasts cover "he's been taking them all round" with exactly
those three tools. Without them, the knockdown is a cutscene.

## §3 — The selection rule, judged

The adapter's order — earliest KNOCKDOWN, else earliest SHOCKED, else highest
total WEAR, ties to the earliest tick — is a broadcast editor's order. Consequence
first. For a 1.4-second slot that can hold one exchange, **that is the right
ranking.**

The cost is real. A night of only the biggest punch in each window is a highlight
reel with no jab. Fight-highlight packages on television and YouTube are
consequence-heavy for the same reason: knockdowns, the punch that opened the cut,
the flurry that forced the referee in. The jab appears as texture inside those
clips, not as an equal citizen with its own slot. A real editor would also cut one
"he's boxing well" passage per round — a jab rally, a clinch break, a minute of
control. They can do that because they have a thirty-second package and the right
to leave the live timeline. **A 1.4-second beat that must remain the exchange
that actually happened in that window cannot reach eight seconds backward for the
jab that set the right hand up.** Stitching the jab from earlier in the window
onto a later payoff is the thing G42 refused: it becomes a different exchange.

**Sometimes show an ordinary exchange?** Only when the window has no knockdown,
no shocked, and wear is flat. In that window the current rule already picks an
ordinary punch (highest wear among ordinary, earliest tick). Do not add a second
rule that prefers ordinary while a shocked punch exists. That hides the fight. Do
not add randomness. Do not reach into a neighbouring window for a "better
representative."

**Recency instead of consequence** (always the last impact in the window). Kill
it. A knockdown at the start of the window would lose to a jab at the end. That
is a different fight.

**Show two adjacent impacts if they are the same engine exchange.** Only if the
engine already groups them as one exchange. Assembling two high-wear punches that
did not touch each other is fabrication. If the grouping is not already a fact in
the adapter, do not invent it here.

**The refusal check:** every alternative that pulls an impact from outside the
window, composites two windows, or demotes a knockdown to make room for texture,
shows a **different** fight. Consequence-first inside the window shows a
**thinner** one. **Keep the rule.** Give the jab its due in the corner totals
(jab vs power, thrown vs landed) where CompuBox already puts it.

## §4 — The round boundary

G44 put the corner minute here. The rest of that minute is the reconcile.

Between rounds the engine has the complete truth: the round's card, both totals,
the damage, the trauma. Broadcasts spend that minute on a stable package: replay
of the round's decisive exchange; CompuBox round board (thrown / landed / jab /
power, sometimes body); unofficial scorecard (Harold Lederman on HBO every two or
three rounds; ESPN's unofficial card often every round; open-scoring
jurisdictions read the real cards at 4 and 8); corner cameras; and, when a
production has it, a body-map of where the round landed. **That package is how a
viewer who blinked still knows what the round was.**

It is also how a highlights-only football broadcast survives half-time. **The
live view can be thin if the break is complete.** Print the round the player did
not see: who out-landed whom, by how much, jab versus power, where the wear
moved, how the judge scored it. Replay the one exchange the adapter already chose.
Do not attempt to animate the other sixty. The corner is text, board, and one
clip. **That is enough to make the live 7% acceptable, provided the live counter
has not been lying for the three minutes prior.**

## §5 — The refusals

- **Do not fabricate punches to pad the animation.** Filling the 1.4 seconds with
  strikes that did not happen is a different fight. The 66 must be a subset of
  the 896.
- **Do not speed the clip to fit more in.** G42 §5 already refused this:
  compressing several exchanges into one beat makes a different exchange. Cite
  G42 §5 and stop.
- **Do not ship a density meter nobody understands.** "830 dropped" and "12 in
  that minute" are production numbers. They belong in this paper, not on the
  punch.
- **Do not let the counter disagree with the card.** Both are computed from the
  896. A counter that follows the 66 is a second, false card.
- **Do not turn the honest gap into a UI apology.** No "we couldn't show
  everything." No toast. No ellipsis over the ropes. The mode can be named
  Highlights once. After that the surface is a fight plus a board, which is what
  television is.
- **Do not make the animation the scoring authority.** That is the Undisputed
  trap. The picture is a sample. The card is the fight.
- **Do not stitch a setup from a different window onto a payoff.** Texture is the
  corner's job.

## §6 — The one-sentence call

**The screen must show a live, complete punch count — thrown and landed, running
through the round and the fight — computed from all 896, and it must spend the
corner minute on the round board and the card; that is the one thing it does not
show today.**

The 66 stay samples. They are not asked to carry volume, rhythm, or justification
for a knockdown. The board carries volume the way CompuBox carries it on a
broadcast that already showed every punch and still needed a number. The corner
carries the sentence the live view cannot: what the other 93% amounted to. At
92.6% drop **the picture cannot be the proof. It can only be the proof's clip.**
The surface that sits between picture and card is not a confession. It is the
same bug graphic television already runs, pointed at an engine that, unlike two
men with keypads, actually has the count.
