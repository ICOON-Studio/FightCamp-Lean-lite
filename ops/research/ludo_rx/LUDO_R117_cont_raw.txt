Trap 1: The "Shout Spam" MMORPG Trap
The Risk: In management sims, giving active mid-round buttons risks transforming a deliberate tactical watcher into an APM-addicted cooldown-masher.
The Safeguards:
Strict Mechanical Ceilings: The 25-second cooldown and 3-call cap per 180-second round limit the player to a maximum of 3 inputs every 3 minutes.
Auditory Fatigue / "The Tuned-Out Corner": If a coach triggers a shout when no relevant tactical condition is met (e.g., yelling "Step on the gas!" while the opponent is completely fresh at 95% stamina and circling), the fighter rolls a Common Sense Check: $$\text{CommonSenseRoll} = \text{Fighter Ring IQ} - 25$$ If passed, the fighter ignores the illogical call entirely, prints a log warning ("Leo looks over at the corner with confusion — ignores the call"), and burns the 25-second cooldown without any benefit.
Trust Degradation: Calling unlearned (Raw) tactics that fail repeatedly chips away at Trust by $-2.0$ per incident, which in turn degrades corner stool recovery efficiency between rounds.
Trap 2: The "Must-Pick" Shout
The Risk: If one shout (e.g., GO_TO_THE_BODY or TIME_THE_COUNTER) yields a strictly superior damage-per-second ratio, players will map it to every fight and ignore the remaining 11 tactics.
The Safeguards:
Symmetric Counter-Risks: Every positive lever has an equal, opposing structural vulnerability coded directly into the damage engine. GO_TO_THE_BODY lowers head defense; if the opponent throws an uppercut, that uppercut gets a $+12%$ accuracy and $+15%$ damage buff.
AI Pattern Adaptation: If the player uses the same shout twice in back-to-back rounds, the opponent's corner adaptive logic notes it. In the third execution, the opponent's counter-strategy probability against that tactic jumps by $+40%$. Repeating a shout turns it from a surprise adjustment into a tactical trap.
Trap 3: Punishing the "Hands-Off" Silent Player
The Risk: FightCamp is primarily an in-depth management simulation. If an autonomous, highly trained fighter loses every fight unless the player micro-manages apron yells, the game breaks its identity and violates its core pitch.
The Safeguards:
The Stool Remains Primary: $75%$ of fight-night coaching impact must remain on the stool between rounds (the tone choice, cutman work, and master tactical plans). Mid-round apron shouts are tactical course corrections, not operational lifelines.
Autonomous Baseline: An unprompted fighter defaults to their natural archetype and camp plan with $100%$ of their natural attribute efficiency. Apron shouts merely apply transient, targeted deltas ($+5%$ to $+15%$) to solve specific micro-crises.
Trap 4: Blunders Feeling Arbitrary or Cheap
The Risk: A player yells "Tie him up!", their fighter blunders, gets flash-knocked out, and the player rage-quits, calling the system rigged dice.
The Safeguards:
Blatant Visual UI Warning: If a shout is in the Raw tier ($<30%$), the button features a high-contrast red warning icon [!], and hovering over it displays explicit tooltip odds: RISK: 20% BLUNDER / 50% IGNORE.
Causal Transparency in the Why-Lines: The fight engine log never reports a generic failure. It explicitly logs the mechanical breakdown:
"Leo Vance attempted an unpracticed Clinch (Mastery 18%) -> failed level change -> absorbed Miller's counter uppercut flush (Clean Hit, Chin 82 vs Power 84)"
Blunder Damage Caps: A blunder grants the opponent a high-percentage counter window, but does not roll an automatic one-hit knockout. It guarantees a clean counter hit that rolls against the standard Chin and Damage formulas already established in r114.

1. What Share of Fights a Well-Drilled Corner Swings vs. a Silent Corner
The 50/50 Matchup Baseline: In a simulated 10-round fight between two statistically identical fighters ($50.0%$ baseline win rate for both):
A corner utilizing Sharp or Drilled-In shouts targeted correctly at live telemetry reads will achieve a $62.5%$ win rate over a completely silent corner.
The Swing: A well-drilled corner accounts for an exact $+12.5%$ net swing in match outcome.
If the matchup is heavily asymmetrical (e.g., an underdog out-matched by $15$ attribute points across the board), corner shouts can elevate winning chances from an impossible $12%$ up to a viable puncher's chance of $22%$, but shouts will never bridge a massive skill gap on their own.
2. Expected Connect/Score Effect per Clean Execution
Single Clean Execution Impact: Over a standard shout duration ($10\text{ s}$ to $18\text{ s}$):
Net Punch Differential: A clean shout produces a net punch connection advantage of $+1.8\text{ landed punches}$ (either via $+1.2$ punches landed offensively or $-0.6$ opponent clean shots avoided via evasive calls).
Round Scoring Impact: In a tight round (typically decided by a margin of 2–3 clean punches on the Compubox-style engine tally), landing one well-timed clean shout shifts the round scorecard swing probability by $+28%$.
Landing two successful shouts in one round swings the round win probability to the caller by $+64%$.

A $68%$ drop in overall fighter composure.
A $-4.2\text{ point}$ deficit on the official scorecards compared to a silent corner baseline.
