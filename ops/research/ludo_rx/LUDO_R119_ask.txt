B. PUNCH FEEL - a benchmark from a shipped boxing game.
We measured (numbers only, nothing copied) the animation event timings of 3,152 clips in a released boxing game. Median seconds from clip start: jab head - hitbox on 0.256, impact 0.365, hitbox off 0.419, control returned 0.491 (impact to control 0.116 s). Cross head 0.350 / 0.501 / 0.562 / 0.636 (0.174). Hook head 0.383 / 0.534 / 0.585 / 0.723 (0.151). Uppercut head 0.326 / 0.489 / 0.572 / 0.690 (0.167). Body shots: impact to control 0.11-0.17 s. After control returns, the clip's tail keeps playing and blends out under the next action. A feint is a cut-off ~0.05-0.1 s before impact; a "vulnerability" window marks when the puncher can be countered.
OURS (contact / fighter locked until): jab 0.25 / 0.667, cross 0.30 / 0.65, hook 0.417 / 0.983, jab to body 0.458 / 1.833, body hook 0.70 / 2.08, rear hook body 0.58 / 1.70. So our fighter is locked 0.42 s (jab) to 1.4 s (body shots) after contact - 3 to 10 times longer than the benchmark. The operator sees stiff, one-at-a-time exchanges.
Questions: (1) Do we adopt "control returns at impact + ~0.12-0.17 s, the tail blends out"? (2) Our body shots also land slow (body hook 0.70 vs 0.45) - target contact time per body punch? (3) Should a counter window (vulnerability) on the puncher be a rule, how long, and does it scale with fatigue/skill?
