
Part 1: Deep Management Training-Floor HUD Specification

The redesign replaces mobile-style floating badges and oversized aesthetic cards with a dense, number-led management interface modeled after Football Manager, Motorsport Manager, and Out of the Park Baseball.

Layout dimensions are based on a 1920×1080 reference canvas using Godot 4.7 Control nodes, with a warm dark palette (PANEL #2a2019, LINE #6b5638, TEXT #f6eddc, MUTED #b5a68c, GOLD #f0c233, GO #4fc46a, BAD #e5433a).

Detailed Element Specifications
Header Block (Height: 56 px):
Top line: Fighter Name in Inter Bold 16 px (#f6eddc), right-aligned record chip in Inter Bold 11 px (#f0c233) within a subtle inset panel (#1e1712).
Sub line: Age (23), Weight Class (Super Welter), Style (counter_puncher), Stance (Orthodox), Reach (72.0") rendered at 11 px regular (#b5a68c).
Current Station & Coach Block (Height: 74 px):
Active Station: "Heavy Bag 1" in 13 px Bold (#f6eddc).
Coach Presence: Chip displaying "[Coach Barone: +25% GAINS]" in #4fc46a if present, or "[UNCOACHED: BASE RATE]" in #b5a68c if the coach is elsewhere.
Session Progress: Thin 4 px horizontal bar (#6b5638 background, #f0c233 fill) showing elapsed time ("38m elapsed | 22m remaining").
Condition & Fatigue Block (Height: 64 px):
Stamina: Label, numeric value (82 / 100), live delta tag ([-1.4/m]), and a 6 px segmented progress bar (#4fc46a fill).
Strain: Label, numeric value (24 / 100), live accumulation rate ([+0.6/m]), and a 6 px segmented progress bar (#e5433a fill).
Day State Indicator: Monospace state chip: "TODAY: FRESH" (#4fc46a), "WORKED" (#f0c233), or "EXHAUSTED" (#e5433a).
Attributes Table (Height: 290 px):
8 Core Attributes grouped into Physical, Technical, and Mental & Durability.
4-Column Tabular Grid:
Attribute Name (12 px Regular, #f6eddc, width: 140 px).
Current Value (12 px Bold, #f6eddc, width: 45 px, right-aligned).
Today’s Training Delta (11 px Mono, +0.4 in #4fc46a, 0.0 in #b5a68c, width: 60 px, right-aligned).
Potential/Ceiling (11 px Regular, #b5a68c, width: 45 px, right-aligned; represents hard ceiling). Behind the current number, a thin 2 px horizontal rule extends to the ceiling value to display visual progress toward career peak.
How the Player Reads "How Good Is He":
At a glance: The Overall Class Bracket (e.g., Regional Prospect vs. World Ranked) is derived from the mean of his active ratings, and the color of the Current numbers shifts: < 50 (Grey #8c8273), 50-69 (White #f6eddc), 70-84 (Gold #f0c233), 85+ (Cyan/Elite #5eead4).
In detail: The player reads the delta column to evaluate training efficiency per minute, checks current vs ceiling to spot plateaus, and hovers over any attribute to inspect modifiers.

Part 2: Coach Walk Timing Architecture & Design Verdict
The Simulation Dilemma
Walking speed: $1.5\text{ m/s}$ (human pacing).
Cross-gym transit distance: $32\text{ m}$ (from southwest corner office/locker room to northeast heavy bag strip).
Real-world transit time: $32 / 1.5 = 21.33\text{ seconds}$.
Game clock scaling: $0.2\text{ real seconds} = 1.0\text{ game minute}$ ($1\text{ real second} = 5\text{ game minutes}$).
Result: $21.33\text{ seconds} \times 5 = \mathbf{106.6\text{ game minutes}}$ spent walking uncoached. In a standard 60-minute training session, the entire block would conclude before the coach even reaches the station.
Analysis of the Options
Option A: Keep it (~100 uncoached game minutes).
Flaw: A coach takes almost 2 hours to walk 30 meters. This causes severe dissonance: either sessions must be unrealistically long (4+ hours), or moving the coach across the gym wastes entire training blocks. It punishes the player for spatial scale rather than strategic planning.
Option B: Count the bonus instantly upon dispatch.
Flaw: Causes immediate disconnect with the 3D game world. If the coach provides active pad instruction while physically stuck walking past the reception desk, the 3D simulation becomes an irrelevant visual layer disconnected from the game mechanics.
Option C: Time dilation during transit (match fighter walk rules).
Flaw: If the clock slows down every time the coach relocates, and the player re-assigns the coach frequently between fighters, the simulation pace drops drastically, making the gym floor crawl.
The Verdict: Two-Tier Transit Speed with "Drill Prep" Buffer

Implement a Three-Part System standard in sim titles like Two Point Campus and Motorsport Manager:

Active Brisk Transit Speed ($2.8\text{ m/s}$ Coach Locomotion):
When commanded to a station across the floor, the head coach moves with urgency ($2.8\text{ m/s}$ purposeful stride / jog).
A $32\text{ m}$ diagonal cross-gym walk takes $11.4\text{ real seconds}$.
Game-Clock Time Dilation Factor ($0.5\times$ during Coach Relocation):
While the coach is actively in transit, gym time dilation temporarily shifts from $0.2\text{ s/min}$ to $0.4\text{ s/min}$.
Under this rate, $11.4\text{ real seconds}$ converts to: $$\frac{11.4\text{ s}}{0.4\text{ s/min}} = \mathbf{28.5\text{ game minutes}}$$
The "Independent Drill Warmup" Rule (Mechanic Rationale):
In real boxing gyms, when a coach says "Get on Heavy Bag 1, I'll be right over," the fighter does not stand idle or train improperly; they wrap up, grease up, stretch, and work preliminary warm-up combinations.
Rule: A fighter assigned to a station receives 90% standard gains during their first 25–30 minutes of a block while warming up independently. The coach's arrival introduces the +25% to +40% mastery bonus for the remainder of the session.
Result: The player experiences realistic spatial consequences (station positioning matters, and pairing adjacent stations is optimal), but a cross-gym walk consumes only about 25 game minutes—leaving the majority of a 60-to-90-minute block for high-gain coached training. Spatial gym planning remains meaningful without grinding the simulation to a halt.
