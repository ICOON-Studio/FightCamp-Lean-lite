A. The Floating, Flared Elbow (Lack of Rib Connection)

In authentic boxing, the rear elbow is grounded against the right floating ribs ($10\text{th}$ rib) to shield the liver and spleen. Here, the humerus (upper arm) is abducted outward at roughly $42^\circ\text{ to } 48^\circ$ from the sagittal plane, projecting the elbow out into empty space.

There is a gaping $16\text{ cm to } 18\text{ cm}$ pocket of empty air between the medial condyle of the elbow and the lateral latissimus/ribcage.
This leaves the entire right flank completely exposed, which contradicts a standard boxing defensive shell.
B. The Sagittal Flare & Humerus Angle

Seen from the side, the upper arm hangs forward at roughly $35^\circ$ from the vertical torso line. Instead of the triceps resting naturally along the ribcage, the arm is suspended by isometric shoulder flexion. This makes the fighter look like he is attempting an awkward peek-a-boo guard without tucking his head, or holding up a telephone receiver.

C. The "Candy-Wrapper" Collapsed Lump (Missing Olecranon Definition)

The elbow joint does not read as an articulated human skeletal hinge.

The bony prominence of the olecranon process (the elbow point) is completely absent.
Instead, the mesh presents a smooth, rounded, sausage-like curved tube with visible volume loss at the medial fold.
This is classic dual-quaternion / linear-blend skinning collapse: as the elbow acute angle closes past $80^\circ$ (measured at $69^\circ\text{--}76^\circ$), the lack of radial twist bones and corrective blend shapes pinches the vertices inward, turning an anatomical joint into a bent rubber pipe.
D. Glove Placement & The Drifting Wrist Defect

The measured wrist-to-head distance ($0.31\text{ m}$ at rest, compressing down to $0.19\text{ m}$ during the lead jab) reveals that the glove is not properly locked to the boxer's head structure:

At rest, $0.31\text{ m}$ ($31\text{ cm}$) places the rear glove too far out in front of the cheek, floating in mid-air.
When the lead jab fires, the shoulder IK or retarget layer pulls the rear hand inward by $12\text{ cm}$, causing the thumb of the glove to visibly collide with and penetrate the right cheek/jawbone ($0.19\text{ m}$ from center of skull is inside the facial skin boundary of a heavyweight/middleweight mesh).

3. ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS IN GODOT 4 / METAHUMAN PIPELINE

The fact that "twist bones on/off render identically" is the critical clue. In Unreal Engine, MetaHumans rely on Control Rig, secondary helper bones, and Pose Driver RBF (Radial Basis Function) corrective morph targets.

When brought into Godot 4 via FBX/glTF, these systems often fail to map cleanly. Here are the four primary failure points and how to isolate each:

                             METAHUMAN -> GODOT 4 FAILURE TREE
                                              |
      +-----------------------+---------------+-----------------------+-----------------------+
      |                       |                                       |                       |
[1. DEAD TWIST BONES]   [2. LOST RBF MORPHS]            [3. RETARGET ROLL ERROR]    [4. POSE LAYER IK BLEED]
Rotations stay on       Skinning loses volume           Humerus bone axis           Lead jab animation pulls
primary joint; mesh     at sharp flexion                points forward/yawed        spine, dragging rear
twists like candy.      (no olecranon peak).            in rest bind pose.          arm out of world-lock.

Cause 1: Twist Bones Are Unweighted or Unassigned in the Godot Skin
Why it happens: MetaHuman rigs have upperarm_twist_01, lowerarm_twist_01, etc. If Godot imports the mesh and binds all vertex weights exclusively to upperarm_r and lowerarm_r, the twist bones will move in the skeleton tree, but have zero vertex weights. Turning them on/off has zero visual effect.
The Diagnostic Test (One Measurement):
In Godot’s Mesh Inspector, inspect the Skin resource on the body mesh. Query the vertex group weight array for bone index lowerarm_twist_01_r.
The Gate: If max_weight == 0.000, the twist bones are dead/unweighted in the importer.
Cause 2: Missing Unreal Engine Corrective Blend Shapes (RBF Flexion Morphs)
Why it happens: MetaHuman elbows do not rely on pure linear skin weights at acute angles ($<80^\circ$). UE fires pose-driven morphs: FAC_Elbow_Flex_90_R and FAC_Triceps_Bulge_R, which push vertices outward to form the pointed bony olecranon process. When exported via simple glTF/FBX, the animation clip contains bone tracks, but the morphological curves are stripped or not evaluated by Godot’s AnimationTree.
The Diagnostic Test (One Measurement):
Measure the cross-sectional bounding thickness of the elbow joint mesh at the apex of the fold.
The Gate: In an anatomical elbow at $70^\circ$, joint depth expands to $\approx 11.5\text{ cm}$ due to bone protrusion. If the mesh measures $\le 8.2\text{ cm}$, you have pure linear skin collapse without corrective blend shapes.
Cause 3: Upper-Arm Roll / Axis Mismatch in Animation Retarget
Why it happens: MetaHuman skeletal templates have specific bone roll vectors ($X$-forward, $Y$-up in Maya; or $Y$-forward, $Z$-up in Godot). If the Godot SkeletonProfileHumanoid mapping has the upperarm_r roll offset off by $30^\circ\text{ to } 45^\circ$, an intended shoulder tuck transforms partially into humerus outward abduction.
The Diagnostic Test (One Measurement):
Print the local transform Euler angles of upperarm_r in the rest pose versus the active guard pose.
The Gate: If the local roll/twist axis exhibits an offset $> 25.0^\circ$ relative to the clavicle plane while the animation clip expects $0.0^\circ$, the retarget profile has an uncompensated bone axis roll.
Cause 4: Additive/Blend Layer Root & Spine Bleed
Why it happens: The user noted: "wrist is 0.31m at rest, but 0.19m when he jabs with the other hand." The lead jab clip contains a spinal rotation (thoracic twist) to drive reach. If the rear arm is animated in local bone space on an additive track, the rotation of the spine swings the right shoulder forward and pushes the rear glove directly into the boxer's face.
The Diagnostic Test (One Measurement):
Measure the world-space translation delta of the rear wrist bone (hand_r) between frame 0 (idle) and the peak extension frame of the lead jab.
The Gate: If the rear hand translates in world space by $> 0.08\text{ m}$ inward toward the skull centerline, the rear guard lacks a world-space IK clamp or compensation node during asymmetric punches.
