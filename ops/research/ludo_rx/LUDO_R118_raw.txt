CRITICAL ARCHITECTURAL DECISION: TWO-PHASE STOOL CARD
Why the 600 px Vertical Space Cannot Hold Everything Simultaneously

A 600 px tall card attempting to stack:

Header + Clock ($48\text{ px}$)
Fighter Vitals / Meters / Scores ($40\text{ px}$)
Four Cutman Job Cards ($4 \times 44\text{ px} + \text{gaps} = 196\text{ px}$)
Three Chief Second Speech Lines with Inline Action Anchors ($3 \times 42\text{ px} + \text{gaps} = 144\text{ px}$)
8 Order Buttons ($2\text{ rows} \times 28\text{ px} + \text{gap} = 62\text{ px}$)
3 Composure Word Buttons ($32\text{ px}$)
Towel & Send-Out Actions ($36\text{ px}$)
Internal margins & padding ($60\text{ px}$)

Total required vertical height: $618\text{ px}$ without damage lines, meters, or margin breathability. This is why the screenshot suffered catastrophic text-clipping, button stacking ("THROW THE TOWEL" and "THE TRUTH" overlapping "SEND HIM OUT"), and meter collisions over the text.

The Solution: Real Boxing Phase Gating

In a real boxing corner, the 60 seconds is strictly structured:

T = 60s to 40s (PHASE 1: THE CUTMAN & RECOVERY): The fighter sits down on the stool. The cutman works on bleeding and swelling; the second rinses the mouthpiece and manages water/ice.
T = 40s to 10s (PHASE 2: THE CHIEF SECOND): The cutman steps down. The trainer grabs the fighter’s chin, locks eyes, and delivers three concise tactical reads (HIM, YOU, TANK) and assigns tactical orders.
T = 10s to 0s (PHASE 3: SECONDS OUT): "Seconds Out!" is called. Final composure word chosen, mouthpiece in, send him out.

Therefore, the card’s lower-right interactive payload ($572 \times 320\text{ px}$) dynamically swaps between Phase 1 (Job Cards) and Phase 2 (The Three Chief Second Lines + Tactical Orders), with a manual toggle tab so the player can switch between them at will at any second.

1. Phase 1: Walking to the Stool (T = 60s to 40s)
Fighter sits down. Heart rate drops dynamically (e.g., $175 \to 158\text{ bpm}$).
Cutman workspace is active by default. The player spends their 2 Action Points on ice, grease, swab, or adrenaline.
The Chief Second slot shows: "[LISTENING TO CUTMAN REPORT...]".
2. Phase 2: The Chief Second Speaks (T = 40s to 10s)
At $T = 40\text{ s}$, the workspace automatically flips to the Chief Second lines (unless manually overridden by the player).
Reveal Cadence (Audio-Synced Fast Typewriter):
$T = 40.0\text{ s}$: Slot 1 (HIM) snaps in with typewriter reveal over $0.4\text{ s}$. Audio: Dry punch-mitt clack.
$T = 38.5\text{ s}$: Slot 2 (YOU) snaps in over $0.4\text{ s}$.
$T = 37.0\text{ s}$: Slot 3 (GAS) snaps in over $0.4\text{ s}$.
All three lines remain fixed on screen until $T = 0\text{ s}$.
3. Phase 3: Seconds Out & Confirmation (T = 10s to 0s)
At $T = 10\text{ s}$, a sharp whistle sample plays (referee_whistle_double.wav).
Header text changes to: "[PHASE: SECONDS OUT — CLEAR THE RING]".
If the player has not touched the composure words, the system flashes [TELL THE TRUTH] in amber as the recommended baseline.
4. The Auto-Pick Protocol (Zero-Click Fallback)

If the player takes no action before the timer expires or presses SEND HIM OUT instantly:

Cutman Auto-Pick: The cutman applies standard Vaseline/Grease and cold water rinse (costs 0 AP, avoids cut escalation).
Tactical Auto-Pick: The system looks at Slot 3 (GAS). If Stamina $< 30%$, it auto-assigns BREATHER. If Stamina $\ge 30%$, it preserves the fighter's existing baseline PLAN.
Composure Auto-Pick: If the fighter is Rattled/Broken, it defaults to CALM HIM DOWN; if Complacent, it defaults to FIRE HIM UP; otherwise TELL THE TRUTH.
Design Guarantee: A player who lets the clock run out is never penalized with a game-breaking blunder; they receive a conservative, baseline maintenance round.
D. THE FIX FOR DEFECTS 1 AND 2 (ELIMINATING OVERLAPS)
Defect 1 Fix: The Meter Collision
The Root Bug: The previous layout placed CUT / SEC / CHI text labels directly across $Y$-coordinates occupied by the dynamic multi-line coach string, causing them to render on top of each other.
The Structural Fix:
The legacy CUT / SEC / CHI vertical meters are removed from the center text area.
CUT risk is moved to its correct home: the Damage Legend beneath the Body Diagram ($X = 546, Y = 650$), expressed in plain medical terms: "DOCTOR RISK: 15%".
SEC (Second / Crew Action Points) is moved to the Top Vitals Bar ($X = 1145, Y = 301$) as discrete UI pips: "CREW AP: [■][■]".
CHI (Chin / Brain Trauma) is integrated directly as a status underline beneath the Tank bar.
The entire center area from $X = 766$ to $X = 1376$ ($610\text{ px}$ wide) is now dedicated strictly to text and buttons with zero background meters behind them.
Defect 2 Fix: Button Stacking and Stray Glyph Bleed-Through
The Root Bug: THROW THE TOWEL and THE TRUTH were positioned in the same bounding rect as SEND HIM OUT, while hidden template buttons from Godot's visual tree were leaking characters ("8", "U") through transparent button backgrounds.
The Structural Fix:
Relocate THROW THE TOWEL: Moved to the top-right of the header ($X = 1270, Y = 248, W = 105, H = 28$). It is completely isolated from the bottom action row. Throwing the towel now requires a two-step click confirmation, eliminating accidental forfeiture.
Isolate SEND HIM OUT: Occupies its own dedicated horizontal row at the bottom ($X = 766, Y = 680, W = 450, H = 38$), entirely separated from the tone buttons.
Position Tone Words: The three tone options (CALM, FIRE, TRUTH) form a dedicated 3-column row cleanly stacked above the exit button at $Y = 640$ ($H = 32\text{ px}$).
Stray Glyph Elimination: Set Godot container nodes to clip_contents = true, set all inactive button nodes to visible = false (rather than modulating alpha to 0), and enforce solid #11161F opaque backgrounds on all order button containers.
E. THE REAL BOXING CORNER: WHY EACH DECISION IS MADE

Why separate the Cutman and Trainer into two distinct phases?

In a real corner, the trainer does not shout tactical instructions into a fighter's ear while the cutman is actively jamming an adrenaline Q-tip into an open arterial bleed; the fighter can only process one stimulus at a time.

Why limit the trainer to exactly three lines (HIM, YOU, GAS)?

A concussed athlete with an elevated heart rate ($170\text{ bpm}$) cannot digest an essay; real championship trainers communicate with a rigid structure: what the opponent is giving away, what error the fighter must correct immediately, and how to manage the remaining gas tank.

Why provide inline order shortcuts directly next to each speech line?

When a coach identifies that the opponent's jab is falling short, the immediate next action is selecting the counter strategy; tying the diagnostic read directly to the solution eliminates UI hunting and respects the manager's mental model.

Why isolate "Throw the Towel" to the top header rather than the bottom action area?

Surrendering a bout is a rare, grave managerial failure that should never share screen space or risk misclicks with the routine flow of sending a fighter back out for the next round.

Why display the opponent's scorecard tally on our corner card?

Corners constantly track round math; knowing whether you are up on the cards or desperately need a finish fundamentally dictates whether the next instruction is BREATHER or FINISH.

Why allow a maximum of two simultaneous tactical orders?

Telling a fighter to simultaneously box, pressure, go to the body, and maintain range leads to cognitive overload; real boxing corners assign one primary objective and at most one secondary modifier.

Why implement an automated zero-click fallback system?

FightCamp is a simulation of competence; an experienced corner team does not freeze and watch their athlete bleed out simply because the manager stepped away from the mouse.
