A1. The Right Cutaway Rule for FightCamp

The rule you designed (near walls cut to a low stub, far walls at full height with all hung decor intact) is the gold standard for isometric, dense indoor management games. Here is the comparative evaluation:

| Approach | Visual Readability | Gameplay Readability | Technical Overhead | Fit for FightCamp |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Fully Down (No Shell Walls) | Poor. Floats props in mid-air (as seen in your screenshot). Loss of interior architectural weight. | Excellent floor view; zero occlusion. | Lowest draw call / geometry cost. | Unacceptable. It breaks the "lived-in, gritty brick boxing gym" aesthetic mandated by your GDD. |
| Alpha / Dither Transparency | Mediocre. Ghostly brick patterns visually fight with 3D characters, heavy bags, and UI stat labels. | Poor during motion. Occlusion persists mentally even at $\alpha = 0.25$. | High (alpha-blended depth sorting bugs, shadow acne, fill-rate penalties). | Reject. Transparent walls produce visual noise, making it difficult to read fighter stamina and pulse bars. |
| 3-Way Manual Toggle (The Sims) | High control, but high cognitive load. Players constantly toggle up to take screenshots and down to play. | Variable. | Two Point Cutaway / Dot-Product Stub (Proposed) | High. Maintains room grounding via stubs while leaving the entire floor visible. | Flawless. Camera-facing obstacles drop; the background backdrops remain solid. | Minimal. Evaluated only when rotating or stepping zoom. | Selected Default. Design Recommendation
System Default: Dynamic 2-Wall Cutaway (Cut to Stub). At any of the 4 discrete viewpoints, the two walls facing the camera drop to a 0.4 m stub. The two rear walls remain at full height (3.6 m).
Settings Override: Provide a binary toggle in Settings / Video: Wall Rendering: [Dynamic Cutaway | Always Flat Stub]. Never include a manual "Walls Full Up" mode during live training rounds, as it completely obscures stations along the South/East boundaries.
A2. Stub Height, Caps, and Door Openings
Stub Height ($0.4\text{ m}$):
$0.4\text{ m}$ matches standard architectural wainscoting/skirting line height.
It sits lower than a boxer’s knee ($~0.5\text{ m}$ on standard stylized athletic rigs), preventing occlusion of footwork, skipping ropes, or dropped weights.
It remains tall enough to visually catch interior floor edges and floor shadow projections.
Top Cap & Materialization:
Do not leave hollow backfaces or open clipped geometry.
Apply a distinct $0.06\text{ m}$ thick wooden or industrial concrete cap rail across the top cut edge.
Palette: Dark industrial painted wood or aged cast concrete (#2C2A29 to #383431). This cleanly frames the cut edge, signaling an intentional architectural cross-section rather than a rendering glitch.
Doors on Cut Walls:
Frame: Cut the door jambs off cleanly at the $0.4\text{ m}$ stub level.
Threshold / Floor Plate: Keep the metal or wooden floor transition strip ($0.02\text{ m}$ high) fully visible on the floor.
Door Leaf: Drop/hide the door leaf completely on any cut wall. If an inner room door is swung open into a cut wall space, hide the leaf entirely. Leaving a floating or half-cut door leaf obstructs walkways and looks broken.
A3. Wall-Hung Items: Vanish vs. Relocate

Floating signs, clocks, and posters look unpolished. Wall-hung items must obey the following rules:

Rule 1: Decorative Items Drop With The Wall.
Fight posters, vintage boxing bills, wall sconces, pipe conduits, and the ANVIL STREET GYM metal marquee sign vanish immediately when their parent wall cuts down to $0.4\text{ m}$. They do not float, and they do not collapse down onto the stub.
Rule 2: Gameplay-Critical Information Relocates to the Screen HUD.
The Round / Training Clock: In a real gym, a 3-minute mechanical round timer hangs on the wall. In FightCamp, the wall clock is visual flavor only. The functional countdown timer must exist permanently on the Top-Center HUD. If the wall it hangs on is cut, hide the 3D model. Never move it to an awkward floor stand.
Mirror Stations (Shadowboxing/Skipping): A mirror cannot float. When the mirror wall cuts, the mirror glass drops to the $0.4\text{ m}$ stub, turning into a low baseboard mirror edge. The functional station zone on the floor remains fully usable.
Suspended Equipment (Heavy Bag Rails / Pull-up Bars):
If heavy bags hang from wall brackets on a cut wall, the brackets cannot hang in mid-air.
Art Solution: Heavy bag rails must be modeled as freestanding floor-anchored steel gantry frames (posts grounded at $z=0.2$ and $z=2.2$) or drop down completely when the wall is cut. We strongly recommend floor-anchored I-beam gantries for your heavy bag lines. That way, bags stay hung even when the South wall drops!
A4. Camera Rotation: Discrete 90-Degree Steps vs. Free Drag
Verdict: Strict 90-Degree Discrete Steps (Yaw: $45^\circ \to 135^\circ \to 225^\circ \to 315^\circ$).
Why Free Drag Fails for FightCamp:
Free rotation requires continuous wall-normal projection checks per frame, inducing visual popping as walls snap between full and stub states.
Pathfinding grids, isometric projection alignments, and Godot 4 screen-space SSAO/SDFGI artifacts look substantially better when tuned to four fixed quadrant matrices.
Two Point Studios uses fixed 90-degree increments for precise room layout alignment.
Rotation Timing & Easing Curve:
Duration: Exactly $0.28\text{ seconds}$. Anything $\ge 0.4\text{ s}$ feels sluggish when managing an urgent crisis; anything $\le 0.15\text{ s}$ disorients the player's spatial awareness.
Curve: TRANS_CUBIC, EASE_IN_OUT (or TRANS_QUINT with strong damping).
Input Lock: Buffer inputs during rotation. If the player double-taps E, queue a smooth single transition to the $180^\circ$ target over $0.42\text{ s}$.
A5. Inner Room Walls (Physio, Office, Changing Rooms)

Inner room partition walls cannot follow the pure exterior shell rule, nor can they simply stay cut at all times.

Facing-Angle Check First: Partition walls facing the camera must drop to $0.4\text{ m}$ stubs by default so you can see inside the rooms from the main hall.
Occlusion Ray Casting (The "Focus Rule"):
When the player focuses on a character (e.g., coach talking to an athlete in the Physio room, or the player clicking the Office PC), cast a thick ray (capsule shape) from the camera to the target.
Any inner wall intersecting this capsule drops to $0.4\text{ m}$, regardless of its orientation.
Active Room State:
If a fighter is receiving treatment on the physio table, that room’s ceiling and camera-facing interior walls are dropped. The back wall of the physio room remains full height, displaying medical licenses, tape racks, and anatomy charts.
A6. Pure GDScript 4 Wall Visibility Module & Test Cases

This class is completely standalone (RefCounted), contains no references to Node or the SceneTree, uses pure vector math, and is 100% unit-testable.

# ==============================================================================
# WallVisibilityManager.gd
# Pure functional calculation of wall states and hung prop visibility
# ==============================================================================
class_name WallVisibilityManager
extends RefCounted

const STUB_CUTOFF_DOT: float = 0.05 # Threshold above which wall faces the camera

## Evaluates visibility for shell and inner walls.
## walls: Array of Dictionaries:
##   { "id": String, "center": Vector3, "normal": Vector3, "kind": "shell"|"inner", "hung_items": Array[String] }
## cam_yaw_deg: Camera Yaw in degrees (0 = North, 45 = default Iso, etc.)
## cam_forward: Vector3 normalized forward vector of the camera
## focus_points: Array of Vector3 (positions of boxers/coaches currently active)
static func compute_visibility(
	walls: Array,
	cam_yaw_deg: float,
	cam_forward: Vector3,
	focus_points: Array = []
) -> Dictionary:
	var full_walls: Array[String] = []
	var cut_walls: Array[String] = []
	var hidden_items: Array[String] = []
	
	# Flat 2D camera direction vector (ignore elevation pitch for wall dot tests)
	var cam_dir_2d := Vector2(cam_forward.x, cam_forward.z).normalized()
	
	for wall in walls:
		var wall_id: String = wall["id"]
		var normal: Vector3 = wall["normal"]
		var normal_2d := Vector2(normal.x, normal.z).normalized()
		var hung: Array = wall.get("hung_items", [])
		
		# Facing calculation:
		# Dot product between wall outward normal and camera viewing vector.
		# If dot > 0, the outward face points toward the camera (near wall -> CUT).
		# If dot <= 0, the inside surface is exposed to the camera (far wall -> FULL).
		var dot := normal_2d.dot(-cam_dir_2d)
		var is_near_wall := dot > -STUB_CUTOFF_DOT
		
		var should_cut := false
		
		if wall["kind"] == "shell":
			should_cut = is_near_wall
		elif wall["kind"] == "inner":
			if is_near_wall:
				should_cut = true
			else:
				# Check ray occlusion to active characters for inner partitions
				should_cut = _is_occluding_focus_points(wall, cam_forward, focus_points)
		
		if should_cut:
			cut_walls.append(wall_id)
			for item_id in hung:
				hidden_items.append(str(item_id))
		else:
			full_walls.append(wall_id)
			
	return {
		"full": full_walls,
		"cut": cut_walls,
		"hidden_items": hidden_items
	}

static func _is_occluding_focus_points(wall: Dictionary, cam_forward: Vector3, focus_points: Array) -> bool:
	if focus_points.is_empty():
		return false
	
	var wall_center: Vector3 = wall["center"]
	var wall_normal: Vector3 = wall["normal"]
	
	for pt in focus_points:
		var to_point: Vector3 = pt - wall_center
		# If the focal point lies behind the wall relative to camera line of sight
		if to_point.dot(cam_forward) > 0.1 and to_point.length_squared() < 64.0:
			return true
	return false

B2. FightCamp Adaptations: Rooms, Stations, Grids, and Clearances

A boxing gym is a blend of specialized communal athletic space and enclosed support modules. FightCamp must not force the player to manually draw toilets, brick by brick, before their fighter can spar.

1. Rooms vs. Training Stations Architecture
Enclosed Support Rooms (Physio, Management Office, Locker Room, Staff Lounge):
Phase 1 / Demo: Fixed footprint locations along the front block ($z=18.0\text{ to }22.5\text{ m}$). The player upgrades equipment inside them, but the perimeter walls are static.
Full Game / EA: Two Point-style rectangular room drafting on the peripheral floor: Drag min $3 \times 3\text{ m}$ for Physio, min $4 \times 3\text{ m}$ for Locker Room. Room is validated only when a Door + Primary Asset (e.g., Treatment Table) is placed.
Main Hall Training Stations (Ring, Heavy Bags, Speed Bags, Mats, Cardio, Weights):
100% Free Placement on a Grid. Training equipment is placed directly on the open hall floor. No internal room walls surround heavy bags or lifting racks.
2. Grid Sizing for FightCamp
Grid Cell: $0.5\text{ m} \times 0.5\text{ m}$.
Why $0.5\text{ m}$ instead of Two Point’s $1.0\text{ m}$?
A $1.0\text{ m}$ grid is too coarse for gym equipment: an Air Bike is $0.6 \times 1.2\text{ m}$; placing it on a $1\text{m}$ grid forces it to occupy $2 \times 1 = 2.0\text{ m}^2$, creating unnatural gaps between cardio gear.
$0.5\text{ m}$ allows realistic pacing:
Treadmill: $2 \times 4\text{ cells}$ ($1.0 \times 2.0\text{ m}$).
Air Bike: $2 \times 3\text{ cells}$ ($1.0 \times 1.5\text{ m}$).
Speed Bag: $2 \times 2\text{ cells}$ ($1.0 \times 1.0\text{ m}$).
Standard Ring ($16\text{ ft}$ platform): $12 \times 12\text{ cells}$ ($6.0 \times 6.0\text{ m}$).
Olympic Competition Ring ($24\text{ ft}$ platform): $16 \times 16\text{ cells}$ ($8.0 \times 8.0\text{ m}$).

# ==============================================================================
# PlacementValidator.gd
# Pure functional placement validation & walkway reachability engine
# ==============================================================================
class_name PlacementValidator
extends RefCounted

const CELL_SIZE: float = 0.5 # 0.5 meters per tile

# ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Footprint & Geometry Helpers
# ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Rotates a 2D integer footprint (width, depth in cells) by rotation index (0: 0°, 1: 90°, 2: 180°, 3: 270°)
static func get_rotated_size(w: int, d: int, rot: int) -> Vector2i:
	match rot % 4:
		0, 2:
			return Vector2i(w, d)
		1, 3:
			return Vector2i(d, w)
		_:
			return Vector2i(w, d)

## Computes array of grid cells occupied by an item given its origin, dimensions, and rotation
static func get_occupied_cells(origin: Vector2i, w: int, d: int, rot: int) -> Array[Vector2i]:
	var cells: Array[Vector2i] = []
	var eff_size := get_rotated_size(w, d, rot)
	for x in range(eff_size.x):
		for y in range(eff_size.y):
			cells.append(Vector2i(origin.x + x, origin.y + y))
	return cells

## Computes clearance boundary cells surrounding the solid footprint (front, sides, back)
static func get_clearance_cells(origin: Vector2i, w: int, d: int, rot: int, clearance_pad: int) -> Array[Vector2i]:
	var cells: Array[Vector2i] = []
	var eff_size := get_rotated_size(w, d, rot)
	var min_x := origin.x - clearance_pad
	var max_x := origin.x + eff_size.x + clearance_pad - 1
	var min_y := origin.y - clearance_pad
	var max_y := origin.y + eff_size.y + clearance_pad - 1
	
	for x in range(min_x, max_x + 1):
		for y in range(min_y, max_y + 1):
			var pt := Vector2i(x, y)
			# Only return cells that are outside the solid footprint itself
			var in_solid := (x >= origin.x and x < origin.x + eff_size.x and y >= origin.y and y < origin.y + eff_size.y)
			if not in_solid:
				cells.append(pt)
	return cells

# ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Core Validation
# ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## item: Dictionary:
##   { "id": String, "footprint_w": int, "footprint_d": int, "clearance": int, "wall_mounted": bool }
## cell: Target origin cell (Vector2i)
## rot: Rotation index (0..3)
## occupied_grid: Dictionary of Vector2i -> String (item_id occupying this solid cell)
## hall_bounds: Rect2i (grid bounding rectangle, e.g., 60x36 cells for 30x18 m)
## walls: Array of Dictionaries defining solid wall boundary lines/cells
## entrance_cells: Array[Vector2i] cells representing the exterior door entrance
## all_approach_points: Array[Vector2i] all existing interact/work points that must stay reachable
static func can_place(
	item: Dictionary,
	cell: Vector2i,
	rot: int,
	occupied_grid: Dictionary,
	hall_bounds: Rect2i,
	walls: Array = [],
	entrance_cells: Array = [],
	all_approach_points: Array = []
) -> Dictionary:
	var w: int = item["footprint_w"]
	var d: int = item["footprint_d"]
	var clearance: int = item.get("clearance", 1)
	var wall_mounted: bool = item.get("wall_mounted", false)
	
	var solid_cells := get_occupied_cells(cell, w, d, rot)
	
	# 1. Hall Bounds Check
	for c in solid_cells:
		if not hall_bounds.has_point(c):
			return {"ok": false, "reason": "Out of gym bounds"}
			
	# 2. Solid-on-Solid Overlap Check
	for c in solid_cells:
		if occupied_grid.has(c):
			return {"ok": false, "reason": "Cell already obstructed by %s" % occupied_grid[c]}
			
	# 3. Wall Mounted Rule Check
	if wall_mounted:
		var against_wall := false
		for c in solid_cells:
			# Wall adjacent if sitting on the exterior grid perimeter
			if c.x == hall_bounds.position.x or c.x == hall_bounds.end.x - 1 \
			or c.y == hall_bounds.position.y or c.y == hall_bounds.end.y - 1:
				against_wall = true
				break
		if not against_wall:
			return {"ok": false, "reason": "Item must be mounted flush against a wall"}
			
	# 4. Clearance vs Solid Overlap
	# Clearance zones of the new item cannot overlap existing solid items
	var cl_cells := get_clearance_cells(cell, w, d, rot, clearance)
	for cl in cl_cells:
		if hall_bounds.has_point(cl) and occupied_grid.has(cl):
			return {"ok": false, "reason": "Clearance envelope blocked by %s" % occupied_grid[cl]}

	# 5. Walkway Connectivity BFS Check
	# Temporarily map new solid cells into a test grid to verify all equipment remains pathable
	if not entrance_cells.is_empty():
		var test_grid := occupied_grid.duplicate()
		for c in solid_cells:
			test_grid[c] = item["id"]
			
		# Derive the approach point for this placed item (1 tile in front of the item relative to rotation)
		var this_approach := _get_approach_point(cell, w, d, rot)
		var targets_to_verify: Array[Vector2i] = []
		for pt in all_approach_points:
			targets_to_verify.append(pt)
		targets_to_verify.append(this_approach)
		
		var path_result := verify_walkway_connectivity(entrance_cells[0], targets_to_verify, test_grid, hall_bounds)
		if not path_result.ok:
			return {"ok": false, "reason": "Blocks critical hallway: %s" % path_result.reason}

	return {"ok": true, "reason": "Valid placement"}

static func _get_approach_point(origin: Vector2i, w: int, d: int, rot: int) -> Vector2i:
	var eff_size := get_rotated_size(w, d, rot)
	match rot % 4:
		0: return Vector2i(origin.x + eff_size.x / 2, origin.y + eff_size.y) # Front (+Z / South)
		1: return Vector2i(origin.x - 1, origin.y + eff_size.y / 2)         # West
		2: return Vector2i(origin.x + eff_size.x / 2, origin.y - 1)         # North
		3: return Vector2i(origin.x + eff_size.x, origin.y + eff_size.y / 2) # East
	return origin

# ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# BFS Navigable Path Checker
# ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Runs an unweighted breadth-first flood search across traversable grid tiles.
## Guarantees an open path from the gym entrance to every station's work/approach node.
static func verify_walkway_connectivity(
	start_cell: Vector2i,
	required_destinations: Array,
	blocked_cells: Dictionary,
	bounds: Rect2i
) -> Dictionary:
	if blocked_cells.has(start_cell):
		return {"ok": false, "reason": "Entrance blocked"}
		
	var queue: Array[Vector2i] = [start_cell]
	var visited: Dictionary = {start_cell: true}
	var reachable_count: int = 0
	
	var dest_map: Dictionary = {}
	for d in required_destinations:
		dest_map[d] = false
		if d == start_cell:
			dest_map[d] = true
			reachable_count += 1
			
	var directions: Array[Vector2i] = [
		Vector2i(1, 0), Vector2i(-1, 0), Vector2i(0, 1), Vector2i(0, -1)
	]
	
	var head := 0
	while head < queue.size():
		var current = queue[head]
		head += 1
		
		for dir in directions:
			var neighbor: Vector2i = current + dir
			
			if not bounds.has_point(neighbor):
				continue
			if visited.has(neighbor):
				continue
			if blocked_cells.has(neighbor):
				continue
				
			visited[neighbor] = true
			queue.append(neighbor)
			
			if dest_map.has(neighbor) and dest_map[neighbor] == false:
				dest_map[neighbor] = true
				reachable_count += 1
				if reachable_count >= dest_map.size():
					return {"ok": true, "reason": "All stations reachable"}

	for dest in dest_map.keys():
		if not dest_map[dest]:
			return {"ok": false, "reason": "Station at (%d, %d) is isolated" % [dest.x, dest.y]}
			
	return {"ok": true, "reason": "All stations reachable"}

C1. Analytical Comparison: Where Our Numbers Differ and Why

The telemetry extracted from Two Point Museum (TPM, 2025) provides an exceptional empirical baseline from the genre’s leading production team. Here is how our engineering choices compare, along with tailored adaptations for FightCamp:

CAMERA SYSTEM COMPARISON:

FightCamp Spec:   [FOV 24° (Sports Depth)] ──> [Height 12-45m]  ──> [Discrete 90° Snapping]

1. Camera Projection & FOV: 17° vs. 36°
Measured TPM: Perspective with FOV $17^\circ$ (effectively an orthographic telephoto perspective, completely suppressing perspective distortion across vast $100\text{ m}+$ museum complexes).
FightCamp Original: FOV $36^\circ$ to $48^\circ$.
The Conflict: At FOV $36^\circ$ to $48^\circ$, tall characters ($1.8\text{ m}$) standing near the near camera edge exhibit intense perspective barrel elongation, distorting their proportions. However, at $17^\circ$, depth cues flatten so heavily that ring ropes, apron steps, and fighter bob-and-weave verticality lose their physical punch.
Reconciliation: Adopt FOV $24^\circ$. This sits directly in the sweet spot: it delivers the near-orthographic readability of a technical management sim while retaining sufficient foreshortening to showcase muscular boxing animations and ring ropes.
2. Camera Rotation: Free Yaw (90°/s) vs. Discrete 90-Degree Steps
Measured TPM: Free continuous yaw rotation via keys ($90^\circ/\text{s}$) or mouse drag, backed by continuous shader updates (SetWallShaderToTransparentMode).
FightCamp PART A: Fixed discrete $90^\circ$ steps ($45^\circ \to 135^\circ \to 225^\circ \to 315^\circ$).
The Conflict: Continuous free rotation in Godot 4 with dynamic cutaway geometry requires either per-frame backface cull passes or runtime dissolve shaders across materials, increasing shader complexity. More importantly, FightCamp features active real-time athletic interactions (coach boosting a fighter at a bag, pad chains executing in the ring) occurring on strict 2-minute clocks. Searching for a manual rotation angle during active gameplay is frustrating.
Reconciliation: Retain Discrete $90^\circ$ Steps as the Default Game Control (Q/E), but adopt TPM’s Free Drag ($90^\circ/\text{s}$) strictly during Build Mode (B). When constructing or arranging equipment, the player gains full rotational freedom to inspect alignments. The moment they return to simulation mode, the camera snaps smoothly to the nearest of the four standard isometric quadrants ($45^\circ, 135^\circ, 225^\circ, 315^\circ$).
3. Wall Rendering: Backface / Shader Cut vs. Geometric Stub
Measured TPM: ShowBackfaceWallsOnly on far walls, and SetWallShaderToTransparentMode on camera-facing walls. Visual wall height is completely decoupled from selection/placement wall height. Ceiling height is $6\text{ m}$.
FightCamp Part A: Geometric physical drop to a $0.4\text{ m}$ stub with cap rails.
Reconciliation: Adopt TPM’s Decoupling Principle with FightCamp’s Stub Geometry.
Visuals: Keep our $0.4\text{ m}$ physical stub cap rail. Transparent walls look washed out over brick gym aesthetics. A clean architectural cutaway is visually clearer than a transparent dither.
Selection & Snapping: Adopt TPM’s exact decoupled logic: even when a wall is cut down to a $0.4\text{ m}$ visual stub, its virtual placement plane remains at full $3.6\text{ m}$ height. When the player drags a poster or round clock near the stub, the full wall highlight appears, snapping the item cleanly to its correct height ($1.8\text{ m}$).
4. Grid Snap: 0.5 m Grid Density (87% Dominance in TPM)
Measured TPM: $0.5\text{ m}$ on 87% of items, $1.0\text{ m}$ on 5%, fine $0.25/0.1\text{ m}$ on decor. Free (off-grid) placement allowed on 99% of items.

1. High-Level Direction: "The Coach Watches His Script Run"

In traditional fighting games, the player’s twitch reflexes drive the boxer. In FightCamp, the player is the architect, and the fight is the stress test of their preparation.
