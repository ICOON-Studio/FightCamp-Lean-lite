1. Roadmap Order Assessment: Pull S259 Before S258
The Diagnosis

The current roadmap plans:
$$\text{S258: Demo .exe of the loop} \longrightarrow \text{S259: Watchable Jab Benchmark}$$
This order is structurally flawed. Delivering an external or internal .exe demo to testers, investors, or publisher partners in S258 before fixing the core visual execution (the jab) creates an immediate negative perception. In sports management simulations (such as Football Manager or Motorsport Manager), if the 3D match engine looks broken, wooden, or amateurish, players judge the mathematical backend as equally broken—regardless of how deep the stats are.

The Exact Metric Formulations
The GAINS Bar (Readiness $0–100%$):
Formula: $$\text{Readiness} = (\text{Plan Familiarity} \times 0.35) + (\text{Weight Cut Progress} \times 0.35) + (\text{Primary Tactic Mastery} \times 0.30)$$
Target for Bout Night: $\ge 80%$.
The ATTRITION Bar (Systemic Wear $0–100%$):
Formula: $$\text{Systemic Wear} = (\text{Accumulated Muscle Fatigue} \times 0.40) + (\text{Micro-Trauma / Chin Wear} \times 0.35) + (\text{Weight Drain Stress} \times 0.25)$$
Thresholds:
$0–39%$ (Fresh): Full attribute performance in gym; $+0%$ injury risk.
$40–59%$ (Productive Strain): Optimal training zone; stat gains are at $1.0\times$ efficiency.
$60–79%$ (Overtrained / Danger Zone): CRITICAL CHANGE POINT. Stat growth falls by $-40%$; sparring head hits cause permanent micro-cuts; injury risk jumps to $+25%$ per session.
$80–100%$ (Exhaustion / Breakdown): Camp disaster. $-15%$ fight-night chin value, $-20%$ stamina recovery between rounds, high risk of bout cancellation.
The "Change the Plan" Trigger
The Crossing Point: The exact day Attrition reaches or exceeds $60%$, the HUD displays an amber border alert.
The Action Required: The player must cancel intensive contact stations (Sparring, Heavy Bag) and swap to Active Recovery / Low-Impact Work (Pool/Stretch Corner, Film Study, Hyperbaric/Massage) for 48 hours until Attrition drops below $50%$.

SECTION B: PUNCH FEEL & ANIMATION ENGINE TIMINGS
1. Control Return and Recovery Benchmarks
The Core Problem

In the current Godot build, our fighter remains locked in animation state for up to $1.4\text{ seconds}$ after landing a punch. In boxing, this creates an unnatural turn-based feel where fighters take turns executing actions rather than engaging in continuous exchanges.

The Rule: Hitbox $\to$ Impact $\to$ Early Control Return $\to$ Blend Out

We will adopt the industry benchmark: Control returns at $\text{Impact} + 0.12\text{ s to } 0.17\text{ s}$.
The animation clip does not stop; rather, its return-to-guard recovery frames are pushed to a secondary blend layer that immediately yields priority to:

Slipping / ducking / blocking.
The next punch in a buffered combination.
Rapid lateral footwork resets.

2. Body Shot Timing Calibration

Our current body hook contact time of $0.70\text{ s}$ is excessively slow. A real fighter does not spend three-quarters of a second winding up an inside rib strike; that constitutes an amateur telegraph that would be countered reliably in any regional bout.

Target Body Hook Contact Time: $0.400\text{ seconds}$ (Lead Body Hook) / $0.440\text{ seconds}$ (Rear Body Hook).
Target Body Jab Contact Time: $0.320\text{ seconds}$.
Level Change Mechanics: The level change (dropping the hips and knees by $0.18\text{ metres}$) must begin at $t = 0.080\text{ s}$, simultaneous with the forward fist extension—not as an isolated preliminary animation that plays before the strike begins.
3. The Counter-Window (Vulnerability Window) Rule

Every punch opens a mechanical counter window where the attacking fighter cannot benefit from their passive defensive stats:

$$\text{Vulnerability Window} = [\text{Hitbox On} - 0.05\text{ s}] \quad \text{to} \quad [\text{Control Return}]$$

Timeline: Lead Cross
[0.00s]--------- [0.19s]=================[0.34s]=================[0.49s]-------------[0.62s]
 Start            Counter Vulnerability    IMPACT                 Control             End Clip
                  Window Opens                                    Returns / Guarded

Mechanical Penalties Inside the Window
The puncher's passive Evasion is set to $0%$.
Incoming strikes that land during this window gain $+25%$ Critical Damage and $+20%$ Stun/Rocked Probability (simulating absorbing a strike while off balance).
Scaling with Skill and Fatigue

The duration of the vulnerability window is governed by:
$$\text{WindowDuration} = \text{BaseWindow} \times \left(1.0 + \frac{100 - \text{Technique}}{200}\right) \times \left(1.0 + \frac{\text{Fatigue}}{100} \times 0.40\right)$$

An elite technician (Technique 90, Fresh): Vulnerability window compresses by $-22%$. The fighter recovers into defensive guard almost immediately.
A tired brawler (Technique 35, Stamina 20%): Vulnerability window broadens by $+58%$, remaining exposed up to $0.35\text{ s}$ after contact.
4. Guarding Against Blend Pops and Broken IK Snapping

When allowing a clip to be interrupted at $t = \text{Impact} + 0.12\text{ s}$, standard linear crossfading will cause visual defects: the puncher's extended arm will unnaturally snap backwards into a guard stance over a single frame.

Technical Implementation Rules (Godot 4 AnimationTree):
Dynamic Inertialization (Inertial Blending): Use an AnimationNodeBlend2 configured with an Inertializer rather than a crossfade node. Inertialization carries the velocity and momentum of the hand forward into the retraction phase rather than interpolating raw bone positions.
Two-Bone IK Soft Constraints: The lead and rear arms must use two-bone inverse kinematics targeting a floating shoulder-relative guard target. When an evasion command interrupts a punch, the arm does not pop to the chest; it follows an elliptical retraction curve back to the cheek bone over a minimum of $0.14\text{ s}$.
Impact Freeze Frame (Micro-Stop): On solid contact, pause bone motion for exactly 2 frames ($33.3\text{ ms}$). This physical pause creates the visual sensation of resistance and impact weight, masking the transition into the recovery blend.
