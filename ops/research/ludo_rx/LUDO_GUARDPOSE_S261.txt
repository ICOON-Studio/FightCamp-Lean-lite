1. ARCHITECTURE: TWO-BONE IK ONTO HEAD TARGETS VS. AUTHORED POSES (INDUSTRY COMPARATIVE)
The Architectural Verdict: A Hybrid Stack

Pure Two-Bone IK directly onto head-relative coordinates without base authored poses will fail to look like an arm.

If you apply analytical Two-Bone IK directly to a raw, un-sculpted, generic A-pose or basic ActionForge idle clip, the solver will solve the triangle mathematically, but it has no intrinsic understanding of:

Clavicular shrugging and scapular protraction (which rotate the base of the arm by up to $15\text{ cm}$).
Deltoid muscle roll and pectoral shelf collision (the forearm will slice right through the pectoralis major).
The subtle twist of the radius around the ulna that squares the glove knuckle plane to the target.
What Fight Night Champion and Undisputed Actually Do
Fight Night Champion (EA Canada): Relied on Authored Base Layer + Procedural Inertial Additive Blend Shapes + World-Space Target Clamping. Fight Night never ran raw Two-Bone IK from scratch on guard hands. They authored dedicated, clean mocap loops for each guard family (e.g., Philly Shell, Loose/Low, High Earmuff, Peek-a-boo). On top of this base, an additive layer adjusted wrist height via localized IK based on the incoming threat trajectory (head or body block), while the clavicle and humerus roll came directly from the authored, anatomically correct animation asset.
Undisputed (Steel City Interactive): Uses an Authored Pose Matrix with Multi-Target FABRIK/Two-Bone Solvers in Unity. Each fighter archetype uses a base hand-sculpted stance posture. IK is used only as an offset modifier ($\pm 10\text{ cm}$) to position gloves dynamically based on fatigue, head movement (slips), and reaching for intercepts. The non-striking hand uses a strict local-space lock relative to the cervical spine/head bone to avoid drift during lateral trunk movement.
The Correct Implementation Stack for FightCamp (Godot 4.7.2)

You must implement a 3-Tier Hierarchy:

[TIER 1: AUTHORED POSE BASE]
  Static, perfectly hand-sculpted 1-frame poses for each of the 5 guard styles.
  Clavicles shrugged, scapulae protracted, thoracic spine curled, twist bones aligned.
            |
            v
[TIER 2: PER-BOXER & IN-FIGHT PROCEDURAL OFFSETS]
  Style Jitter + Guard Attribute Scale + Fatigue Morph + Threat Bias (Head/Body).
  Offsets applied to virtual Target & Pole markers in Head-Local Coordinate Space.
            |
            v
[TIER 3: TWO-BONE IK PASS (MICRO-CORRECTION & HEAD PIN)]
  Two-Bone IK runs strictly to clamp the wrist to that final synthesized target,
  with Pole Vector pinned down-and-in toward the rib cage margin.

Punching Arm IK Blend Weights
Phase 1: Release ($0\text{ ms} \dots 120\text{ ms}$): The striking hand’s Two-Bone IK weight blends linearly from $1.0 \to 0.0$ using a cubic ease-in curve (ease(t, 2.0)). The authored strike animation assumes full control.
Phase 2: Ballistic Strike ($120\text{ ms} \dots \text{Impact}$): IK weight remains at $0.0$. The authored animation controls the punch apex.
Phase 3: Recoil Capture ($\text{Impact} + 60\text{ ms} \dots \text{End}$): As the arm returns past $65%$ of its retraction trajectory, IK blends smoothly from $0.0 \to 1.0$ over $90\text{ ms}$ using a smooth-step curve (smoothstep(0.0, 1.0, t)). This completely prevents any pop or snapping discontinuity as the hand re-enters the guard target pocket.

6. GODOT 4.7.2 ARCHITECTURE & SKELETAL TRAPS
Trap 1: The Manual Posture Pass vs. SkeletonModifier3D
The Trap: The user notes they run an "AnimationPlayer in MANUAL mode, then a per-frame posture pass writes bone poses after the clip." If you write bone transforms via Skeleton3D.set_bone_pose_rotation() directly in _process() or _physics_process(), Godot’s internal transform cache gets dirty, causing one-frame latency jitter and bypassing skin bounding-box recalculation.
The Production Fix: Subclass SkeletonModifier3D (native to Godot 4.3+ through 4.7.2). Implement the guard solver inside _process_modification(). This executes precisely inside the engine's internal skeletal update loop after the animation player evaluates, but before skinning matrices are dispatched to the Vulkan vertex shader.
Trap 2: Bone Rest Axis & Roll Mismatch on MetaHumans
The Trap: MetaHuman arm bones in FBX format use Maya-style joint orientations ($X$-axis down the bone, $Z$-axis bend). Godot’s TwoBoneIK3D natively expects the primary bone vector to align with the positive or negative $Y$-axis. If uncompensated, solving Two-Bone IK causes the elbow to violently snap $90^\circ$ outward or inward on the first frame.
The Production Fix: Apply an explicit basis transform in your solver to handle the MetaHuman offset:
var bone_roll_offset = Basis(Vector3.UP, deg_to_rad(90.0))

Ensure all pole vectors are calculated in the coordinate space of the parent clavicle bone.
Trap 3: Pole Vector Flipping / Inversion
The Trap: When the glove moves directly in line between the shoulder and the head target, the plane defined by (Shoulder, Hand, Pole) collapses to collinearity. The IK solver experiences a gimbal flip, instantly snapping the elbow through the chest cavity.
The Production Fix: Never place the pole vector in world space. Compute the pole target dynamically as an anterior-inferior projection from the ribcage: $$\vec{P}{\text{pole}} = \vec{P}{\text{Shoulder}} + (\text{TorsoBasis.Down} \times 0.6) + (\text{TorsoBasis.Forward} \times 0.2) + (\text{TorsoBasis.Inward} \times 0.15)$$ Because the pole is pinned relative to the ribcage, the elbow is geometrically prevented from ever rotating upward or flaring outward past the legal envelope.
Trap 4: Skin Collapse at Deep Elbow Bend ($<60^\circ$)
The Trap: The MetaHuman mesh uses corrective helper joints (lowerarm_correctiveRoot, bck, fwd, in, out). In Godot, these bones sit idle unless driven by script, producing severe "candy-wrapper" mesh collapse at acute angles.
The Production Fix: Drive the helper bones procedurally inside your SkeletonModifier3D:
# Drive olecranon bulge based on elbow acute bend
var elbow_interior_angle = rad_to_deg(upper_arm_dir.angle_to(-lower_arm_dir))
if elbow_interior_angle < 80.0:
    var flex_ratio = clamp((80.0 - elbow_interior_angle) / 40.0, 0.0, 1.0)
    # Translate the posterior helper bone outward to preserve the elbow point
    skeleton.set_bone_pose_position(bck_bone_idx, Vector3(0, 0, -0.022 * flex_ratio))
