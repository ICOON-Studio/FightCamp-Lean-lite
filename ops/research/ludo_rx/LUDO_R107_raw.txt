EXECUTIVE VERDICT: OPTION 2 IS THE MATHEMATICAL & DESIGN SWEET SPOT

The studio head's intuition is picking up on a real tension: wide perspective dollhouse cameras cause angular distortion at the edges of the screen, make horizontal distances difficult to judge, and cause high walls to occlude adjacent rooms.

However, jumping to a literal 2D sprite engine (Option 1) would be fatal to this project.

Option 1 (True 2D/2.5D Sprite Switch): HARD REJECT. It requires throwing away 100% of your MetaHuman rigs, mocap assets, Cascadeur pad synchronization, dynamic lighting, and Godot 4.7 Forward+ pipeline. Cost: 8–12 weeks of wasted labor, transforming a high-end 3D sports title into an indie pixel clone.
Option 2 (3D World + Orthographic Isometric Projection): STRONGLY RECOMMEND. Keep every single 3D asset, mocap clip, MetaHuman rig, and material as-is. Simply swap the camera projection to PROJECTION_ORTHOGONAL at standard isometric angles (35.264° pitch, 45° yaw). This gives the clean, readable, architectural precision of Two Point Hospital while preserving your 3D animation progression.
Option 3 (Leave Current High Perspective Dollhouse Camera): ACCEPTABLE AS A FALLBACK, BUT HAS PERSISTENT FLAWS. Perspective distortion stretches actors in screen corners, causes focal-length compression when panning between the gym floor and the office, and makes station click-boxes vary depending on screen coordinates.

Option 1: True 2.5D Art Switch (Pre-Rendered / Sprites)
What the player gains: Crisp edges, deterministic 2D sorting, zero 3D shadow artifacts on low-end hardware.
What the player loses:
Destroys Part D Progression: Micro-variations in punch cadence (snappy EaseOut curves, dynamic mitt compression, footwork jitter) cannot be conveyed through sprite sheets without rendering hundreds of frames per stat band.
Visual Disconnect: The transition from a 2D pre-rendered gym to a full 3D fight ring would feel jarring, like two completely different games built by different studios.
What it costs: 8–12 weeks of engineering. You would have to build an entirely new 2D sprite rendering pipeline, state-machine animator, and depth-sorting logic in Godot from scratch.
Verdict: Instant disqualifier.
Option 2: Keep 3D Gym, Lock Camera to Orthographic Isometric
What the player gains:
Architectural Clarity: The gym floor and all 6 connected club rooms (reception, office, ring, physio, store, lockers) sit on a clean, readable grid. Wall heights and room proportions remain uniform across the screen.
Reliable Click Targeting: A punching bag at the screen edge has the exact same visual footprint and hit-box dimensions as one in the center.
Unified Art Style: The MetaHuman fighter, coach, materials, dynamic lights, and decals look identical in both the gym and the ring.
What the player loses:
True depth foreshortening. In an orthographic view, a fighter further back along the Z-axis doesn't appear smaller; scale is constant regardless of camera distance.
What it costs: 2 to 3 engineering days. It simply involves switching Camera3D.projection to PROJECTION_ORTHOGONAL, setting the rotation angles, tuning zoom levels, and adding a 30-line wall transparency shader.
What it throws away: Nothing. Every model, bone constraint, animation, and floor decal remains intact.
Verdict: The optimal design choice.
Option 3: Leave Camera as Current High Perspective Dollhouse
What the player gains: A sense of spatial presence and natural 3D parallax when the camera pans between rooms.
What the player loses:
Edge Distortion: With wide-angle lenses (50°–70° FOV), objects near screen borders suffer noticeable angular stretching.
Occlusion Issues: Foreground walls block adjacent rooms (such as the office partition visible in the user's reference image), forcing awkward camera panning or manual room masking.
What it costs: 0 days.
Verdict: Acceptable if dev time is at an absolute premium, but mathematically inferior to Option 2 for a management title.

The lesson from Two Point Studios and Eggcode (Mad Games Tycoon 2) is clear: Modern management sims build their environments in full 3D, but render them through an orthographic or near-orthographic lens. This delivers the readability of a classic tactical game while keeping the fidelity of high-end 3D character rigs.

Camera Transform & Projection Mathematics
Projection Mode: Camera3D.PROJECTION_ORTHOGONAL
Rotation Pitch (X): -35.264° (The true mathematical isometric angle: arcsin(tan(30°))). This ensures equal foreshortening along all three axes, making square floor tiles render with standard 2:1 pixel-ratio symmetry.
Rotation Yaw (Y): 45.0° (or 135° / 225° / 315° depending on the orientation of your back brick wall).
Rotation Roll (Z): 0.0°
Zoom Levels (Camera3D.size in meters)
In orthographic mode, camera distance does not affect zoom; only the viewport size property changes the frame scale.
| ZOOM STEP | ORTHO SIZE | FRAME COVERAGE | PRIMARY PLAYER USE CASE |
| STEP 1: MACRO | 24.0 meters | Entire GYM4 Club (All 6 facilities) | Overview of all 6 rooms: Reception, Office, Mats, Ring, Lockers, Physio. Quick routing. |
| STEP 2: FLOOR (DEFAULT) | 14.0 meters | Active Gym Floor (Pads, Bag, Ring) | Main training view: Stations, coach walking, boxer punch cadence, gain meters, Cue Strip. |
| STEP 3: WORK (CLOSE-UP) | 6.5 meters | Single Station / Office Desk | Inspection view: Mitt contact snap, IK accuracy, boxer breathing, sweat, office PC screen. |
Zoom Controls: Mouse wheel up/down steps smoothly between sizes using an exponential smoothing curve (0.25s duration).
Wall Cutaway & Occlusion Rules
To prevent foreground walls from blocking the action without leaving awkward open voids, use this 3-tier occlusion hierarchy:
CAMERA LINE-OF-SIGHT OCCLUSION LOGIC (Iso Camera Ray Vector: [ -1.0, -1.0, -1.0 ] normalized)
[ SOUTH & WEST WALLS ] (Facing away from camera / In foreground) --> RULE: Cut down to 0.45m high brick wainscoting automatically. Never draw to full ceiling.
[ NORTH & EAST WALLS ] (Structural background walls) --> RULE: Render full height (3.2m). Holds fight posters, chalkboards, CRT TV, ring signs.
[ INTERIOR PARTITION WALLS ] (Office / Physio dividers) --> RULE: Dynamic Dither Fade Shader. When the player pans to or clicks into the office, the dividing partition wall drops to 20% alpha dither within 0.15s.
Godot 4 Spatial Dither Fade Material
Foreground walls use a material with Distance Fade or Screen-Door Dithering enabled:
Material Settings -> Transparency -> Distance Fade:
- Mode: Object Dither
- Distance Min: 8.0m
- Distance Max: 12.0m
This guarantees zero hard pop-ins or opaque walls blocking fighter-to-coach sightlines.

5. PRODUCTION SCRIPT: ISOMETRIC CAMERA CONTROLLER (GDSCRIPT)
Add this script to your primary camera gimbal to test the isometric setup in the engine in under 5 minutes.
# IsoGymCamera.gd
class_name IsoGymCamera
extends Node3D
@export var camera: Camera3D
@export var pan_speed: float = 18.0
@export var smooth_time: float = 0.12
# Standard Orthographic Zoom Steps
const ZOOM_LEVELS: Array[float] = [6.5, 14.0, 24.0]
var current_zoom_index: int = 1 # Starts at 14.0m (Standard Floor View)
# True Isometric Math: pitch = -35.264 deg, yaw = 45.0 deg
const ISO_PITCH: float = -35.264
const ISO_YAW: float = 45.0
var target_position: Vector3
var target_ortho_size: float = 14.0
func _ready() -> void:
if not camera:
camera = $Camera3D
# Force pure isometric setup
camera.projection = Camera3D.PROJECTION_ORTHOGONAL
rotation_degrees = Vector3(ISO_PITCH, ISO_YAW, 0.0)
target_ortho_size = ZOOM_LEVELS[current_zoom_index]
camera.size = target_ortho_size
target_position = position
func _process(delta: float) -> void:
_handle_input(delta)
_apply_smooth_transform(delta)
func _handle_input(delta: float) -> void:
# Screen Edge / Keyboard Panning along Isometric Planar Coordinates
var input_dir := Vector2.ZERO
if Input.is_action_pressed("ui_right") or Input.is_key_pressed(KEY_D):
input_dir.x += 1.0
if Input.is_action_pressed("ui_left") or Input.is_key_pressed(KEY_A):
input_dir.x -= 1.0
if Input.is_action_pressed("ui_down") or Input.is_key_pressed(KEY_S):
input_dir.y += 1.0
if Input.is_action_pressed("ui_up") or Input.is_key_pressed(KEY_W):
input_dir.y -= 1.0
if input_dir != Vector2.ZERO:
input_dir = input_dir.normalized()
# Transform 2D screen input vectors into 45-degree isometric world coordinates
var forward_iso: Vector3 = Vector3(-1.0, 0.0, -1.0).normalized()
var right_iso: Vector3 = Vector3(1.0, 0.0, -1.0).normalized()
var move_vector: Vector3 = (right_iso * input_dir.x + forward_iso * -input_dir.y) * pan_speed * delta
target_position += move_vector
# Zoom Step Logic
if Input.is_action_just_released("zoom_in") or Input.is_mouse_button_pressed(MOUSE_BUTTON_WHEEL_UP):
zoom_step(-1)
elif Input.is_action_just_released("zoom_out") or Input.is_mouse_button_pressed(MOUSE_BUTTON_WHEEL_DOWN):
zoom_step(1)
func zoom_step(direction: int) -> void:
current_zoom_index = clampi(current_zoom_index + direction, 0, ZOOM_LEVELS.size() - 1)
target_ortho_size = ZOOM_LEVELS[current_zoom_index]
func focus_on_station(world_pos: Vector3, zoom_step_index: int = 1) -> void:
target_position = world_pos
current_zoom_index = clampi(zoom_step_index, 0, ZOOM_LEVELS.size() - 1)
target_ortho_size = ZOOM_LEVELS[current_zoom_index]
func _apply_smooth_transform(delta: float) -> void:
position = position.lerp(target_position, 1.0 - exp(-delta / smooth_time))
camera.size = lerpf(camera.size, target_ortho_size, 1.0 - exp(-delta / smooth_time))
