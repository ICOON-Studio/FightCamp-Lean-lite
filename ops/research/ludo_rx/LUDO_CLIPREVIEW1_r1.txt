2. FOOTWORK KINEMATICS & CODEABLE FOOT RULES

In real boxing, punches are generated from the ground up: Force = Foot Anchor $\to$ Hip Rotation $\to$ Scapular Extension. In the current build, hands fire while feet are in mid-translation.

       ORTHODOX JAB (PUSH STEP)                 ORTHODOX REAR CROSS (REAR PIVOT)
    [Lead Foot]        [Rear Foot]           [Lead Foot]          [Rear Foot]
    Steps fwd 8-12cm   Pushes, then drags    Planted firmly       Ball pivots 45-60 deg
    PLANTED at impact  up same distance      DO NOT MOVE          Heel kicks OUTWARD
        |                  |                     |                    |
     (LOCK IK)          (TRAIL)               (LOCK IK)          (ROTATIONAL PIVOT)

The Three Fundamental Striking Footwork Rules
Rule 1: The Push Step (Lead Jab)

When a boxer steps into a jab:

Initiation ($t = 0.00\text{ s}$ to $0.08\text{ s}$): The rear foot pushes against the canvas.
Step Phase ($t = 0.08\text{ s}$ to $0.18\text{ s}$): The lead foot lifts a maximum of $0.03\text{ m}$ off the canvas, stepping forward between $0.08\text{ m}$ and $0.15\text{ m}$ (governed by reach).
Plant Lock ($t = 0.18\text{ s}$): The lead foot MUST plant before contact. At $t = 0.18\text{ s}$, the lead foot contacts the canvas and is hard-locked via Two-Bone IK with zero translation.
Impact ($t = 0.28\text{ s}$): Impact occurs only when the lead foot is planted.
Rear Foot Trail ($t = 0.28\text{ s}$ to $0.42\text{ s}$): The rear foot slides forward the identical distance ($0.08\text{ m}$ to $0.15\text{ m}$) to re-establish the baseline stance width ($0.45\text{ m} \pm 0.05\text{ m}$).
Rule 2: The Pivot (Rear Cross / Straight Right)

A rear cross never translates the lead foot:

Lead Foot: Clamped to the canvas (IK weight $= 1.0$). Maximum translation allowed $= 0.00\text{ m}$.
Rear Foot: Heel raises off canvas; the ball of the foot acts as a fixed pin ($X, Z$ coordinates stationary). The rear heel rotates outward by $45^\circ\text{ to } 60^\circ$ around the vertical $Y$-axis.
Weight Transfer: The center of mass shifts from $50/50$ to $60%$ lead leg, $40%$ rear leg.
Rule 3: The Lead Hook Pivot
Lead Foot: The ball of the lead foot pins to the canvas. The lead heel kicks outward $45^\circ\text{ to } 70^\circ$, driving the hip rotation.
Rear Foot: Remains planted, absorbing the rotational torque.
Codeable State-Machine Invariants (Godot GDScript Implementation)
# Kinematic constraints enforced during strike animations
func enforce_striking_foot_constraints(boxer: BoxerBody, current_punch: PunchType, punch_time: float):
    match current_punch:
        PunchType.JAB:
            if punch_time >= 0.18 and punch_time <= 0.45:
                # Lock lead foot firmly to canvas
                boxer.left_foot_ik.target_weight = 1.0
                boxer.left_foot.linear_velocity = Vector3.ZERO
        PunchType.CROSS:
            # Lead foot completely immobilized
            boxer.left_foot_ik.target_weight = 1.0
            boxer.left_foot.global_position = boxer.lead_foot_anchor_pos
            # Rear foot pinned at ball, heel yawed
            boxer.right_foot_ik.pin_to_canvas_ball_of_foot()
        PunchType.LEAD_HOOK:
            boxer.right_foot_ik.target_weight = 1.0
            boxer.left_foot_ik.apply_heel_pivot(45.0, 70.0)

3. PUNCH SPEEDS: SIM READABILITY VS. REALITY
Why Real Boxing Speed Looks Broken to a Manager
In real professional boxing, an elite lead jab reaches full extension in $0.12\text{ s to } 0.16\text{ s}$ (4 to 5 frames at 30 fps).
In a third-person management camera (isometric/high-angle at $Z = 2.6\text{m} \dots 5.0\text{m}$):
A 4-frame punch lacks intermediate frames. The glove appears to instantly disappear from the cheek and reappear in the opponent’s face. This produces the operator's complaint: "the jab fires like a bullet why is it so fast."
The human eye needs at least 6 to 8 frames of wind-up/extension from a distant vantage point to register wind-up, target intent, and collision.
The Management Sim Formula: $1.35\times$ Broadcast Readability Scaling

We do not use real-life speed; we apply a $+35%$ time dilation to strike extension while preserving snappy, realistic impact audio.

\hline

\text{Rear Cross} & 0.22\text{ s} & \mathbf{0.340\text{ s}} & 10 \text{ frames} & 0.033\text{ s (1 fr)} & \mathbf{0.220\text{ s}} \text{ (7 fr)} \

Total Jab Cycle: $0.260\text{ s (Extension)} + 0.033\text{ s (Impact)} + 0.180\text{ s (Retract)} = \mathbf{0.473\text{ s}}$ (Approx. 14 frames at 30 fps).
The "Tell" Phase (First 3 frames, $0.00\text{ s} - 0.10\text{ s}$): The fighter's lead shoulder elevates $2\text{cm}$ and hips rotate $5^\circ$ before the fist moves. This telegraph gives the viewer's eye the visual clue required to track the punch before it extends.
4. SPACING, COMBAT DISTANCES & PREVENTING MESH MERGING
The Fatal Flaw: $0.557\text{ m}$ Chest Distance

In the measured data, the engine held a chest-center distance of $0.557\text{ m}$.

An average male boxer torso has an anterior-posterior chest depth of $0.24\text{ m}$ and shoulder width of $0.46\text{ m}$.
Two boxers facing each other at $0.557\text{ m}$ chest-center have less than $0.077\text{ m}$ (7.7 cm) of clearance between their chest surfaces.
Any standard arm swing or lean forward causes the skeletal meshes to intersect.
       OUTSIDE (JAB)                   INSIDE (HOOK / INFIGHT)               CLINCH (CHEST TO CHEST)
[BOXER A] <---- 1.45m ----> [BOXER B]   [BOXER A] <-- 0.85m --> [BOXER B]     [BOXER A] < 0.65m > [BOXER B]
- Arms extend fully                     - Elbows bent 90 deg                  - Underhooks, hands pinned
- Zero mesh overlap                     - Clean 0.37m chest clearance         - Dedicated clinch animation

The Capsule Separation Solver

To prevent meshes from ever merging below $0.80\text{ m}$ outside of a clinch:

Kinematic Torso Buffer: Attach a vertical capsule collider to the root spine bone ($Radius = 0.38\text{ m}$, $Height = 1.2\text{ m}$).
Repulsion Velocity: If chest-center distance falls below $0.78\text{ m}$ and no Clinch State is active, inject an immediate non-linear separation force: $$\vec{V}{\text{pushback}} = \hat{D}{AB} \times \left(\frac{0.80 - \text{dist}}{0.80}\right) \times 2.8\text{ m/s}$$
Guard Elbow Bumping: If boxers collide at $<0.80\text{ m}$, play the Forearm Frame state: the boxers post their lead forearms against the opponent’s shoulders rather than letting the arms clip behind the opponent's back.

Worst Moment 1: The Disarticulated Spine (Red in Frames F03–F04, F09)
What Happened: Red's spine twisted past $54^\circ$ while hips stayed static. Red's neck snapped backward into an unnatural hunch.
The Fighting-Game Animator Fix:
Clamp Spine Yaw: Clamp bone Spine_02 and Spine_03 rotational yaw to a hard maximum of $\pm 18^\circ$ each (cumulative maximum spinal rotation $= \mathbf{36^\circ}$).
Pelvis Distribution: Any rotational requirement beyond $36^\circ$ must be forced into the Pelvis/Hips bone ($50%$) and the Rear Foot Ball Pivot ($50%$). If the feet cannot pivot, the punch cannot reach; the punch animation must abort or fall short rather than tearing the torso apart.
Worst Moment 2: The $0.55\text{ m}$ Torso Fusion (Frames F03, F08)
What Happened: Both boxers moved into the same coordinate space, shoulders clipping inside each other's ribcages while throwing punches behind each other's backs.
The Fighting-Game Animator Fix:
Enforce Hard In-Fight Perimeter ($0.82\text{ m}$). If an attack animation moves the boxer closer than $0.82\text{ m}$, clip translation is halted at the threshold while arm IK extends forward.
Implement an Auto-Frame Guard: when distance $< 0.80\text{ m}$, fighter arms automatically default to a high tight earmuff or collar-tie frame, preventing arms from flailing behind the opponent’s model.
Worst Moment 3: The Gliding / Ice-Skating Plant (Frames F01, F06, F10)
What Happened: Red’s lead foot traveled $0.22\text{ m}$ across the canvas while the left hand was fully extended in a jab.
The Fighting-Game Animator Fix:
Implement Foot Contact Locking (IK Ground Pinning) in Godot's SkeletonModification2DTwoBoneIK (or 3D equivalent).
The moment the animation tag reaches PLANT_LEAD ($t = 0.18\text{ s}$), save the world-space transform of the foot bone. During all active striking frames ($t = 0.18\text{ s} \dots 0.40\text{ s}$), lock the foot mesh to that exact world transform, allowing the torso root to translate forward over the planted foot (closing the hip angle) without moving the boot on the canvas.
