## 0. Answer first

- **Two Point is not beautiful because of a big budget or fancy tech. It is beautiful because one strict style was chosen on day one and every asset obeys it.** The art director Mark Smart was the only artist at the start and picked "a simple, cartoonish style that was easy to put together and where the characters' expressions were clearly visible" (Gameliner interview, 2022-05-26, translated from Dutch). The rules were "clean and simplistic", "slightly chunky, a lot more rounded and soft", "no real dirt or noisiness" (Hardcore Gamer, 2018-07-18). SOURCED.
- **The team was small when it shipped.** Two Point Hospital started with about 6 people and shipped with about 16 after roughly 2 years (Gamingcypher, 2018). Sega bought a 17-person studio in May 2019 (Sega IR, 2019-05-09). Wikipedia now lists 40 staff, date of that figure NOT FOUND. These were 20-year veterans from Bullfrog, Lionhead and Mucky Foot, not beginners. SOURCED. So "small team" is true, but it was a small team of experts. JUDGEMENT.
- **Why our gym looks bad: our assets do not match each other.** We are mixing three styles: toy-like Kenney furniture (CC0), realistic MakeHuman/MPFB2 bodies, and the photoreal Poly Haven / Sketchfab gear that J65 proposed. INTERNAL (brief). Two Point's main lesson is a single style that holds together, and our mix breaks exactly that rule. JUDGEMENT. That clash, plus flat lighting with no ambient occlusion or colour grading, is the most likely reason the floor looks "ugly". JUDGEMENT.
- **Pick one direction: stylised, not semi-realistic.** I recommend a "chunky stylised" look, like Two Point or a toy diorama. Keep the J65 Gleason's/Repton cues (red walls with photos, grey concrete, black steel columns, red/black heavy bags), but build them as clean, simple, slightly oversized shapes with flat colour, not photo-scanned leather. JUDGEMENT. Reasons: (a) CC0 and cheap packs exist in this style, with gym and boxing items (see 3a). (b) Stylised characters avoid the uncanny look of realistic bodies seen from above. (c) A 1-2 person team cannot produce or clean up photoreal assets consistently. JUDGEMENT.
- **Fastest route (weeks, not months):** (1) a lighting and post-processing pass in Godot (AgX tonemapper, SSAO, SSIL or a baked LightmapGI, a colour-grading LUT). (2) Replace the MPFB bodies with stylised CC0 characters (Quaternius Ultimate Modular, CC0) driven by CC0 or Mixamo animations. (3) Rebuild the props from ONE stylised gym pack. Candidates: Synty POLYGON Shops Pack, USD 199.99, which ships a Godot 4.6.2 project and includes treadmills, squat racks, boxing bags and gym characters. Or Shrimple3D Low Poly Fitness Gym Pack, USD 39.99, 900 prefabs including 4 boxing rings/MMA cages. SOURCED prices, checked 2026-10-11. (4) Readability rules: walls-down cutaway, colour-coded floor zones, props scaled up slightly. (5) Polish: tilt-shift DOF and an optional outline. Total about 60-100 hours. JUDGEMENT.
- **Licence traps found today:** Synty's licence works in any engine and covers 5 seats per purchase, but bans feeding Synty files into AI 3D generators (Synty EULA, as at 2026-07-09). Synty's new "Sidekick" characters list only Unity/Unreal files, with no FBX or Godot project. Hunyuan3D 2.1's licence explicitly does NOT apply in the European Union, UK or South Korea, so it is unusable for a Cyprus-based studio. Tripo's free tier is non-commercial. Meshy's free tier is CC BY 4.0 (credit required). SOURCED.
- **AI 3D generation is a helper, not the answer.** Paid tiers cost about USD 20/month (Meshy Pro: 1,000 credits, about 20 credits per model; Tripo Pro: about 200 models/month, billed annually). Independent tests still report topology that needs cleanup (top3d.ai, 2026-01-02). SOURCED. Generated textures will clash with a flat stylised palette unless repainted. JUDGEMENT.
- **GDC / 80.lv / Unity art-pipeline talks about Two Point's art: NOT FOUND.** Facts on the pipeline come from interviews and artists' portfolio pages. Exact details like hand-painted vs PBR texturing, outline use and post-processing are NOT FOUND (see section 6).

## 4. Top-5 changes, ranked by visual gain per hour (JUDGEMENT throughout, hours are rough for one generalist)

Before anything else, **write a one-page style sheet** (about 2 h). It should cover: "chunky stylised gym; Gleason's palette (brick-red walls, grey concrete floor, black steel columns, red/black/white leather); flat colours, no photo textures; characters with big hands and feet; props 10-25% oversized; warm key + cool fill; bright and alive." Every later step is checked against it. This matches the house rule "bright and alive, never dark or empty" (INTERNAL).

| Rank | Change | Why it gives the most per hour | Rough hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lighting + post pass**: AgX tonemapper, SSAO on, SSIL on, warm directional key (about 3,500-4,500 K feel) + cool fill/ambient, a few warm point lights over the ring and bags, one 3D LUT grade, slight bloom on windows | Touches every pixel without new assets. Contact shadows alone ground the props. Fixes the "flat/empty" feel quickly | 6-10 |
| 2 | **Swap characters to stylised**: Quaternius Ultimate Modular (CC0), scaled hands and feet up about 15-20% in Blender, retarget Quaternius UAL / Mixamo / KayKit animations; 4-6 colour schemes per role (boxer, coach, member, staff) | Realistic MPFB bodies seen from above clash most with toy props. Characters are what the player watches | 16-30 |
| 3 | **One prop source**: buy Synty Shops Pack (USD 199.99, Godot project) or Shrimple3D Fitness Gym Pack (USD 39.99, rings/cages), recolour to the style sheet, remove all Poly Haven/Sketchfab photoreal gear and any Kenney piece that does not match | Removes the style clash. Gym machines, bags and racks become one family | 12-20 |
| 4 | **Readability rules**: walls-down/cutaway toggle (camera-facing walls drop to stubs), colour-coded floor mats per zone (ring = blue canvas, bag zone = red mats, weights = black rubber, cardio = grey), one hero object per zone (ring, bag row, rack), red brick walls with framed fight posters as decals | Two Point's own top feature was "see behind the walls". Zones read at a glance | 10-16 |
| 5 | **Polish**: depth-based tilt-shift (subtle), A/B-test an outline shader, small bevels/edge highlights on props, idle animation variants for the most-seen actions (skipping, bag work, resting) | Gives the "miniature diorama" charm. Animation variety is where Two Point puts its comedy | 8-14 |
| | **Total** | | **about 54-92 h, plus about 2 h for the style sheet** |

What NOT to do (JUDGEMENT): do not mix photoreal scanned gear with stylised characters. Do not use AI generation as the main asset source. Do not add realistic grime textures (Two Point: "no real dirt or noisiness"). Show wear through shape, such as a slightly sagging bag or tape on gloves, and through colour, not through texture noise.

### On the J65 tension (semi-realistic vs stylised), JUDGEMENT

- J65 (2026-10-08) recommended semi-realistic Poly Haven CC0 + Sketchfab CC-BY gear for a Gleason's/Repton look. INTERNAL.
- Two Point's charm is explicitly stylised, clay-like and "not too rendered". SOURCED (Retronauts 2018-05-09).
- Recommendation: **keep J65's mood board, change its rendering style.** Gleason's is a palette and a set of objects: red walls, photos, concrete, steel, a ring in the middle. All of that can be built as chunky stylised assets. Semi-realism needs consistent photoreal characters, PBR texturing and lighting skill that a 1-2 person team cannot maintain. Mixing semi-real props with Kenney toys and MPFB humans is the most likely cause of today's ugliness.
- Sketchfab CC-BY gear also adds attribution bookkeeping per asset. JUDGEMENT. (CC-BY requires credit. Sketchfab per-model terms were not re-checked today: NOT FOUND.)
