# Design wanted: how the player READS the other man in Ring Switches

You are the game designer. A playtester played nine bouts and scored FUN 6, DEPTH 7, CLARITY 6, VARIETY 6 out of 10.
Her verdict: the ORDER of actions is a real, original idea and it works; the fun between those moments is missing
because the most important guess, WHICH LINE IS OPEN, is blind. We want your design for the read. Plain words, short,
a table first.

## The game, as it is today
Each beat both men set three switches at once: the LEAD hand, the REAR hand, the FEET, and the order they are thrown in
(a beat is three moments: your first action meets his first, and so on). A hand that does not punch is held HIGH or
LOW and blocks one line; a punching hand is out of its guard only in the moment it punches.

| Held hand | Stops | The punches on that line (health they take) |
|---|---|---|
| Lead hand high | straight head punches | the jab (1), the cross (3) |
| Lead hand low | body punches | the body jab (1), the body hook (2), the body cross (2) |
| Rear hand high | head punches from the side | the lead hook (3), the overhand (4) |
| Rear hand low | punches from below | the lead uppercut (3), the rear uppercut (4) |

Three ranges: Long (jab, body jab), Mid (most punches), Inside (hooks, uppercuts, the clinch). Health 18, stamina 8,
3 rounds of 6 beats. A punch of his that misses or is blocked makes your later punches that beat counters (+1 health).
A fast big punch that lands stops his slower punch of the same moment.

Three opponents (the Jabber, the Slugger, the Body Snatcher). Each plays the beat's best mix of plans, leaning to his
style; a share of his beats are plain beats of his style; and if you throw the same plan in two of your last three
punching beats he may read it and play the best answer to exactly that.

## What the player is shown before he chooses, today
- The other man's last three beats, newest first, holds included ("1 Step in, 2 Body cross - lead hand high"). New this
  week. On hover: what each of his hands did this round (held high / held low / thrown, as counts).
- A mark on your hold button when he threw the same punch in two of his last three punching beats.
- Health, stamina, guard, the range, the ground behind each man.
- A side-view picture of both boxers. Between beats it shows YOUR held hands. It shows nothing of what he is about to do.

## What the playtester said (her words, shortened)
- "The game asks its most important question - which line is open - when the answer is still hidden, so it is a coin
  flip rather than a read." His lead hand held low blocked her body hook while his body cross landed, twice running,
  and both times she could only explain it afterwards.
- "The deepest skill today is pattern-memorising a man's beats."
- "From the player's seat I cannot see whether he is reading me or playing his own game."
- What she could not judge from words: "the opponent's body language between beats, which is where a player would
  learn to read a guard".
- Measured after her play: an opponent plays the identical beat three times running in 0 to 10% of bouts, so a plain
  memory of his last beat is NOT a reliable read either.

## What we want from you
1. ONE design for the read: what the player sees of the other man BEFORE he chooses, that makes "which line is open" a
   read and not a coin flip, without making it a certainty. A table: what is shown, where (his picture, his card, a
   switch), what it tells, how often it is true.
2. It must keep "both men act at once": he never sees your switches, you never see his.
3. It must be different for each of the three men (a Jabber's tell is not a Slugger's), and the player must be able to
   get BETTER at it over ten bouts.
4. Say what it costs the man who is read (does a tell make him weaker, or does he pay for hiding it?) and whether the
   player has tells of his own that the opponent reads.
5. The pictures: we have one authored picture set of two boxers (guard, each hand held high or low, every punch landed /
   blocked / missed). Say what could be shown with those alone, and what would need a new picture.
6. The case against your own design, in three lines.
7. End with what would make this a 10 out of 10 for FUN, most important first, specific enough to build.

## What would a human do?
A boxer watches the shoulders, the hips and the front foot, not the gloves; a coach says "he drops his right before he
hooks". A card player reads a habit and a hesitation.
