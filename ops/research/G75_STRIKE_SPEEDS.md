Research only. Numbers are ranges from measured athletes, not clip-vs-clip. Peak is linear effector speed unless noted. Contact itself is 11–14 ms in the best punch studies — sub-frame at 30 fps. What reads on a broadcast is wind-up + travel + follow-through, not the contact spike.

Method caveat used everywhere: bag/pad/dummy lab work ≠ in-fight speed. Combination punches are slower than single-max (Piorkowski 2011). Air kicks are faster than targeted kicks (Wąsik & Góra 2016). Track the striking surface, not a standing foot.

## 1. Peak effector speed by class

### Jab
4.5–9.5 m/s fist/glove (wrist/knuckles).
- Stanley et al. 2018, IJPAS: 15 amateur ♂, 3D optical mocap, punch bag. Peak fist 5.85 ± 0.85 m/s. Delivery 405 ± 150 ms.
- Kimm & Thiel 2015, Procedia Eng.: IMU on wrist, 10 ♂ / 6 ♀ boxers. Jab-out 8.1 ± 1.4 (♂) / 6.6 ± 1.6 (♀) m/s.
- Ueberschär et al. 2021, Sensors: wearable, experienced. Jab peak 7.9 ± 0.9 m/s.
- Whiting et al. 1988, AJSM: 4 experienced, 3D film. Contact 5.9–8.2; peaks 6.6–12.5 m/s, 8–21 ms before contact (hooks vs jabs differ).

### Cross / rear straight
6–11.5 m/s fist/hand.
- Stanley 2018: 6.97 ± 0.86 m/s, 495 ± 150 ms.
- Walilko, Viano & Bir 2005, Br J Sports Med: 7 Olympic boxers, 5 weights, high-speed video onto Hybrid III. Hand velocity 9.14 ± 2.06 m/s.
- Atha et al. 1985, BMJ: 1 pro HW (Frank Bruno), 2D + instrumented pendulum. Fist at impact 8.9 m/s; start→impact 100 ms; 0.49 m travel; follow-through 110 mm.
- Piorkowski et al. 2011, J Sports Sci: single-max cross contact 8.22 ± 1.08 m/s; all single-max punches 9.26 ± 2.09 m/s.
- Dinu et al. 2020, Front. Sports Act. Living: elite vs junior IMU. Elite cross 8.1 ± 2.1 m/s (same mean as juniors; force much higher).
- Beránek et al. 2023 meta, Sports Biomechanics: straight/reverse punches "over 10 m/s" in the pooled combat-sport set.

### Lead hook
8–14 m/s fist. Fastest boxing peak in the 3D bag studies.
- Stanley 2018: 11.95 ± 1.84 m/s, 657 ± 145 ms (longest delivery).
- Dinu 2020 elite hook 11.2 ± 2.0; junior 8.9 ± 0.9.
- Piorkowski 2011: hooks > jab/cross at contact.

### Rear hook
8–14 m/s fist.
- Stanley 2018: 11.48 ± 1.90 m/s, 586 ms.
- Ueberschär 2021 experienced rear-hook peak only 6.93 ± 0.9 (different instrument/definition; treat as lower bound).

### Uppercut (lead / rear)
6.5–13.5 m/s fist.
- Stanley 2018: lead 10.60 ± 2.30, rear 11.55 ± 1.72; 606–627 ms.
- Dinu 2020 elite 10.2 ± 1.8, junior 7.3 ± 1.0.
- Ueberschär 2021 experienced 6.8 ± 0.9.

### Overhand
NOT FOUND as a separately tracked class. Coaching literature treats it as a steeper, longer-path cross (more torso side-bend). Gate as cross band, slightly slower start, same peak ceiling (~6–11 m/s fist) until someone measures it.

### Horizontal elbow
Standing olecranon linear velocity: NOT FOUND in peer-reviewed standing Muay Thai/MMA mocap.
- Beránek et al. 2020, IJERPH: G&P (kneeling) elbow vs palm vs fist — force only (elbow Fpeak up to 13 188 N). No m/s.
- Closest analog, not an elbow: karate downward hammerfist / knife-hand 10–14 m/s (classic technique tables; not olecranon).
- Do not invent an elbow m/s from punch data. Elbow path is shorter than a hook; peak can look "too fast" even at real speed because travel distance is small.

### Upward elbow
NOT FOUND.

### Spinning back elbow
NOT FOUND.

### Straight knee
Knee / distal thigh ~4–8 m/s in the usable studies; one outlier.
- Weerawat et al. 2020, IOP Conf. Ser. MSE: Qualisys, 5 pro Muay Thai. Straight knee-kick 6.29 ± 1.01 (max 7.95); diagonal knee 6.42 ± 1.75 (3.87–8.68). Marker set includes shank/knee complex.
- Shapie et al. (IOP 1020): 10 MT beginners, knee forward 1.0–1.2 m/s, vertical 4.2–4.4 m/s — novices, not a ceiling.
- AESA "Kinematics Analysis of Muay Thai Knee Techniques": 5 pros, "knee straight 15.15 ± 4.60 vs knee strike 6.01 ± 1.47". Treat as unverified (path-integrated or different point; 15 m/s is kick-foot territory). Prefer Weerawat 6–8 until replicated.

### Jumping knee
NOT FOUND (no peer-reviewed jumping-knee linear peak).

### Teep / front kick
Foot 5–16 m/s; targeted elite typically 7–12.
- Vagner et al. 2023, Sports: systematic review. FK foot velocity (target, mid) elite mean ~9.6 ± 1.1; air higher (~10.7–12.3). Full published span 5.2–16.7.
- Pearson (ISBS / kung-fu): front 11.77 ± 0.18 m/s, duration 0.23 s.
- Weerawat 2020: MT straight foot-thrust 6.01 ± 1.47 (4.38–7.93); sideways 5.28 ± 1.24.
- Falcó / Estevan line (TKD): kicking-foot velocity often 12–14 m/s depending on stance angle.

### Low roundhouse (thigh)
Same mechanical class as body RH; shin/instep/foot 8–18 m/s.
- Weerawat 2020 "Diagonal Kick" (MT round kick): 17.15 ± 2.90 (13.58–21.03). Foot/metatarsal tracking.
- Gavagan & Sayers 2017, J Sports Sci: expert RH max linear foot MT 13.24 ± 2.30, karate 13.66 ± 1.06, TKD 14.66 ± 1.18. Impact speed much lower (MT 7.22 ± 1.47 — they decelerate into the pad).
- Aandahl et al. 2018 kickboxing RH cited to ~17.4 m/s.
- Vagner 2023: RH foot 6.9–18.3 across papers.

### Body roundhouse
Same band. O'Sullivan et al. 2009 (college TKD): body shots 17.7 ± 1.8 vs head 16.5 ± 0.6 m/s (body slightly faster).
MT body kick: Varde'i et al. 2021 IMU — circular kick peak linear 7.1 m/s at 0.48 s before impact (impact-referenced, not peak-path).

### Head roundhouse
Foot 8–17 m/s; often a hair slower than body because of lift.
- Gavagan 2017 pad at mastoid: max 13–15, impact 5.6–7.2.
- Moreira / TKD literature: up to ~16 m/s.
- Sport-version ITF/TKD RH (Słomka et al. 2022 style): execution 0.45–0.46 s, max vel 12.3–12.4 (range 8.4–16.5).

### Spinning back kick
Foot / heel 6–12 m/s (slower peak than snapping RH; more mass).
- Kim et al. 2011, J Appl Biomech: TKD back kick peak kicking vel 11.5 ± 1.0 m/s, kick time 0.27 ± 0.06 s.
- Wąsik 2016-ish dwit-chagi case: resultant 6.00 ± 1.39 (4.01–7.34), duration 0.77 ± 0.46 s.
- Pieter reported back-kick vel 6.71–9.14 m/s.
- Elite vs youth back kick (ISBS): peak foot 8.86 ± 0.93 vs 8.36 ± 1.6.
- Tornado / spinning hook are different techniques (snapping; Jones ISEA 2026 groups them with RH ~fast, contact ~27 ms vs thrust kicks ~46 ms).

## 2. Duration and what reads at 30 fps

1 frame @ 30 fps = 33.3 ms. Contact of a max punch is 11–14 ms (Atha 14 ms rise; Walilko ~11.4 ms). That is 0.3–0.4 frames. The audience never "sees contact." They see prep, travel, and recovery.

### Onset → impact (delivery)
| Class | Onset→impact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Jab | 100 ms (Bruno, after 201 ms trunk advance) to 405 ± 150 ms (amateur bag, INIT→CONTACT) | Atha 1985; Stanley 2018 |
| Cross | 223 ± 99 ms (Piorkowski ACTION→CONTACT single max) to 495 ± 150 ms (Stanley) | Piorkowski 2011; Stanley 2018 |
| Hooks | 477 ± 203 ms (Piorkowski all modes); 586–657 ms (Stanley) | same |
| Uppercuts | 606–627 ms (Stanley) | Stanley 2018 |
| Experienced wearable split | throw 71–117 ms, contact 122–144 ms, retract 169–262 ms, total 385–523 ms | Ueberschär 2021 |
| Front / turning kick | 0.23–0.25 s whole kick | Pearson ISBS |
| Sport RH | 0.34–0.67 s execution (mean ~0.45) | Słomka-type 2022 |
| TKD RH to target | execution ~0.59–0.73 s by stance; peak vel ~310 ms into movement | Estevan 2013; high-perf TKD 2019 |
| Gavagan RH "execution" | MT 1.02 ± 0.15 s, TKD 1.54 ± 0.52 s | includes a long load; do not treat as travel-only |
| Back kick | 0.27 ± 0.06 s (Kim) to 0.77 ± 0.46 s (Wąsik) | Kim 2011; Wąsik |
| Elbows, jumping knee, spinning elbow | NOT FOUND | — |

Whiting: peak fist speed 8–21 ms before contact. A readable clip must still be accelerating through the last 2–3 frames, not already past peak.

### Impact → recovery
- Punch retraction in experienced boxers: ~170–260 ms (5–8 frames @ 30 fps) — Ueberschär 2021.
- Atha follow-through distance 110 mm after 8.9 m/s impact (a few frames of arm extension past the head).
- Kick recovery is the chamber-down / plant; often as long as the outbound kick. Snapping RH contact ~27 ms, thrust kick contact ~46 ms (Jones ISEA 2026).

### Elbow on a 30 fps broadcast
No lab duration. Geometry: olecranon travel is ~20–40 cm vs jab ~50 cm (Atha 49 cm) vs hook longer. At even a modest 6–8 m/s over 30 cm, travel is 38–50 ms = 1–1.5 frames. That is why an elbow with no load frame is a smear.

What makes a fast real strike read:
- Anticipation / load — hip and shoulder start before the effector (proximal-to-distal). Stanley: sequencing present in jab/cross, messier in hooks/uppercuts.
- Silhouette change that lasts ≥3–4 frames (weight shift, elbow chamber, knee lift).
- Contact hold is useless at 30 fps (11–14 ms). Games fake this with hit-stop / freeze / impact frames.
- Follow-through + retract 5–8 frames so the arm/leg doesn't teleport home.
- Broadcast combat sports add slow-mo on highlight elbows/knees for the same reason.

Operator complaint ("elbows … weird hand movement") is consistent with: short-path strike + missing load frames + hand markers sliding because the clip was authored as a punch path. Speed multiplier 0.88x of source is irrelevant if the source already has no readable load.

## 3. How shipped games present strike speed

They do not play raw mocap at 1.0× and hope. They add time that physics does not have.

### EA UFC 3/4/5
- Strikes are animation-time + frame windows (execution frames), not lab m/s.
- UFC 5 patches repeatedly slowed moves that "played too fast for the animation": straight→lead overhand combo "too fast" (post-beta); haymaker 22→24 f; switch-kick "too fast for its animation… beginning had to be rushed" so they slowed the switch and paid it back with damage (Apr 2024 / Dexerto patch notes; EA post-beta notes).
- They also sped heavy-division slow strikes by 2–4 frames so weight classes didn't look floaty.
- Major-lunge punches "looked way too fast" at sprint range — range cut (Nov 2023).
- Reviews: strike spam / elbows chewing block; KO camera slow-mo copied from Fight Night (Gamepur). Readability is a live tuning knob, not a physics constant.

### Fight Night Champion
- Dropped 60→30 fps vs Round 4 but "plays faster" (VideoGamer 2011).
- Counter window + brief slowdown on clean counters — players argued it was a readability/UX signal, not realism (GameFAQs 2011).
- Punch response felt delayed or stacked depending on input queue — animation authority over button speed.

### Undisputed
- Designed slower/heavier than Fight Night; career-low stats "fights feel slow"; Steam Deck review: "even the fastest punches take half a second."
- Opposite complaint also exists: flicker-jab animations looked unreal and were patched out (Martial Mind / r/undisputedboxing).
- Power modifier explicitly slows the punch for force (Shacknews). Authenticity = duration gated by stamina and planted feet (PC Gamer / GDC-adjacent interview with Habib).

### Tekken / Street Fighter
- Currency is frames at 60 Hz, not m/s.
- Startup / active / recovery. Ryu st.HP: 38 total frames, 7 active (EventHubs).
- Hit-stop freezes attacker+victim a few frames so the hit reads (TV Tropes; SF6 commentary: hit-stop is extra freeze, not simulation time — jchensor).
- Tekken 8 updates collisions every frame in 3D; still uses hit-stop and round-end slow-mo (Tekken 7).
- Design threshold: ~24 f startup is the "see and guard" line in Tekken (geppopotamus). That is 400 ms — coincidentally the Stanley jab delivery mean.

Pattern: if a strike is mechanically fast, shipped games add anticipation, active frames, hit-stop, or camera slow-mo. If a strike is mechanically long (kicks, spinning), they sometimes cut dead time so it doesn't feel floaty. Nobody ships a 40 ms elbow at 1.0× with no load and expects it to read.

## 4. What the game should gate

Measure the striking surface (glove knuckle / olecranon / patella / instep or distal tibia — pick one and keep it). Reject standing-foot peaks. Compare peak linear speed and onset→impact duration.

| Class | Peak band (m/s) | Min on-screen onset→impact @30 fps | Source of band | Source of duration floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jab | 5–9.5 fist | 6 frames (200 ms) — 3 frames is Bruno's stripped 100 ms and will flicker | Stanley, Kimm, Walilko/Atha, Ueberschär | Atha 100 ms is the elite floor; Stanley mean 405 ms is the readable mean |
| Cross | 6–11.5 fist | 7–8 frames (230–270 ms) | Stanley, Walilko, Atha, Piorkowski, Dinu | Piorkowski 223 ms; Stanley 495 ms |
| Lead hook | 8–14 fist | 10 frames (330 ms) | Stanley, Dinu, Piorkowski | hooks take longer (Stanley 657; Piorkowski 477) |
| Rear hook | 8–14 fist | 10 frames | Stanley | Stanley 586 ms |
| Uppercut | 6.5–13.5 fist | 10 frames | Stanley, Dinu | Stanley 606–627 ms |
| Overhand | 6–11.5 fist (inferred) | 8–10 frames | NOT FOUND — inherit cross + extra load | UFC 5 had to add frames when overhand combos "played too fast" |
| Horizontal elbow | NOT FOUND | 6 frames load+travel minimum even if physics is 2 frames | no standing m/s | geometry + UFC/Fight Night practice: short weapons need extra hold/hit-stop |
| Upward elbow | NOT FOUND | 6 frames | none | same |
| Spinning back elbow | NOT FOUND | 12 frames (spin must read) | none | spin kicks/elbows in UFC 5 are the moves they keep retiming |
| Straight knee | 4–8 knee (15 m/s paper discarded pending replication) | 8 frames | Weerawat 2020; beginner Shapie | no duration paper; use kick-short floor |
| Jumping knee | NOT FOUND | 10 frames (leave ground must read) | none | UFC flying-knee is a long animation on purpose |
| Teep / front kick | 5–12 targeted foot (air may hit 16) | 7 frames (230 ms) | Vagner 2023; Pearson 0.23 s; Weerawat teep 6.0 | Pearson 0.23 s |
| Low RH (thigh) | 8–18 foot/shin | 10 frames (330 ms) | Weerawat 17.2; Gavagan 13–15; Vagner 6.9–18.3 | sport RH ~0.45 s; do not use Gavagan's 1.0–1.5 s "execution" as travel |
| Body RH | 8–18 | 10–12 frames | same + O'Sullivan body 17.7 | same |
| Head RH | 8–17 | 12 frames (lift) | O'Sullivan head 16.5; Gavagan mastoid | Estevan 0.59–0.73 s |
| Spinning back kick | 6–12 heel/foot | 12 frames | Kim 11.5 / 0.27 s; Wąsik 6.0 / 0.77 s | use ≥0.27 s; if clip is 8 frames the spin is fake |

Hard readability rule (game, not physics): below 6 frames onset→impact at 30 fps (~200 ms), a strike stops reading unless you add hit-stop or a camera punch-in. That floor is Atha's elite 100 ms doubled so a 30 fps viewer gets 3 frames of load + 3 of travel. Elbows and teeps violate it first.

Hard speed rule: a clip whose striking-surface peak sits outside the band above is not "real speed." 9.6× on a standing foot is a tracking error, not a kick.

Classes with no measured source: overhand (as its own class), horizontal / upward / spinning elbow (linear m/s and duration), jumping knee. Straight-knee 15 m/s report is not usable. Female and in-fight (not bag) speeds are thin outside Kimm's jab/cross IMU.

Still unknown: standing elbow-tip m/s and onset→impact on elite Nak Muay/MMA, overhand vs cross peaks, jumping-knee peaks, and whether 30 fps readability needs 6 frames or 8 once hit-stop is in.
