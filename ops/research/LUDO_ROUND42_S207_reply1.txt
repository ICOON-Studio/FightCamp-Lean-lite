SECTION A — FIRST CUSTOMER CHECK-IN AT RECEPTION
A1. The Exact Trigger, Frequency, and Prompt Wording
Trigger Conditions

The check-in event does not poll randomly. It evaluates during the gym floor simulation loop when the global calendar time step is running (sim_speed > 0):

First-Time Trigger: Fires precisely when the gym's cumulative reputation score passes 100 points (e.g., after the gym's first amateur fight or week 2 of club tier operation), and an unassigned walk-in NPC agent enters the outer perimeter door node (nav_door_exterior) and paths to reception_counter_front_node (Vector3(4.2, 0.0, -1.8) in Tier 1 gym coordinates).
Post-First-Time Throttle (Never a Nag):
Check-in prompts will never interrupt the player if a bout is currently booked in Fight Camp mode, or if the manager has a modal menu open.
Maximum frequency: 1 prompt per in-game calendar week for Tier 1 (Club); 2 per week for Tier 2 (Commercial); auto-handled by Front Desk Staff if the player has hired an Administrative Assistant (unlockable staff role at Tier 2), completely eliminating manual prompts unless a rare "high-potential prospect" arrives.
If a customer arrives while the player is paused or reviewing a screen, the walk-in waits at the counter for up to 48 in-game hours (visible as an NPC standing at the desk) without pausing the game or throwing an intrusive popup. The prompt only pops when the player returns to the Gym Floor camera view.

A4. Long-Term Value of a Member (Detailed Mechanics & Numbers)

A member is never a disposable coin-fountain. They are a multi-dimensional club resource governed by three interconnected systems:

1. Economic Cashflow (The Working Capital Engine)
Commercial pro fights pay out irregularly (purses arrive every 8 to 12 weeks).
A stable of 30 general/amateur members paying an average of $65/month produces $1,950/month in guaranteed recurring revenue.
In Tier 1 (Club Gym, $1,400/month facility rent + utilities), amateur memberships cover base operational overhead, ensuring the manager does not go bankrupt while developing young prospects whose pro purses are only $800–$1,500.
2. The Internal Sparring Ecosystem
Hiring external sparring partners for an 8-week camp costs between $250 and $600 per week per partner.
Having in-house amateur members with specific traits (e.g., Marcus Brody: collegiate wrestling pedigree, high physical conditioning) allows your pro fighters to spar against specific looks without paying external contract rates.
Trade-off / Wear: If your pro boxer (Leo Vance, Power 76) spars recklessly against an amateur member, the amateur can suffer injuries (cuts, concussions). An injured member freezes their dues, drops gym reputation, and may cancel their contract.
3. Gym Reputation & Organic Recruitment
Every amateur member who competes in regional amateur bouts (Golden Gloves / state championships) generates gym prestige:
Amateur win: +2.5 Gym Prestige.
Amateur title: +15.0 Gym Prestige.
Higher gym prestige directly increases walk-in applicant quality, boosting the probability of unearthing high-ceiling prospects with 80+ stat potential.
A5. Why This Stays a Deep-Management Decision (Not a Casual Tap)

This mechanic avoids hypercasual/mobile design through capacity constraints, overcrowding friction, and resource competition:

Physical Capacity & Station Congestion:
In Tier 1 (30.0m $\times$ 18.0m floor space), the gym only has 4 Heavy Bags, 1 Sparring Ring, 2 Treadmills, and 1 Multi-Rack Weight Station.
If the player indiscriminately accepts every walk-in customer for quick cash, the floor becomes congested.
Consequence: When pro boxer Leo Vance arrives for his mandatory camp session at 16:00, all heavy bags are occupied. Vance either waits (losing 40% session efficiency) or experiences a morale/stress penalty (+3 Stress: Gym overcrowded).
Coach Attention Slicing:
Coaches have finite hours per day (40 hours/week max before fatigue degrades their teaching quality).
Allocating coach pad-work hours to high-paying amateurs increases immediate revenue but starves pro prospects of technique training, slowing down their progress on the 54-node technique tree.
Locker Room & Hygiene Constraints:
Member counts beyond locker capacity (24 lockers in Tier 1) degrade gym hygiene, driving up facility maintenance costs and raising the baseline infection/illness risk for everyone in the gym.

B2. The Final Unified Stress System Specification
STRESS VALUE: Integer 0 to 100 (Stored in fighter.stress)
PASSIVE DECAY (Evaluated weekly on Sunday midnight):
  - If Stress < 40: -8 / week (Fighter rests naturally, relaxed baseline)
  - If Stress 40-69: -4 / week
  - If Stress >= 70:  0 / week (Chronic stress blocks passive recovery)

Complete Stress Inputs (Gains & Deductions)

All stress increases are multiplied by the fighter's Composure Modifier:
$$\text{Effective Gain} = \text{Base Gain} \times \left(1.6 - \frac{\text{composure}}{100}\right)$$
(A fighter with 80 Composure takes $0.8\times$ stress; a fighter with 20 Composure takes $1.4\times$ stress).

B3. The Threshold Event, Affliction Roster & Virtue
The Resolve Check Mechanics (At Stress = 100)
The Roll: The moment stress reaches 100 during a day's simulation tick, the calendar pauses.
Virtue Calculation: $$\text{Virtue Chance (%)} = 15% + \left(\frac{\text{composure}}{5}\right)$$ (At 20 Composure: 19% chance; at 50 Composure: 25% chance; at 90 Composure: 33% chance).
If Virtue Passes: Fighter gains the STEELED virtue. Stress resets to 50.
If Virtue Fails: Fighter gains an Affliction determined deterministically by their Dominant Stress Contributor over the prior 4 weeks. Stress drops to 60 (Catharsis buffer).
If Training Overreach was highest $\rightarrow$ OVERCOOKED
If KO / Knockdown trauma was highest $\rightarrow$ GUN-SHY
If Weight Cut depletion was highest $\rightarrow$ STARVING
If Untreated Injury / Physical Wear was highest $\rightarrow$ FRAGILE
The 4 Certified Demo Afflictions & 1 Virtue
1. OVERCOOKED (Negative — Physical / Neuroendocrine Overreach)
Primary Trigger: Sustained high training volume, double sessions, consecutive hard sparring without rest.
Gym & Training Effect:
Training attribute gains slashed by $-50%$ across all stations.
Acute injury risk on all strength/conditioning stations increased by $+25%$.
Animation: Drops hands low on heavy bag; leans against ropes with heavy panting between rounds.
Fight Night Effect:
Late-round stamina decay: In rounds 5 and above, stamina drain per punch and footwork move is increased by $+25%$.
Recovery between rounds restored at only $65%$ normal rate.
Duration: 3 to 6 weeks.
Cure: Mandatory Deload Protocol: Reduce training volume by $\ge 50%$ for 2 consecutive weeks. Zero hard sparring allowed. Completely clears once fatigue metrics drop below baseline.
2. GUN-SHY (Negative — Psychological Trauma Post-Stoppage)
Primary Trigger: Suffering a KO/TKO loss, multiple knockdowns, or heavy damage in camp sparring.
Gym & Training Effect:
Refuses hard sparring: Slotting the fighter into ring sparring results in a red warning icon; fighter refuses to engage at full intensity (sparring XP reduced by $-75%$).
Animation: Flinches visibly when holding pads; steps away from sparring partners before combos finish.
Fight Night Effect:
Punch output volume reduced by $-25%$.
Fighter heavily biases toward defensive moves (boxing_slip, boxing_pull, boxing_catch, boxing_clinch_tieup).
Strike damage with power reduced by $-10$ until they land at least 5 clean punches.
Duration: 4 to 8 weeks.
Cure: 2 consecutive weeks with zero sparring, 1 Head Coach Office Talk, followed by 1 sanctioned bout against a lower-ranked opponent resulting in a victory.
3. STARVING (Negative — Severe Weight Cut Depletion)
Primary Trigger: Cutting $>5%$ of body mass during fight week, aggressive water fasting.
Gym & Training Effect:
Base conditioning regeneration drops to $60%$.
Morning training sessions suffer a $20%$ chance of skipped workouts due to lethargy.
Animation: Sits slumped on locker room bench; noticeably sunken facial model.
Fight Night Effect:
stamina attribute permanently reduced by $-15$ for the entire bout.
chin attribute reduced by $-8$ in rounds 4 and later (drastically elevated susceptibility to flash knockouts).
Duration: Persists through fight night; clears 7 days post-fight once hydration and calories normalize.
Cure: Immediate transition to eucaloric diet, or moving up one weight division (e.g., Welterweight $-66.7\text{ kg}$ to Super Welterweight $-69.9\text{ kg}$).
4. FRAGILE (Negative — Structural Fatigue & Pain Avoidance)
Primary Trigger: Cumulative region wear ($>60%$ on any of the 6 body regions: head_neck, torso_core, left_arm, right_arm, left_leg, right_leg) without rehabilitation.
Gym & Training Effect:
The fighter refuses any training station that loads the afflicted region (e.g., refuses heavy bag if left_arm or right_arm is fragile).
Regional wear accumulation on that zone accelerates by $2.0\times$.
Fight Night Effect:
Damage sustained to the fragile region is magnified by $+30%$.
The fighter alters their guard to protect the injured region, reducing general defense by $-12$ and creating openings elsewhere.
Duration: 4 to 10 weeks (or until structural integrity is restored).
Cure: 2 Physiotherapy/Clinic sessions plus bringing wear on that body region below $25%$.
VIRTUE: STEELED (Positive — Hardened Resolve)
Primary Trigger: Passing the Resolve Check at 100 Stress.
Gym & Training Effect:
Stress accumulation rate halved ($0.5\times$) for all events over the next 4 weeks.
Technique acquisition rate increased by $+15%$.
Fight Night Effect:
composure locked at $99$ for the entire bout; immune to ring-walk jitters and early-round pressure.
Damage from opponent power punches reduces stamina by $20%$ less.
Duration: Exactly 4 calendar weeks.
Cure: N/A (Beneficial state; naturally expires after 28 days).
