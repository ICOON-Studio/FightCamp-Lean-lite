Self-contained research brief for a browser research agent. No repo, no code. Answer in plain text with a source

## Why (operator 2026-09-30, verbatim): "you need to make the stamina have impact on the moves and decision making,
## this will explain why the 70% exists, you just need to tune it right"
Our boxing simulation drains a stamina tank per punch and per second of movement and refills it ONLY between rounds.
Real boxers spend ~75-85% of a round at low intensity; we believe part of that is recovery-seeking. We need numbers to
make (a) in-round recovery and (b) fatigue-driven decisions realistic.

## Questions (answer each; tables welcome)
1. IN-ROUND RECOVERY RATE: after a burst of punching, how fast do boxers (or combat-sport / intermittent-sprint
   athletes) recover during low-intensity spells? Phosphocreatine resynthesis half-time (seconds), heart-rate recovery
   in the first 30-60 s, blood lactate - with the protocol each study used. Any boxing/MMA-specific measurements.
2. WORK:REST INSIDE A ROUND: measured high-intensity bout lengths (s) and the pauses between them in boxing (amateur and
   pro), and how they change from round 1 to the last round.
3. OUTPUT DECLINE ACROSS ROUNDS: punches thrown / landed per round by round number (CompuBox or studies) - does output
   fall, stay flat, or spike in the last round? Any split by fighter style or by fitness.
4. WHAT TIRED BOXERS DO DIFFERENTLY (observational studies, time-motion or technical-tactical analyses): more clinching?
   more jabs vs power punches? fewer combinations? more backing off / circling? less head movement? Quantify where
   possible (e.g. "combination share fell from X% in R1 to Y% in R3").
5. The FIRST 10-20 s after a big exchange: do boxers typically reset distance (step out, circle, clinch)? Numbers if any.
