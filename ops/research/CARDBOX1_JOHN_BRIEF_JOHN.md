## The ten things that most decide a professional round, most important first
The order is my ESTIMATE; no source publishes a ranking. Each line rests on the ABC Boxing Judge Manual [S30], the duBoef judging study [S28] or the counts above.

1. **A knockdown.** It makes the round 10-8 under the rules, and the man who scored it won every knockdown round here, on every card.
2. **Visibly hurting the other man.** The manual says "the effect of the punch is very important", and in duBoef's model a max-impact punch was worth 10.5 times a minimum-impact one.
3. **Clean power punches landed.** The man who landed more power punches won 24 of the 29 rounds here where one man led.
4. **Total clean punches landed.** The man who landed more won 24 of the 33 rounds that weren't level.
5. **Work rate.** The man who threw more won 27 of 34 rounds, and the manual says not to reward a boxer who does nothing.
6. **Body punches.** The manual says solid body punches must count and judges shouldn't concentrate on head shots.
7. **Effective aggression, only when it lands.** Pressing while being beaten to the punch isn't effective, and duBoef found walking a man down barely predicts the judges.
8. **Ring generalship.** Controlling where the fight happens counts, but running and simply avoiding action is not rewarded.
9. **Defence that leads to scoring.** Slipping and countering counts, but holding to avoid contact does not.
10. **Late moments and fouls.** The manual says a punch counts the same whenever it lands and warns against "last second flurry" steals. Reporters still credited late punches for Fury's R2 and Usyk's R11 in Usyk–Fury I. An intentional foul that causes an injury costs two points.
