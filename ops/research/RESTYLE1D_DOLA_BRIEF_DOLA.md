✨ Design Changes & Why It Reads Better
Everything in rounded cards with subtle borders instead of flat panels. Cards create clear visual containers — your eye knows exactly where one group of information ends and the next begins, so you scan faster without getting lost.
Small-caps amber section labels + plain larger body text. Creates automatic visual hierarchy without bold clutter; you spot category headers instantly while content stays comfortable to read.
Centre-aligned headline + key-value tiles at a glance. The most important result info hits you immediately; tiles make those 4 critical numbers visually equal and scannable in a single glance.
Two-colour split bars meeting in the middle. Direct visual comparison — your boxer is always blue on left, opponent always red on right — no legend needed, your brain processes the difference instantly.
Exactly one filled primary button at the bottom. No ambiguity about what to do next; all other actions are plain/outlined so they don't compete for attention.
⭐ To Reach 10/10
Add subtle hover states to cards (slight border brighten) and plan lines (background tint) so users feel which item they're reading/interacting with — currently static but clean.
Include tiny portrait placeholders next to boxer names in the header and round-by-round — faces build quick emotional connection and reinforce "your boxer vs opponent" without reading text.
Animate the bars and tiles on load — bars grow from centre outward; tiles fade up sequentially. Subtle 0.3s entry motion guides eye flow from headline → stats → details → action button without overwhelming the screen.
Add a "REPLAY PLAN" secondary button next to the primary one — users often want to review their choices before returning to the gym; it belongs here but stays secondary so it doesn't compete.
