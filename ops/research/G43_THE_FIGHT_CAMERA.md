# G43 — THE CAMERA, WHEN MOST PUNCHES ARE NOT DRAWN

Grok, Expert, 2026-09-10. Driven in the in-app browser by Claude; 56 sources.
Research only — no FightCamp shot list, no nodes, no transitions table. G42's

**The hole G42 named:** if the live unit is short punches and most engine impacts
are not drawn, something still has to occupy the frame. That something is
**camera plus HUD.** The HUD half already exists (heart rate, cut pips, seat
chip, round scores, stoppage reason). The camera half had not been researched.

## §1 — THE BROADCAST GRAMMAR, SHOT BY SHOT

**Who cuts:** a director in a truck, not the camera operator. **Rick Tugman**
(ProBox, interviewed at length by Bad Left Hook, 2023) states the job as
*pictures under the words* — he listens to the commentators and puts a frame on
what they just named. **Sara Chenery** (Sky Sports boxing director, SVG Europe
2017 and an NFTS masterclass) states the other half: give the living room a
ringside seat without chopping.

**Hold durations in seconds are not published.** Anyone quoting "hold the wide
for eight seconds" is inventing. What *is* published is which shot, why it
changes, and who calls it.

### The default wide — head-to-toe

Tugman's **Camera 1**: elevated, front of ring, fighters head-to-toe. The home
shot. Sky's older "long-lens head-to-toe in-out" is the same object; Chenery says
they use it less on big shows because **ropes eat the frame**, and they moved a
front jib ringside so the camera can *walk* when the referee blocks the view
instead of cutting.

**Trigger off it is not time elapsed.** It is action concentrating (a corner
barrage), a commentator naming stance or distance, a knockdown, or the bell.
Chenery: come back out head-to-toe when the referee separates them.

### The tight — trunks-up

Tugman's **Camera 2**, same riser: waist-up. He goes here "nine times out of ten"
on a barrage in the corner, because if it breaks he has Camera 1 as the escape.
Also when a commentator talks reach. **This is the shot that asserts contact. It
is the dangerous one for us.** The published rule is directional — tighten when
the pocket forms, widen when it breaks — not a clock.

### Ninety-degree and apron

Tugman's Camera 5 sits 90° off the matched pair; Cameras 3 and 4 are handheld on
the apron. He will **not** cut apron-to-apron (too much jump) and will not use an
apron camera unless the action is in its face. Chenery is explicit that cutting
every time the ref crosses is what first-time boxing directors do, **and it is
the thing they trained out.**

### Jib / overhead / beauty / venue

Tugman uses the jib sparingly live and prefers it as a replay camera; he'll take
it for a few seconds to show stance and distance. A fixed venue-wide (his Camera
9; Euroscena's "beauty shot" on the lighting rig; Sky's Spidercam on
Joshua–Klitschko) is atmosphere, walkouts, and the shot after a finish. Chenery:
do not stay on the walk-in too long — the fight is about to start.

### The corner, between rounds

**The one interval the book already gives a minute.** Cinevideo / DAZN (SVG
Europe, 2021): "Between rounds, boxing allows us to broadcast the replay inserts
in super slow motion." The minute is the replay slot, and also the injury slot.

Tugman's example is the one that matters for our pips: a fighter did not come out
after the third; the apron camera heard the corner and shot the swollen foot; he
took Camera 4 immediately. **Cuts are a story you tell wide, then tight.** Sky
puts boom poles on the trainers for audio (TVBEurope, 2024). Boxing corners are
stools in numbered corners — that geometry is a camera assignment, and it is why
a ring is easier to cover than a cage between periods.

### The replay — when, how many, how soon

**Two different replays exist. Do not mix them.**

- **Broadcast replay.** EVS operators in the truck (Euroscena: two slomo ops; UFC
  Apex has dedicated Replay-A / Replay-B rooms with EVS LSM). They offer
  packages; the director takes one. Wilder–Fury II had seven high-speed cameras
  (two at 8×, five at 6×) plus a 120-camera 4DReplay array; DAZN Wembley used the
  HDC-3500V ringside for 4× superslow. A knockdown earns an immediate package,
  often from a mat/bumper camera that exists for that fall (ESPN's
  buried-in-canvas bumper cams; PBC MattCam).
- **Commission replay.** Nevada's cut-versus-butt review. Yellow light, official
  at the table — **not** the same feed the audience is watching. Thomas Hauser
  quoting Greg Sirb: this is not football, you do not get a timeout.

**Folklore to refuse:** "the broadcast always shows three angles of every punch."
It does not. It shows a package of a **closed** moment. And how soon after a
knockdown is unpublished as a number — practice is hold the live tight through
the count, then roll slomo. Inventing "replay at +4 seconds" would be G42's ±20%
mistake.

### The knockdown and the stoppage

Live: Tugman tight (Camera 2) with Camera 1 as escape; mat/bumper if they have
it; then **the referee's body becomes the subject** (count, wave-off). Chenery's
Board of Control constraint: no extra lights once boxing is on, limited bodies in
the ring after, interview only when the director says. The stoppage is a referee
shot plus the hurt man's face, then a cut to the corner, then the replay package.

### The decision read

Wide on the centre of the ring, tight on faces, graphic of the cards. **Ceremony,
not an action cut.** Nobody is inventing punches here.

### Boxing versus MMA, ring versus cage — as a camera problem

- **Occlusion.** Ropes eat a long-lens frame. Chain-link is worse. Inertia
  Unlimited's **FenceCam** (SVG, 2013) exists because the mesh *is* the job: lens
  faceplate against the fence, shallow depth of field so the wire disappears and
  the man five feet away is sharp. **A cage is not a ring with eight sides. It is
  a fence you have to optically delete.**
- **Where a human can stand.** Ring apron handhelds; a cage you shoot through or
  over. A camera inside the octagon is rare enough to be newsworthy.
- **Between periods.** Boxing has two stools and a published minute. MMA has a
  fence, a stool if the promotion puts one, and a shorter break — a different
  assignment (UFC puts PTZs on the coaches).
- **The finish picture.** A boxing knockdown is a fall onto canvas, hence
  bumper/mat cams. An MMA finish is often grounded — out of scope here.
- **Scale of the truck.** ProBox-scale boxing ≈ 9 assigned jobs; Sky
  Joshua–Klitschko 16 on the ring; UFC Fight Night ≈ 12; UFC 300 27; Sphere 55
  over two days. **Those numbers are production budget, not grammar.** The
  grammar at eight cameras and at fifty-five is the same: a wide that tells the
  truth about distance, a tight that tells a story about contact, a replay of a
  closed event.

### Folklore, marked

- **Hold lengths in seconds.** Not in Tugman, not in Chenery, not in the SVG
  production pieces.
- **"Always three replay angles."** Unsourced. Packages vary; 4DReplay is a PPV
  toy.
- **"The director follows the punches."** Named directors follow **commentators
  and concentration of action**, not each impact.
- **"Cage and ring cut the same."** The mesh is a published,
  solved-with-hardware problem. They do not.

## §2 — WHAT A MANAGEMENT GAME'S FIGHT SCREEN DOES INSTEAD

**Football Manager.** The 3D pitch is optional dressing on a match engine.
**Highlight saturation is the actual camera language** — Full / Comprehensive /
Extended / Key / Commentary-only, with FM26 adding Dynamic (more highlights when
the game is close). Between highlights the player does not watch football; they
look at staff advice and the tactics panel. Camera options are *vantages*
(broadcast, sideline, manager, 2D dots), not a directed cut. SI's own manual:
**make sure the view you pick lets you manage.** That sentence is the whole
genre.

**Madden Play the Moment / Super Sim.** Fast Super Sim is GameCast text; Slow
Super Sim is a 3D broadcast you spectate. The player looks at the play-call and
the score.

**Boxing / MMA management titles.** World Championship Boxing Manager 2 sells a
"cinematic Fight Camera" on 32-bit hand-drawn fighters — one theatrical angle,
not a truck. Boxing Manager (mobile) added "Ring mode and classic": two
presentations of one sim, plus commentary and punch stats. MMA Manager 2 is
gym-and-roster first; the fight is a result you witness, not a production you
direct.

**Where the player looks:** not at the glove — at the thing they can still act
on. Corner instruction, the heart rate, the pips, the round scores, the stoppage
reason, the clock. **The 3D confirms the sentence the HUD already wrote.**

**What they refuse to show:** the dead minutes, the incomplete passes, the
punches that did not matter. FM's Key mode is "shots on target and the build" —
that is G42's exchange-then-replay, in another sport.

## §3 — THE CUT THAT WOULD LIE

**A shot lies when it asserts a fact the engine did not write. A shot is honest
when it is visibly a summary, or a picture of something we do have.**

### Usable without lying

- **Head-to-toe wide while a flurry happens and we are not drawing the punches.**
  Distance and stance are still true; the picture says "they are exchanging,"
  which the tick stream also says. This is the home.
- **Venue / beauty / crowd cut** during an un-animatable stretch. Asserts
  atmosphere, not a punch.
- **Corner, between rounds, on the stool.** The engine stopped the round; sitting
  them down is not a claim about a hook.
- **Replay LABELLED as a replay of an event the engine named** — knockdown,
  stoppage, the punch the stoppage reason points at. **This is where the 2.47 s
  combo clip lives. The label is the honesty.**
- **Decision-read wide** plus the card graphic we already compute.
- **HUD as a picture** — heart rate, pips, region, score. This is the engine
  talking; it cannot lie unless the data is wrong.
- **Tighten because exchange density went up**, still on a two-shot that does not
  pick a winner of the exchange. "They are in the pocket" is a distance fact and
  we have distance.

### Lies

- **Tight trunks-up of a clean hook when the engine recorded a glance, a miss, or
  a different region.** Camera 2 is the lie-shot if we let it name the punch.
- A contact close-up that picks a target the six-region model did not score.
- A replay of "the" knockdown punch that is **not** the punch the stoppage reason
  named.
- **A face insert of a cut we do not have a pip for.** Tugman is right that you
  cannot show a cut from wide — and we cannot show a cut we did not record.
- Holding a committed combo clip live over five engine impacts (G42, in camera
  terms: the lens is now telling a different fight).
- A slomo that invents weight or direction the tick did not have.
- **Cutting to the hurt man's face as if stunned when the engine has not applied
  a stun.** Fight Night's stun cam works because the sim already said stun.

### The line, one sentence

**Wide, corner, labelled replay of a named event, and the HUD are summaries.
Tight contact is a claim. We may only make the claims the engine already made.**

## §4 — WHAT WE COULD NOT EXPRESS TODAY, NAMED

We have six body regions, heart rate, discrete trauma pips, a ground seat-chip,
per-round scores, a stoppage reason. A real broadcast makes pictures we have no
noun for.

1. **Cut versus punch.** Commission replay exists because the live picture cannot
   tell a clash of heads from a jab. **We have pips. We do not have a cause
   flag.**
2. **The referee as a subject.** Chenery's entire jib argument is "the ref got in
   the way, so the camera walks"; Tugman's stoppage picture is the referee's
   body. We have a stoppage reason. **We do not have a referee state, a
   count-in-progress, or a position in the ring.** Any ref-in-frame shot is
   fiction until that noun exists.
3. **Which punch of a flurry was the one.** Broadcast slomo picks a frame. Our
   engine can emit seven impacts while one clip plays. We can name a stoppage
   punch *after the fact*; we cannot, mid-flurry, hand a director "this one."
4. **Commentator-driven coverage.** Tugman cuts on "stance," "reach," "distance"
   — words a colour man says. **We have no commentary object.** Distance we have;
   the prompt to use it as a cue is a person we did not hire.

Also real, also unsupported, said once: sweat and facial damage beyond pips;
crowd heat; judge-by-judge running cards; corner audio; who is walking whom.
**A television director is listening to things we do not simulate.**

**Ground, one line:** a grounded sequence is what half of MMA coverage points at,
and with one grappling clip in 97 there is still nothing to point at. Stop.

## §5 — WHAT YOU WOULD NOT BUILD

### Worth having — the grammar, not the truck

1. **A home wide, head-to-toe.** Distance remains true while punches are not
   drawn. The shot FM already decided is how you manage a match.
2. **Permission to stay there.** Chenery's lesson: first-time directors chop.
   **We will look like a first-time director if we cut every time the engine
   emits an impact we are not drawing.**
3. **A tighten allowed only on an engine fact we already have** — they are in
   range, exchange density is high, a named event. Not on "a hook landed clean."
4. **The between-round minute as the replay slot.** The book gives us the minute;
   that is where the purchased combo clip and the knockdown package go, labelled.
5. **The HUD as the second camera.** Heart rate, pips, region, card — where the
   player actually looks, per every management title in §2.
6. **Stoppage and decision as ceremony on the wide**, with the reason and the
   card we already compute.

### Television we should never attempt

- A multi-cam director: no Camera 1/2/3/4 assignment, no apron-to-apron rules, no
  jib that walks around a referee we do not have.
- FenceCam / bumper cam / 4DReplay / EVS operator — those solve occlusion and
  slomo packaging for a **live audience**. We are not one.
- Commentator-following cuts. We have no colour man.
- Cinematic shallow-depth coverage; promo units shoot off the program for a
  reason.
- Walkout production, phone-bowl beauty, Spidercam. Event television.
- **A tight-contact library large enough to narrate every region-accurate hook.**
  That is the per-strike animation budget G42 already refused, with a lens on it.
- Any camera that needs the referee as geometry.

## The call, in one sentence

**Leave the camera on a wide that does not pick a punch; put the truth on the
HUD; spend the only cut we need on a labelled replay of an event the engine
already named.**
