## WHY THIS ONE, AND WHAT YOUR OWN J43 ALREADY SETTLED

Your **J43** landed and it refused our chest shape outright: the **bony thoracic
inlet is 45–65 x 90–110 mm**, so our thorax capsule's radius is **4.9–5.7x the
half-inlet AP**. You also found that games use a capsule or a box torso and that
any superior dome is *capsule-primitive geometry, not an anatomical lid*, and
that Hybrid III / THOR / WorldSID use rib cages rather than capsules. **None of
that is being re-asked.**

What we are about to do is replace the chest capsule's **hemispherical top cap**
with a **flat disk** at the Neck bone — the cap becomes a plane perpendicular to
the chest axis instead of a dome bulging a full radius upward. The geometer who
proposed it immediately attacked her own answer, and this is her objection
verbatim:

> *"Flat caps fail the shoulder girdle. The dome was not just obstruction — it
> was the anatomical transition between chest and clavicle. Flattening it
> exposes the neck but creates a sharp rim where the collarbone should smoothly
> follow."*

**Her objection is right or wrong depending on ONE anatomical fact: how much of
a man sits ABOVE the plane through his suprasternal notch.** If the acromion and
the trapezius ridge sit well above that plane, a flat disk there cuts real flesh
off the model and she is right. If they sit at or below it, the dome was
covering space a human does not occupy and the flat cap is free.

## THE QUESTION — three measurements, all from ANSUR II, all vertical offsets

Give each as an **unrounded mean with N and SD**, male and female separately,
exactly as you always do. These are differences between standing heights in the
same subject, so they subtract cleanly:

1. **Acromion above suprasternale.** `acromialheight - suprasternaleheight`.
   How far the shoulder point sits above the notch. **This is the headline
   number** — it is the height of the flesh a flat disk at the notch would
   refuse to cover.
2. **Cervicale (C7) above suprasternale.** `cervicaleheight - suprasternaleheight`.
   The back of the neck base against the front. It tells us whether the "top of
   the chest" is even a horizontal plane, or whether it is tilted — and if it is
   tilted, by how much.
3. **The trapezius ridge above suprasternale**, if any dataset gives it. This is
   the soft-tissue crest between neck and shoulder — the part a collar tie
   actually presses against. **If it is NOT FOUND, say NOT FOUND.** Your Q4 in
   J43 came back NOT FOUND on trapezius–jaw clearance and that was a result;
   the same applies here.

For (1) and (2), also give the ratio to **stature**, so the figures scale to
rigs at 1.78 m and 1.88 m rather than being fixed millimetres.

## THE THING THAT WOULD MAKE THIS DECISIVE

Then answer this in one short paragraph:

**At the level of the suprasternal notch, what is the transverse (side-to-side)
breadth of the body — and is that breadth the CHEST, or is it the neck plus the
two shoulder masses?**

Our model puts one cylinder of radius ~0.14–0.19 m there. If the real breadth at
that level is chest, the cylinder is roughly honest and only its top cap is
wrong. **If the real breadth at that level is mostly neck with the shoulders
hanging off to each side, then the cylinder is wrong at the top regardless of
what shape we put on it**, and flattening the cap is treating a symptom.

That is the sentence we need. Say which it is.
