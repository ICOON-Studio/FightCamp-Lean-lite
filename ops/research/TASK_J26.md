## THE SITUATION

We are building a fighting-simulation game with a judo ruleset. A bout is simulated
as a sequence of exchanges of roughly **14 seconds** each. When a throw lands, the
two athletes go to the mat and the simulation continues there.

We just gave our judo ground techniques (pins, chokes, arm locks) the positions they
are applied from. That made judo LESS like judo: across 40 simulated bouts, wins by
ippon fell from 31 to 11, decisions rose from 9 to 29, and athletes stayed on the
mat for 77% of all exchanges. The reason is that our simulation has **no matte** —
nothing stands the athletes up when ground work stalls — and a pin can never
become an ippon.

We already have, and do not need again: the current IJF osaekomi thresholds
(10–19 s waza-ari, 20 s ippon) and that osaekomi is a judo rule only.

## THE QUESTIONS

1. **Matte in ne-waza.** Under the current IJF Sport and Organisation Rules (and
   the refereeing guidance that goes with them), when does the referee call matte
   during ground work? Quote the rule wording. Is there a stated or customary time
   ("no progress for N seconds"), or is it purely the referee's judgement? If
   judgement, what do referees actually do: give any published figure for how long
   unproductive ne-waza is allowed to continue.
2. **How long ne-waza actually lasts.** From time-motion or notational analyses of
   senior IJF competition (Olympic Games, World Championships, Grand Slams): the
   average and typical range of a ne-waza sequence in seconds, and the share of
   total contest time spent in ne-waza versus standing (tachi-waza) and stoppages.
3. **How senior judo contests end.** The share of contests decided by ippon (any
   form), by waza-ari or accumulated score, in golden score, and by hansoku-make or
   penalties. Then, of the ippons: what share come from throws versus ne-waza, and
   within ne-waza, osaekomi (pin) versus shime-waza (choke) versus kansetsu-waza
   (arm lock).
4. **What changed with the rules.** The 2022–2024 and 2025–2028 IJF rule cycles
   changed scoring (including the return of yuko). Say which cycle each figure you
   give comes from, because a pre-2017 ippon rate is not today's.

## HOW TO ANSWER

For every number: the figure, the population it describes (event, years, weight
categories, number of contests), the source by name, and a grade — **DIRECT** (the
source measured or states it) or **INHERITED** (the source cites someone else).
**Where you cannot find a figure, say NOT FOUND.** Do not estimate one and do not
fill a gap with a general impression — a missing number is more useful to us than
an invented one.

Keep it to what the four questions ask. A short table per question is ideal.
