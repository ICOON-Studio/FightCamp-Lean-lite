# John - real-world numbers for a boxing weight mechanic (S535, WEIGHT2)

We make a boxing gym management game (amateur club to first pro fights). The owner wants weight to be a deep mechanic: the
player manages a boxer's weight up to a point, and weight affects performance and size match-ups. We need COUNTED numbers
(a named study or federation document, sample size, year) - say plainly where none exists and give a range with its basis.

1. Performance lost per % of body mass cut (rapid weight loss) in combat sports: power, repeated-effort output, aerobic capacity,
   reaction time. Amateur (weigh-in ~1-3 h before, no rehydration time) vs pro (weigh-in ~30 h before).
2. How much a pro regains between weigh-in and the fight (kg and % body mass); how much an amateur can regain in 1-3 hours.
3. Dehydration and head injury: any evidence that a dehydrated or weight-cut boxer is more likely to be stopped / concussed.
4. Typical safe cut: % of body mass a boxer can lose in the final week, and over a 6-8 week camp; what federations allow
   (World Boxing / England Boxing / IBF day-of-fight weight check, the WBC 10% rule).
5. Weight gain of male and female athletes aged 16-23 per year (growth into a heavier class), and how cuts get harder with age.
6. Moving up a class: does punch power / chin "carry over"? Records of pros who moved up (win rates, KO rates before/after) -
   any counted study, else a careful sample you can count yourself, with names and numbers.
7. Size gap inside a bout: height / reach / weight advantage effect on win rate in boxing (counted datasets, e.g. BoxRec studies).
8. How often amateurs and pros miss weight, and what happens (federation rules: walkover, fine, bout off).
9. Natural "walking weight" over a class limit for amateurs vs pros (typical kg above the limit between camps).
10. What nutrition support costs a small club or pro boxer (a sports nutritionist per week / per camp), UK or US.

Answer each with: the number, the source (title, author/body, year, sample), and how sure you are.
