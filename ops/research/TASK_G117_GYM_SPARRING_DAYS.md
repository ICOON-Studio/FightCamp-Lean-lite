## Why
FightCamp (a Godot combat-sports gym management sim) will let the player book two gym-mates into the ring for a light
spar, played by the game's own fight engine at training intensity in the 3D gym. The winner gains a small visible edge,
the loser risks a bruise, and a repeated pairing can become a gym rivalry. We need how real gyms do this so the numbers
and the rules are a real gym's, not a guess.

## What would a human do?
Answer first: how does a head coach of a small boxing / kickboxing / MMA gym decide who spars whom, how hard, how many
rounds, how often per week, and when he stops it? What does "light", "technical", "hard" and "fight-camp" sparring
mean in practice, and what do fighters actually gain from it that bag and pad work does not give?

## Questions
1. Intensity levels: the names coaches use (flow, technical, light, medium, hard / "gym wars") and what each means in
   power, pace and protective gear. Sourced where possible.
2. Volume: rounds per session and sessions per week for an amateur, a pro between camps and a pro in camp (weeks out
   from a fight, including when hard sparring stops before a bout).
3. Matching: how coaches pair partners - weight gap, skill gap, experience, the "bigger/better partner" rule, female/male
   pairing norms in real gyms; what a coach will not allow.
4. Outcomes: injury per sparring round or per hour (amateur boxing, kickboxing, MMA training studies), cuts, hand
   injuries, concussion signs; how often a spar ends early.
5. What sparring builds that other work does not (timing, distance, composure, reading), and any study measuring it.
6. Gym rivalries: how common, when they help, when a coach separates two fighters.
7. Recommend, as JUDGEMENT, a first-release rule set for the game: booking limits per week, intensity choices, round
   counts, a per-round bruise/cut chance, and a rivalry trigger (how many pairings, what result pattern).
