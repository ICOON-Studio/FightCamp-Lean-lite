G75 — THE MAN UNDERNEATH

How often does he get out, and to where? · Research brief for a fight-sim ground graph. No code.

Question: from each bottom seat (closed guard, half guard, side control, mount, back, turtle), when the man underneath changes the position, what share goes to recover-guard / sweep-to-top / stand-up / concede-worse / get-submitted — and how long did the seat last.

Headline result. The destination-share tables this task asked for do not exist in published match analysis. Time-motion papers measure phase time per bout, not conditional destination given a bottom-initiated change. FightMetric recorded Time-in-Position to the fraction of a second but never published per-position escape destinations. What follows is every measured number that touches the question, tagged, plus the empty cells.

How to read this document

MEASURED = a number with source, N, and population. JUDGEMENT = a design-facing inference from those numbers, or from coaching consensus, not a count. BJJ Graph site probabilities (e.g. “turtle-to-guard 44%”) are instructional estimates with no published N — they are not used as measurements.

Time-motion “guard / side / mount” seconds in BJJ papers are almost always cumulative seconds of that phase inside one match, not median dwell of a single occupancy. Using them as per-visit duration inflates how long a pin lasts. Flagged wherever it matters.

Female and male samples are never mixed in the tables below. Where a cited paper pooled sexes, that is stated on the line.

What was searched

Targeted: IBJJF / ADCC positional-transition film studies; FightMetric / UFC Stats ground TIP; sports-science time-motion (Andreato, Del Vecchio, Miarka, Spanias, Brito/Coswig female BJJ series); BJJ Heroes instance logs (ADCC 2015–2024); UFC submission-position papers 2014–2024. Instructional sites and unsourced “success rate” pages were read and discarded as measurements.

2. How long a position lasts

This is the only part of the brief that has published numbers close to the question. Even here, most studies report bout-level phase time, not per-occupancy duration.

Read-out for the engine: a pin that decides the match (side, mount) lives on the order of tens of seconds, not minutes, in elite female gi. Guard eats most of the clock because it is entered more often and is legally “home.” This is not “median time until the bottom man escapes this visit.”

2.4 MMA — fight-level ground time, not seat-level

MEASURED. Bloody Elbow / FightMetric analysis (Reed Kuhn, 2014). Fighters spend 38.1% of fight time (1:54 of every 5:00) on the ground with someone in control. Every 5.6 rounds, one fighter has ground control for ≥ half the round. Population: UFC FightMetric corpus of that period. Sex not split in the article.

MEASURED (qualitative TIP, same corpus family). FightMetric Time-in-Position write-up circulated as “Here’s How UFC Fights Are Changing” / associated TIP chapter (~2010s UFC): of average fight time spent on the ground, most is full guard or “hovering”; full guard ≈ one-quarter of all mat time; only about one-third of ground time is a position more advanced than full guard; half guard / side / mount are “increasingly hard to achieve, and fighters hold these positions for less time”; mount is “the most difficult position to hold.” Exact per-seat medians were not in the public extracts.

MEASURED. Same TIP chapter: “the defense rate for each position is actually very similar; about one third of all position advances will eventually be reversed or escaped from by the bottom fighter.” N not restated in the public extract; this is the closest published escape-rate figure in MMA. It is per advance, not per bottom-initiated change, and not split by destination.

MEASURED. Amateur MMA (IMMAF / WMMAA family), n=279 bouts / 615 rounds: mean standing 194.2 s vs mean ground 168.3 s per bout. Sex not split in the published totals.

MEASURED. Miarka et al., JSCR 2016, 645 UFC rounds paired by outcome (2014 events). Winners had more advances to half guard, side, mount, and back than losers in every round. Back control and side control “accounted for the finishes in 6.9% and 1.2% of all bouts.” Those last two figures are finish-from-position shares of all bouts, not of ground phases. Sex not split.

NOT FOUND: median or IQR seconds per occupancy of half guard, side control, mount, back, or turtle in UFC/MMA.

Ruleset warning: ADCC turtle rates are inflated by the scoring rule (flat back for 3 s). Copying 69% “turtle → back” into an MMA career sim is a design error — MMA has strikes to the head of a turtled fighter and no pass-points to dodge. Label any such copy JUDGEMENT, and expect MMA turtle to resolve faster and more often toward back, GnP stoppage, or stand-up against the cage.

JUDGEMENT, stated as such: holding all skill equal, the bottom man leaves the pin faster in MMA than in gi, because strikes and the referee make holding a losing guard expensive. 4. Stand-up from the bottom in MMA

NOT FOUND: a published rate of “downed fighter returns to his feet” per ground engagement, or a median time-to-stand, in UFC or any other MMA promotion.

What is measured instead:

Fight-level mix has shifted standing. Ground share of UFC fight time fell from a majority in the early 2000s to under one-third, then toward ~¼ in later FightMetric public write-ups. That is not an escape rate. It is fewer takedowns sticking and more time never leaving the feet.
Control-time / top-position-time exists per fighter (UFC Stats). High control-time per takedown (public handicapping heuristic: 120+ s) means the bottom man is not getting up. The inverse — stand-up rate per takedown — is not on the public leaderboard.
Referee stand-ups are a second path to the feet that the bottom man did not earn. Criteria (Herb Dean public comments, 2025): progress = posture to strike, advance position, or chain toward a finish. Static closed guard from bottom plus pitter-patter from top gets stood up. No public count of stand-ups per event or per position.
Coaching / historical: Liddell-era technical stand-up from guard is real and now standard. It is not a rate.

JUDGEMENT for the MMA graph only: treat stand-up as the default successful bottom action from closed guard and half guard against a non-grappler top; treat it as rare from side, mount, and back without an intermediate (half / turtle / cage-walk). Time-to-stand from a settled closed guard, when it happens, is typically one burst (a few seconds of frames + post) plus a scramble — not a 40-second project. That last sentence is coaching, not a study.

Argue against yourself: why elite footage is the wrong anchor

The strongest case that IBJJF / ADCC / UFC film is the wrong prior for a career sim of mostly-not-elite fighters:

Skill floor, not skill ceiling, sets escape rates. Elite bottom players retain guard and peel hooks because that is their job. A regional fighter under mount does not have the elbow-escape timing of a Pan-Am medallist. Using winner-side female mount totals (40 s) or ADCC wrestle-up shares as the sim’s mean will make average AI bottoms too slippery. The loser-side cells (mount 16 s bout-total, defense block 39 s) are closer to “what happens to the worse grappler,” and even those are elite losers.
Rulesets manufacture destinations. 69% of ADCC backs-from-turtle is a scoring artefact. 31% of one IBJJF Worlds reversal pool being 50-50 is a scoring artefact. UFC referee stand-ups are a broadcast artefact. A career sim that is trying to be “a fight,” not “a ruleset,” cannot swallow those shares raw.
Selection. Published analysis is Worlds, ADCC, UFC. Those athletes were filtered for years on not giving up the back and not stalling in a pin. The sim’s bronze-level and prospect pool was not. Escape success should scale with the existing skill / grappling-IQ / fatigue attributes, with elite footage as the cap, not the mean.
Sex and size. The best duration numbers in this brief are female gi. Female MMA already submits at a higher bout rate than male MMA (21.1% vs 17.3%, 2014–17). Do not average them. Do not use female gi side-control clocks as male MMA side-control clocks.
Strikes delete time. A gi side-control that can last a median 30–50 s bout-total will not last that long when the top man is punching. Either the bottom explodes out sooner or the ref or the doctor ends it. Elite no-strike footage overstates pin duration for MMA.
What to do instead of pretending a table exists. Anchor the graph on the few measured constraints — MMA ~⅓ of advances later reversed (pooled), more-dominant seats held less long, guard eats most ground time, modern finishes concentrate on the back, stand-up is an MMA-native exit that studies never counted — then let fighter skill draw the destination. Mark every default share in the engine as JUDGEMENT.

Bottom line for wiring week: you cannot source destination probabilities. You can source duration envelopes (tens of seconds in pins, guard eating the clock), a pooled MMA reverse-rate of about one advance in three, a modern finish-concentration on the back, and a hard ruleset warning against copying ADCC turtle or IBJJF 50-50 shares into MMA. Everything else in the bottom-man graph is a design call. Label it that way in the engine.
