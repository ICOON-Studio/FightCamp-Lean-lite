# CARDREAL1 - how position really changes between two fighters, move by move (research, S543)

You are the researcher. We are making a turn-based card game of a fight between two different martial arts (first: a
boxer against a Brazilian jiu-jitsu fighter; later kickboxing, karate, wrestling, sambo). Our prototype got the fight
wrong: it treats "where the fight is" as one shared place, so when the man on top stands up out of the guard, the man
on his back is instantly on his feet and gets hit with a jab and a cross in the same breath. The owner, who has watched
fights for 40 years, saw it at once. We need the real sequences, with sources, so every card can follow one.

## What to deliver (plain words, tables welcome; name every source, link clips where they exist)
1. BOXER AGAINST JIU-JITSU FIGHTER, standing to ground and back. For each of these, say who is where before and after
   (each fighter separately: standing, seated, on the back, on the knees, on top inside the guard, past the guard), about
   how long it takes, what the OTHER fighter can do while it happens, and how it usually ends:
   - the jiu-jitsu fighter pulls guard or sits to guard against a standing striker;
   - the striker refuses to follow the grappler down (stays standing over a seated or supine opponent);
   - the striker is inside the closed guard and wants out: posturing, standing up in the guard, backing away;
   - the fighter on the bottom gets back to the feet (the technical stand-up, wall-walking) and when that gets punished;
   - sweeps from the guard against an opponent who is standing up out of it;
   - strikes from the standing fighter to the grounded one, and from the grounded one upward (up-kicks), under each rule set.
2. THE RULE SETS that decide it, side by side: unified MMA rules (kicks and knees to the head of a grounded opponent,
   the referee standing a fighter up), Pride / ONE rules, combat jiu-jitsu (open-palm strikes on the ground), pure sport
   jiu-jitsu (guard pull scoring, stalling), boxing (nothing below the waist, no ground). For our game we need ONE honest
   mixed rule set: propose it and say what each choice changes.
3. THE SAME for the clinch: who can strike, who can take down, how a boxer keeps a grappler off (frames, underhooks,
   breaking and circling out), how long a referee lets a clinch run.
4. Numbers where they exist: share of guard pulls that lead to a sweep or submission against a striker; how often
   takedowns are stuffed; how long ground exchanges last before a stand-up; typical damage from ground strikes against
   standing strikes. Say plainly where no good numbers exist.
5. A LIST OF THE THINGS A GAME GETS WRONG most often about style-against-style fights, from your reading.

Keep it to what happens in real bouts and under real rules. Do not design the game.
