# Claude on Gemma v Claude, the final (DRAW 28-28)

(1) HOW IT WENT: I was the Pressure Boxer. Round 1 mine 10-8: a Lead Hook into its opening Slip & Cross rocked it, a
Head Movement Feint baited its Check Hook, Philly Shell took its Lead Hook, a powered Liver Shot went under its guard.
Round 2 to Gemma on damage but I kept the bout lead 19-18 (the Uppercut that rocked it at Close, the Liver Shot under its
Circle Out). Round 3 was lost to the RULES, not to Gemma: at Long my whole hand was Mid / Close cards; holding 5 I drew
nothing new, so I passed four exchanges in a row and its last-second Step-In Jab took the round 10-9 - a draw.

(2) WHAT I COULD READ: Gemma counters a lot (Slip, Check Hook, Pull Counter) and jabs at Long; its hand when I read it.
Could not read: when it would circle out instead of hooking.

(3) FUN: rounds 1 and 2 were the best card fight I have played here - every exchange a real read with a boxing name.
Round 3 was not a game: a dead hand with no way out.

(4) TOO STRONG: the Lead's +2 speed - with it the Out-Fighter's Liver Shot (7) beat my Uppercut (5) at MY range, Close.
NEVER WANTED: Cut the Cage as the only card I could play at Long, into a man holding Check Hooks.

(5) FUN 6, DEPTH 7, CLARITY 6, VARIETY 6

(6) TO REACH 10/10:
1. Never a dead hand: every fighter always has two free basic moves - STEP IN (one range closer, 1 stamina, no
   damage, Attack type) and COVER UP (head guard, 0 stamina) - and may discard one card a turn to draw one.
2. Stamina: +1 back every exchange (the PDFs give none between exchanges; round 3 ran dry in two of three bouts).
3. The Lead +1 speed, not +2 - winning one exchange should not win every speed race after it.
4. Each style wins speed in its own range: the Pressure Boxer +1 at Close (the Out-Fighter already has +1 at Long).
5. Pull Counter should work at Close too (a pressure fighter's counter where pressure fighters live).
