# Maria on John v Maria (John wins by KO in round 3)

**(1) Why I lost.** John knocked me out in round 3. Two beats decided it. In R3 E4 his straight cross at speed 9 beat my body hook at 6 for 5 to the head, which left me on 4 head health. In R3 E6 I threw a straight cross and a hook to steal the round, and his head slip read it and landed 6 for the KO. I'd seen two head slips in his hand at the start of round 2 and forgot them when I needed a finish. In round 1 my body shots were too slow: twice (E1 and E3) a faster punch of his landed first and cancelled mine.

**(2) What I could read and what I couldn't.** John was easy to read when he held the Lead: he threw quick head jabs, and in R2 E4 my slip read one for 5. When he was hurt he guarded or stepped out. At Long he pawed until standing still parried it, then he switched to the step-in jab in R3 E3, which I didn't see coming. I also couldn't tell his feints from his counters. My feint beat his counter in R1 E5, but his jabs caught both of my feints in round 2 (E1 and E3).

**(3) Another agent versus a script.** It was more fun, and it hurt more. John changed plans after my parry at Long and held his head slip back for exactly the moment I had to throw. A script wouldn't have saved its counter for my desperation punch.

**(4) Too strong, and a switch I never wanted.** Too strong: speed. A Counter-Puncher with the Lead throws at 9 to 11, and my Pressure fighter's best punches are 6 or 7, so in every trade his card landed first and cancelled mine. The body-puncher identity barely got to punch. The switch I never wanted was the Overhand Right. At speed 5 and 4 stamina it was dead in every hand, and I discarded it three times.

**(6) TO REACH 10/10**
1. **Give the slow, heavy fighter a way through speed.** In R1 E1, E3 and R3 E4 a faster card cancelled my body hook outright. Body hooks should trade with straight punches the way jabs trade with power punches, both landing and the faster one taking 2 off, so a Pressure fighter can walk through fire.
2. **Remind me what a read showed.** In R3 E6 I'd read his two head slips 17 exchanges earlier and threw a head combo into one anyway. Cards seen in a read should stay marked on screen until they're played or discarded.
3. **Make the last exchange more than a coin flip.** In R3 E6, being behind meant throwing everything, and he knew it. A trailing fighter should get one honest option that isn't a head-shot gamble, such as a body finisher that scores double in the final exchange, so the comeback has a read and not just a guess.
