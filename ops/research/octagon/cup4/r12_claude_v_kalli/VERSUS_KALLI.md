# Kalli on Claude v Kalli (Kalli wins by DECISION 28-27)

**1. Why I won.** I won 28-27 after losing round 1 by two points. Two beats decided it.
- R2 E6: my straight at 7 beat his straight at 6 and landed 4. That won round 2 and left him on 5 head going into round 3.
- R3 E1 and E2: two straight speed wins. The step-in jab landed 4 (he was down to 1 head), then the body hook landed 5. That built a 9-point round, which scores 10-8 and takes the fight.

The finish itself was defence. With a 9-0 lead I blocked three of the last four exchanges.

**2. What I could read.** Claude attacked whenever he held the Lead: R1 E5, R2 E5 and R2 E6. Late in the bout he turned to recovery cards (Second Wind, then elbows) once his stamina dropped. What I couldn't read was his opening straight. In R1 E1 I expected the counter opener and feinted into a punch for 4. His Read Tendencies in R3 E4 also baited my check hook clean.

**3. Agent or script.** The agent was more fun. Claude dropped the counter opener I'd beaten before and opened with a straight. That was a real adjustment to me, and it cost me round 1.

**4. Too strong, and never wanted.**
- Too strong: speed. My Out-Fighter won every attack-against-attack exchange on speed (R2 E6, R3 E1, R3 E2). Against a slower fighter, matching his punch is a free win.
- Never wanted: Elbows Down. It looks like a guard, but in R1 E5 his uppercut went straight over it for 5 and rocked me. It blocks only body shots, and most attacks go to the head.

**5. Scores**
- FUN: 8. Coming back from a 10-8 round was a real story.
- DEPTH: 7. Choosing when to bank a lead was a real decision.
- CLARITY: 7. Scores now carry names and read cleanly. The half-point speed result ("9.5 v 9") was a surprise.
- VARIETY: 6. Five of the eighteen exchanges were block against block or block against feint, with nothing landing.

**6. TO REACH 10/10**
1. **Show the speed tie rule and fractions up front.** R3 E1 was decided 9.5 against 9 by a bonus I couldn't see in my hand. Whatever makes the half point should be printed on the card, the same way NOW speeds are.
2. **Make Elbows Down warn which zone it leaves open,** or let it halve head shots. In R1 E5 it cost me 5 and a rocked turn, and the cards I chose from looked like a defence that worked.
3. **Give block against block an outcome.** R3 E3 and R3 E5 were both block against block with nothing happening, and that let me run out the clock with a lead. Something should change in those exchanges, such as a step toward the fence for whoever is backing up, so a lead has to be defended rather than waited out.
