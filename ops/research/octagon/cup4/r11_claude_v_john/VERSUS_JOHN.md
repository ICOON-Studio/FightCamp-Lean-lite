# John on Claude v John (John wins by KO in round 3)

**(1) Why I won.** I won by KO in R3 with two Pull Counter Rights at Mid. The first deciding beat was R2 E2: his Stiff Flicker Jab walked into my Pull Counter for 6, which turned a 9-2 round-one deficit into a round I won. The second was R3 E4: after he landed a cross through my whiffed Lead Check Hook (R3 E3), he threw the same cross again, and the Pull Counter took him to 2 head. From there a speed-10 Step-In Jab in R3 E6 beat his Overhand Right to the punch and ended it. That last beat was a trade, though: his Overhand still landed 3 through my jab.

**(2) What I could read.** I could read his Pressure rhythm. He always mixes in a feint after I defend: Cut Off the Cage in R1 E4 and R2 E3, and Shoulder Feint in R2 E4. And when he's ahead, he repeats an attack that just worked, as with his two crosses in R3. Read Tendencies (R1 E6) showed me his jab and cross, which set up R2. What I couldn't read was when he would hold or breathe instead of hitting. His Hold Ground in R2 E1 and Second Wind in R3 E2 both came straight after a feint, and nothing on the table hinted at them.

**(3) Another agent or a script?** Playing Claude was more fun. He punished my habits: three times my defend at Mid got broken by a feint, and that is a learning opponent, not a fixed table. A script wouldn't have repeated the cross on purpose, or baited me into a guard and then gone to the body.

**(4) Too strong, and never wanted.** The Pull Counter Right at Mid is too strong. It landed 7, 6 and 6, decided all three rounds, and gives stamina back, while its only real cost is a feint, which a man with the Lead rarely throws. The switch I never wanted was Elbows Down: I held it for two rounds and never played it, because head attacks go over it and the feint breaks it anyway.

**(6) TO REACH 10/10**
1. **Make the Pull Counter Right cost more to read.** After a landed power counter, the counterer should lose 1 speed the next exchange, or the counter should show the opponent a tell (R2 E2, R3 E4, and R1 E3 for him). Right now one guess swings a whole round by 6 to 7 points.
2. **Make a feint into a defend pay less when it's repeated.** A second feint in a row against the same man should give no push to the fence, so the cornered man can escape (R2 E3 and R2 E4 pinned me at cage 0 twice). As it stands, one read becomes a lock on the fence.
3. **Make the end-of-fight trade on the jab clear.** Show on the card that a step-in heavy punch "goes through a jab for half" (R3 E6: I was surprised his Overhand still landed 3). Say it in the card text, and let the judges show the trade, so a finishing jab is a choice with known risk.
