# Kalli on John v Kalli (John wins by DECISION 28-27)

**1. Why I lost (28-27 on points).** Two beats decided it. In R1 E2 I threw a second jab with the Lead, he slipped it and hit me for 7, and I was rocked. In R1 E3 I guessed he'd attack and countered, but he faked: my pull counter whiffed and cost me 5 stamina. That gave away round 1 by 10-8, and the 2-point gap from that round is exactly what I lost by. I won round 3 by 12 points to 0, and it still wasn't enough.

**2. What I could read, and what I couldn't.** I could read him once he was out of answers. In round 3 he stood still five times in a row, and my range jab hit every time. I also read his double guard in round 2, and my body hook traded through his jab in R2 E6. What I couldn't read was his round 1 sequence: slip, fake, hook, fake. A counter-puncher who fakes as often as he counters made my counter guess a coin flip, and I lost both flips.

**3. Another agent or a script?** More fun for two rounds. John played a real counter-puncher's game: slipping, faking and going to the body. Round 3 was the opposite. He stood still for five exchanges and took 12 damage, so the last round turned into a formality instead of a comeback fight. A script would at least have thrown something.

**4. Too strong, and never wanted.** Too strong: the 10-8 round from an early burst. One slip counter plus one hook in round 1 earned him a 2-point round that my 12-0 round 3 couldn't overturn, because a round caps at 10-8 no matter how big the gap is. Never wanted: Hold Ground. He played it five times and lost 10 head health. I discarded it in eight of my turns.

**5. Scores**
- FUN 6: rounds 1 and 2 were tense, but round 3 was a jab drill against a man standing still.
- DEPTH 7: the slip, the fake and the body hook all decided real exchanges.
- CLARITY 8: showing real card speeds and who wins ties made my choices readable.
- VARIETY 5: I played the range jab six times in a row because nothing at long range could beat it.

**6. To reach 10/10**
1. **Make a big round worth more than 10-8 (R3, 12 to 0, still lost).** Let a gap of 12 or more score 10-7, or let a fighter who doesn't throw for three exchanges lose a point. A round won 12 to 0 should beat a round won 14 to 5.
2. **Give long range a real defence (R3 E2 to E6).** Neither fighter has a block or counter that works at long range, so the jab can't be answered there. Add a long-range parry, or let Hold Ground stop a jab of 1 or 2 damage.
3. **Make guessing wrong on a counter cost less (R1 E3).** A whiffed counter plus the fake's extra drain cost me 5 stamina in one exchange, and with a counter-puncher who fakes a lot that's a coin flip. Cap the loss at 3 stamina, or show a tell when the opponent is faking.
