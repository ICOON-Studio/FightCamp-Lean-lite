# John on Claude v John (Claude wins by KO in round 2)

**1. Why I lost.** Two beats decided it. In R1 E5, I'd caught him faking twice with jabs, so I threw the harder cross. He'd switched to Slip & Cross. It made me miss and landed 7, I was rocked on the fence, and he took round 1. In R2 E1, at Long with only Read or Hold Ground playable, I played Read Tendencies. His Overhand Right stepped in and landed 8, which rocked me again at 4 head. The second overhand broke through Cover Up for 4, and that was the KO. Round 2's +5 stamina didn't help, because the 3 head health back between rounds couldn't cover 15 damage in three punches.

**2. What I could read.** Claude led with fakes, three of his first four cards. My cheap jab punished two of them for 6 total. What I couldn't read: the switch to a straight-punch counter right after two jabs, and the overhand straight from Long at the start of round 2. My Read card never showed me anything, because it lost to the very attack it was meant to scout.

**3. Agent against script.** Yes, it was more fun. Claude changed his plan after my second jab, which a script wouldn't do, and that's the reading game this format promises. But the bout ended four exchanges after his read paid off, so there was no time to adjust back.

**4. Too strong, never wanted.** Too strong: the Overhand Right as a step-in from Long. It does 8, it pushes me back a range, and it can be thrown again next exchange from the same spot. My rocked hand had nothing to answer it, because every counter I held was Mid-only. Never wanted: Hold Ground again. At Long, my whole hand was dead except Read and Hold Ground, so a fake that loses to any attack was my only real play.

**6. TO REACH 10/10**
1. **Stop a step-in punch from landing twice from the same spot** (R2 E1 to E2). Being pushed back a range after a hit sends the hurt man back to Long, which is exactly where the overhand starts. The pushed-back fighter should stay at Mid, or a rocked man's Cover Up should stop a step-in punch entirely.
2. **Give every style one card it can play at Long beyond Read and Hold Ground** (R2 E1). The Counter-Puncher needs a Long counter that answers a man stepping in, like the Check Hook, in the starting hand of every round. Then Long isn't a guessing coin flip I can only lose.
3. **Cap a single punch below one full rock-and-finish sequence** (R1 E5 to R2 E2). Three punches did 7, 8 and 4, and a 17-head fighter went out in four exchanges. Either a rocked fighter's next hit is capped at 3, or rocking can't happen two exchanges in a row. That way one good read wins a round, not the whole bout.
