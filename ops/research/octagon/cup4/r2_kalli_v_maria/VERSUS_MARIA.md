# Maria on Kalli v Maria (Maria wins by KO in round 2)

**(1) Why I won.** I won by KO in round 2 off two beats.
- **R1 E3:** my Pull Counter Cross read his second Ripping Liver Hook at Mid and landed 8, which rocked him and dropped him to 4 head health. That one counter decided the fight, because after it every clean jab was a threat to finish him.
- **R2 E4:** right after his overhand rocked me, I jabbed at speed 10 against his speed 8 cross and took back both the Lead and the fight. Speed 8 plus the Lead bonus made the jab a punch he could never beat to the target.

**(2) What I could and couldn't read.** His fighter digs to the body, and his card history said so twice. Once I saw the same body hook come back after he'd been rocked, a counter at Mid was close to free. Reading two of his cards in R1 E4 told me he'd step in rather than sit back. What I couldn't read was R2 E2. I stood still at Long, expecting him to spend a turn closing the distance, but the overhand covers that distance and hits in one card, and I took 7. That was the only real damage I took all fight.

**(3) Agent or script?** The agent was more fun. Kalli adapted: after being rocked he covered up, read my hand, then picked the one punch that punishes standing still at Long. A script wouldn't have read my hand and then thrown the overhand. Still, he went back to the same body hook straight into my counter, which a sharper reader wouldn't do.

**(4) Too strong and never wanted.** The fast jab with the Lead was too strong. At speed 10 or 11 it beats every attack in the game to the target, so at low health nothing he holds can stop it. The switch I never wanted was the circle-out defend. It never fit the range, and I threw it away twice.

**(6) TO REACH 10/10**
1. **Cap how fast a jab can be when it's thrown with the Lead (R2 E4, R2 E5).** Once I had the Lead, his crosses and hooks couldn't win a speed race. A fast jab should beat a slow punch by chipping in first, not by cancelling it outright. A jab could trade instead, or the Lead bonus could stop applying to jabs.
2. **Make Long a real choice (R2 E1 to E3).** For three exchanges my hand was dead at Long, and standing still was my only play, which the overhand punished. Every fighter should hold at least one live card at Long, like a jab or a step back that isn't free but works.
3. **Make his read cost me something I can see (R2 E1).** He read my hand, and I got no sign of what he saw or what it set up. Showing both players which cards were read would make that a real exchange of information instead of a hidden advantage.
