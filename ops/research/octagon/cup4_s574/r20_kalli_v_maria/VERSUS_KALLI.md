# Kalli on Kalli v Maria (Kalli wins by DECISION 29-28)

**1. Why I won, 29-28.** Two beats decided it. In R1 E5 my jab caught his fake for 2, which won a round that was otherwise all blocks. In R3 E2 he had jabbed from Long three times running, so my step-in jab beat his at speed 9 against 8 and landed 4. That paid for the 2 he took back in R3 E4, and blocking both of his last jabs locked the round. Round 2 I lost 5-0, because my jab loses to his on reach every single time.

**2. What I could read.** Maria fell into a pattern. He guarded through Round 1, then jabbed almost every exchange from mid-Round 2 to the end, which made his Long jab easy to block and finally to beat on speed. What I couldn't read was when the fake was coming. R2 E5 broke my guard, gave him a judges' point and pushed me to the fence. I also never found out what his other four cards were.

**3. Agent against scripted.** Yes, an agent was more fun. He changed his plan between rounds, from guarding to jabbing, and I had to adjust to that. A script tends to repeat a fixed mix. The catch is that both of us ended up trading Long jabs, and that got flat.

**4. Too strong, and never wanted.** The plan that felt too strong was jab and block at Long. Fourteen of the 18 exchanges were jabs or guards, and neither of us threw a power punch all fight. The switch I never wanted was the Check Left Hook. It sat in my hand all bout with nothing to answer, because his jab never steps in. Half my hand, the crosses and the Slip, was dead at Long.

**6. TO REACH 10/10**
1. Give Long range a reason to leave it. The free jab should cost more on repeat, or lose its Lead bonus after two in a row, so the R2 E2 to R3 E6 jab trade ends and the crosses get played.
2. Break reach ties a different way at Long. Every jab against jab went to him, 8.5 against 8 (R2 E2, R2 E6, R3 E4). One stat deciding a whole round is not a read. Same-speed jabs should both land.
3. Let the Check Hook answer a jab from Long. Then R2 E3 to E6 becomes a guessing game instead of a free guard for me, and a dead card comes alive.
