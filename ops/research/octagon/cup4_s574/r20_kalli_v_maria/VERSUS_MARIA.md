# Maria on Kalli v Maria (Kalli wins by DECISION 29-28)

**1. Why I lost, 28-29.** Two beats decided it. In R3 E2 his Step-In Jab came in at speed 9 against my jab at 8 and landed 4. That one punch outscored everything I landed in the round, and it handed him round 3. In R1 E5 I faked into a man who jabs almost every exchange and got caught for 2. That made round 1 his, and a single lost round was the whole margin. R2 shows the plan that worked: jab-for-jab at neutral I win the race, and one well-timed fake against a run of guards scored. I just started it a round too late.

**2. What I could read and what I couldn't.** His rhythm was easy to read. He jabs, and after one or two guards he goes back to the jab. The colour tell in R2 E6 was the best moment of the bout, because it told me attack-or-counter and my jab was obviously right. I couldn't read when he'd use the Step-In Jab. It looks like the Range Jab from the outside, but it hits for twice as much. I also couldn't tell when he'd guard twice in a row, as he did in R3 E5 and E6, so I threw two jabs into a shell and lost the round by 2.

**3. Agent or script?** The agent was more fun. Kalli changed pattern: three guards in R2, then a heavy step-in jab once he'd seen me jab four times. A script would have kept jabbing, and I would have farmed it. His switches felt like a real opponent adjusting to me.

**4. Too strong and never wanted.** The plan that felt too strong was the Stance Guard at Long. It's cheap, it strips the Lead, and both of us turtled behind it, with 11 of the 18 beats having a guard on one side. The switch I never wanted was stepping in to Mid. Every counter in my hand (`slip3`, `cross3`, `lhook3`, `circle3`) was Mid-only and dead all fight. As a counter-puncher I had nothing to counter with at Long.

**6. To reach 10/10**
1. Give a counter-puncher one way to answer a step-in at Long. In R3 E2 my best card was locked to Mid, so the step-in jab landed free. A Long counter for a step-in should be a guaranteed card for the counter-puncher style, not luck of the draw.
2. Make the step-in jab show itself. In R3 E2 it looked like any other jab until it hit for 4. Let it cost the thrower the Lead or flash a different colour, so reading for it becomes a skill.
3. Make the Stance Guard cost more after the first use at Long, or have a guarded exchange give the judges nothing. R3 E5 and E6 were two blocks that kept him ahead with no punch thrown. Hiding behind a guard shouldn't protect a lead late in the round.
