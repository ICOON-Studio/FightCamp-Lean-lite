# Kalli on Kalli v John (Kalli wins by DECISION 30-28)

**1. Why I won, 30-28.** Two beats decided it. In R1 E1 the hook met his walk-in for a clean counter of 3, and that set up a 7-1 first round. In R2 E3 and R2 E6 the free fake beat him twice while he stood still to parry, which gave me 2 judges' points, put him on the fence and made Round 2 a 6-0 shutout. Round 3 was even at 4-4, and that only happened because his hook chipped 2 through my last block.

**2. What I could read.** John's cut showed me one of his cards every exchange, which was huge: I knew his hooks and uppercut were useless at Long. He also fell into standing still to parry once my jab started landing. What I couldn't read was when he'd swap to his own jab. R3 E1 caught my fake, and I never saw a feint coming from a pressure fighter in R1 E2.

**3. Agent against scripted.** Yes, an agent was more fun. He adapted, switching from walking in to standing still to parry, and that gave me a pattern I could punish. A script wouldn't have rewarded me for reading him like that.

**4. Too strong, and never wanted.** The plan that felt too strong was the cut. Once he was cut, I saw a card every exchange for the rest of the fight, and that gave me more information than any read card ever could. The switch I never wanted was the High Guard in R1 E6. I was ahead, so a reset taxed me 1 for playing it safe while I was winning, and that felt like a fine for being smart.

**6. TO REACH 10/10**
1. Showing a card because of the cut should stop at one card a round, or only start at cut 2. From R2 E2 on, his hand was half open, and that's why his whole plan at Long fell apart.
2. Standing still to parry should cost him once he does it twice in a row. In R2 E5, R2 E6 and R3 E3 both of us used it as a free way to stall at Long, and a fake was the only answer.
3. A heavy punch should chip only 1 through a guard that steps out. In R3 E6 my block took 2 and still cost stamina, which made a 2-point round lead feel like nothing.
