STORY1_JOHN: "Anvil Street"

Structure used: the inherited gym with a mentor's debt, a rival promotion that wants to own the ladder, and a rise, fall and comeback in three acts. The calendar drives the story the way a school term drives a calendar-based RPG. No names, plot or text are taken from any other work. Boxing first. MMA hooks are marked [MMA].

1. Logline and tone

A founder's stroke hands you a failing boxing gym and a debt you did not know about, owed to the one promotion that wants your best fighter. You have one teenager with fast hands, a head coach whose own hands are shot, and a calendar that does not care. The story is a working-class sports drama: warm in the gym, cold in the office, honest in the ring.

At the 10-hour mark the player feels the weight of "one more week." Kit, the first fighter, is a person you worry about, a rival has a name you hate, and a bill is due on Friday.
At the credits the player feels they built something that will outlast any one fighter: a gym with a photo wall full of people they remember.
2. The spine

Protagonist: you, the new owner of Anvil Street Boxing Club. You were the club's cutman and bookkeeper for nine years. You never boxed. You taped hands and read the ledger. Dessa Marlowe, the founder, trusted you with both. When she has a stroke in week 1, she leaves the keys to you because you are the only one who knows where every bill is. You are not a former champion. You are the person who notices.

First fighter: Kit Alvarado, 19, lightweight, fast and impatient, from the block behind the gym. His mother's rent is late. He wants a purse more than a belt.

Antagonistic force: Crown Ring Promotions and its owner, Victor Lasko. Crown is not a cartoon. It runs clean shows and pays on time. It also holds the gym's loan through a lender it owns, has a right-of-first-refusal clause on any Anvil fighter who reaches national level, and poaches talent by buying their contract the week before it matters. The deeper antagonist is the sport itself: bodies wear out, and the ladder is built for people who can afford to wait.

5. Choices that matter
Kit's style (beat 1). It changes his fights for the whole career. If ignored, the game picks the balanced default.
The sponsor (beat 4). Taking Crown money saves the gym in Act I and makes Act II harder. If ignored, the late fee is paid and the bank goes negative.
The rematch with Jax (beat 5). Rivalry heat carries into every later fight with him. If ignored, no callout is accepted and Jax simply appears on the ladder.
Dessa's clause (beat 8). This is the story's central bargain. If ignored, the clause stands and Crown gets first call on Kit.
The offer for the gym (beat 11). It sets the ending. If ignored, the default is refuse.

How the story still works if the player ignores everything. Every choice has a default that fires after 7 days: the cheapest, quietest option. Every card is dismissable. The game has three story switches: Story On (cards interrupt), Story Beside (cards sit in the inbox and never interrupt), and Story Off (free play only). Rivalry, press, rival promotions and money all work without the story. Story flags only add cards and a few extra offers. They never change a fight's engine.

6. Fight-night moments (no scripted outcomes)
Corner talk. Holl's lines in the corner are drawn from the player's own reads. When his memory is failing, he repeats a cue ("Jab, jab, jab"). The cue is flavor text only and never changes the engine.
A rival's entrance. Jax walks out with a callout line scaled to the rivalry heat. At heat 70 or above, the arena director holds a close-up on him. Nothing about the fight changes. The player sees it matters.
A grudge round. When heat is high, the commentary names the grudge between rounds, and the scorebug shows a small flame icon (tooltip: "Rivalry: this one is personal"). The corner prompt reminds the player of the stakes without changing any numbers.
An injury callback. An old cut or a taped hand shows as an icon on the fighter. Commentary refers to it by the date it happened ("the left eye from March"). The existing injury system decides whether it matters. The story only remembers it.
7. Ending

The campaign ends after the last booked fight of Act III. The ending is read from state, never from one dialogue line.

Standing. The gym is solvent, the roster holds at least one national-level fighter, and Crown's clause is dealt with. Credits show the photo wall. Carries over: the gym, the roster, the wall, and a new rookie who walks in the next week as the first fighter of free play.
Partnered. You took Crown's partnership. The gym has money and a Crown logo on the ring. Carries over: the gym and fighters, plus a standing sponsor deal and a permanent rival-promotion flag.
Sold. You took the buyout. The credits end on the empty building. Carries over: the cash and the fighters you kept, in a new, smaller gym.

The campaign never ends the game. After the credits, free play opens with the same gym, bank, roster and photo wall, and the story switches back to Story Beside. [MMA] A second campaign could start from the same wall with a grappling gym next door.

10. The story shapes the menus, the UI and the loop
(a) Title menu and new-career flow

The title menu has four icons with tooltips: Continue (tooltip: "Chapter 2, week 14. Next fight in 3 weeks"), New Career, Free Play, Options. For a returning player, Continue shows a single line: the chapter, the next fight, and who is waiting ("Jax is waiting").

New Career asks only three things:

Your name.
Story mode: On, Beside, or Off. The tooltip says what each does.
Your gym's name, with "Anvil Street" prefilled.

It does not ask about difficulty or a fighter. Kit arrives in the story.

(b) The weekly loop

A week has five days of training, one day of rest and one of fight or admin. The player always sees NEXT UP first, a banner at the top of MY GYM: the next fight, the next bill, or the next call, whichever is closest.

Interrupts. A story card can interrupt a week only at the end of the week, in the week review. It cannot interrupt a fight or a booking. Story Beside moves every card to the inbox.
One more week. The pull is a visible promise. The NEXT UP banner always shows one story promise: "Fight in 3 weeks," "Lasko calls Friday," "Bill due: $1,200 in 5 days."
Loop (plain text):
WEEK (train / rest / spar / bills)
  -> DECISION (book, hire, pay, accept a call)
  -> FIGHT (camp, fight week, fight night)
  -> AFTERMATH (result screen, press, purse, injuries)
  -> CHAPTER BEAT (a card, a call, a door opening)
  -> NEXT UP names the next promise -> WEEK

(f) UI rules the story implies
Icons with tooltips over text labels. The tooltip carries the name and the stake.
The facility is a gym. "Camp" only means the pre-bout preparation.
One clear next action: NEXT UP is always visible.
A story card never blocks a fight or a booking.
Every choice shows its cost before it is taken.
Story is a layer. It can be turned off and the game stays whole.
