## What would a human do?
A coach reviews a week by watching the room, not a spreadsheet. He notices ONE visible thing - hands loose or stiff,
whether the last rounds still had snap, attendance and effort, honest weight, a cooked face - and says it in one short
sentence. He never says "power is 40% of a point sharper".

## 1. Tracked vs said
Said out loud: sharpness/timing, gas tank by round, stiffness vs looseness, willingness in sparring, weight, attendance
(SOURCED: Bozy Ennis camp talk - fight week is defense, catch, move, rounds cut 8 -> 6 -> 4; Shane Mosley on camp sleep).
Kept by S&C, rarely said as numbers: HR, HRV, readiness % (SOURCED: Dan Lawrence on Benn, Whoop to the coach's phone;
Corey Peacock, sleep consistency). Character, not stats (SOURCED: Teddy Atlas, New Yorker 2000-08-21).

## 3. Tone
Short: one praise, one work-on (SOURCED Warwick coach-talk study: 71% of between-round speech is directives, praise is
brief). Head coach = craft and character; S&C = load, sleep, "pull him". Fight week: statements, no outcome talk.

## 4. Numbers
Only gym numbers: rounds done, the round he faded, weight, "drop him to four", "60%", "two hard days". Never "+0.4".

## 5. Rule set for the game (JUDGEMENT)
- One sentence, 8-18 words, one topic.
- Priority: injury/overtrained > fight week > hard spar > breakthrough > regress > improve > flat.
- Noise week with no spar/injury/fight -> "Quiet week. Leave him."
- No stat-delta templates in the coach's mouth. Head-coach voice; S&C feeds the flag, not the words.
- Templates: "{fighter}'s hands were loose this week. Timing's back." / "{fighter} faded after round {n}. Gas tank's the
  job." / "{fighter} looked stiff on {station}. Pull the power." / "Good week. Last rounds still had snap." / "Same
  mistakes on {skill}. He's not concentrating." / "{fighter}'s cooked. I don't want him hard this week." / "Hard spar.
  Technical days only." / "Coming back - moving, not fighting. Pads and defense." / "Fight week. Nothing hard. Catch and
  move." / "Quiet week. Leave him."
