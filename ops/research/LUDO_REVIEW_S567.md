# Ludo's review of Puncher's chance, from the fight screen (S567)

Owner S567: "and ludo ... can they jointhe league and review the game ?". Asked in Ask Ludo (the FightCamp project chat, his
Chrome), with the still `ops/previews/s567_gauges/order_g2_plan2.png` uploaded and a plain description of the rules and
the screen. Ludo said itself that these are design estimates, not playtest scores: it has not played.

## Scores (Ludo, from one still and the description)
| | score | its reason, in short |
|---|---|---|
| Fun | 7 | planning a combination and watching it resolve; a dominant combo threatens it |
| Depth | 8 | hand cover, range, timing, stamina and habit reading, if counters are reliable and visible |
| Clarity of the screen | 5 | the ring is prominent, the text that decides a beat is small; consequences are not clear at a glance |
| Variety | 6 | free choice of moves, but 18 beats may repeat if opponents and situations do not demand different plans |
| Originality | 7 | closest: Toribash and Frozen Synapse; the boxing hand cover and range puzzle read as distinct |

## Could a new player choose a beat with no manual?
"Submit one, yes; choose well, no." Missing, in its order:
1. how the three moments resolve, and what happens when two attacks meet;
2. the current range and what a chosen step turns it into, and when;
3. what each hand blocks or leaves open, per moment, in plain words;
4. stamina before and after the beat, held-hand recovery included, and what happens if a plan cannot be paid;
5. how backing off uses up ground, with a warning before the ropes;
6. one suggested legal opening beat with a one-line reason (not "the best move").
It called the see-through preview useful for motion but not the way to teach cover or range.

## Its three risks on Steam
1. a solved dominant plan (the feint and overhand) stops players experimenting;
2. a demanding first impression: simultaneous planning is hard to teach, small text and unexplained results make early
   losses feel arbitrary;
3. limited replay value if the opponents keep the same habits.

## To reach 10, its order
1. make the dominant combo answerable and name the answer in the result line (it suggested "rear hand high blocks the
   overhand when it arrives" - our rule already does, the feint is what removes it; FEINTFIX2 is measuring the fix);
2. a moment-by-moment plan preview (what each hand covers in each of the three moments);
3. a guided first beat: choose a hand, the other hand or hold, the feet and the order, then "both resolve moment 1 together";
4. one readable cause-and-effect line per interaction, resource changes included (we have this; it could not see it);
5. the current range above the fighters and a ground track with a labelled ropes end, movement previewed on it;
6. his last three beats as compact rows plus one stated pattern ("rear high in 2 of the last 3");
7. opponents with distinct, counterable habits (pressure, retreat, body, counter-punching);
8. legibility at 1920x1080 (text sizes).

## What I take from it (Claude)
- Its clarity 5 agrees with Kalli's 5 and Arena's 7: the rules are deeper than the screen shows. Items 2, 3 and 5 are
  page work with no rule change: a per-moment cover row in the strip, a first-beat guide, the range named large.
- Items 4, 6 and 7 exist already and were not visible in one still: a second still of a fought beat would have shown them.
- Joining the league: Ask Ludo has no tool of its own, so every beat would be relayed by hand in his Chrome (about 90
  messages for five bouts). Not done; an exhibition bout against the league winner is the cheap form.
