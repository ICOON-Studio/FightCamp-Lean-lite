# Why the cross does not read as powerful (S439)

Owner: "pro right cross , the punches do not feellike the y have power , not sure if it is the clips issue". Measured by one reader, re-measured and challenged by a second (this is the second's corrected version).

## Verdict

Both are to blame, and the earlier reader's main story holds up when re-measured. The biggest single cause is something nobody chose on purpose: the game marks the moment of the hit as the frame where the glove gets closest to the face (that is the rule in tools/clip_contact.gd). By definition, the glove has stopped moving toward the face at that frame. In three of the four cross takes it is already drifting back by 0.4 to 1.2 m/s. Everything that says "hit" fires on that frame: the thud, the 3-frame freeze, the head snap and the shake. The glove's fastest moment comes 52 to 92 ms (3 to 6 frames) earlier. PunchReach, the code that carries the glove onto the face, uses a blend that also ends flat. Measured with the game's real arm solve, the glove peaks at 5.0 to 7.0 m/s about 0.1 s before the hit. It is down to 1.1 to 2.0 m/s one frame before and 0.3 to 0.8 m/s on the hit frame, and it sits within 3 to 5 cm of the face for the last 2 to 3 frames. So the eye sees a glove that slows down, settles on the face, and then the hit happens. The clip is weak on its own too. The glove's striking face tops out at 4.4 to 5.4 m/s (the earlier 3.6 to 4.3 was the wrist, which moves slower), against 6 to 11.5 m/s for real crosses. The hips never move forward in the game (0 cm; real fighters move about 28 cm), and nothing carries through the target (0 to 2 cm; real 11 cm). I would drop or shrink two of the earlier claims. First, the corner-call slowdown does not make "many" crosses slow: no new punch starts while that bar is open (FightView.gd lines 332-334), so at most the one punch already in the air finishes slowly. Second, the join blend cannot touch the fast part of a single cross, because in all four takes the top-speed frame falls where the blend is about 93% done or finished. How sure I am: about 85% on the order of causes. The numbers come from my own run on the staged pair, using the real PunchReach arm solve. How strongly a player feels each part can only be settled by watching it on film.

## Fixes, most important first

1. **Move the moment of the hit to the glove's fast frame, and make the carry onto the face arrive moving. (1) Change the hit rule from 'closest to the face' to 'the frame after top glove speed'. For the cross takes that is 3-6 frames earlier, with the elbow at about 90-115 deg. Then remake clip_contact.json, so the sound, freeze, snap and reaction all fire on that frame. (2) Replace PunchReach's flat-ending S-curve before the hit with a curve that is still rising at the hit (a straight ramp, or speeding up). (3) Then, instead of pinning the glove for 0.05 s, push the face point 6-11 cm on into the head over 2-3 frames, so the glove and the head travel together.** [medium] - why it reads as power: Today the glove's fastest moment is 3-6 frames before anything says 'hit', and it reaches the face at 0.3-0.8 m/s. Real crosses peak 8-21 ms before the hit, land at about 8-9 m/s and carry 11 cm (G75 lines 20, 26-27; K54 line 34). Professionals land with the elbow at about 102 deg (K52 line 47), which is where our top-speed frame already is. This one change turns 'placed on the face' into 'driven through it'. Where: tools/clip_contact.gd (the rule, lines 1-2) -> assets/data/clip_contact.json rows pro_right_cross, _a, _b, _c, _ft, _hurt; scripts/fight3d/PunchReach.gd weight() lines 105-117 and HOLD_S line 14; the target from FightStage.target_point lines 237-250

## Only a film can settle

- What he actually watched. 'Pro right cross' may mean he judged the clip on its own, on a review film or in a move viewer. In that case there is no PunchReach, no freeze and no sound, and only the clip numbers apply (glove 4.4-5.4 m/s, fastest 3-6 frames before the hit, hips still, no carry-through). Only the film he saw can settle this.
- How the shown glove moves during the 3-frame freeze. The face point is read live, so the glove may ride the 16-degree head snap a few cm. I did not run a live fight frame by frame.
- My 'shown glove' numbers come from the real PunchReach arm solve on the staged pair at the standard 0.903 m gap, with the other boxer standing in guard. In a fight the gap breathes and the other boxer moves, so the exact m/s will vary. The slowing-down shape follows from the code either way.
- How much each fix changes his read: whether moving the hit frame plus the faster drive is enough, or whether the body carry is also needed, can only be judged on film, before and after.
- Where the punch starts is a matter of definition. With my rule (the first frame under 15% of top speed) a and c take 0.35-0.38 s; the earlier reader's rule gave 0.17-0.18 s. Only a film can say whether the wind-up reads.
- That the uppercut 'reads right' to him comes from the earlier analysis. I did not see that judgement myself.
- Whether a cross started late inside a combination has its fast part blended away by the 0.3 s join: plausible from the code (it can start 0.10 s before its hit), not measured.
- Whether he played with -- rokoko (then a plain cross is rk_cross, not measured here). It is off by default since S432 (RkClips.gd lines 30-31 and 77).
- There are no real figures for: rear heel lift in cm and rear-foot turn in degrees (K46 lines 19-20), head snap angle (K51 line 10), wind-up angle, hip return after the hit (K54 line 90), or camera shake size. The freeze frame counts are expert estimates, not measurements.
