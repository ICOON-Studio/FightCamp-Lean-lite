Ideas wanted: LONG RANGE is a corridor - what should a man do out there, in a game of switches?
You are a game designer. We want your own ideas, not a review. Nothing below is a proposal to judge: it is the game as
it stands and the problem.
The game (a small standalone prototype: Ring Switches, boxing, one man against one man)
A bout is 3 rounds of 6 BEATS. In a beat both men set their switches at the same time, in secret, then the beat plays.
Each HAND does one thing a beat: throw one punch, or stay home held HIGH or LOW. A hand at home blocks one line: lead
hand high stops straight head punches, lead hand low stops body punches, rear hand high stops hooks, rear hand low
stops uppercuts. A hand that punches is out of its guard only in the moment it throws.
The FEET do one thing a beat: step in (1 stamina, one place closer), hold, or back off (1 stamina, one place further;
you give ground, two and you are on the ropes; your next punch that beat is a pull counter, +1 if it lands).
Three ranges: Long, Mid, Inside. EVERY ROUND STARTS AT LONG.
A beat is three MOMENTS. The order you click your switches is the order you do them; your first action meets his first.
When both strike in one moment the faster lands first, and a big one (3 health or more) that lands stops his slower one.
Health 18, stamina 8 (+1 a beat, +1 for each hand held). The judges score each round 10-9 (10-8 when well ahead).
The punches and where they reach (the page's own table)
punch	hand	stamina	damage	speed	reaches
jab	lead	1	1	fast	Mid, Long
body jab	lead	1	1	fast	Mid, Long
lead hook	lead	2	3	fast	Inside, Mid
body hook	lead	2	2	medium	Inside, Mid
lead uppercut	lead	2	3	medium	Inside
cross	rear	2	3	medium	Inside, Mid
body cross	rear	2	2	medium	Inside, Mid
overhand	rear	3	4	slow	Inside, Mid
rear uppercut	rear	3	4	slow	Inside
Also: a feint (1 stamina: his hand reacts, your next punch cannot be covered) and a push off (Inside).
The problem (five playtesters, each in their own words)
At Long only the two jabs reach, for 1 damage each. So Long is a corridor you walk through: every round opens there, the
rear hand has nothing to do, and the only real choice is "step in now or poke first". All five testers named it; one
called it "a corridor where only 1-damage pokes reach". We do NOT want Long removed, and we do not want a long-range
bomb that ends rounds there. We want Long to be a place where a fight can be WON on points by a man who is better at
distance - the real outfighter - and lost by a man who walks in carelessly.
What we ask you for
THREE different designs. For each:
THE RULE in at most five plain sentences (what changes, what the player sets, what it costs).
ONE BEAT walked through at Long: both men's switches, what happens moment by moment.
THE DECISION it gives the player at Long: what is he weighing, and when is each choice right?
WHO IT HELPS: the outfighter, the pressure fighter, or both? Should a pressure fighter still be able to get Inside?

A designer gives each range its own job, makes crossing a range a cost or a risk the other man can punish, and checks
that each range is used in real numbers of beats.

THREE DESIGNS FOR LONG RANGE — Ring Switches
Each design preserves your existing table, does not add one-shot finishers, and keeps Long as a viable, meaningful fighting ground rather than just a hallway.
✦ DESIGN 1 — The Range-Control Step
1. THE RULE
At Long range only: your foot choice also sets how far you will let him come. Step In = you advance 1 and reduce his maximum forward movement this beat to 0. Hold = you hold ground and he can close 1 if he Steps In. Back Off = you retreat 1 and gain +1 reaction speed on your first hand action this beat. Step In costs 1 stamina, Back Off costs 1 stamina, Hold costs 0. No new punches are added — only movement and timing change.
2. ONE BEAT WALKTHROUGH
Table
Moment	Outfighter (Long)	Pressure Fighter	What happens
Setup	Switches: Back Off → Jab	Switches: Step In → Jab	Both reveal simultaneously
Moment 1	Back Off executed: Outfighter retreats 1, stays Long; reaction speed bonus active	Step In blocked — opponent backed away, distance unchanged	No contact yet; distance still Long
Moment 2	Jab — speed boosted to fast+	Jab — standard fast	Outfighter's jab lands first (1 damage); pressure fighter's jab now lands on empty air — opponent just moved back out of reach
End	Distance still Long; stamina: Oufighter −1 (step) −1 (jab) = 6; Pressure Fighter −1 (step) −1 (jab) = 6	Score edge: Outfighter	
3. THE DECISION
Step In — when you are ready to commit to Mid range immediately and want to deny him space to work. Hold — when you want to bait him into overcommitting but don't want to trade ground. Back Off — when he is rushing you: you give ground on your own terms, stay out of reach, and punish the miss. You weigh: control of distance vs. willingness to trade space for safety.
4. WHO IT HELPS
Strongly favours the outfighter first, but pressure fighter still gets in — they just have to either feint to freeze your movement or accept they will eat 1–2 jabs while closing over 2 beats instead of 1. No free entry anymore.
5. HOW IT CAN BREAK
Break: Back Off + Jab repeated every beat → untouchable forever. Cap: You can only Back Off 2 consecutive beats; third time you must Hold or Step In. Also — repeated retreats move you toward the ropes; after 3 retreats you hit them and can no longer Back Off.
6. REAL BOXING
"The outfighter doesn't just stay away — he steps exactly when his opponent lunges, turning their own momentum against them."
✦ DESIGN 2 — The Threatened Lead
1. THE RULE
At Long range only: your lead hand position changes what your opponent dares to do. Lead Held HIGH = opponent's Step In costs +1 stamina this beat (your extended hand threatens to meet him coming). Lead Held LOW = opponent cannot use Feint toward you this beat (low hand covers the body line so he can't fake it). If your lead hand throws a jab/body jab, both effects vanish for 1 beat. Holding your lead hand still costs 0 stamina as normal.
2. ONE BEAT WALKTHROUGH
Table
Moment	Outfighter	Pressure Fighter	What happens
Setup	Switches: Hold Lead High → Jab	Switches: Step In → Jab	Reveal
Moment 1	Lead High active — Step In cost increases	Step In attempted: pays 2 stamina instead of 1; still moves 1 closer (now borderline Long/Mid)	Distance closes but at a price
Moment 2	Jab lands clean — he paid stamina to move and cannot afford to match your output	Jab lands too — equal exchange but he's now at 5 stamina vs your 7	End beat: he's closer but tired; you still have options
3. THE DECISION
Hold High = tax his approach — slow him down, drain his tank. Hold Low = shut down feints so you always know what's coming. Punch = give up that control to deal damage directly. You weigh: preventing him from getting there vs. hurting him while he's there.
4. WHO IT HELPS
Both. Outfighter makes crossing the corridor expensive and slow — wins on accumulated damage + stamina edge. Pressure fighter can still close — but he has to budget stamina properly and mix up his timing so you can't just sit on High every time.
5. HOW IT CAN BREAK
Break: Hold High every single beat → he never has enough stamina to reach you. Cap: Hold High only works if you don't punch with it; 2 consecutive beats holding High = he learns the rhythm — effect drops by half (cost +0.5, rounds to +1 every other beat). Also he can switch to Step In + Hold to advance without throwing — paying no extra stamina but doing no damage.
6. REAL BOXING
"An extended lead hand isn't just for jabbing — it's a fence that tells the opponent how close he's allowed to get."
✦ DESIGN 3 — The Angle & Half-Step
1. THE RULE
At Long range only: add a Half-Step foot option (0 stamina, changes distance by ½ a range band). Two Half-Steps = one full band change. More importantly: if you end a beat further away than he started from you, your next jab gains +1 priority (it lands first if equal speed). If you end closer, he gets that priority bonus instead. Ranges now have soft edges — Long→Mid is a zone, not a wall. No new punches.
2. ONE BEAT WALKTHROUGH
Table
Moment	Outfighter	Pressure Fighter	What happens
Setup	Switches: Half-Step Back → Jab	Switches: Full Step In → Jab	Reveal
Moment 1	Half-Step Back — distance opens slightly	Step In — moves forward 1 band, overshoots — now just inside Mid range	He committed too far too fast
Moment 2	Priority bonus active — Jab lands first (1 damage)	Jab lands second — you're already slightly out — grazes, no damage	He's now at Mid range but you set the tempo
Next beat	You can choose: Half-Step Back again (return to Long) OR commit to Mid	He's overextended — must either hold ground or Back Off to reset	You decide when the exchange happens
3. THE DECISION
Half-Step Back = trade nothing for safety, gain priority to punish his rush. Full Back Off = maximum distance but no priority bonus. Step In = meet him halfway — you become the one with priority penalty if you misjudge it. You weigh: how much ground do I want to give up to make him miss?
4. WHO IT HELPS
Both equally. Outfighter uses half-steps to threaten counters and keep Long range his safe zone. Pressure fighter learns to use Half-Step in — matching your rhythm instead of rushing — so he doesn't overshoot and get punished. Closing distance becomes a dance, not a dash.
5. HOW IT CAN BREAK
Break: Half-Step Back + Jab every beat → permanent priority advantage, untouchable. Cap: Priority bonus lasts only 1 beat; if you don't throw the jab that beat, it vanishes. Also you can only Half-Step 3 times per round — after that your feet are set and you must move normally.
6. REAL BOXING
"You don't have to run away to make him miss — a small adjustment is all it takes to turn his own weight against him."
FINAL CHOICE & REFERENCES
✅ Which I'd build first: DESIGN 3 — The Angle & Half-Step
Why:
It changes how you move, not what you can throw — fits cleanly into your existing beat/moment structure without rewriting your punch table
It solves your exact problem: Long becomes a place where you win by timing and positioning, not just who jabs more
It naturally creates the real-boxing dynamic you wanted: outfighter controls entry; pressure fighter can still get in — but only patiently, not by mashing Step In
It's intuitive: "if you overcommit you get hit" is something players feel immediately
