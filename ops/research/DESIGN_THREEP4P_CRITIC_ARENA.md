# Design review: The offer, the corner, and the walk-in
SCORE depth=6 general=7 play=8

## Why these scores

- **Depth — 6/10.** The three ideas form one good coaching loop: pick what your boxer needs, see whether the answer works, then decide whether the gym can take on the opponent. There are real trade-offs: take a winnable bout for income or wait for one that teaches more; spend a shout on escape or save it for a better opening; sign a useful sparring partner or keep the place and stipend free. But the map mostly adds advice and a reward to choices that already exist. It doesn't make the offer's risk something you can compare and change, or turn the ring clue into a clear next decision. If wins and worked lines both earn recruits, beating weaker opponents with a reliable line risks becoming the best recruiting strategy. A fight you lose after good coaching teaches something, but the built recruitment rule never rewards it. **Below the 7/10 depth bar; not designed yet under the brief's rule.**
- **General design — 7/10.** This is work for a gym owner, not a promoter or a viewer. It reuses the bout offer, the corner and reception instead of adding a currency or a screen. Situations, steps, weight and the roster are already game rules. But the same words must mean the same thing everywhere: a short rest and a hard cut aren't reasons to train harder; winning exchanges isn't necessarily taking space; an opponent who signs must be the same boxer who fought. Boxing makes the distinction clear: a boxer can give ground on purpose, land the cleaner shots and win the exchange. Show space and success separately.
- **Play — 8/10.** There's a reason to open the next offer, watch an exchange you planned for, and check reception after a good night. An opponent naming something you actually did is a stronger reward than a reputation number. But if the read keeps saying “Take it,” the map shows a step your boxer never meant to take, or the visit quietly expires on Continue, the loop stops feeling earned. Slow weeks should stay fast; don't make every quiet week a recruitment check.

## Missing and useless options

| Moment | Keep: what the option changes | Missing: what it would change | Cut or demote |
|---|---|---|---|
| Offer | **Accept** books this date and opponent. **Pass** gives up this offer. **Full report** shows terms and the evidence behind the estimate without booking. **Ask for a fight**, where allowed, seeks a different date or opponent; it doesn't guarantee a yes. | **Check the week** opens the weight and rest forecast without booking. Compare this offer with a later eligible show if the date is the problem. When style is the problem, open the matching plan line to see available answers without committing to the bout. | “Free points” is not a promise the game can make. “Nothing that worries me” isn't an against reason. Keep one material downside or say none was found. Don't add a button to obey the coach. |
| Fight | **Top-down / 3D** changes the view, never the outcome. **Shout** trades attention (and sometimes energy or trust) for an answer in an unsettled exchange. **Say nothing** saves the call. **Change one plan line** changes what your boxer can try next round. | A short note at the next decision: what was planned, what happened, and which *existing* call or plan change could help. Keep the note through the corner minute and the Why for players who skip the film. | Don't offer a call on a settled exchange. Drop the ownership wash if all it says is who won the last few exchanges; keep the place and gas clues that can change a decision. |
| Reception | **Meet the opponent** opens the interview without signing. **Choose a starting focus** puts the signing benefit into a move, a habit or room to grow. **Sign** spends a roster place and the weekly stipend; **Pass** gives up the visit. | Show the weekly cash after signing and who will lose one-to-one time under the current week. Show the answer-by date before Continue crosses it. If the gym is full but there's a real way to open a place before the deadline, link to that choice; otherwise show why Sign is locked. | No paid hold, no guaranteed signing and no new trial button unless a trial can reveal something the bout didn't. Waiting already has a cost: the visit can end. |

## To reach 10/10

1. **Turn the read into a decision, not an order.** Use one reason builder on amateur list/detail and pro bout rows, with a separate terms check for debut offers without a fixed opponent. First show booking blocks, then weight at weigh-in and rest on the bout date, then the strongest real gain and cost. Use goal rules and deadline, the ladder's own forecast, and known style against usable lines. Keep estimates marked as estimates; staff quality can narrow uncertain evidence, never change eligibility or reveal undiscovered tells. Replace “camp hard” for short rest or a cut with “Look for a later date” and link to the existing week or ask flow only when legal. Check a rested even match, an unsafe novice, a heavy cut, a higher-ranked rival where a win won't gain a place, and a pro tune-up where pay and progress disagree.
2. **Make the corner a place to decide.** Record intended line, actual step and why they differ (held, wasn't ready, or no plan line). Show the last settled exchange and the next chance to act. Highlight a side pinned by the card or duel data, not the last side to lose. At the next live call or bell, offer just the relevant existing choices and their cost. For instance, if your boxer is on the ropes and the exit wasn't ready, **Tie up** may buy a break at the cost of a call, while **Say nothing** saves attention; at the bell a usable exit can replace a line. Don't change combat odds through the view. Verify a held step is not sold as a failure and a retreating winner is not shown walking the opponent down. Persist one clue in the Why for skipped fights.
3. **Make the visit earned, rare and fair to the roster.** For beaten amateur opponents only at first, require a recorded coaching moment: e.g. a fitted call followed by a successful escape, or a planned answer that worked on at least two attempts under pressure. Match the interview line to that evidence. Keep a seeded roll and modest reputation boost, but cap repeat credit from the same move and use actual club standing if available (omit the penalty until it is). Set a cooldown per rival on pass or expiry. New work: persist the evidence, store a stable rival link, and move the regional identity on signing without duplicating the record, losing bout history or clearing any applicable medical rest.
4. **Close the week without extra chores.** Send the visit to the existing amber need, with a date and a warning on Continue if it crosses the deadline, not a forced stop. Check the real shared roster limit at the ask and again at signing. Show the stipend and the effect on the existing coach schedule before Sign; don't promise a pad-night split the scheduler doesn't enforce. Use the existing starting focus and goal flow. Test a full mixed roster, a last-day pass, a missed visit, and a save/load with an open visit. A good night should still have a useful Why even when nobody asks.
