# ALLPLAY1 - Claude (S558, three bouts in words with play_cli.mjs, seeds 51 / 52 / 53)

## Verdict
It is fun, and the fun is real boxing fun: all three bouts were decided on the last beats of round 3 with both men at
3 health or less (lost 28-29 to the Jabber, beat the Slugger 29-28, beat the Body Snatcher 30-27). The best thing in
the game is that no plan stays right: each good plan worked about three beats, then the man answered it. What holds it
back is not the rules but what the player is TOLD: the guard bar is never explained, a hand that punches later is
secretly held high, the judges' cards ignore knockdowns, and the three men do not fight like their cards say.

FUN 8, DEPTH 8, CLARITY 6, VARIETY 6.

## NOT SURE
- I am not a cold player: I am the developer seat and had read the S557 handover (not the page's code, and not the
  rules code this session). My reads may have come faster than a new player's.
- 54 beats is a small sample: "he clinches at 2 stamina or less" and "his cover follows what hit him" are what I saw,
  not measured.
- Whether the jab is as weak as it felt: I threw it once. The balance tool says it lands 80% of the time.
- Whether backing off is ever right: I never did it once in 54 beats, so I cannot say what it buys.

## The play, point by point
**Three best moments**
1. Bout 1, round 2, beat 1: I had seen his opener twice (jab, step in, big rear hand). Lead high took the jab, my body
   cross landed in moment 2 as a counter, and my rear hand was back up in time to block his overhand in moment 3.
   Three things planned, three things happened. This is the game at its best.
2. Bout 1, round 2, beat 5: he was ON THE ROPES at 3 health. In round 1 his back off had beaten my feint and cross;
   on the ropes he could not back off. Feint, cross, he went down. The ropes changed the answer to a question I had
   already asked once.
3. Bout 2, round 3, beat 1: 19-19, 3 health against 4. I guessed a rear hand that is waiting to punch is held up, so the
   under line is open while he loads: step in, rear uppercut in moment 2, lead high. His cross was blocked, the
   uppercut landed as a counter for 5, he went down.
Close behind: bout 3, round 2, beats 4 and 5 (I clinched ON PURPOSE with full stamina so his overhand was wasted, then
hit an empty man), and bout 1, round 3, beat 3 (my clinch ate the feint and cross that would have stopped me).

**Three dullest**
1. Bout 3, round 1, beats 3 and 4: the same beat twice, word for word (my cross lands, he pushes off, steps in, body
   cross blocked). Nothing new to read.
2. Bout 1, round 2, beat 6 and bout 2, round 3, beat 4: 1 stamina left, so there was nothing to choose: both hands up.
   The stamina made the choice.
3. Bout 2, round 1, beat 3 and round 3, beat 5: I spent 4 stamina on a feint and a big punch, he clinched for 1,
   nothing happened. The first time it felt like a robbery, because nothing had told me a clinch was likely.

**Unfair, or not explainable from what was shown**
- The GUARD bar ("guard 6/6") is on every line and in no line of the help. It moved to 5 twice (bout 1 round 1 beat 2,
  bout 2 round 2 beat 2) and I never learned what it does. In 54 beats it changed nothing I could see.
- A hand that punches is held HIGH in the other moments of the beat ("still up", "back up"). The help does not say so.
  It cost me bout 3, round 1, beat 6: his card said "rear hand held low 2, held high 0", so I threw the overhand at
  the open side, and it was blocked because the rear hand that crosses in moment 3 is up in moment 1. The round's
  count of held hands only covers beats where the hand did not punch, so it misleads exactly when he is attacking.
- A man who is knocked down keeps punching: bout 2, round 3, beat 6 (he was at 0 after moment 1 and still stepped in
  and threw a cross in moment 3); bout 3, round 3, beat 6 (I went down in moment 1 and my cross in moment 2 put HIM
  down).
- Going down gave me health: bout 1, round 3, beat 4: I was at 2, went down, got up on 6 against a man on 1.
- Every round is 10-9. Bout 2, round 3 was 12 points to 1 with two knockdowns: 10-9. Bout 1, round 2 was 10-2 with a
  knockdown: 10-9. So in bout 3 I was 20-18 up and round 3 could not change the cards at all.
- "Lands as a counter" when his punch missed in the SAME moment (bout 1, round 2, beat 3: his jab smothered in moment
  2, my uppercut in moment 2 a counter). The help says "later punches".
- Words: when HE clinches the line reads "HE: ties him up" (bout 2, round 1, beat 3). "Mid to Inside - you step in, he
  backs off" (bout 1, round 3, beat 5) reads like a bug: he backed off and ended closer.

**Is "which line is open" a read now?** Yes, about two beats in three, and for two reasons a player can learn:
(1) HIS COVER FOLLOWS WHAT HIT HIM, a beat or two late. Bout 2, round 2: three body crosses landed, then his lead hand
went low, and the cross landed twice at the gap. Bout 3: three crosses and his lead went high; two overhands and his
rear went high for the first time. (2) HIS STAMINA. At 2 or less he cannot pay for a big punch: the beat is free, or
he clinches (bout 2, round 3, beat 3: free overhand; bout 3 round 2 beat 5). His last three beats helped me most; the
line of round counts helped least. It is a coin flip only on the first beat against a new man.

**A plan that is right every beat?** No, and that is the best news. Three came close and each broke:
- "Lead hand covers, step in, rear body cross in moment 2" as the opener at Long: worked 3 times (bout 1 round 2
  beat 1, bout 1 round 3 beat 1, bout 2 round 2 beat 1), failed when he backed off (bout 3, round 1, beat 1).
- "Step in, lead uppercut, rear high" against the Jabber: three in a row (bout 1, round 2, beats 2 to 4), then he hit
  first and left.
- "Lead low and the cross, feet hold" against the Body Snatcher: it costs 2 stamina and gives 2 back. Worked 4 of 6,
  broke at bout 3, round 2, beat 3 when he delayed his lead hand by a moment. This one is the nearest to a free plan.
**Switches I never wanted:** back off (0 of 54 beats), the body hook (0), rear hand low (once, at 1 health), the jab
(once: it is 1 health and it is smothered Inside), the body jab (once, smothered).
**A range where nothing is worth doing:** Long. Every round opens there and every time I walked in. It is a corridor.

**The three men.** Less different than their cards. The Jabber "stays at Long" but walked in on beat 1 and fought
Inside most of the bout. The Slugger "walks in" but backed off at Long (bout 2, round 2, beats 1 and 5). The Body
Snatcher opened by jabbing and running (bout 3, round 1, beat 1). All three open with "lead punch, step in, rear body
cross in moment 3", and all three throw three switches and then stand empty. What I did feel: the Slugger's 21 health
and his clinches; the Body Snatcher's low hands and his push off - step in - body cross; the Jabber leaving on the last
beat of a round. Hard enough: all three bouts were level going into round 3. Reading him pays, clearly.

**Rounds 2 and 3.** They play differently and better than round 1: health carries over, my stamina bar was cut from 8
to 5 by body punches in bout 2, and the clinch turned from a rest into a weapon and a rescue. The knockdown changed my
plans (bout 2, round 3: I played the clock). The ropes changed one beat, for me, against him; I never felt my own
ropes because I never backed off. The stamina bar is the best read in the game.

**What I could not judge from words:** the pictures, the pace, sound, whether the three moments can be followed on the
screen, and whether the page shows a hand "still up".

- DEPTH 8: order, stamina, the cover that follows the hit, the clinch as trap and rescue. Feet and Long add little.

## TO REACH 10/10 (most important first)
1. SHOW WHERE EACH HAND IS IN EACH MOMENT. After a beat, a strip of the three moments for both men: each hand out,
   high or low. And one line in the help: "a hand that punches is held high in the other moments of the beat".
   (Bout 3, round 1, beat 6.)
2. SCORE THE ROUND BY ITS MARGIN: 10-8 for a round with a knockdown or twice the points, so round 3 is never dead and
   a knockdown is worth more than a jab's edge. (Bout 2 round 3; bout 3 round 3 beat 1.)
3. A KNOCKDOWN ENDS THE BEAT, and a man does not get up healthier than the man who dropped him. (Bout 2 round 3
   beat 6; bout 3 round 3 beat 6; bout 1 round 3 beat 4.)
4. EACH MAN FIGHTS LIKE HIS CARD and opens his own way: the Jabber refuses to come Inside, the Slugger never backs off
   at Long, the Body Snatcher never runs. One beat each that no other man has. (Bout 1 round 1 beats 1 and 3; bout 2
   round 2 beat 1; bout 3 round 1 beat 1.)
5. MAKE LONG A PLACE AND BACKING OFF A CHOICE: 0 of 54 beats backed off, every round's first beat is "walk in". Give
   the man who keeps him at Long something (a jab at Long that scores as a big punch when he is walking in, or his
   step in costing more after a jab landed). (Every beat 1.)
6. THE CLINCH MUST BE SEEN COMING OR COST LESS TO THE MAN IT ROBS: all his clinches came at 2 stamina or less. Say so
   on his card ("he is hanging on") or give back half of what a smothered plan cost. (Bout 2 round 1 beat 3.)
7. EXPLAIN THE GUARD BAR OR CUT IT. (Bout 1 round 1 beat 2.)
8. NO BEAT THAT READS AS A BROKEN MAN: he "steps in" while already Inside, or feints with no punch behind it (bout 2
   round 1 beat 5; bout 3 round 3 beat 5). If the step is a delay, the words say "he waits a moment".
9. NO IDENTICAL BEAT TWICE: he does not repeat, switch for switch, a plan that just failed. (Bout 3 round 1 beats 3-4.)
10. WORDS: "ties you up"; "he backs off, you follow him in"; a counter only when his miss came in an earlier moment,
    or the help says "the same moment counts".
