# Research wanted: orthodox against southpaw in real boxing - what changes, in numbers

You are a boxing researcher. We need SOURCED facts, each with where it comes from and whether it is a measured number, a
coach's teaching or your own memory. Plain words. If a number does not exist, say NOT FOUND; do not estimate.

## Why
Our small boxing game (Ring Switches) has two hands a man: the LEAD hand (jab, lead hook, lead uppercut, body hook) and
the REAR hand (cross, overhand, rear uppercut, body cross). A hand that is held blocks one line: lead hand high stops
straight head punches, lead hand low stops body punches, rear hand high stops hooks and the overhand, rear hand low stops
uppercuts. Every fighter is orthodox today. The owner asked whether stances belong in it. We will add a southpaw
OPPONENT only if the open stance (orthodox against southpaw) really asks different things of a boxer, and we want the
rule to follow real boxing.

## What we want
1. In the open stance, WHICH PUNCHES LAND MORE AND WHICH LESS than between two orthodox boxers? Any counted numbers
   (CompuBox, academic counts of amateur or pro bouts): jab land rate, rear-straight land rate, lead hook, body punches.
2. WHAT STOPS WHAT in the open stance: which hand does a boxer use against the other man's jab, and against his rear
   straight? (Our two guesses to check: the lead hands get in each other's way so the jab is easier to stop; the rear
   straight comes down the middle past the lead hand, so the rear hand has to stop it.)
3. THE FEET: the "lead foot outside" rule. What does the man with his lead foot outside gain, in plain words, and is
   there any count of how often it decides an exchange?
4. THE BODY: which body punches open up or close in the open stance (the liver side)?
5. RESULTS: do southpaws win more often against orthodox boxers than the reverse? Any counted figure, with its sample.
6. One paragraph: if a game could add only ONE rule to make a southpaw opponent feel different, which should it be?
