# Angle 3 - hype around a FIGHT, and how games model hype (club scale)

Research only, S432, 2026-11-11. Web sources beside each claim; all wording is ours. "Anecdotal" = one fighter, one
club, one forum post or a local paper, not a measured sample. Earlier work read first: G89_FIGHT_HYPE.md (top-level PPV
research), G102_FIGHT_HYPE_SEQUENCES.md (fight-week sequence, skimmed), BOXING_MANAGER_COMPARE_S425.md /
BOXING_MANAGER_PLAYTEST_S426.md (our read of Boxing Manager).

## The short answer

At club level a fight's hype is not a TV number. It is **people walking through the door**, and almost all of them come
because they **know someone on the card**. Tickets are sold hand to hand by the fighters and their families, a show
lives or dies on that, and a promoter cancels a card when the fighters' sales are too low two weeks out. What lifts a
bout above "a friend is fighting" is a **local or club rivalry with a team score**, a **rematch after a close or
disputed fight**, and **a competitive match** (fans say they will not pay to watch a prospect beat a journeyman).
Fight week at this level is a poster, a ticket push in the last two weeks, a weigh-in (sometimes public), and social
clips. Hype fades within days of the fight; what lasts is the fighter's **following**, which grows fight by fight from
amateur friends to hundreds and, for a rare few, thousands.

Games: the systems players praise make hype **visible and spendable** (the WMMA "hype" gamble, WWE rivalry levels that
lift match ratings, Football Manager's supporter groups with different patience). The systems players mock are
**canned text** (EA UFC's three social posts, Football Manager's repeating press questions).

## Part A - real fights at club scale

### A1. Who buys the tickets: the fighter's own people

- Small-hall pro and amateur shows have no TV money, so ticket sales are the show's whole income; the fighters sell
  most of them to friends and family. ([Boxing News, did sporting clubs save boxing](https://boxingnewsonline.net/features/did-sporting-clubs-save-boxing/);
  [Warrior Code](https://warriorcode.com/article/the-beauty-and-struggles-of-small-show-boxing), via search summary)
- A community-club guide calls boxers selling to family and friends the main revenue model of community shows.
  ([TidyHQ game day guide](https://tidyhq.com/blog/boxing-game-day-planning-guide-uk))
- A UK promoter's site put the basic ask at roughly **100 tickets** to friends and family per fighter, with money above
  that split between fighter and promoter. ([Frank Warren / Queensberry, via search summary](https://www.frankwarren.com/just-the-ticket-for-warrington/); page now gone, so treat as one promoter's model)
- **Irish numbers (the best breakdown found):** a debutant's contract needed about **€2,750** of sales before the fighter
  earned anything; at **€60** a ticket the bare break-even was **46 tickets** fighting for free, about **67 tickets** to
  earn **€1,250**; above break-even the usual split is **50/50**, and for a strong seller the fighter's share can fall to
  **10%**. A card with eight fighters was **cancelled** about two weeks out because some had sold as few as **3**.
  ([Irish Boxing](https://www.irish-boxing.com/?p=146189))
- A forum estimate puts a non-TV boxer's cut at **5 to 20%** of the tickets they sell (anecdotal).
  ([BoxRec forum](https://boxrec.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=4788871))
- White-collar (charity, first-fight) shows ask each fighter for at least **10 tickets**.
  ([Ultra White Collar Boxing](https://ultrawhitecollarboxing.co.uk/?p=8050))
- Promoters give each fighter a promo code to track who sold what. ([tickts guide, via search summary](https://tickts.co.uk/blog/boxing-mma-show-ticketing); vendor page)

**Debut counts (all anecdotal, self-reported):** Ryan Woolridge **140+** (six tables of family and friends)
([Boxing News](https://boxingnewsonline.net/inside-a-professional-debut)); Mikey Harrison about **300**
([Sheffield Star](https://www.thestar.co.uk/sport/boxing/popular-sheffield-boxer-is-chasing-a-dream-4694097));
Sahil Khan **300+** ([FightCity](https://www.fightcity.uk/news/khan-sold-over-300-tickets-for-his-debut)). Fighters
say the amateur following carries over into the pro debut ([Sheffield Star](https://www.thestar.co.uk/sport/boxing/boxing-sheffield-debutant-helps-boost-world-championship-show-with-his-amazing-support-159617)).

**The rare outlier:** Johnny Fisher (two amateur bouts) and family sold about **2,000** tickets for one London card,
all to people they knew or friends of friends; other reports say **1,400** to save a card and **4,000** for one bout.
([BoxingScene](https://www.boxingscene.com/articles/astonishing-unexplainable-popularity-johnny-fisher);
[Planet Sport](https://planetsport.com/boxing/news/is-johnny-fisher-matchroom-boxing-eddie-hearn-dazn-new-heavyweight-draw);
[The Sweet Science](https://tss.ib.tv/boxing/chalk-up-another-quickie-for-the-romford-bull-wipes-out-alen-babic-in-36-seconds)).
A small-hall promoter said a fighter selling about **1,000** by the fourth or fifth bout is already too big for small
halls. ([Boxing News, stress of small hall](https://www.boxingnewsonline.net/the-stress-of-small-hall-boxing-is-incredible/))

**Amateur club show crowds (club self-reports):** Team Shoe-Box open show, **500+**, sell-out, **15** bouts, visiting
clubs from five towns ([England Boxing](https://www.englandboxing.org/?p=36235)); Eastbourne Christmas show **500 to 600**;
Sevenoaks dinner show **200+**; Gosport dinner sold out, door tickets **£15**; Wisbech dinner **£30**, no-dinner
**£12** (search summary of [Portsmouth News](https://www.portsmouth.co.uk/sport/boxing/gosport-boxing-club-prepare-for-annual-show-1179128),
[Wisbech Standard](https://www.wisbechstandard.co.uk/sport/tickets-still-available-for-wisbech-boxing-show-featuring-two-time-5456734)).
Lydney's first town-hall show since 2006 drew about **300** ([Forest Review](https://www.theforestreview.co.uk/news/sell-out-for-towns-boxers-201375)).

**For a game:** a club show's gate = the sum of each fighter's personal draw + the club's loyal base + the opposing club's
travelling supporters. A fighter's draw is a number that starts at tens (debut) and grows bout by bout.

### A2. What makes one bout sell more than another

1. **A club-vs-club rivalry with a team score.** Liverpool vs Manchester amateur finals: nearly **4,000** at Liverpool
   Stadium in 1973; a trophy for the area with most winners ran from **1957** (Liverpool won 9-1 that night). The area
   split guaranteed the two cities met every year and the trophy gave pride a prize.
   ([Boxing News](https://boxingnewsonline.net/liverpool-vs-manchester-a-great-amateur-boxing-rivalry/))
   Today's form: Wallop BC vs One Nation ABC, **15** bouts (11 scored), a **captain's bout** as the headline, team
   trophy, **250+** crowd ([Redditch Standard](https://redditchstandard.co.uk/sport/boxing-wallop-boxing-club-win-trophy-after-beating-one-nation-abc-in-club-versus-club-home-show/));
   Cambridge Town v Gown, run **30+ years**, **12** bouts, sold out, scored as a team (4-3)
   ([Varsity](https://varsity.co.uk/sport/29145)).
2. **A local derby between two named fighters.** Crawley's Massey vs Horsham's Bailey sold out a social club and
   a rematch was called for at once ([Sussex Express, via search summary](https://www.sussexexpress.co.uk/sport/other-sport/massey-the-star-of-crawley-boxing-clubs-big-show-3476593));
   Sheffield v Leeds was previewed as exactly the bout small-hall fans want ([Sheffield Star](https://www.thestar.co.uk/sport/boxing/sheffield-v-leeds-battle-who-wins-4685543)).
3. **A rematch after a disputed result.** Crocker vs Donovan 1 ended in a disqualification; the rematch moved to a
   football stadium and had **15,000+** sold before the night, prices **£46 to £68**.
   ([Irish News](https://www.irishnews.com/sport/boxing/lewis-crocker-vs-paddy-donovan-tickets-everything-you-need-to-know-F4NVIVLOTBAXTKGHRGMAWPOQ3E/);
   [Boxing News](https://boxingnewsonline.net/news/windsor-park-eyed-for-crocker-donovan-rematch-after-controversial-first-bout/)).
   Pro level, but the pattern (close or unfair first fight -> the town wants the second) is the same at a club show.
4. **A competitive match, not a mismatch.** Small-hall promoters argue fans will not pay to see prospects beat the same
   journeymen; one proposes **50/50 or 60/40** fights from about **4-0**, instead of waiting until 10-0; journeyman fees
   rose **40-50%** after the pandemic. ([Boxing News](https://boxingnewsonline.net/news/competitive-matchmaking-is-the-key-to-saving-small-hall-boxing/))
   Another promoter's rule of thumb: step up at about **10-0 or 11-0**. ([Boxing News](https://www.boxingnewsonline.net/the-stress-of-small-hall-boxing-is-incredible/))
5. **Hometown plus an outside identity.** Josh Warrington boxed in Leeds once in the first three pro years, then the
   following grew with Leeds United fans; a promoter claimed sales five times a normal fighter's at that stage.
   ([YEP](https://www.yorkshireeveningpost.co.uk/sport/other-sport/josh-warrington-growing-loyal-fanbase-memorable-journey-has-made-me-very-proud-642395);
   [Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/2431046-boxing-eddie-hearn-encourages-football-fan-culture-but-warning-signs-are-there), promoter claim)
6. **A title alone does not fill a hall.** The 2018 national amateur finals at York Hall had a sparse crowd and three
   media present, though streamed. ([PressReader / Boxing News 2018](https://www.pressreader.com/uk/boxing-news/20180426/281556586434537))
   A national final with no local pull draws less than a club derby with one.
7. **The night out, not only the fight.** Sioux Falls Boxing Classic (pro, dinner gala, charity) sold out four times
   since 2022 and is moving to a bigger hall; organisers credit the atmosphere and food, not a fighter.
   ([Dakota News Now](https://www.dakotanewsnow.com/2026/11/05/sioux-falls-boxing-classic-moving-convention-center-after-4-straight-sellouts/))
   Club dinner shows (tables, three courses) work the same way.

**Risk that kills a show:** late pull-outs. A Newark card planned with **7** bouts ran with **3**; another lost four
opponents in a day ([Boxing News](https://www.boxingnewsonline.net/the-stress-of-small-hall-boxing-is-incredible/)).
Tickets sold on a bout that then falls through are a real cost to the club's name.

### A3. Fight week at club scale (the build-up)

- **Announcement and poster** start the selling; at club level the poster names the club, the date and the local
  fighters (G89: the two-name poster is the one marketing act with a measured lift at top level).
- **Sales come late.** Vendor data: sports events sell about **31%** of tickets in the final week (all events **22%**)
  ([RSVPify](https://rsvpify.com/event-ticketing-trends-2026/)); a rule of thumb has **20-40%** sold 60 days out and
  **60-80%** by 14 days out ([Ticket Fairy](https://www.ticketfairy.com/tools/ticket-sales-pacing)). Both are vendor
  blogs. The Irish card was cut at about **two weeks** out, which fits: that is when a promoter reads the curve.
- **Weigh-in.** Usually the day before. Big local names hold them in public places: hundreds at a Leeds shopping centre,
  about **500** at Leeds City Museum, a Dundee shopping centre for a Commonwealth title
  ([YEP](https://www.yorkshireeveningpost.co.uk/sport/boxing/heros-welcome-for-josh-warrington-at-trinity-leeds-warm-up-to-elland-road-title-fight-644168);
  [STV](https://prod.news.stv.tv/v/paul-kean-weighs-in-ahead-of-commonwealth-title-clash)). Small-show weigh-ins are
  mostly closed; no source on a public club-show weigh-in.
- **Face-off and social clips.** The face-off clip is the shareable item (a top-level second face-off clip drew close
  to **3 million** views within hours); stunts can backfire with fans who wanted a normal face-off.
  ([Bloody Elbow](https://bloodyelbow.com/2025/09/23/jake-paul-kisses-gervonta-davis-during-face-off-video-that-shocks-fans-ahead-of-grudge-fight)).
  No source gives post cadence or follower gains for a local fighter.
- **Coach's part (seen in the sources, small):** at club shows the coach builds the card and picks the opponents
  (the matchmaking that decides whether a bout is a derby, a 50/50 or a mismatch), speaks to the local paper (most
  quotes in the club-show reports come from the coach), and names the captain for a club-vs-club night.

### A4. How long heat lasts, and what turns into lasting fans

- **Spikes fade in days.** A UFC fighter's demand turned down within about **3 days** of a loss; demand held for a
  winner and for a fighter who stayed visible between fights; inactivity drags even a champion down.
  ([Parrot Analytics](https://www.parrotanalytics.com/insights/ufc-324-demand-volatility-and-the-new-talent-leverage-playbook/))
- **Follower gains are lumpy.** One fight card: average gain about **212,000**, median about **3,950** (a few stars take
  almost all of it) ([Medium analysis, via search summary](https://medium.com/@wesley_russell/the-conor-mcgregor-effect-how-being-on-a-conor-mcgregor-card-impacts-a-fighters-following-ab5b5d59f01b), fan analysis).
  A loser with a show can gain more than the winner: **118K** vs **53K** in one case
  ([EssentiallySports](https://www.essentiallysports.com/ufc-mma-news-paddy-pimblett-beats-justin-gaethje-outside-octagon-with-one-twenty-one-thousand-gain-after-ufc-324/)).
  A knockout of a star: **520,000** new followers ([BJPenn / Nielsen](https://bjpenn.com/mma-news/ufc/dustin-poirier-sees-large-boost-in-social-media-following-after-defeating-conor-mcgregor)).
- **Habit is what lasts.** Sports economics treats attendance as partly habit: fans who come keep coming
  ([Lancaster, recession and football](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/149556/1/Recession_and_Football_Final_Nov2020.pdf)).
  A new attraction's lift lasts years in some sports (about **8** seasons baseball, **9** basketball, none in US
  football) ([Coates & Humphreys 2005](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:coecpo:v:23:y:2005:i:3:p:436-455)), and
  whether it stays depends on how loyal the newly drawn fans are ([Tinbergen paper](https://papers.tinbergen.nl/24010.pdf)).
- **No club-level decay number exists.** Our old Rivalries.gd used 1 heat point a week (G89); nothing found to confirm
  or replace it. The nearest real shape: a fight's buzz is gone within a week or two; the fighter's following ratchets
  up after a good night (a win, a war, a knockout) and slips slowly with inactivity or a bad loss.

**Model in plain words:** two numbers, not one. **Bout heat** (per fight; rises with rivalry, rematch, derby, close odds,
a good weigh-in; spent on the night, gone within about two weeks). **Following** (per fighter and per club; the people
who buy tickets; grows by a share of each good night, shrinks slowly when idle). Heat sells this show; following sells
every show.

**What players reward, across these:**
1. Hype is **a gamble you choose** (WMMA hype; MyGM booking a rival brand's star) and it can **blow up** on a loss.
2. Hype **does something you can see**: a bigger venue unlocks, a match rating rises, a fan group cheers, a morale
   arrow moves at once.
3. A rivalry is **an object that grows in levels** and pays every time the pair meet, then ends.
4. Fans come in **kinds with different patience** (FM's six groups).

**What players punish:** canned text that repeats (FM questions, EA posts); a meter that changes nothing in the
building or the walkout; grind to keep fame topped up (Punch Club); choices whose effect is invisible (MMA Manager plans).

## What G89 is missing for a club-scale game

G89 is good on top-level demand (PPV, gate, title and star coefficients, presser incidents, gym wars) and its build
order. For one club with four fighters at amateur to first pro fight, it lacks:

1. **The fighter as ticket seller.** G89 has no per-fighter ticket draw. At club level that is the economy: tens at a
   debut, about 100 to 300 for a decent local, rare thousands; ticket minimums (10 white-collar, ~46-67 Irish debut,
   ~100 one UK promoter); 50/50 above break-even.
2. **Following vs heat.** G89 has one HYPE per bout plus pair heat. It lacks a lasting per-fighter (and per-club)
   following that a good night grows and idleness drains, which is what turns one show's buzz into the next show's gate.
3. **Club vs club with a team score and a captain's bout.** G89 found no gym-war demand data at top level; at amateur
   level there is: the team trophy format (Liverpool v Manchester since 1957, crowds near 4,000; today 250-500 club
   nights, Town v Gown 30+ years). This is the club-scale "gym heat" G89 wanted, and it is a real event format.
4. **The cancel risk and the two-week read.** Sales come late (about a third in the final week); a promoter reads the
   curve about two weeks out and can pull the show. Late pull-outs (7 bouts down to 3) hurt the club's name.
5. **Competitive matchmaking sells, mismatches do not.** G89's undefeated record as a multiplier is a star-level idea;
   at club level fans pay for 50/50s and derbies, and the step-up timing (from about 4-0 to 10-0) is a real choice.
6. **Titles are weak at this level.** A national amateur final can be near empty; a derby fills a room. G89's "put a
   title on it" does not hold for a club.
7. **The night as a product.** Dinner shows, tables, charity, door price vs dinner price (£12-15 door, £30 dinner).
8. **Decay numbers.** G89 gives none; this file gives the shape (days for buzz, slow drain for following) but no
   club-level figure exists.
9. **What games teach.** G89 compared EA UFC, Fight Night, TEW, FM, Punch Club. Missing: WMMA's chosen hype gamble,
   MyGM's rivalry levels, FM's supporter kinds, Boxing Manager's following-unlocks-venue ladder, and the clear
   player verdict that canned press text is worse than none.
