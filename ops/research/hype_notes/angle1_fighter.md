# Hype angle 1 - how a FIGHTER becomes a draw (amateur clubs and small-hall pro boxing)

Research for HYPE1 (S432). Web sources, written in our own words, URL beside each claim. Where a number is one
person's account rather than a count, it says "anecdotal". UK first, US second, white-collar only as contrast.

## 1. The money underneath: why a small show needs a fighter's own crowd

- Small-hall shows have no TV money, so the gate is nearly all the income; a fighter who cannot bring money in can be
  left off the card, talent or not. https://www.irish-boxing.com/?p=146189 and
  https://ultrawhitecollarboxing.co.uk/tickets-boxing-events/
- The usual rule on the UK small-hall circuit: a novice pro is expected to move roughly 100 tickets, mostly to friends and
  family (promoter's own blog, Queensberry/Frank Warren). Quoted via search of
  https://queensberry.co.uk/blogs/frank-warren-digest/just-the-ticket and https://www.frankwarren.com/just-the-ticket-for-warrington/
- A white-collar organiser says a starting pro may need 100+ tickets to get on a bill, and may have to do it 4-5 times a
  year. https://ultrawhitecollarboxing.co.uk/tickets-boxing-events/
- Worked example from Irish boxing (illustrative numbers, the writer says they vary by show): a debutant owes the
  promoter about EUR 2,750 (show costs + opponent fee) before earning; at EUR 60 a ticket that is 46 tickets just to fight
  for nothing, 67 tickets to also earn a EUR 1,250 purse. Above the target the fighter usually splits ticket money 50-50
  with the promoter (as low as 10% for big sellers - unclear in which direction; read as "terms change for big sellers").
  From the EUR 1,250: manager 15%, trainer 10%, cutman 5%, leaving about EUR 875 after a 6-8 week camp.
  https://www.irish-boxing.com/?p=146189
- UK early-career take-home for a four-rounder is put at about GBP 1,500-1,800; one Kent debutant sold just over half
  the allocation and could pay everyone except the boxer (explainer site, medium reliability).
  https://www.sportsboom.com/boxing/how-much-do-boxers-make/
- Since the pandemic, foreign opponents cost 40-50% more (promoter Prior, Boxing News), so home fighters must sell more
  tickets to cover them. https://www.boxingnewsonline.net/competitive-matchmaking-is-the-key-to-saving-small-hall-boxing/
- US equivalent at club level: Matchroom hoped young Philadelphia fighters on TV cards would sell "300 or 400 tickets
  each" (search summary of a Ring/Inquirer report; anecdotal target). Philadelphia's 2300 Arena seats about 1,500 for
  boxing; a local headliner sold it out plus ~200 standing tickets. https://www.boxingscene.com/rosados-philly-special-show-2300-arena-officially-sold-out--136083
  Club-show prices in Philadelphia (Broadway Boxing): $60 general, $100-150 ringside.
  https://www.ringmagazine.com/news/hall-of-famer-lou-di-bella-is-bringing-broadway-boxing-to-broad-street-this-thursday-in-philly-2ZmAu7AGG2ZmgRpRCsMDgV

Contrast, white collar: a participant commits to only 10 tickets to friends and family after 8 weeks of free training;
the tickets pay for the night. https://ultrawhitecollarboxing.co.uk/tickets-boxing-events/

## 2. Amateur level: the club show and the local crowd

- Amateur club "home shows" in England regularly sell out at 200-500 people: Southport Star ABC over 400
  (https://www.southportvisiter.co.uk/news/southport-west-lancs/southport-boxing-clubs-sell-out-11203681), Team
  Shoe-Box open show 500+ (England Boxing, Nov 2022, https://www.englandboxing.org/?p=36235), an Islington dinner show
  500 for 10 bouts (https://www.camdennewjournal.co.uk/article/dinner-punch-wows-boxing-fans), Lions Den ABC Newark 200+
  (https://newarkadvertiser.co.uk/sport/home-show-sees-ten-winners-9290103).
- Ticket prices at club shows are low: GBP 12-20 adults, GBP 5-10 children (Boston ABC, Synwell, Birtley; search summary
  of https://www.lincolnshireworld.com/sport/boston-abc-boxers-fighting-fit-ahead-their-home-show-724285 and
  https://www.chantrycentre.org/event-details/synwell-amateur-boxing-club-show-2). A London Christmas show charged GBP 50
  with a drinks reception (https://theringboxingclub.com/an-evening-of-championship-boxing).
- How the club fills the hall: most advance tickets are sold BY THE BOXERS, each asked to move about 20-40 to family and
  friends, door sales making up the rest (club-software guide, practice not a rule; England Boxing sets no allocation
  rule that I found). https://tidyhq.com/blog/boxing-game-day-planning-guide-uk
- Why clubs run them: a club director says the home show gives the club and its boxers exposure
  (https://redditchstandard.co.uk/sport/boxing-wallop-boxing-club-to-host-first-home-show-this-weekend/); shows are
  pitched as "come and support local boxers in front of a home crowd"
  (https://www.sussexexpress.co.uk/sport/boxing/horsham-boxers-are-keen-to-put-on-a-show-4011281). Local papers write
  up every home show with names and results - that is the amateur's "press".
- US amateur: Golden Gloves nights fill hotel ballrooms and halls; the loudest cheers go to home-state boxers and each
  gym's supporters cheer their own (Vermont, Kansas City, Wisconsin reports).
  https://www.sevendaysvt.com/arts-culture/vermont-boxers-take-to-the-tournaments-2296288/ ,
  https://kshb.com/sports/kc-golden-gloves-boxing-tournament-showcases-local-talent , https://madison365.com/wisconsin-golden-gloves-knockout/
- A decorated amateur carries the crowd into the pros: Sahil Khan (Dudley; Merridale ABC; 70 bouts, 7 losses, six-time
  national champion) had 300+ fans buy tickets for the pro debut on a Matchroom card in Birmingham.
  https://www.fightcity.uk/news/khan-sold-over-300-tickets-for-his-debut

So: 30-70 = a weak seller, ~100 = the expected minimum, 150-300 = a good local draw, 300+ = a strong one (usually a
known amateur). A seller of "a thousand tickets" by fight 4 or 5 is rare and promoters will not risk that fighter (Carl Greaves,
promoter, Boxing News 2021). https://www.boxingnewsonline.net/the-stress-of-small-hall-boxing-is-incredible/

Who sells: the fighter and family, face to face. One boxer says they love the home atmosphere but hates selling the tickets
(search summary, https://www.thestar.co.uk/sport/boxing/popular-sheffield-boxer-is-chasing-a-dream-4694097). Trainer
Anthony Hull (D&A gym) says the old way was posters in takeaways, phone calls and meeting people - Hull's unlicensed shows
drew about 3,000 - and that social media posts and padwork clips bring viewers, not buyers; Hull wants sponsors to back a
fighter only in return for selling about 20 tickets. https://www.fightcity.uk/news/small-hall-boxings-been-cursed-by-social-media-hull
A British boxer writing on social media makes the same point: early sales depend on who your friends and family are.
https://www.boxingnews24.com/2025/08/the-rise-of-the-content-king-how-fighters-like-shakur-stevenson-are-using-social-media-to-earn-millions/
Ticket platforms give each fighter, gym and sponsor its own promo code so the promoter can count who sold what, and
local papers and radio readily cover a show when local fighters are on it. https://tickts.co.uk/blog/boxing-mma-show-ticketing

## 4. How a promoter builds a prospect (the record, the streak, the step-up)

- The traditional build: lower-level opponents who test but do not threaten the unbeaten record, gradually harder, so the
  prospect meets different styles before title level (Aarron Morgan, Boxing News 2018). Its costs: fans get bored and
  suspect a hidden weakness; the fighter can get complacent. Decorated amateurs now skip steps - Joe Joyce won the
  Commonwealth title in fight 4, Josh Kelly fought for it in fight 7.
  https://boxingnewsonline.net/news/how-to-build-a-prospect/
- The journeyman's role, from a matchmaker (Mickey Helliet): 40-36 points wins build confidence and an unbeaten record,
  which TV wants (Helliet cites 10-0); most fighters peak in year 3-4 of a pro career.
  https://www.boxingnewsonline.net/in-defence-of-40-36-why-journeymen-are-vital-to-the-development-of-prospects/
  Woolridge's debut opponent had lost 15 of 16 and was fighting again two weeks later.
  https://boxingnewsonline.net/inside-a-professional-debut
- The ticket-seller is matched most carefully: a seller "who has to remain unbeaten to keep selling" is a named
  matchmaking category (Boxing News opinion). https://boxingnewsonline.net/opinion/the-question-that-all-promoters-should-be-asking-themselves-now/
  A Boxing News column says sellers' records are built on journeymen paid out of those same ticket sales, and their
  managers turn down opponents with winning records unless a title is on the line.
  https://boxingnewsonline.net/opinion/a-loss-in-boxing-isnt-all-doom-and-gloom/
  Meanwhile, talented non-sellers sit without fights and end up in the away corner.
  https://boxingnewsonline.net/opinion/advice-to-young-pros-in-this-brutal-business
- The shift since 2021-22: promoters say fans no longer pay to watch prospects beat the same journeymen; they push 50/50
  or 60/40 fights at 4-0 instead of 10-0, and small-hall area titles (e.g. seven on one UK weekend) as the step.
  https://www.boxingnewsonline.net/competitive-matchmaking-is-the-key-to-saving-small-hall-boxing/
- Timeline of a real build (Josh Warrington, Leeds; Wikipedia record): debut Oct 2009 (4 rounds, Huddersfield leisure
  centre, ~70 friends) -> English title in fight 13 (Nov 2012, 3 years) -> Commonwealth in fight 16 (Nov 2013) ->
  British title at Leeds arena in fight 18 (May 2014, 4.5 years). By 2015 Warrington sold just under 10,000 tickets for a world
  title eliminator, nearly double the previous hometown fight; Hearn said that was about five times what Brook, Froch or
  Joshua sold at the same stage. Crowd style: football terraces - songs, Leeds United fans.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Warrington ,
  https://bleacherreport.com/articles/2431046-boxing-eddie-hearn-encourages-football-fan-culture-but-warning-signs-are-there
- Activity: a common target for a building prospect is 3-4 fights a year (Kalle Sauerland planned 3 for Josh Kelly;
  Woolridge said four a year as a minimum). https://ringmagazine.com/en/news/josh-kelly-set-for-three-quick-fire-outings-before-end-of-the-year ,
  https://www.expressandstar.com/sport/boxing/ryan-rhino-woolridge-wont-run-before-he-can-walk-5927867
- The "next big thing" through social media (US): Ryan Garcia went from about 857,000 Instagram followers in May 2018 to
  about 3.7 million in 2019, when Golden Boy re-signed Garcia on what it called one of the richest prospect deals ever; one
  writer suspected bought followers (unconfirmed). https://www.boxingscene.com/articles/golden-boys-garcia-velez-doubleheader-averaged-281k-viewers ,
  https://medium.com/@NateSwanner/is-ryan-garcia-really-instagram-famous-6ad5838c37fc
  Turning likes into seats is the hard part (industry commentary). https://stormy.ai/blog/influencer-events-boxing-leagues-future-brand-affinity

## 5. What makes a crowd follow a fighter

- Hometown and community above all. Johnny Fisher (Romford, "Romford Bull"): the father, Big John, sold about 3,500 tickets by
  hand from the family front room and a chip shop, notebook and T-shirts, for a Copper Box show expecting ~7,000; Fisher
  calls it a community thing, not just one person winning. https://boxingnewsonline.net/johnny-fisher-is-aiming-for-the-top-table/
  Fisher sold 4,000+ for the Babic fight (July 2024, 36-second KO, 12-0) (Steve Bunce, The Independent, via search;
  https://tss.ib.tv/boxing/featured-boxing-articles/81304-chalk-up-another-quickie-for-the-romford-bull-wipes-out-alen-babic-in-36-seconds).
- Personality and openness: Fisher and Dave Allen both draw through self-mocking humour and showing daily life; Fisher
  ran a produced online show with the father and the family home; Allen talks openly about addiction, mental health and
  money. ~8,000 at their rematch, mostly for Fisher. https://www.ringmagazine.com/news/johnny-fisher-dave-allen-ii-showed-magic-of-domestic-level-clashes-2fmJ89PGtRR9yOhnk4SAkV
- Knockouts: Jaron Ennis (Philadelphia, 31-0, 28 KOs) - the father, Bozy Ennis, says fans love knockouts; Ennis's last hometown fight
  at 2300 Arena (2018) drew 1,300 standing room; for the first big Philadelphia arena date Ennis presold 4,500 against a
  7,000 goal - Hearn: selling the first fight is the hard part.
  https://inquirer.com/sports/jaron-ennis-cody-crowley-welterweight-title-fight-20240513.html
- What research says (TV, not live gate): across 210 HBO/Showtime broadcasts 2006-2018, viewers preferred domestic
  boxers, rematches and heavier divisions, and a close (uncertain) fight did NOT raise viewing - names beat closeness.
  https://cora.ucc.ie/handle/10468/10747 . A tennis-fan study found that reputation, behaviour, personality and
  charity work go together in why fans attach to an individual player, and matter more the longer someone is a fan.
  https://digscholarship.unco.edu/dissertations/406 . No combat-sport fan study turned up.
- Rivalry with a story: Fisher-Allen II sold on a disputed first decision 147 days earlier.
  https://www.ringmagazine.com/news/johnny-fisher-dave-allen-ii-showed-magic-of-domestic-level-clashes-2fmJ89PGtRR9yOhnk4SAkV

## 6. How heat fades (and how fast)

No study measures a boxer's ticket draw decaying week by week. What exists:
- Inactivity: ESPN calls it the great momentum killer and dropped fighters from its rankings for it; Keith Thurman: long
  gaps bring questions and suspicion instead of affection. https://africa.espn.com/boxing/story/_/id/32252125/where-paging-gennadiy-golovkin-andy-ruiz-keith-thurman-gary-russell-jr ,
  https://www.boxing247.com/boxing-news/keith-thurman-admits-inactivity-breeds-suspicion-ahead-of-fundora-fight/303049
  Gary Russell Jr: after nearly two years out, fans had given up on a return (opinion piece).
  https://www.essentiallysports.com/boxing-news-nobody-noticed-these-boxers-who-didnt-fight-in-twenty-twenty-four
  Chris Kongo: about 14 months without a win after a loss, called it a hard year.
  https://southlondon.co.uk/sport/boxing/it-was-hard-for-me-bermondsey-fighter-reflects-on-year-out-of-the-ring-after-triumphant-return-on-easter-sunday/
  Counter-case: a star with a big online following can come back stronger - Ryan Garcia's ticket sales nearly
  quadrupled after a 15-month layoff (the earlier fight was under COVID limits: 4,218 tickets -> 12,314).
  https://www.boxingscene.com/articles/garcia-tagoe-april-9-dazn-show-generates-1307735-live-gate-from-12314-tickets-sold
- Online attention after an event: Wikipedia studies model a rise before a planned event and a decay after it, and in
  football the result changes the after-event curve; no day-count given in the abstract.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.10900 . Long-run, athletes stay talked about for 15-30 years vs 5-10 for songs/films.
  https://plus.maths.org/how-do-we-remember-and-forget
- The dull win: promoters say fans won't keep paying to watch a prospect beat the same journeymen - boredom erodes the
  base even while the record stays perfect. https://www.boxingnewsonline.net/competitive-matchmaking-is-the-key-to-saving-small-hall-boxing/
- The pull-out: Carl Greaves' Newark show planned 7 bouts and ran 3 after injury/medical withdrawals; Greaves paid one
  fighter's fee out of pocket for a show that was then cancelled. https://www.boxingnewsonline.net/the-stress-of-small-hall-boxing-is-incredible/
  A Chester boxer (Economides) refunded their own fans when the opponent pulled out, judging the substitute show poor value.
  https://www.chesterchronicle.co.uk/sport/other-sport/more-frustration-chester-boxer-paul-9344891
  A promoter whose sold-out charity show was axed two days out had to refund hundreds of tickets.
  https://www.basildonstandard.co.uk/news/17525381.charity-boxing-axed-with-just-two-days-to-go/
- The loss - softer than expected for a local draw:
  - Frank Buglioni lost a 12-0 record (stopped in 6, April 2014) and still sold 400+ tickets for a Wednesday-night
    York Hall comeback, and said the pressure was off. https://boxingnewsonline.net/frank-buglioni-i-have-learned-lessons-i-will-come-back-stronger-and-i-feel-i-am-ready-for-rocky-fielding
  - Callum Simpson (Barnsley; Barnsley Star ABC) lost the titles to Troy Williamson and personally sold 1,500+ for the
    rematch; the town's fans stayed loyal. https://www.thestar.co.uk/your-world/callum-simpsons-supporters-give-him-lift-before-rematch-8837419
  - Josh Warrington: the Lara rematch at Headingley (2021) had ~20,000, months after the first career loss to Lara.
    https://boxingnewsonline.net/news/when-everything-changed-for-josh-warrington/
  - The hard version: Johnny Fisher, built fast on a big following, was stopped in round 5 by Dave Allen (May 2025, first
    pro loss); Hearn said Fisher had no experience and planned a slow rebuild with no main events for a while; the
    writer doubted all those fans would pay again. https://ringmagazine.com/en/news/eddie-hearn-laments-johnny-fisher-s-inexperience-wants-dave-allen-to-finally-fulfil-his-potential
  So: a hometown base forgives a loss (it is about belonging); the neutral "next big thing" heat does not - the hype tag
  goes, the community stays.

## 7. Risks of hype

- Pressure: Conor Benn said fear of losing weighs on young shoulders; Delicious Orie says the pressure is always there;
  Buglioni felt relief after losing the 0. https://ringmagazine.com/en/news/delicious-orie-i-always-feel-pressure-but-i-m-glad-to-be-up-and-running ,
  https://boxingnewsonline.net/frank-buglioni-i-have-learned-lessons-i-will-come-back-stronger-and-i-feel-i-am-ready-for-rocky-fielding
- After the loss: Koen Mazoudier describes depression that began after the first pro defeat; Skye Nicolson says that
  loss hit harder than any setback before. https://boxingnewsonline.net/koen-mazoudier-opens-up-on-the-struggles-of-defeat-and-modern-boxings-mentality ,
  https://boxingnewsonline.net/news/skye-nicolson-reflects-on-loss-and-prepares-for-redemption/
- Moved too fast because the crowd is there: Fisher (headlining = selling all the tickets, heavy pressure, per Hearn).
- Protected too long: a padded record leaves a fighter exposed the first time a real opponent arrives (Boxing News,
  advice to young pros). https://boxingnewsonline.net/opinion/advice-to-young-pros-in-this-brutal-business
- Crowd trouble: football-style crowds bring police worries (Warrington, Bleacher Report).
- Burden: selling tickets takes time and energy from training; trainer Hull says fighters should not carry it all.
  https://www.fightcity.uk/news/small-hall-boxings-been-cursed-by-social-media-hull

## 8. Where the COACH appears in this angle (pointers for the coach angle)
- The coach/trainer takes ~10% of the purse (Irish example) and is the one who first spots and vouches for the boxer
  ("made for the pro game", Woolridge's coach of 6 years). https://www.irish-boxing.com/?p=146189 , https://boxingnewsonline.net/inside-a-professional-debut
- A gym head (Hull) sets the ticket culture: face-to-face selling, sponsors tied to ticket targets.
- Family can be the promoter: Fisher's father ran ticket sales and the online show.
- The trainer and manager now check opponents on BoxRec/YouTube and refuse bad matches - the coach decides how much
  risk the hype can take. https://www.boxingnewsonline.net/the-stress-of-small-hall-boxing-is-incredible/

## 9. Numbers worth carrying into the design (plain summary)
- Amateur club show: 200-500 seats, GBP 12-20 a ticket, each boxer sells 20-40. White collar: 10 each.
- Pro debut (small hall): ~100 tickets expected; 30-70 weak, 150-300 good, 300+ strong (often a known amateur).
- Fighter must sell ~45-70 tickets just to break even on a debut (EUR 60 tickets, EUR 2,750 costs); above target, ticket
  money is split about 50-50 with the promoter.
- Build: 3-4 fights a year; area/English title around fight 10-13 (Warrington 13), British around 15-20 (Warrington 18),
  3-5 years. Promoters now push a real test at ~4-0 rather than 10-0.
- Growth of a real draw: ~70 (debut) -> ~5,000 (hometown arena) -> ~10,000 (world eliminator) over 6 years (Warrington).
  Fisher: thousands within ~12 fights, mostly through family and community.
- Heat fade: no measured rate. Reported pattern: a year without a fight = doubt and lost rankings; two years = fans give
  up. A loss costs the hype tag but a hometown base mostly stays (400+ after Buglioni's loss, 1,500+ after Simpson's).
- Pull-outs cost the show (7 bouts -> 3) and can mean refunds paid by the fighter.
