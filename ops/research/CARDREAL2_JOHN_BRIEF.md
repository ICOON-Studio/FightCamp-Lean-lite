## What to deliver (plain words, tables welcome; every source named)
1. JUDO against a striker and against a wrestler. For each: who is where before and after (each fighter separately),
   about how long, what the OTHER fighter can do meanwhile, how it usually ends.
   - the grip fight with no jacket (collar tie, underhook, overhook, body lock): what a judoka can still throw from;
   - a hip throw or a foot sweep that lands: where does the THROWER end up (standing over, in side control, in a
     scramble) and where the thrown fighter; what follows in the next two seconds;
   - a throw that fails: giving the back, being countered, ending underneath;
   - the pin under MMA rules (it scores nothing by itself: what it is worth).
2. WRESTLING against a striker and against a jiu-jitsu fighter.
   - the shot from distance and the takedown from the clinch: the level change, the sprawl, the front headlock that
     follows a stuffed shot, the guillotine risk, where both end up in each branch;
   - the takedown that lands: in guard, in half guard, past the guard - how often each (your Fightnomics figures);
   - riding and the turtle: the bottom fighter on the knees, what each can do, how the bottom fighter stands up
     (the wrestler's stand-up against the jiu-jitsu fighter's guard recovery);
   - mat returns: the fighter who gets up and is put straight back down.
3. SAMBO: the throw into a leg lock, and leg locks in general under MMA rules.
   - the entries (from a caught kick, from a failed throw, from the bottom, the rolling entry you named for Imanari);
   - the leg entanglement itself: BOTH fighters are seated or on the back - who can strike, who can stand, who is
     ahead; how the defender gets out (and what it costs); how often leg locks finish in MMA (numbers, by type);
   - which leg locks are legal where (heel hooks, knee reaping: IBJJF, ADCC, sport sambo, combat sambo, unified MMA).
4. KARATE (the in-and-out point striker) against a boxer and against a grappler.
   - the blitz from long range and the exit: where each fighter is before and after, and what a boxer does to a
     fighter who leaves in a straight line; what happens when the grappler catches the kick or times the entry;
   - the bladed stance: what it gives and what it exposes (the leg kick, the takedown);
   - real bouts (Machida, Thompson, Wonderboy-style fighters): what they show, with sources.
5. GETTING UP AFTER A THROW OR A KNOCKDOWN, across the arts: every way a downed fighter gets back to standing, who is
   attacking meanwhile, and under which rules the standing fighter must wait.
6. NUMBERS where they exist (and NOT FOUND where they do not): throw and takedown success by type; how often a stuffed
   shot ends in a front headlock or a guillotine; leg lock finish rates; how often a thrown fighter is back on the feet
   within a few seconds; time in the turtle.
