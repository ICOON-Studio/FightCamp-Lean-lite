# TASK J-COMBOENERGY1 - JOHN - what combinations cost a boxer in energy, and how tiredness changes them (numbers, sources)

Kind: research
Model: John (Grok research seat). Discipline: boxing (demo). Assigned 2026-10-29 (S302).

## Why
We are adding trainable combinations to a women's amateur boxing game (3 x 3 min rounds, 1 min breaks). The operator:
"combos should also be associated with energy (stamina) in the fight". Our first numbers are ESTIMATES; we need real
ones. Say clearly when a number is measured (cite it), derived, or an estimate. Read the papers, not summaries: a
summary of Piorkowski 2011 claimed "125 ms between punches" and the paper itself says ~0.35 s contact to contact.

## Questions
1. ENERGY COST: oxygen uptake, heart rate or blood lactate for combinations versus single punches (bag, pad or
   sparring studies; e.g. Davis et al., Smith 2006, Arseneau, Hukkanen & Hakkinen, Kravitz, Bruzas). Per punch or per
   minute, by combination length if anyone measured it.
2. OUTPUT ACROSS ROUNDS: punches thrown and landed per round in amateur bouts (women if possible), round 1 vs 2 vs 3;
   does combination share or combination length fall in later rounds (e.g. Davis 2013/2018, El-Ashker, Thomson)?
3. TIREDNESS AND TECHNIQUE: how fatigue changes punch speed, force, accuracy and the time between punches in a
   combination (fatigue protocol studies). Percent changes, please.
4. RECOVERY: how much heart rate / lactate recovers in the 60 s break between amateur rounds, and how conditioning
   (VO2max, training level) changes it.
5. Translate into a game rule in plain words: e.g. "a fighter at X% of her energy throws combinations Y% shorter and
   Z% slower"; give your best numbers for a 100-point energy tank over 3 rounds.
