# Skills layer review (S404), coach's view

Read: CRITIC_PACK, CRITIC_ACCESS, docs/mockups/s403/profile.png. Not read: engine code (I do not know exactly how skills would feed the exchange dice).

## 1. Score of the draft: 5 / 10
- The vocabulary is mostly real. A coach does say "his takedown defence is good, his guard is poor". Nothing in the list is nonsense.
- But it is two lists glued together: 7 striking skills and 10 grappling skills, one level of detail each. A boxer reads 6, an MMA fighter 17. Seventeen bars is a spreadsheet, not a glance (the mockup already hides 10 of them in a text box).
- Several entries are the same thing counted twice or are not trainable on their own: Accuracy (move level + Speed + Fight IQ), Control vs Passing, Submission defence vs Escapes, Scrambling (mostly Explosiveness + Fight IQ + the other grappling skills). Kick defence only exists in 3 of the sports.

## 2. Skill by skill
Use the rule: a skill earns a bar only if (a) it needs its own kind of training and (b) it changes a decision the player makes in the game plan, the week or the corner. Best hook for (b): the game already has ranges (outside, long, mid, inside, clinch, ropes) and plan lines like "At long range -> One-two". Each skill should say which range or situation it wins.

| Skill | Verdict | Sports | What it changes in a fight | Trained by | Decision for the player |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | MERGE into Timing (rest lives in move levels) | box, KB, MT, MMA | Whether a thrown shot lands. But a sharp jab already lands more; this double counts the move level | Pads, mitts | Almost none. "Train accuracy" is not a thing a coach does apart from timing |
| Timing | KEEP, rename "Timing" = hit the gap, counter, read the shot | all striking | Counters, set-ups ("NOW!" gap), exchanges at mid range | Sparring, light drills, film | Counter-puncher plan, how much sparring, which counters to drill |
| Blocking | KEEP (call it Blocking; includes shelling, parries) | box, KB, MT, MMA | Damage taken when she cannot avoid the shot; the high-guard / pressure style | Defensive drills, taking body work | Style choice: absorb and press vs slip and counter. Plan line "When she loads up" |
| Head movement | KEEP | box, KB, MMA (less MT) | Shots avoided, opens counters; costs when tired | Slip rope, defence drills, sparring | Out-boxer or counter style; train it vs a power puncher |
| Footwork | KEEP, widen to "Footwork and range" (angles, cutting the ring, pivots) | all standing | Who controls outside / long; ring cutting for pressure fighters; entering for takedowns | Footwork drills, shadow, sparring | Plan: keep it long vs press. Cage or ring size matters |
| Inside fighting | KEEP | box, KB, MT, MMA | Short hooks, tie-ups, body work, winning the inside range | Inside sparring, heavy bag, pad rounds at close range | Plan line "When she is inside"; pressure fighter vs counter-puncher |
| Clinch | KEEP, move to a stand-up grappling family | MT, MMA, (KB limited) | Muay Thai: knees, off-balance, dumps; MMA: wall/cage control, takedown entries | Clinch sparring, wrestling | In MT it is the sport; in MMA it decides cage fights |
| Kick defence | KEEP (checks, catches, distance) | KB, MT, MMA | Leg and body kick damage; stops a leg kicker | Partner kick drills, sparring with kickers | Prep vs a leg-kick opponent. Absent from boxing and BJJ |
| Takedowns | KEEP | MMA, BJJ (a little), wrestling later | Whether the fight reaches the mat on your terms; setups from striking range | Wrestling drills, live shots | Plan: shoot early, or pull guard. Opponent file: "weak vs sprawl" |
| Takedown defence | KEEP (not merged with Takedowns: great sprawlers are not great shooters) | MMA, BJJ, (box/MT: stay standing in clinch) | Whether she can keep it standing; fight stays in your range | Sprawl drills, wall wrestling, live wrestling | Strikers choose to fight in the clinch or not; wrestlers choose to chain attempts |
| Scrambling | KEEP only for MMA/BJJ with doubt, see note | BJJ, MMA | Transitions from bad to neutral, who ends on top | Live rolling, wrestling-up drills | Mostly gets a read-out of Explosiveness + Fight IQ. Weakest case on the list |
| Guard | KEEP (bottom game: retention, sweeps, guard attacks) | BJJ, MMA | Whether being on the bottom is dangerous for her; sweeps and submissions from bottom | Positional rolling, drilling | Pull guard vs shoot; bottom player style |
| Passing | MERGE with Control into "Top game" | BJJ, MMA | Getting past legs and keeping the position; ground and pound base in MMA | Passing drills, top position rolling | Top-pressure vs scrambler plan |
| Control | MERGE (see Passing) | BJJ, MMA | Holding mount / back / side after the pass | same | not separate in the real training room |
| Submissions | KEEP (finishing attacks; each submission is a move) | BJJ, MMA | Whether a dominant position turns into a finish | Submission drilling, rolling | Finish hunter vs points player; stripe and belt road |
| Submission defence | MERGE into Escapes (rename "Escapes") | BJJ, MMA | Surviving the attack while still in position, and getting out of it | Escape drills, rolling from bad spots | One number is enough: "can she be put away on the mat" |
| Escapes | KEEP as the merged "Escapes" (sub defence + position escapes) | BJJ, MMA | Survival from the bottom or the back; takes damage when it fails | same | Whether to take risks on the mat or hold the stand-up |

Note on Scrambling: coaches do say "he is a scrambler", but it is mostly Explosiveness, Fight IQ and Flexibility with Escapes and Takedowns. Keep it only if the fight rule really has a transition step. If it does not, cut it. I am unsure.

## 3. Missing
- Nothing big for boxing. Ring cutting / pressure is already inside Footwork: if the owner wants a "Pressure" bar, I would make it a style slider (Patient to Forward), which is already agreed.
- Leg locks / leg entanglements for no-gi BJJ are a distinct modern skill. For the first release fold them into Submissions (each leg lock is a move); if the owner wants a real no-gi feel add "Leg entanglements" as an optional 8th BJJ skill. I am unsure of the value.
- Ground and pound / top striking for MMA: not a skill; it is moves + Top game + Strength. Do not add.
- Wrestling as a sport (folkstyle/freestyle): Takedowns, Takedown defence, Clinch, Top game, Escapes already cover it.
- Conditioning, sprawl speed, grip strength: attributes (Stamina, Explosiveness, Strength), not skills.

## 4. Recommended list, per sport (Timing replaces Accuracy+Timing, Top game = Passing+Control, Escapes = Escapes+Sub defence)
- Boxing (5): Timing, Footwork, Head movement, Blocking, Inside fighting
- Kickboxing (6): + Kick defence
- Muay Thai (7): Timing, Footwork, Head movement, Blocking, Inside fighting, Kick defence, Clinch (Clinch replaces nothing; it is the sport's core)
- No-gi BJJ (7): Takedowns, Takedown defence, Scrambling, Guard, Top game, Submissions, Escapes
- MMA (14): the 7 striking incl. Clinch and Kick defence (Timing, Footwork, Head movement, Blocking, Inside fighting, Kick defence, Clinch) + 7 ground skills as BJJ, with Takedowns and Takedown defence counted once
How many is too many: a player reads a fighter at a glance with 5 to 7 bars; 8 is the ceiling. A fighter page with 14 is only readable if the skills are shown in three families (Striking 7, Wrestling and clinch, Ground) with one family number each and the bars opened on a click. Do not show 17. Or cut MMA to a "strongest three / weakest three" row.
Note: I counted Clinch twice for MMA in the 14 above by intent: it sits once in the list (Striking row: 6 + Clinch = 7). Clean count: striking 6 + Clinch 1 + wrestling 2 (Takedowns, TD) + ground 5 (Scrambling, Guard, Top game, Submissions, Escapes) = 14.

## 5. Crossover (rough; I would let the owner tune it)
Rule of thumb: skills carry when the situation is the same and the body mechanics are close.
- Timing, Footwork, Head movement: boxing to kickboxing 75-85%; boxing to Muay Thai 55-65% (kicks, stance, different distance); to MMA 55-65% (cage, takedown threat, no gloves-up stance).
- Blocking: boxing to kickboxing 70%; to MT 50% (blocks must cover legs); to MMA 50%.
- Inside fighting: boxing to kickboxing 70%; boxing to MT about 30% (elbows, knees, clinch).
- Kick defence: from boxing 0 to 10%; from kickboxing to MT 70%.
- Clinch: wrestling to MT 50%; MT to MMA 60%; boxing to anything 15%.
- Takedowns, Takedown defence: wrestling to MMA 90-100%; BJJ to MMA 50-60%; no-gi BJJ to MMA 60%.
- Guard, Top game, Submissions, Escapes: no-gi BJJ to MMA 65-75% (strikes make posture and risk different; guard drops most). Gi BJJ is not in the game.
- Scrambling: no-gi BJJ to MMA 75%, wrestling to MMA 75%.
- Crossover costs time (agreed): the carried part is a starting level and a faster climb up to it; the rest is new.

## 6. Overlaps to settle, so nothing is counted twice
- Footwork vs Speed and Explosiveness: Footwork is skill (angles, ranging); Speed is the raw foot and hand speed. The game's old derived Footwork from speed and defence must not carry into the new Footwork.
- Timing vs Fight IQ: Fight IQ = reading the plan, adapting round to round; Timing = hitting the gap. Keep the reasons separate in the tooltips.
- Accuracy vs move level: delete Accuracy. A move level already says whether she hits it.
- Blocking vs Durability: Durability = taking damage; Blocking = not taking it. Different, but say so on hover.
- Takedown defence, Clinch vs Strength: Strength is a modifier, not a skill; do not show it again here.
- Escapes vs Flexibility, Heart; Scrambling vs Explosiveness, Fight IQ: the attribute feeds the skill (better ceiling), it must not be added as a second bonus to the same roll.
- Submissions, Takedowns, Passing: each submission and takedown type (armbar, double leg) is a move with its own level. The skill is the multiplier across all of them. Rule: if a bar's name is a technique, it is a move; if it is an area of the sport, it is a skill.
- Perks overlap too: Counter sense, Iron chin and Ring general sit on skills and attributes (Defence 65, Chin 70, Ring IQ 65). They must be re-pointed at Blocking/Head movement, Durability and Fight IQ, or they will read the old stats.

## What I am unsure of
- How the exchange rule would use skills; I recommended tying each skill to a range or plan situation, but I have not read the code.
- Whether Scrambling has a place in the rules, and whether leg entanglements are worth an 8th BJJ bar.
- Crossover percentages are coach's rules of thumb, not measured.
- Whether 14 MMA skills read at all, even grouped: I suspect not unless families are shown.

## Verdict
The draft is a good ingredient list and a poor skill model. Cut it to 5 to 7 per sport, merge four pairs, show families for MMA.
