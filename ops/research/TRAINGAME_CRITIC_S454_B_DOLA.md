YOUR JOB (fresh critic): one fighter's Training screen, judged as a GAME screen, not as an information sheet

training, what are your options and what each improves, fatigue and intensity meters maybe would be nice, how much do you rate it for a

What the picture shows (the player's one boxer, 12 days before a club-show bout; this is the last week whose pad nights count for it):
Header (one strip): the fighter, the next bout and a countdown, a LAST PAD WEEK badge, a WIN dial (the coach's week against the
player's week), the fighter's goal bar, mood and the weekly card waiting.
THIS WEEK (the big centre board): seven days. Each day has a MORNING card (picture, what it grows as arrows, fatigue as flames, its own
Light / Normal / Hard lever), an EVENING card (pads with the coach - the punch drilled with a ring filling to 30, the level at which it
can be thrown in a fight - film of the opponent, sparring, or free) and a FATIGUE TANK that fills day by day, with a dashed line where
injury risk doubles. A green tag on each card = its points on the WIN dial. Changed cards say YOURS. A sparring card is being dragged
onto Saturday evening and warns before the drop.
SESSIONS strip (bottom): every session as a picture card to drag; mornings left of the divider, evenings right.
Right column: the clicked card (Wednesday evening's pads) with what it does on fight night and what it gives up; what the week builds
(stat bars with the week's gain hatched, the moves as rings with the word ladder); the weekly card with its two answers; the week load
dial and the diet switch with the weight against the limit.
Undo / Coach's week / Last week / Clear above the board. CONTINUE (top right, the one pulsing button) runs the week.

Verdict: This is a genuine trade-off system, not a puzzle with one correct answer — exactly what a management sim needs.
