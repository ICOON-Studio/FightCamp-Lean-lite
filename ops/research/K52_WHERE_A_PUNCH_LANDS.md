## DECISIONS
Straight (jab/cross) aim: jaw / lower face in lab work; full bout facial-zone % NO STUDY. DOCUMENTED (Walilko 2005; Viano 2005).
Hook aim: side of jaw / mandibular angle for KOs; temple in lab protocol. Uppercut → jaw. DOCUMENTED (Cournoyer 2019; Viano 2005).
Elbow at impact: straight 137±12° (not 180° full extension); hook ~90° held. DOCUMENTED (Cheraghi 2014; Stanley 2018 / Gatt 2021 citing ~right angle).
Glove sink: absolute padding compression mm NO STUDY; 10 oz compression ratio ~70% at high energy. Follow-through 110 mm (Atha). Operator 5–15 cm was a guess. DOCUMENTED / SECONDARY.
Soft-tissue head indentation under a boxing punch: NO STUDY.
Hook path width (cm) and arrival angle (°): NO STUDY.
Games: EA uses relative IK + “touch tags” (no published cm/blend limits found); Thrill of the Fight softens hits past 45° fist–force angle. SECONDARY.
Height of impact below vertex (cm) by punch type: NO STUDY.
1. The point of impact
Bout / video distributions
NO STUDY publishes a % breakdown of all landed punches across chin / jaw / cheek / nose / forehead / temple / ear for jab, cross, lead hook, rear hook, and uppercut in amateur or pro bouts.
French multi-camera work annotated 2508 impact frames for hit detection but did not publish impact-zone shares; authors list “impact zone” as future work. DOCUMENTED (ECSS abstract, elite rounds, Qualisys).
Cournoyer & Hoshizaki (2019), Clin Biomech: video of pro bouts (400 matches screened, 2014–2016) where KO = loss of motor tone / posturing. Hooks to the side of the jaw (perpendicular to the mandibular angle) were the most common punch causing LOC. No % table in the abstract/public summary. DOCUMENTED (video analysis of KO subset, not all punches).
Secondary medical review: hooks to side of jaw / uppercuts to chin more associated with KO than straight face punches. SECONDARY (e.g. Front Neurol “How Can a Punch Knock You Out?”, 2020, citing boxing KO literature).

Angle convention in these papers: relative elbow angle; larger ≈ more extended; 180° = straight arm.

Is a straight fully extended at contact? No. Cheraghi: impact 137°, max 143°, well short of 180°. DOCUMENTED.

Whiting 1988: peak fist velocity 8–21 ms before bag contact (glove already decelerating into the hit). DOCUMENTED.

Shoulder at impact
Cheraghi straight right: shoulder ImpactAngle 86±5° (onset 20±4°; max 90±5°). DOCUMENTED (Table 3).
Hook / uppercut: shoulder contribution at impact ~50–71% of upper-body segment contribution (elite vs junior); cross elbow-dominant ~27–39%. DOCUMENTED (Dinu & Louis 2020, Front Sports Act Living). Exact flexion/abduction ° at hook impact: NO STUDY in that paper.
Fist height relative to puncher’s own shoulder
Cheraghi: fist rises +12.5±6 cm (FistYDisplacement) from ready to impact on a head-height pad; not “cm below/above shoulder joint.” DOCUMENTED displacement; NO STUDY for fist–acromion vertical offset at contact.
Fist–shoulder height in ° or cm at impact for jab/cross/hook: NO STUDY.
3. How far the glove goes in
Glove padding compression (10–16 oz)
Chadli et al. 2018, Measurement: six 10 oz competition gloves (3 new, 3 worn), energies 4 / 18 / 44 J. Compression ratio at powerful impact ≈ 70.5% (new) / 73.5% (worn). Absolute compression in mm not published (needs undeformed foam depth). DOCUMENTED (ratio only).
Absolute foam depth varies by brand; training gloves often cited ~40–45 mm mould thickness, competition denser/thinner. SECONDARY (manufacturer/coach guides). Converting ratio×depth → mm would be INFERENCE only — do not ship as measured.
Smith & Hamill (1986) and related ISBS glove papers: momentum / force transmission vs bare fist and karate foam; no mm compression reported in accessible abstracts. DOCUMENTED (kinetics, not thickness).
Soft-tissue / head indentation
NO STUDY of facial soft-tissue indentation depth (mm) under a gloved boxing punch to a live or cadaver head that we found.
Unrelated CPAP/cheek indentation (~5 mm) must not be used.

Fist travel past first contact (follow-through)
Atha 1985: distance of follow through = 110 mm (0.11 m) for the analysed Bruno punch (start→impact travel 490 mm, impact velocity 8.9 m/s). DOCUMENTED.
Per-punch-type follow-through (jab vs cross vs hook vs uppercut): NO STUDY.
K50’s ~11 cm is this Atha number — still the best measured figure; not improved by a newer multi-punch dataset here.

Operator note: today’s 5–15 cm “glove enter” allowance mixes padding crush + tissue + follow-through. Only 110 mm follow-through is measured; padding mm and tissue mm remain open. INFERENCE.

4. The hook’s path
Lateral path width (cm from a straight line)
Piorkowski et al. 2011, Sports Biomech: hooks use a curved transverse (X) swing; straights are mostly anterior–posterior. Figures show path shape; no published cm of max lateral deviation from the straight-line chord. DOCUMENTED (qualitative); width in cm: NO STUDY.
Cheraghi straight fist Z (medial) displacement 5.6±5 cm — that is a straight punch’s medial drift, not hook width. DOCUMENTED.
Arrival angle at the head (° from straight ahead)
NO STUDY with measured arrival heading (degrees from AP) for lead vs rear hook at contact.
Cournoyer: LOC hooks described as delivered perpendicular to the mandibular angle. DOCUMENTED (qualitative).
Coaching: “tight” vs “wide” hooks — no validated cm/° table. SECONDARY.

FightCamp read: last-instant arm bend toward a jaw (straight) or jaw-angle/temple (hook) aim matches how labs and KO video define the real target; keep elbow short of lock on straights (~135–140°) and ~90° on hooks; prefer ~11 cm follow-through over a 5–15 cm guess; treat glove crush as unknown mm until measured.
