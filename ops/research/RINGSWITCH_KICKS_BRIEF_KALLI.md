Welcome back. I'm writing the three kick designs now, and the playtest bout can wait until you want to pick it up again.

**Build Design 1 first: moving feet beat the low kick.** It needs no new switch, and it turns the most common feet choice, holding your ground, into the thing a low kick punishes. That makes the check worth spending.

**ONE RULE FOR ALL THREE DESIGNS (it fixes the Mid problem):** a kick thrown in the moment right after your own punch from the other hand is one speed faster. Jab then rear body kick, or cross then lead low kick, is the real Dutch combination. A slow kick at Mid then becomes a medium kick if you set it up, so it no longer always loses to the cross. At Long, the body kick and head kick cost 1 more stamina, so a fight can't turn into kick-only beats.

### DESIGN 1: MOVING FEET BEAT THE LOW KICK

**Jobs**
- **Teep:** stops a man walking in. If his feet step in during its moment, he goes back to the range he came from, and his punch in that moment falls short.
- **Low kick:** punishes the man who plants. It lands only on feet that are holding in its moment.
- **Body kick:** hurts the high guard from Long. It shortens his stamina bar, and his lead hand held low catches it.
- **Head kick:** the only big hit at Long. His rear hand held high blocks it.

**Leg defence**
A low kick lands only on a man whose feet hold their ground in its moment. Stepping in during it jams the kick: no damage, and the kicker's next punch this beat can't be big. Backing off makes it miss. A man who wants to stay put and be safe sets CHECK instead of holding: 1 stamina, held all beat, it stops the low kick and costs the kicker 1 health on the shin. So every beat, your feet choose between moving (1 stamina, which changes range or ground), checking (1 stamina, no move) and holding free with your legs at risk.

**A beat at Long**
- You: jab, rear hand held high, low kick, in the order lead then feet. He: lead hand held high, cross, feet hold, in the order rear then feet.
- **Moment 1:** your jab is blocked by his lead hand held high, and his cross falls short at Long.
- **Moment 2:** your low kick finds his feet holding, so it lands for 2. That's 1 of 4 toward his limp.

**A beat at Mid**
- You: lead hand held high, cross, teep, in the order feet then rear. He: lead hook, rear hand held high, step in, in the order feet then lead.
- **Moment 1:** your teep meets his step in, so he goes back to Mid.
- **Moment 2:** your cross meets his lead hook. Both are big, the hook is fast and lands first for 3, and a landed big punch stops a slower one, so your cross is stopped.

The teep won the ground, but you set the wrong hand. Your rear hand held high would have blocked that hook, which is the read.

**The decision**
Should you plant this beat (free, and best for big punches) and risk the low kick, move and spend stamina and ground, or check and spend stamina without moving? Planting is right when he's out of kick range or busy punching. Checking is right when you need to stay on the spot (near the ropes, or holding the centre), and moving is right when you want the range changed anyway.

**How it breaks, and the cap**
The lazy plan is to step in every beat, which beats the low kick for free. The teep is the cap: a step in that meets a teep loses its moment and its punch, so walking in blind gets teeped.

**The real kickboxing**
You low-kick the man who stands still. A moving target's leg isn't loaded, and a man who wants to stand his ground lifts his shin.

### DESIGN 2: THE LIGHT STANCE (the check rides on how you hold, not on a moment)

**Jobs**
- **Teep:** stops the step in, as in Design 1.
- **Low kick:** punishes the heavy, planted puncher.
- **Body kick:** beats the man who is checking. He's on one leg, so a body kick that lands means his next punch this beat can't be big.
- **Head kick:** punishes a busy rear hand. At Mid it lands if his rear hand is punching in that moment.

**Leg defence**
When your feet hold your ground, you choose HEAVY or LIGHT. LIGHT checks every low kick and every teep that beat: the kick does nothing, the kicker loses 1 health, and the teep doesn't move you. The price is that your rear-hand punches do 1 less that beat, because they have no legs under them. HEAVY gives full power, and kicks land. You can't stay light more than two beats running.

**A beat at Long**
- You: jab, rear hand held high, low kick, in the order lead then feet. He: lead hand held high, rear hand held low, holding LIGHT.
- **Moment 1:** your jab is blocked.
- **Moment 2:** your low kick is checked, and you lose 1 health on the shin.

He paid nothing at Long, because his rear hand had no punch that reaches there anyway. That's why the light stance is only cheap at Long.

**A beat at Mid**
- You: lead hand held low, cross, body kick, in the order rear then feet. He: jab, overhand, holding LIGHT, in the order lead then rear.
- **Moment 1:** your cross and his jab both land: 3 to him, 1 to you.
- **Moment 2:** your body kick comes one speed faster after the cross, so it lands before his slow overhand. It shortens his stamina bar and knocks him off balance, so his overhand can't be big and lands for only 1. Without being off balance, it would have been 3 (4 minus 1 for the light stance).

**The decision**
Power or safe legs. Heavy is right when you mean to throw a cross or overhand. Light is right when you'll jab and wait. The kicker reads it: heavy invites the low kick, light invites the body kick, the body kick is caught by the lead hand held low, and a lead hand held low leaves the head kick open.

**How it breaks, and the cap**
The lazy plan is light and jab forever, because the light stance costs a jabber nothing. The caps are two beats running at most, and the body kick punishes it.

**The real kickboxing**
A fighter whose weight is on the back leg is ready to check but punches without power. Coaches call it being "on the back foot".

### DESIGN 3: THE LEG BAR AND THE EARNED CHECK

**Jobs**
- **Low kick:** takes his movement away. It does 1 health and adds 1 to a visible LEG bar of 6 points. At 3, backing off costs 2 stamina. At 5, he can't back off, and a step in costs 2. At 6, his leg gives and it's a knockdown. The bar heals 1 point between rounds.
- **Teep:** buys a beat. If it lands, the rest of his beat is one range further away.
- **Body kick:** drains him from Long. It shortens his stamina bar, and his lead hand held low catches it.
- **Head kick:** the finisher on a man with a damaged leg. If his leg bar is at 3 or more, his rear hand held high only takes 2 off it, because he can't sit down under it.

**Leg defence**
Any beat in which you hold your ground with at least one hand held low banks one CHECK, and you can hold only one at a time. A banked check goes off by itself on the next low kick within two beats: the kick does nothing, and the kicker's own leg bar gets 1. You can still spend your feet on a check as today. The cost is a calm, half-defensive beat spent to earn it.

**A beat at Long**
- You: lead hand held low, rear hand held high, holding your ground. You bank a check, and nothing lands.
- **Next beat:** he throws a low kick, it fires your check, and his leg bar goes to 1.

**A beat at Mid**
- You: jab, cross, low kick, in the order lead, rear, feet. He: lead hand held high, rear hand held high, no check banked.
- **Moments 1 and 2:** your jab and cross are both blocked by his lead hand held high. Each punch of his that's blocked or misses makes your later punches counters, but here it's your punches that were blocked, so nothing changes.
- **Moment 3:** your low kick comes one speed faster after the cross. He's planted, so it lands, and his leg bar goes to 3. From now on, backing off costs him 2 stamina.

**The decision**
Should you spend a quiet beat banking a check (and give up attacking with that hand), or keep attacking with your legs open? The bank is right when his leg kicks are coming (the Kicker style, or his low kick in two of his last three beats). Attacking is right when he's pinned or hurt.

**How it breaks, and the cap**
The lazy plan is to bank a check, attack, bank again, so you're never open. The caps are that the check lasts two beats, and that the low hand that banks it leaves your head open to the head kick (your rear hand held high only takes 2 off it once your leg bar is at 3).

**The real kickboxing**
Low kicks take a man's legs first, then his head. The fighter who sits calm in his stance is the one who checks.

**WHY DESIGN 1 FIRST**
- **It uses only the feet switch you already have.** Holding your ground, today's free choice, now carries a risk.
- **It puts the check to work.** You pick it whenever you want to stay put, not only when you guess a low kick.
- **It gives the teep a job no punch has:** stopping a man walking in.
- **To measure:** each kick thrown in at least 3 beats in 100, each landing 40 to 60%, at least 30% of beats with a punch and a kick together, and the check chosen in at least 10% of beats. All of this is NOT COMPUTED.

**GAMES WORTH STUDYING**
- **EA Sports UFC:** a leg damage meter that leads to a limp, plus checking. It's the clearest picture of Design 3's leg bar.
- **Absolver:** every attack leaves you in a stance, and the stance decides what you can throw next. It's close to the light and heavy choice.
- **Yomi:** a card game where each choice beats one and loses to another. It's the model for the kick-and-answer cycle in Design 2.
- **Exceed:** a card game where range decides what hits. It's useful for why kicks must not own Long.
