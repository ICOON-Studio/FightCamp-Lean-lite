## Part A: Comebacks

**Q1. Knocked down first: who wins?**
- **Boxing: NOT FOUND.** No published count exists for pros or amateurs. BoxRec holds the knockdown data, but nobody has published the count (COMMENTATOR, a Reddit reply, not cited as a source).
- **UFC (SOURCED):**
  - **All fights with a knockdown:** the first to score one won 86.1%, so the dropped fighter won 13.9% of 3,005 fights [S1, S2].
  - **Fights that went past the knockdown round:** this is the split that matters. If the bell saves the dropped fighter, he wins **34.4%** (313 of 910) [S1].
  - **By round of the knockdown:** dropped in round 1 and survived, 36.8% (627 fights); dropped in round 2 and survived, 29.5% (251). The source says the gap "clears zero, but only just" [S1].
  - **Other counts agree in direction:** 11.6% (353 of 3,038) [S3]; 14.5% of 8,787 bouts [S4]; 19% after one knockdown and 7% after two, with no sample size [S5].
  - **More knockdowns:** the first scorer won 87.4% of fights with one knockdown (2,486), 79.8% with two (421), and 77.6% with three or more (98). The source reads this as reflecting which fights last long enough to have a second knockdown, not as extra knockdowns hurting the scorer [S1].

**Q2. Behind on the scorecards: who wins?**
- **Boxing: NOT FOUND.**
  - No count of "behind after 4 or 6 of 12 rounds, then won" turned up.
  - One paper, "Conditional Outcomes in Boxing Matches", was blocked by a security check, so it isn't used.
- **UFC, judges' real cards: NOT FOUND as a direct count.** The nearest figures use model or strike-count scoring:
  - **Round-1 winner on a model card:** won 68% of fights that reached round 2, and 72% of those that went to a decision [S7]. DERIVED: the man behind after round 1 won **32%**, and **28% of decisions**.
  - **Close and clear rounds differ:** a close round 1 (a strike edge of 0–5) picked the winner only 62% of the time; a lopsided one (30+ strikes) picked it 86% [S7].
  - **Strike leader after round 1:** won 67.2% (6,007 bouts) and 69.5% of decisions. The bigger the lead, the higher the win rate: 55.6% for a 1–4 strike lead, 81.8% for 20 or more [S8].
  - **By stoppage:** 29% of UFC finishes after round 1 (659 of 2,271) were made by the man who was behind on Unofficial Card's own rescoring [S6]. That count mixes KOs and submissions.
  - **"Down 0–2 after two rounds, wins X%":** NOT FOUND. A 2010 journalist said such a fighter "rarely" finishes in the third, with no count (COMMENTATOR, a search snippet I didn't open, so not cited).

**Q3. Share of knockouts that come from the fighter who is behind**
- **UFC:** 29% of finishes after round 1 (659 of 2,271) came from the fighter who was behind [S6]. That mixes KOs and submissions; a KO-only share and any count by round are NOT FOUND.
- **UFC, dropped then knocked the other man out:** 83 fights [S1]. DERIVED: 2.8% of all fights with a knockdown (83 ÷ 3,005), and 9.1% of those that went past the knockdown round (83 ÷ 910).
- **Boxing:** only the count exists: 16 last-round, come-from-behind KOs in title fights in all history up to March 2025, four of them in 2022–2025 [S9]. The share is NOT FOUND, because the total number of title fights isn't given.

**Q4. Does the man who is behind throw more power punches late, and land fewer?**
- **A collected CompuBox figure for trailing fighters:** NOT FOUND. Per-round CompuBox sheets exist, but no source gathers them for trailing fighters.
- **UFC, a study using the judges' scores [S13]** (SOURCED, abstract only):
  - Fighters who were behind going into the last round "slightly adjust their behavior, moving away from takedowns and late-stage submission attempts".
  - Leaders didn't coast.
  - How their striking changed is NOT FOUND.
- **Elite women's amateur boxing, 12 world finals [S14]** (SOURCED). These are the losers of the bouts, not fighters known to be behind at the time (CHECK).
  - **Each group's share of its own punches, by round:** winners 35.6 / 30.9 / 33.4%; losers 33.2 / 31.7 / **35.1%**. So in this sample the losers threw most in round 3.
  - **The losers' power punches by round:** rear straights 150 / 169 / 191; rear hooks 49 / 46 / 58.
  - **How effective the losers' attacks were:** this didn't fall: 0.26 / 0.28 / 0.29.
  - **Missed body punches:** rose in round 3 for both sides, 31 each, which the authors read as possible tiredness.
- **Novice amateurs (32 boxers):** winners threw more combinations than losers in rounds 1 and 3 (44.3 against 28.8) [S15] (SOURCED).
- **Single pro fights, CompuBox (SOURCED):**
  - **Wood–Conlan, 2022:** Wood was behind and won by KO in round 12. Over the fight he landed 33% of his power punches to Conlan's 44%, and was outlanded 171–135 in total [S11].
  - **Wardley–Huni, 2025:** Wardley was behind on all three cards and won by KO in round 10.
    - Power punches, DERIVED from the totals in [S12] (total minus jabs): Wardley landed 49 of 113 (43.4%); Huni landed 114 of 225 (50.7%; CompuBox's own figure is 51%).
    - Wardley landed "just 4 power shots between Rounds 6 and 9" [S12].

**Q5. What a fighter risks by going for broke**

There is no count of how often an all-out attack backfires. How often the trailing man gets stopped while going for broke is NOT FOUND, and so is how often a big wind-up gets countered.

The one count is the UFC knockdown audit [S1]. Of the 313 fighters who were dropped and still won, 57.2% won on points and only 26.5% (83) knocked out the man who dropped them. Even after surviving the round of the knockdown, the dropped fighter lost 65.6% of fights [S1].

**What coaches teach (COACH):** loading up for a big hook is a mistake, because "if your opponent knows what's coming, they have more time to defend, slip, or counter" [S16].

**Real comebacks point away from wild swinging (COMMENTATOR):**
- **Leigh Wood (the Conlan KO):** the finishing right hand was "a shot we drilled… hundreds of times". His corner had called for it two rounds earlier, and he said Conlan "was very fatigued, which was down to the body work" [S10].
- **Fabio Wardley (the Huni KO):** kept throwing his right hand "even when being countered and outboxed" [S10].
- **Dennis Andries, 1989, the warning case:** he was ahead on all three cards, but his trainer told him after round 10, "You have to knock this man out". He was floored twice in round 12 and stopped, and said afterwards, "I was told I was behind" [S9].

So on the counts alone, the most common outcome for a fighter who is behind is simply to lose.

## Part B: Kicks

**Q6. How often each kick lands (see Table 2)**
- **Main count: K-1 rules sparring** (10 bouts, 20 professionals; a strike counted only if it scored) [S17] (SOURCED):
  - Kicks landed 36.3% (322 of 886); punches landed 42.9% (630 of 1,470).
  - The low kick was the kick thrown most; the right low kick alone was 33.63% of all kicks.
  - The right middle kick worked best, with a median of 54.18%.
  - Kicks to the head: 18 of 149 scored.
- **Muay Thai** (32 bouts, winners only, counted if it "had some effect") [S18]: body and neck kicks had an effect 66.15% of the time (the paper's own figure). The teep (56.7%) and the leg kick (52.3%) are DERIVED from the paper's per-fighter averages.
- **WAKO K-1 rules** (142 elite amateur bouts) [S19]: kicks landed 78.3% for winners and 59.4% for losers. The text gives no split by kick type (NOT FOUND).
- **UFC fallback** [S21]: the UFC records the target, not the technique.
  - Leg strikes at distance landed 80%.
  - Body strikes at distance landed 63%, and head strikes 31%; both mix punches and kicks.
  - Whether all the leg strikes are kicks is CHECK.
- **Glory kick stats by type:** NOT FOUND.

**Q7. How often a low kick is checked**
- **A check rate:** NOT FOUND.
- **The nearest count:** 96 K-1 World Grand Prix bouts (192 fighter appearances, 1995–2012) [S20] (SOURCED). The figures are averages for each fighter in each fight:
  - shin checks with the lead leg: **1.79 ± 2.98**;
  - hand blocks against leg kicks: 5.31 ± 7.16;
  - rear-leg low kicks reaching the lead leg: 6.82;
  - lead-leg low kicks reaching the lead leg: 3.83.
- **Why I haven't built a check rate:** in that study, "reaching" includes kicks that were blocked, and the table lists only the 20 most common moves.
- **Blocking leg kicks with the hands:** each one went with 10.4% lower odds of winning [S20].
- **Whether a checked kick hurts the kicker:** NOT FOUND as a count. A 2026 review names "blocked low kicks causing tibial or metatarsal trauma" as one way the kicker gets hurt, but gives no rate [S24].

**Q8. The distance for each kick, and which kick meets a step forward**
- **Measured distances for each kick in kickboxing or Muay Thai:** NOT FOUND.
- **Nearest measured figure, taekwondo** (kicks only, 10 international athletes) [S25] (SOURCED): the usual distance for starting an attack was 188 cm in competition and 206 cm in training. The distance where fighters spent the most time was 177 cm in competition and 187 cm in training.
- **Muay Thai, share of each technique thrown at the right distance** [S18], DERIVED. This is a judge's quality rating, not a measured range:
  - teep 73.0%;
  - body kick 82.8%;
  - leg kick 80.6%;
  - punches 81.4%;
  - knees 96.9%.
- **Karate:** the rear punch thrown while moving forward works from further out, and the lead-hand jab from closer in [S31] (SOURCED as a description, with no distances given).
- **Against a step forward, coaches name the teep (COACH):** "Teep the moment they start to step toward you… so they stop at kicking range instead of getting close enough to punch" [S26], and "teep as they step, not after" [S27]. A count of which kick is actually thrown is NOT FOUND.
