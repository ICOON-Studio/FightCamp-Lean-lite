# Ideas wanted: what is each KICK for, and how does a man DEFEND his legs, in a game of switches

You are a game designer. We want your own ideas, not a review. Nothing below is a proposal to judge: it is the game as
it stands and the problem.

## The game (a small standalone prototype: Ring Switches, one man against one man)
- A bout is 3 rounds of 6 BEATS. In a beat both men set their switches at the same time, in secret, then the beat plays.
- Each HAND does one thing a beat: throw one punch, or stay home held HIGH or LOW. A hand at home blocks one line: lead
  hand high stops straight head punches, lead hand low stops body punches, rear hand high stops hooks, rear hand low
  stops uppercuts. A hand that punches is out of its guard only in the moment it throws.
- The FEET do one thing a beat: step in, hold your ground, or back off. Three ranges: Long, Mid, Inside. Each punch only
  reaches at some ranges (a jab at Long and Mid, a cross at Mid and Inside, uppercuts Inside only).
- A beat is three MOMENTS. The order you click your switches is the order you do them; your first action meets his first.
  When both strike in one moment the faster lands first, and a big one that lands stops his slower one.
- Punches cost stamina; a hand held gives stamina back. Health 18; a knockdown at set losses; judges score the rounds.
- The player sees every result before he commits, and the opponents play the best mix of plans, so nothing can be a
  move that always works.

## The kickboxing version (the same game, kicks added on the FEET switch)
A kick takes the feet's moment, so a man who kicks does not step that beat. Today: a push kick (teep: light, pushes him
one range back), a low kick to the thigh (four that land and he limps: no backing off), a body kick (shortens his
stamina for the bout; a lead hand held low catches the leg), a head kick (the big one; a rear hand held high blocks
it). A rear-leg kick drops the kicker's rear hand in that moment. The only leg defence is a CHECK: also on the feet
switch, held all beat, it stops a low kick and hurts the kicker's shin.

## The problem
Measured over many bouts between two equal men who both play the best mix:
- The low kick lands almost every time. A man rarely spends his feet on a check, because the check is only good against
  one kick and the low kick is thrown in about one beat in eight.
- When the body kick and the head kick reach at Long, fights are kick-only beats with few punches; when they are cut back
  to Mid, nobody throws them at all - a slow kick loses to a faster cross there.
- We want every kick used in at least a few beats in a hundred, each landing about half the time, punches and kicks
  mixed in the same beat, and a real choice in how a man protects his legs.

## What we ask you for
THREE different designs. For each:
1. THE JOB of each of the four kicks in one line each (what it is for that no punch does).
2. THE LEG DEFENCE: the rule in at most five plain sentences (what the player sets, when, what it costs him).
3. ONE BEAT walked through at Long and one at Mid: both men's switches, what happens moment by moment.
4. THE DECISION it gives the player: what is he weighing, and when is each choice right?
5. HOW IT CAN BREAK: the lazy plan that would win too often, and the cap you would put on it.
6. THE REAL KICKBOXING it stands for (one line).
Then: which ONE you would build first and why, and any published game (video, card or board) that handles kicks or leg
defence in a way worth studying.
