# ALLPLAY1_ARENA - six honest bouts, seat ARENA (2026-11-25)

I played six bouts myself through `proto/twohands/tools/play_cli.mjs`, two against each opponent, playing to win,
deciding every beat with one line of WHY before it. I wrote no script and no bot, and I read no page, no rules file and
no tool code until all six were finished. State files and full logs sit beside this report: `b1.json`...`b6.json` and
`b1.json.log.txt`...`b6.json.log.txt`. Only files under `allplay1_arena/` were added.

**Record: 6-0.** The Jabber 30-27, 29-28. The Slugger: stoppage in round 3, 29-28. The Body Snatcher 29-28, 29-28.
Five of six bouts were decided on the last card; two went to the ground tiebreak, one to a third knockdown.

## VERDICT

**The read works. The last three beats are shown, and I built my whole game on them.** From bout 3 on I chose nearly
every beat off one line of data: his lead low two or three beats running meant the straight was bare and the cross
landed for 3 (b5 R2B4-B5, b6 R2B4-B6); his lead parked high meant the body was open (b6 R3B3, my body cross); his
rear out throwing meant the side was open and my fast hook beat it to the punch (b4 R2B5, b5 R3B2). That is a READ, not
a coin flip - and the layer above it is that he rotates to the line you just scored on, so the second read is a
counter-read (b4 R1B4: after my uppercut he went rear-low; b5 R2B6 and b6 R2B6: after two of my crosses landed, his
lead hook beat my cross to the punch in the identical beat of both bouts). The guard game has two levels and both are
visible in the words.

**The ORDER is still the best idea in it.** My two favourite beats of the six bouts are order beats: the feint that
drew his guard so the cross went behind it with nothing able to cover (b5 R3B3 and b6 R3B5 - the second one with the
cards level at 19-19), and my hook beating his overhand to the punch to stop it dead (b5 R2B4, b6 R1... b4 R1B5). Same
punch, different moment, wins or loses - I felt that rule decide three bouts.

**What is not there is the fun when the read fails.** His body work can shut my choices down to a crawl in one beat
(b5 R2B1: his two body punches landed as counters off my blocked jab, 6 damage and my stamina bar 8 -> 5, permanently),
and at bar 4-5 against the Body Snatcher my honest options were one2-stamina punch a beat for the rest of the bout
(b5 R2B2-B3, b6 R3B4 - both were holding a hand and one punch). The blocked-jab sequences at Mid and Long are the dullest
thing I played (b5 R1B3-B4, b6 R1B1-B2: four beats of jab into a raised lead before anything happened). The bones are
excellent; the crawl is the flaw.

**Scores: FUN 7/10, DEPTH 7/10, CLARITY 7/10, VARIETY 7/10.** One line each is at the scores section. Ziva's first
playtest gave 6,7,6,6 - my numbers are honestly my own and I only read her report after finishing; where we differ I
say so in the last section. If the first three changes at "TO REACH 10/10" landed I would expect DEPTH 9 and FUN 8.

## NOT SURE

- **Whether winning 6-0 inflated my scores.** Ziva went 2-4 in her six honest bouts and I went 6-0. Winning is fun; I
  cannot fully separate the game's quality from the pleasure of the record, and a reader should weigh that.
- **The counter-read may be the AI remembering me, not solving me.** After I landed two crosses he played the lead hook
  that beats it - in two different bouts, in the same beat (b5 R2B6, b6 R2B6). From the seat I could not tell whether I
  was being read (his plan memory) or solved (his best mix). Both feel identical in the log, and that legibility question
  is Ziva's too - I confirm it rather than settle it.
- **Pace, sound and the click.** Words only. I cannot say whether a beat feels fast or slow, whether the animation keeps
  up with the decision, what the bell does to a round, or whether choosing the order by clicking is pleasant - I typed
  every order.
- **Health 18 against 17.** I only played 18. It felt right: two knockdowns were survivable (I ate one in b4, b5 and b6
  and won all three bouts), and I delivered one stoppage. I cannot call the change good or bad without the other number.
- **Whether a stoppage-while-winning feels bad.** I stopped the Slugger while winning R3 big (b3) and it was the best
  moment of my session; Ziva was stopped while winning and called it her least-in-control moment. Same rule, opposite
  seats - I only felt one side.
- **The pictures and the real page.** I judged the words, like Ziva. Her picture findings (body jab reads as chest
  height, overhand mapped to rear-hook frames) stand untested by me.

## The three best moments

1. **Bout 3, round 3, beat 4 (the Slugger, stoppage).** I was down one round and 2-9 in round 2 when I turned it: my
   jab, then the two crosses that flipped the round, then - after he stepped Inside - the lead uppercut that put him
   down, and the second uppercut in the next beat that ended it. "YOU WIN - the referee stops it." Every rule arrived
   across four beats: the under line his rear-high guard could not cover, the knockdown's3 points and health reset, the
   third knockdown ending the bout whatever the cards said. The only stoppage I delivered in six bouts, from behind.
2. **Bout 4, round 2, beat 6 (the Slugger, knockdown to flip a round).** He was 4-1 up in the round when I read his
   rear out throwing and fired the hook into the open side - landed, stopped his punch, he went down, got up on 6, and
   the round closed 5-4 to me. A knockdown as a comeback mechanic inside a single round, not just a finisher.
3. **Bout 5, round 3, beat 3 (the Body Snatcher, feint draw).** Cards level at 19-19, he parked both hands in a high
   shell, and the feint drew his guard so the cross landed where nothing could cover it - down he went and the round
   opened7-0. The feint was my best switch of the session (also b6 R3B5, same beat, same result) - Ziva called it dead
   weight, and for me it was the punch that won two deciders.

Honourable mention: **b6 R3B6** - at Inside with the round already won, his back-off moved him out and my cross still
landed before the bell, 10-9 and 29-28. The last punch of an unbeaten session.

## The three dullest

1. **Bout 5, round 1, beats 2-4 and bout 6, round 1, beats 1-2 (the jabber's jab at Long/Mid).** Four and two beats in
   a row of my jab into his raised lead - blocked, blocked, nothing - while his rear fell short at Long. Nothing to

2. **Bout 5, round 2, beats 1-3 (the crawl).** His two body punches off my blocked jab took 6 health and my bar 8 -> 5

3. **Bout 5, round 3, beat 6 (the stand-off).** Both men holding, nothing thrown, the round already decided. Correct

## The beat that felt unfair, and the one I could not explain

**Nothing in my six bouts felt truly unfair** - every knockdown I ate was the speed rule working: his lead hook was
fast, my cross was medium and out, he landed first and stopped it (b4 R3B4, b5 R2B6, b6 R2B6 - the same beat three
times, which is itself the finding). The harshest legal beat was **b5 R2B1**: my jab blocked, his body hook and body
cross both landed as counters (3+3 with +1 inflation each) and my stamina bar went 8 -> 5 in a single beat. Explained
line by line, still felt like the floor dropping.

**Unexplainable until I inferred it: the points.** Ziva's complaint is correct and I hit the same wall - his body
punches at -3 health gave him ONE point (b5 R2B1) while my hook at -3 gave me TWO (b4 R2B5). I worked it out mid-bout-2
from pattern: points come from base damage (2 for a3+ punch, 1 otherwise), and a counter's +1 inflates the damage shown
without changing the points. The log's numbers are consistent once you have the rule; the rule is in no line of the
words. One line of help text fixes it.

## Is "which line is open" a READ now?

**Yes - with a built-in one-beat lag that is itself the depth.** His last three beats are shown, and so is the round's
guard census ("lead hand held low 3, thrown 2"), and between them I could name the open line before I punched:

- lead low two or three running -> straight open (b5 R2B4-B5: two crosses,6 damage; b6 R2B4-B6: three beats of the
  same read);
- lead parked high -> body open (b6 R3B3: body cross landed, and it was the only clean score of a level round);
- rear out throwing -> side open, and the fast hook beats the slow punch to the punch (b4 R2B5, b5 R3B2).

The lag: he rotates to the line you just scored on (b4 R1B4 after my uppercut; b5 R2B6 and b6 R2B6 - my cross landed
twice and the third time his lead hook beat it, in both bouts, identically). So the read answers "what is open now" and
the counter-read answers "what will he close next" - two guesses, both visible in the words, one beat apart. That is a
read with a trap in it, and the trap is the best-hidden good thing in the game.

## A plan right every beat? A switch never wanted? A range worth nothing?

**A plan right every beat: no, and the game punished me for trying.** My best line was cross into his lead-low guard -
it landed six times across the six bouts for 18 damage and it is the punch that lost me b5 R2B6 and b6 R2B6 (his lead
hook, fast, beating my medium). The AI's rotation to my last success means a repeated plan has a counter waiting by the
second or third use. Good design; I confirm Ziva's finding from the winning side.

**A switch never wanted: the push-off.** I never threw it once in six bouts (108 beats). It costs2, it displaces, and
the only time I felt it was against me (b4 R3B6: he shoved me Inside -> Mid so my Inside hook died to his recovered
glove, then finished the beat). Feint, by contrast, was my most valuable switch (2 knockdown-setups from it) - Ziva
dismissed it, and I think that is a seat difference, not a balance fact: feint+cross is an Inside/Mid tool where his
clinches cannot reach, and it was decisive for me twice.

**A range worth nothing: Long - almost.** Everything with real damage is out of reach there; my Long beats were
jab-or-hold (b2 R3, which I won by turtling and which was tense but thin; b5 R1, four beats of it). But the body jab
gives it the second line S556 claims: I used it at Long against the Body Snatcher to score and cut his bar (b6 R1B3,
blocked by his lead-low - the guess fired correctly, I just guessed wrong). Long is a real range now, a small one: two
1-point punches and a bar-cut.

## The three opponents

**The Jabber - the easiest read and the right first opponent (2-0: 30-27, 29-28).** His guard states are the most
obvious (passive lead low -> straight open; lead up -> body), his back-off habit handed me the ground tiebreak in b2 R2,
and his feint is genuinely his best weapon (it cost me health in b2 when I ate the uncovered punch behind it). Bout 1
was dominant; bout 2 went to the last card because I turtled R3 at Long. Reading him pays immediately.

**The Slugger - the most dangerous man, and the reads pay most against him (2-0: stoppage, 29-28).** 21 health, the
widest bomb selection, and the only opponent who knocked me down while I was winning (b4 R3B4, his lead hook). But his
opener repeats verbatim ("1 Step in, 2 Rear uppercut" - b4 R1B1 and it walked into my guards twice), his rear-out
moments fed my hook, and the under line his rear-high guard cannot cover was my road to the stoppage (b3 R3B2-B4, three
uppercut-family beats). The most violent fight and the most readable one.

**The Body Snatcher - the narrowest margins and the hardest per beat (2-0: 29-28, 29-28).** Both bouts went to the
last card of round 3; both were level19-19 after two rounds. His body work cut my bar to 5 in one beat (b5 R2B1) and to
4 by round 3 (b6), which is the crawl Ziva describes - at bar 4-5 I could afford one punch a beat and no more. But:
his own bar bent the same way under my body work (down to 1-2 by round 3 of both bouts, at which point he held or
feinted and I scored for free - b6 R3B5-B6), the straight-open-on-lead-low read was my most profitable punch of the
session against him, and at 1-2 stamina he is the same harmless man Ziva found. Hardest, closest, still 2-0.

## Rounds 2 and 3 against round 1

**Round 1 is probing, round 2 is the bar war, round 3 is the decision.** In round 1 of all six bouts I spent the first
two beats finding his guard habit (jab into his lead, see what is up) and lost the jab-versus-lead-up exchange more than
I won it - but I won round 1 of all six (10-9 five times, plus one on the ground tiebreak). Round 2 is where his body
work finds my bar (b5 R2B1, b6 R2B1: both rounds the bar moved before the points did) and where the level rounds
appear; I split round 2 4-2. Round 3 is a different game: stamina at 1-5, feint+cross becomes the best punch in the
arsenal (b5 R3B3, b6 R3B5), turtling with both hands up is legitimate when ahead (b2 R3, b5 R3B6), and the knockdown
math changes what a lead is worth. I won round 3 in four of six (one by stoppage) and lost it twice while still winning
the bouts on the cards.

**Knockdowns, ropes, the bar.** I went down three times (b4 R3, b5 R2, b6 R2 - one per Slugger/Snatcher bout) and won
all three: getting up on 6 made me the fresher man against an opponent who had spent the punch, and it never felt like
a reset I could not play back from. I delivered the only stoppage (b3) and never went down twice. On the ropes: I never
gave ground carelessly - the ground tiebreak decided three of my rounds (b2 R2, b5 R1, b6 R1), all of them won because
I held my feet while he backed off, and I started playing for it deliberately from bout 5 on (feet=hold as a scoring
choice, not just a default). The bar: cut to 4-5 against the Body Snatcher both bouts, it genuinely changes the texture
from boxing to bookkeeping - and cutting HIS bar changed him from a puncher into a man who held and let me feint-cross
him (b6 R3). The bar is the body snatcher's whole character and it works.

## SCORES (1 to 10)

- **FUN 7/10** - the stoppage from behind (b3), two knockdowns I built myself (b4 R2B6, b5 R3B3), three tiebreak
  wins I played for on purpose, and a 6-0 card; against the jab-into-lead blocks and the two crawl rounds.
- **DEPTH 7/10** - the order, the speed rule, the rotation counter-read and the ground rule all interact, and the
  last-three-beats display made the guard game a real read for me; it stops short of 8 because the counter-read is
  one beat behind by design and the plan memory means the second half of a bout is partly a lookup.
- **CLARITY 7/10** - the beat log is the best-written thing in the project (who blocked, why the punch landed, whose
  hand was out and why); it loses points for the points formula (inferred, not told) and for the bar-shortening that
  only a careful reader notices is permanent (b5 R2B1: 8 -> 5, one line).
- **VARIETY 7/10** - three genuinely different men and I beat each twice with a different plan each time; inside a
  bout the Body Snatcher repeats whole beats (his "step in, jab, cross" opener three times in b6) and across six bouts
  the decisive beat was usually one of three pictures: the cross into a low lead, the hook onto an out rear, or the
  feint-draw.

**What I could not judge from words:** pace, sound, the click and the feel of choosing the order through a UI; the
animation in motion; hover text on the score line; the opponent's body language between beats (the place a player
would learn a guard with his eyes instead of his memory); and whether the page's pictures match the log's actions.

## Where my play agrees and disagrees

**Agrees with Ziva**
- The guard read is usable now and the rotation trap is real (her coin-flip finding is my counter-read; same beats,
  different words).
- The points formula is the one place the log's numbers contradict themselves until you infer the rule (b5 R2B1 vs
  b4 R2B5).
- The body hook is a bot-artifact statistic: I used lbody/rbody constantly (my primary tool for cutting his bar in both
  Snatcher bouts), so "4% of beats, watch it" is measuring the wrong player.
- The push-off is a switch a player learns to ignore - I never threw it in108 beats.
- Long is thin; the body jab helps it a little, not a lot (b6 R1B3: the guess fired, I read it wrong).
- The Body Snatcher is the hardest man per beat - my only two bouts decided on the last card.

**Disagrees with Ziva**
- **The feint is not dead weight.** It was my best switch: b5 R3B3 and b6 R3B5, both feint-draws into an uncoverable
  cross, both knockdowns, both in deciders. Her seat never found the Inside/Mid use where his clinch cannot answer it.
- **"A plan right every beat" is more available than she says** - not a single plan (she is right that spam dies), but
  the lead-low -> cross read held for six straight beats in b6 R2 and across both Snatcher bouts. The read is steadier
  than a coin flip; the rotation is what resets it.
- **Slugger too hard: no.** He is the most dangerous (he dropped me while I was winning) but his opener repeats
  verbatim, his rear-out moments feed the hook and his rear-high guard cannot cover the under line - he is the man the
  reads pay most against. 2-0 with a stoppage.

**Agrees with S556**
- The ground rule is worth having: it decided three of my rounds (b2 R2, b5 R1, b6 R1), all deliberately played for.
- The AI does not play for it: he backed off into those tiebreaks repeatedly, and I never gave ground in return.
- The body jab gives Long a real guess (used it, blocked by lead-low, the read fired).
- Health 18 was the right try: three knockdowns eaten, zero lost bouts from it, one stoppage delivered.
- Difficulty order: narrowest margins vs the Body Snatcher exactly where S556's answering player is weakest (40%).

**Disagrees with S556**
- **"The jab lands too often (73%)" is a bot number.** My jab was blocked again and again by a lead that was up
  planning a later punch (every bout opener; b5 R1B3-B4). For a player the jab's land rate is the guard read itself,
  and the bar that fails may be measuring look-ahead, not humans.
- **"Switches never worth a click: none"** - the feint was worth a click in two of my deciders (b5 R3B3, b6 R3B5).

## TO REACH 10/10

Most important first. Each one is tied to a beat I actually played.

1. **Show the rotation before it fires.** (b5 R2B6 and b6 R2B6: my cross landed twice and the third time his lead
   hook beat it - the identical beat in both bouts.) The last-three-beats chips already exist; add the one-line
   consequence: "his lead covered straight after your cross - watch the side." The counter-read Ziva and I both found
   by losing would become a read you can act on a beat earlier - that is the difference between DEPTH 7 and DEPTH 9.
2. **Price the block.** (b5 R2B1: my jab blocked, his two counters landed for 6 and my bar 8 -> 5 in one beat; b6
   R3B4: my hook blocked, his body hook countered for 3.) Blocking is free money for the man who guesses right - make
   it cost guard (the state line already tracks guard 6/6 and I saw it move once in six bouts), so turtle-into-counter
   is a trade rather than a print.
3. **One honest punch at Long.** (b5 R1B2-B4: three beats of jab into his raised lead at Mid/Long while his rear fell
   short; b2 R3: three rounds of turtle - winning, but thin.) Let the cross reach Long, or make the step-in that answers
   a back-off free in the moment it chases. Long is a real range with two 1-point punches in it; it needs a third.
4. **Print the points formula in help.** (b5 R2B1: his -3 gave him 1; b4 R2B5: my -3 gave me 2.) Base damage sets the
   points, a counter inflates the damage shown - one line, and the log's only internal contradiction disappears.
5. **Shout the bar-cut.** (b5 R2B1: 8 -> 5 in one beat; b6 R3: fighting on a bar of 4.) "STAMINA BAR SHORTENED -
   PERMANENT FOR THE BOUT" on the line where it happens. Ziva read it as temporary for two bouts; I only caught it by
   watching the number.
6. **Break the literal repeats.** (b6: his "step in, jab, cross" opener three times; b5 R2B6 = b6 R2B6 verbatim; b5
   R2B5 and b4 R3B6 both feint-then-big-punch.) Keep each man's lean and his favourite line, but vary the order and
   the timing inside it, so a read stays alive instead of becoming a lookup by round 3.
7. **Print the life count where it counts.** (b4 R3, b5 R2, b6 R2: I went down and got up on 6 each time, and the
   only line I wanted was the one for HIM: "one more knockdown ends it whatever the cards say" - I delivered that
   stoppage in b3 from the other side.) One line of free drama, and it cuts both ways.
