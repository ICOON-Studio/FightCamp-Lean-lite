## Three lines for the owner
- **How it plays:** two places instead of three. Outside you live off the jab and the body jab and pick the moment to step
  in; in the pocket everything lands and it is a trade; when you want out you back off and the beat simply stops there -
  and he gets the lead. It reads faster than the main page: no "Mid or Inside?" question.
- **What got better:** Outside is a place you fight from (39% of beats start there, a punch timed for his step in is 8% of
  all landed punches), backing off is a tool instead of a habit (it ends 17-18% of beats; on the main page a man backs
  off in 33% of his beats), and the feint is a read you can lose.
- **What got worse:** it is less balanced than the main page - 5 and 4 of 9 bars against 8 and 8. Punches land too often
  (the jabs 75-80%), bouts end early too often (stoppages 38-43%, 2.9 knockdowns), the body jab crowds out the head jab,
  and the feint is bitten two times in three. The measure was stopped before the feint-then-power loop was run.

## The rules as built (the page = M2)
1. **Two places + the clinch.** Outside (only the jab and the body jab reach) and the pocket (every punch reaches). A step
   in goes Outside -> pocket. The clinch is from the pocket; the referee breaks them in the pocket.
2. **Long range = the jab and the body jab.** A miss or a block of one gives him no counter. A punch that lands in the
   moment he steps in from Outside is a step-in counter (+1). A jab or body jab in your beat stops his clinch.
3. **Create distance = the back off from the pocket.** The men are Outside and the beat ends after that moment: nothing
   later is thrown, by either man, and what was not thrown costs nothing. He has the lead. His step in in the same moment
   cancels it, the beat goes on, and he takes the lead.
4. **The lead.** When both men throw at the same speed in the same moment, the man with the lead lands first (and a big
   punch that lands stops the other). A new round starts with nobody in the lead.
5. **The feint is a mode of a lead-hand punch** (1 stamina, no damage). His hand that guards that side goes to it unless
   it is busy in that moment: it guards nothing for the rest of the beat, you take the lead, and he is off rhythm next
   beat (no counter bonus; in moment 1 his hands are where he held them last beat). A busy hand calls it: he takes the lead.

Everything else is today's: damage, stamina, guards, knockdowns, judges, 6 beats a round.

## Two versions, and why the page is M2
- **M1 = the brief's five rules as written** (a bitten feint only makes him off rhythm NEXT beat; "bitten" = he covered
  that line). One-rep trial n=12: Outside 26-34%, jab 14%, create distance 14.7% of beats, 0.83 lead changes a round -
  and **the feint in 0.0% of plans**. That bar killed it.
- Why: a beat in the pocket without a real lead-hand punch costs about 1 health (a man who only feints and guards takes
  1.7-2.6 a beat). Off rhythm as written is worth 0.3-0.5 health. Nine stronger versions were tried on the beat's solved
  mix before M2 (no guard at all in his first moment, in two moments, for the whole next beat; the feint free; no lead
  lost when called; the lead won when bitten; both hands drawn on a covered line): the feint stayed at 0.0% in all of
  them. A feint that pays only next beat cannot be a choice here.
- **M2 = M1 with the feint rewritten** (rule 5 above): it pays in the same beat again, but only on the side he guards,
  only if that hand is home, and a called feint costs the lead. This goes against the brief's "no feint-then-overhand chain
  beyond that" - decided because the alternative was a dead toggle on the page. The loop bar is there to judge it, and it
  is the one number not measured (below).
