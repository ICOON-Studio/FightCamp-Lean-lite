# FOURSTART1: a career starts with four fighters, and the week is dealt on one board

Source: the owner's scope change: "we sshould force the player to have 4 fighters,different weight classes and bjj so
menus shows better and players has more things to do", and later: "on the new 3-4 fighter start try to use the guide to
pinpoint different trainings according to different ratings and styles of fighters"; "fix the hole" became "fix the weakness".
Measured (MONEYFOUR1, a year, 10 careers a cell): one paid seat keeps the club at 1.9 to 2.9 months of costs on today's start
cash; two: 1.2 months by week 52; four: 8 clubs in 10 close; none: $31,000 in the bank.

## 1. The idea in five lines
- You take over a club. Four fighters stayed: three boxers in three weight classes and one grappler. You pick which three
  boxers (from six), and each one's starting focus.
- Three things are scarce and shared: PAID SEATS (money), COACHED TIME (Reid's and your own mornings and pad evenings), and
  PLACES ON THE CARD (one bout offered, two who want it; one corner, two shows).
- The whole week for all four is dealt on ONE board, "Club week". Every move shows what it gives one fighter and what it
  costs another, before you let go.
- The old trainer (the guide) reads each fighter and shows the two or three honest pulls, with numbers and dates. He marks
  his favourite. He is not always right.
- The fight itself rewards a style's weapon and punishes a weakness, so "fix or sharpen" is a real argument.

## 3. What the player decides, all on the Club week board
### 3.1 The paid seat, shown as mornings
- Full time = six mornings. Day job = two mornings (Saturday, Sunday). The hidden x0.85 goes.
- Every weekday evening a day-job boxer is not on the pads is a SOLO evening: one light session (0.6 of a morning) or bag
  work on a card (half the pad gain).
- A seat moves only on a Monday and then stays four weeks. The fighter who loses it: mood -8.
- No cap on seats, and "nobody" is allowed. The switch shows the money for each count from MONEYFOUR1 (none: the bank grows
  about $270 a week; one: steady; two: thin by the year's end; four: most clubs close).
- Each fighter has a line of their own: Lucy (call centre) would hand in notice tomorrow. Samira (college) has Tuesday and
  Thursday mornings free even without it. Tess (warehouse shifts) turns it down under $350. Ana (barista) on a seat takes
  five classes a week, not three: the belt and new mat members come sooner (a mat member pays $110 a month, a boxing
  member $35). So the seat is a choice between the best sport play, the best money play and the best people play.

### 3.2 Coached time
- Reid's $450 buys three pad evenings (two places each: six) and TWO mornings, which you place. You have four mornings and
  two pad places of your own a week.
- A morning with a coach present trains in full; alone trains at 60% (CoachAreas.ALONE_FACTOR, already in the game); a
  fighter with discipline under 45 misses one alone morning in three, shown as a chip ("Samira skips mornings alone").
  So the fastest gain in the gym (Samira's defence, +1.25 a week) belongs to the fighter least likely to work unwatched.
- A senior holds: a boxer with 6 or more bouts (Tess) can hold one pad place a week at 0.7 of Reid's gain. It costs that
  evening's own work and lifts mood +3 in the first month.
- Your own pad places are the only ones that train a habit out.
- More hands can be bought: a fourth pad evening costs one of Reid's evening classes (about $115 that week); the full-time
  coach ($1,090) holds five evenings.
- Each pad place, held over a fighter, shows what it buys with a date: "Check hook 22 to 27: usable in 2 weeks, not 3:
  before the York Hall show". The rule already built and measured is switched on: a planned line fires 1 time in 2 at
  Usable, 3 in 4 at Sharp, always at Signature (about 2.8 wins in 100 a step), so pads after 30 still matter.
- Sparring is a pair you pick. Within 5 kg: full for both. 5 to 10 kg: full for the lighter, half for the heavier, and +6
  tiredness for the lighter if the heavier has power 65 or more. Over 10 kg: not allowed (Tess has nobody in the gym: an
  outside mate for $40, or shadow work at 0.85).

### 3.3 What each one trains: COACH SAYS (the guide's read, rebuilt)
The trainer checks in this order and shows what applies:
1. THE SCALE: over the limit with a bout inside 14 days ("Tess is 1.1 kg over. Start today.").
2. FIGHT: the last seven days before a bout. He does not choose the week's shape for you.
3. REST: the week's forecast peak is 80 or more, or the fighter is hurt.
4. A HABIT the next opponent punishes ("drops the right: Croft's overhand"): only your own pads train it out.
5. Then the pulls, side by side, each with a number and a date, his favourite marked:
   - WEAKNESS: a number 6 or more under the fighter's own average that still grows at full pace. "Slip rope: defence 50,
     +1.2 a week."
   - WEAPON: the number the style is built on. "Heavy bag: power 66, +0.6 a week. Finisher at 70: about 7 weeks."
   - GOAL: what the fighter asked for. "Samira asked for the engine: +3.1 in 21 days. Mood +10 or -20."
   - AHEAD: who is next on the ladder and how many answers are owned. "Okoro, Finch. Answers owned: 1 of 3."
   - WHAT IS LEFT: any number still growing at 0.6 or better (Tess's speed).
6. TEACH A PUNCH: only when every number grows under 0.5 a week.
A favourite holds for at least two weeks and needs a full point of clearance to change. One ceiling is used everywhere
(Recruit.ceiling). The guide's light rings the session of the pull you pick, on that fighter's row.

### 3.4 Why weakness against weapon is a real argument (two changes to the fight)
- In fight strength the number a style is built on counts 1.5, the number it lacks 0.5, the rest 1.0 (today it is a plain
  average, so "train the lowest number" always wins). Against a named style one number counts 1.5 for that bout (a pressure
  fighter: your defence; an out-boxer: your engine).
- A number 6 or more under the fighter's own average is a WEAKNESS the other corner can find: from the fighter's third bout
  (there is tape by then) the opponent gets +1.0 in the matching exchanges from round 2, until it is within 3 of the average.
- Test bar before the build: a bot that always trains the lowest number must not beat a bot that picks by weapon, goal and
  ladder by more than 3 wins in 100 over a season, and neither may beat a bot that mixes.

### 3.5 Who fights first: one place, two takers, one corner
Monday of week 1, the matchmaker: "York Hall, Saturday 17 January. I can use ONE of yours: a middle or a welter."
- Tess against Amy Croft (4-3): a likely win, 1.1 kg to lose, $450 at home, a step up the table, teaches her little.
- Samira against Jade Okoro (2-1): meets Samira's "a bout inside 21 days" goal (missed: mood -12), but defence 50 against a
  straight right, and a stoppage loss is 30 days out.
- Pass on both: a favour lost with the matchmaker and the next call two weeks later.
Every offer shows: "This fight week takes from the others: Wednesday's pad place."
In week 3 Ana's open night and the next club show fall on the same Saturday: you corner one; the other gets Reid (his calls
land at 0.85).

### 3.6 Ana is inside the club
She can hold a seat (3.1). Her classes are a dial: 2, 3 or 5 a week at $34 a class. Her open can clash with your corner
(3.5). The mat keeps a tally of the position she lost each match from, so her COACH SAYS has two true drills, each with its
date: "the position the last open found" (fewer losses next open) or "the move closest to owned" (the stripe sooner).
