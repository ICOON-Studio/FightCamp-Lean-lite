# AMATEURWEEK1: amateur bouts need no camp, they need a fight week

Source: the owner's scope change: "make it so amateur fights do not require fight camp and can happen within a week".
Real world: the 8 to 12 weeks is the SHOW's permit; the bout sheet closes 24 hours to 3 days before; a senior amateur can box
about 18 times a season; bouts are matched by weight and experience, often at a week's notice; opponents pull out.
Measured in the game: the mean camp today is 3.9 weeks from offer to bell (HANDSPROBE1); four fighters box 28 times a year
between them (7 each) and bring about $12,600 in ticket cuts (MONEYFOUR1). At a lead of 1 to 2
weeks with today's camps kept they box 37 times (9 each), bring $16,650, and win the same share, 41 in 100 (AMATEURPROBE1).

## 1. The idea in five lines
- An amateur bout comes 1 to 2 weeks out and has no camp. Camps stay for pro fights, title fights and the nationals.
- The player CHOOSES THE OPPONENT from a bout sheet, and the choices pull in different directions.
- Everything the fight week does is paid on ONE bar the player can read: THE TANK AT THE FIRST BELL (0 to 100), plus
  whether the boxer starts SHARP or FLAT in round 1, plus how many plan lines are READY.
- The coach's evenings are the shared money of the week: film, finishing a punch and keeping lines ready all spend them,
  and every evening spent on one boxer is shown by name against the boxer who loses it.
- A routine bout costs one click. A box appears only when it holds a real choice.

## 2. What the player decides
### 2.1 Which bout (the bout sheet)
When a boxer is free, the Fights page shows up to THREE possible bouts from the coming club shows (each weight has 6 to 8
regional boxers; the game's data has five a weight today and uses three). Taking one closes the others. They differ on:
- WHEN: this Saturday week or the one after.
- HOME OR AWAY: home pays the club the $450 ticket cut, and a round closer than one point goes to your boxer 6 times in 10.
  Away pays nothing, costs $40 for the van, and that close round goes the other way 6 times in 10.
- RANK: a win over a name above takes that place; a win over a name below adds a win and no climb; a loss to a name below
  drops two places; a loss to a name above costs nothing. (RankLine shows the line; this is the rule behind it.)
- WHAT YOU KNOW: the opponent's file out of four facts; "best punch: not seen yet" when it is not.
- THE RISK: the chance of being stopped, and the days out if it happens (30).
The whole week this yes will buy is shown BEFORE the yes (section 4, the Bout page).
A pass costs no cash; the next sheet comes in 7 days with different names. Passing the same club twice in a row: that club
does not call for 8 weeks.
Each boxer has a switch on the Fights page: READY, BUILDING until a date, RESTING. The next sheet comes 21 days after a
points bout and 28 after a stoppage: 9 to 11 bouts a year for an active boxer.

### 2.2 The weight (four answers, none free)
Every offer carries an AGREED WEIGHT (the class limit unless asked). The weigh-in is at 4 pm on fight day, the first step of
fight night. When a boxer is over:
- EAT LESS in the last seven days: 1 off the tank for every 0.1 kg lost this way, up to 10.
- TRAIN IT OFF: the extra work costs tiredness (and so the tank, 2.3).
- ASK FOR A KILO: the other coach says yes when their boxer walks over too, or when you hold a favour with that club. Both
  may then weigh a kilo more and the opponent starts with 5 more in the tank.
- HOLD and hope: a miss on the scale means the bout is off and a favour is lost with the host.
Between fights, the weight a boxer walks at decides whether a seven-day bout can be taken at all. The fighter card says so:
"can take a bout in 7 days: yes / no".

### 2.3 The shape of the week (three shapes, the best one depends on the boxer and the opponent)
Heavy legs count on the night without a camp: 1 off the tank for each point of tiredness over 50, at most 12.
A second number, SHARP: pad or sparring work in the last five days keeps it; a full rest week loses it (flat in round 1 only).
| Shape | Tank at the bell | Round 1 | Banked this week |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rest up | full | flat | about a fifth of a normal week |
| Tick over | about 8 off | sharp | about half |
| Train through | about 12 off | sharp | the full week |
Against a slow starter you want round 1 (stay sharp); against one who fades you want the tank in round 3 (rest up). A young
boxer far from the ceiling in a soft match should train through: nine eased weeks a season cost real growth. Tess, near her
ceiling, should ease off. The opponent's file already holds "Slow to start" and "Fades after round 2".
A day-job boxer gets the same three shapes on the club evenings.

### 2.4 The coach's film evening (one a week for the whole club, one question)
Film takes REID'S WHOLE EVENING: both pad places that night. The player picks the night; the board names who goes on the bag
("Samira's uppercut: usable a week later"). One evening answers ONE of three questions, the player's pick:
- the BEST PUNCH (allows a plan line against it),
- a HABIT (the corner's read is right from round one),
- WHEN the opponent starts slow or fades (tells you the week shape and the round to push).
Free facts: at every show one of your boxers is on, you see up to two other boxers at your weights and get one fact on each.
So the choice of show in 2.1 has a second reason: who you get to watch.
A fact goes stale after six months (a news line says a rival has changed). In a rematch the other corner has seen your
winning line: it is worth half.
Two boxers in fight week the same week cannot both have the film.

### 2.5 The plan (lines need upkeep; a nearly-learnt punch can be finished)
- A plan line counts in full only if the boxer walked it on a pad place in the last 14 days (one pad place walks two lines);
  otherwise it runs at 80%. With two pad places a week a boxer keeps four lines ready OR learns something new, not both.
- A step or a tie-up is a "Works" answer. Only a learnt punch or combination can be "Beats it".
- A punch at 21 of 30 can be finished on short notice: "two pad evenings short, three left before the bell". Pad work in the
  last three days before the bout does not count on the night. Those evenings come off the other boxers, shown by name.
- With a full plan, a new line asks which line to drop and shows what the dropped one was worth.

### 2.6 Things that happen to you
- PULL-OUT: 15 in 100 that the opponent drops out between Monday and Thursday. The matchmaker offers ONE late replacement:
  less known, up to a kilo heavier or three bouts more experienced (never across the novice line). Take it, or go home
  (no bout, mood -3, the eased week spent for nothing). The Continue button stops for it.
- TWO SHOWS THE SAME NIGHT: you pick a corner. Reid takes the other; his shouts are right about 6 times in 10.

### 2.7 Routine bouts
"Reid runs this week" fills the standard week in one click (cut by eating less if over, tick over, no film for a known
opponent). The weigh-in stops the clock only when the margin is under 0.2 kg.

## 3. The numbers and what must be measured before the build
- Stakes: the best fight week against the laziest should be worth 10 to 12 wins in 100, and no single job more than half
  of that. NOT MEASURED YET: today's game has no fight week, and a boxer who skips the camp never cuts weight in the
  harness (every such bout was called off at the scale, AMATEURPROBE1), so the stake needs a model of this design: the
  tank-price probe, which also sets the prices to hit the target. What AMATEURPROBE1 did measure: the short lead alone
  (camps kept) gives 9 bouts a boxer a year at the same win share, 3 injuries a year against 2, 2 missed weights against 0.
- Remove for amateurs: the 1.5-point "no camp" loss (BoutRules night_edge) and its line in the Why, the "Set camp" flag, the
  peak bonus, and the coach's Peak week ten days out. One stoppage ban: 30 days, in design and code.
- Let the tape run without a camp (it needs one today). A fight-week phase on the week board that halves pad evenings.
- A "southpaw rounds" evening at another club (7 or more days out, $40, one pad evening, +5 tiredness) so the round-one
  southpaw penalty can still be answered.
- Money: ticket cut only at home. With about half the bouts at home, four boxers at nine bouts bring about $8,000 a year.
  A win away: about +3 members, +2 reputation; at home +8 and +5.

## 5. What is built, in order
1. Probes: AMATEURPROBE1 (done: the short lead alone), then the tank-price probe to hit 10 to 12 wins in 100.
2. Pure rules with tests: the FightWeek rule (tank at the bell from weight, legs and shape; sharp; lines ready), the bout
   sheet (three names, home or away, the pass memory), plan-line upkeep, pull-outs, the rank rule by who was beaten.
3. Clearing the camp rules for amateurs (section 3).
4. Screens, one a task: the bout sheet, the Bout page's bill, the week board's shapes and film evening, the Why's sizes.
5. The golden path: sheet, pick, the bill, the week, weigh-in at 4 pm, fight, the Why with sizes, the rank earned.
