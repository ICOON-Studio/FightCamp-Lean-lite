# R28 Cutman: critic review, corner coach and cutman lens (S423)

## Verdict
Right place, right instinct (the closing eye is the real corner crisis), but as drawn the decision is mostly fake. The cutman option is free and always right, so the first row is not a choice. Stop-or-go is a choice only if the player can see how the bout stands. The map treats a swelling and a cut as one "eye" number; in a real corner they are two jobs with different treatment, and the minute allows one.

## Is the decision real?
- Cutman row: Cutman +10 and keeps the one change; Coach +5 and burns the change; Leave it 0. With Tom on staff, Cutman wins every time: it costs nothing in the minute. "Coach does it" and "Leave it" are dead options. Without a cutman the row is a real trade (the change versus half a band), which is the only time it matters.
- Stop or go: Pull = a certain loss (win chance 0). Send = about a 1 in 8 doctor stop plus a 1 in 5 lasting cut. A certain loss against a gamble you will usually survive: send wins almost always, whatever the cards say. Real corners pull when the boxer is BEHIND, cannot see, or the bout is worth nothing; they send when level or ahead. The screen shows "Level" but no win chance, so the player cannot weigh it.
- Order problem: the map shows stop-or-go "at the bell" for a closing eye, but the cutman lifts 28 to 38, above the 35 line, so the eye is no longer closing. Then what is "1 in 8 after the cutman"? Fix: the lift must leave a closing eye still closing (or the stop-or-go decision is moot).

## Missing (what a real corner has in that minute)
1. Cut and swelling are different jobs. Swelling: ice and enswell, pressure, it grows with time. Cut: adrenaline (1/1000, only once a cut exists), pressure, jelly; it reopens on the same spot. One cutman, one minute: two problems = one job. This is the best source of a true choice.
2. Vaseline before the bout and between rounds (non-petroleum jelly in World Boxing/England Boxing): lowers the chance a cut opens at all. A cutman's value starts at the first bell, not at the first cut.
3. Telling the boxer to protect the blind side: move away from the closed eye, keep the guard on that side, jab out. Costs output. This is the real answer to "send out with a closed eye".
4. The check: "how many fingers?" Coaches test the sight. The player learns the true state at the price of an instruction.
5. The doctor and referee own the final word. The player should see "Doctor stops it if sight under 25" and a referee look, not just a dice roll.
6. The boxer's voice: "I'm fine, coach." Trust and morale decide whether Pull is obeyed. Fighters beg for one more round.
7. Stakes: what the bout is worth (quarter-final, pro record, prospect or veteran). A prospect is pulled sooner.
8. A nosebleed (adrenaline on a swab, pressure) as a third thing competing for the same minute.

## Useless or weak
- "Leave it" with a cutman present: never right. Keep it only when nothing is wrong.
- "Coach does it" when Tom is present: dominated. Make it "Coach takes the second job" at half strength instead.
- "Back training in 1 week" as a Pull benefit: nobody pulls a boxer for that; show the real reason, the eye is safe.
- The flat 1 in 5 lasting cut: it should grow with every punch landed on the cut in round 3, so the risk is earned, not a coin.

## Numbers
- Eye 52 to 28 in one 3-minute round is plausible for a 3x3 loser taking nine on one side; a closing eye in 5% of amateur bouts is believable (headguards, shorter bouts). Pro 12% closing is fair against Nevada 14% eye injuries.
- Cutman lift: ice cannot reverse swelling in 60 seconds. Real cutmen stop it growing. I would change it to: swelling growth next round halved, and a small lift (+4 to +6). That stays honest and keeps the eye closing, so stop-or-go still bites.
- Coach half-lift: fine. But a coach in an England Boxing corner may not treat a cut, only ice swelling: keep that.
- Doctor 1 in 4 without a cutman, 1 in 8 with: low for a CLOSING eye. Between rounds a doctor checks a visibly closing eye; I would use 1 in 3 and 1 in 6, and a near-certain stop at band 15 or below. Drive it from sight, not a flat roll.
- Blind side +25%: believable in direction (lost depth and the unseen hand). Add -10% to the boxer's own output on that side (misjudged range).
- Cutman price: $50 an amateur night is fair; a volunteer in many amateur clubs. A staff quality level (volunteer, accredited, pro) would make hiring a choice.
- Retired = loss: in pro this is a TKO loss on a record, a bigger cost than in amateur. Scale it.

## Screens
Corner minute is correct. The left face card with the eye band is a strong read. Add a band scale with the doctor line (fine 55, closing 35, doctor 15) so the numbers mean something. Live fight tag "EYE x" and the pre-bout stage (Vaseline, the cutman's brief at the tale of the tape) are right additions. After-bout: The Why line is good; add what the other branch would likely have cost ("if pulled: loss, eye safe"). Staff page: show the cutman's quality and accreditation.

## Scores
- Realism: 7/10 (rules and sources are right; swelling and cut merged, lift too generous)
- Clarity in 5 seconds: 7/10 (face and number read fast; the two bottom cards are dense and the order of cutman and stop-or-go is unclear)
- Depth of the decision: 4/10 (cutman row dominated, send always beats pull)
- Excitement: 6/10 (the stop-or-go moment is tense; no pre-bout or in-round payoff)

## To reach 10/10
1. One job for the cutman in the minute, with two problems (a swelling and a cut, sometimes a nosebleed). Different treatments, different failures. Kills the always-right cutman row.
2. Show the win chance for Send versus Pull next to the judges' cards. Pull is right when behind, Send when level or ahead. Without that number stop-or-go is not a decision.
3. Make the cutman stop growth (halve next round's eye loss) with a small lift, so a closing eye stays closing and the stop-or-go still bites.
4. Add "Send and work off the blind side": cuts blind-side hits (+25 to +10) at the price of output (-10%) and the one change.
5. Make the lasting-cut risk grow with each punch landed on the cut in round 3, and the doctor stop depend on sight (stop at under 25), shown on the screen.
6. Add the stakes line (quarter-final, pro record) and the boxer's pushback with a trust number; Pull costs more in a pro record and for a veteran than for a prospect.
7. Add the "how many fingers" check for one instruction to reveal true sight.
8. Cutman quality on the staff page (volunteer, accredited, pro) and Vaseline before the bout as a pre-fight prevention choice.
9. Band scale (doctor 15, closing 35, fine 55) under the eye number.
10. After-bout The Why names the choice and the branch not taken.
