# R28 Cutman: sports-management veteran review (S423)

Read: CRITIC_PACK.md, R28_CUTMAN_DESIGN_MAP.md, cutman_corner.png. Judged as a design map, before any build.

## Verdict
A good moment and a good screen, but the decision is not yet a good decision. Two of the three choices are dead on arrival, and the headline choice (stop or go) is almost always "go".

- WHO WORKS THE EYE has a dominant option. With a cutman on staff, "Cutman" is better than "Coach does it" and better than "Leave it" in every case (eye 28 to 38, and you keep your one change). Nothing is given up. A veteran clicks the gold card without reading. And the map's own pack says the one change is measured not to change who wins, so "Coach does it" also beats "Leave it" almost everywhere. The row is a ladder, not a choice.
- STOP OR GO is lopsided. "Pull Lucy out" is a certain loss. "Send Lucy out" costs: 1 in 8 the doctor stops it (a loss anyway), 1 in 5 a lasting cut, +25% on the blind side, and keeps a win chance above zero. Any player with a real chance of winning round 3 sends the boxer out, every time. Pull wins the argument only in a bout already lost. That is a tutorial, not a dilemma. What makes the real sport agonising (a prospect you must protect against a bout you can win) is not on the card: the cost of going on lands on the career, and the card shows only weeks.
- The information is good but incomplete. The player sees the eye number, the three risks and the cards. The player does not see: the win chance of each path, what another round of 9 punches does to the eye (the forecast), or how close 28 is to the doctor line at 15. 52 to 28 is a 24-point drop in one round, so a repeat is past the doctor line most of the time. The card does not say that, and it is the one fact that decides the call.

## Missing
1. A forecast: where the eye lands after another round like the last (a range), and the three lines (closing 35, doctor 15). Changes the choice from a guess to a read. Highest value.
2. The win chance of each path, with and without the eye ("41% against 48% with two good eyes"). "Round 3 can still win it" is a green tick with no number; the sim player wants the number.
3. A scarce cutman. Give the cutman one minute and more than one wound (the eye, a brow cut, ice on the ribs). Then the choice is triage, and Cutman is not automatically the best card. The 3D corner already has the job row; this gives it teeth.
4. A middle option under stop or go: go on but circle away from the eye (blind side +10% instead of +25%, your output down). Stop or go becomes a dial, not a switch.
5. A towel trigger: a standing order "stop it myself at the next exchange if the eye passes the doctor line". The fight is already 30-second exchanges with a shout each time, so this costs nothing, and it makes the choice live through round 3, not once at the bell.
6. The boxer's say. Heart and morale already exist. A boxer with high Heart asks to go on; a Low-morale boxer who is sent out ignores part of the corner. Pulling a willing boxer costs morale and trust; sending an unwilling one costs output.
7. A tail risk. One small chance (about 1 in 40 at the doctor line) of a serious eye injury (months out, a permanent small loss). It is what makes "protect the prospect" a real argument, and it is the thing a veteran will weigh against a 41% chance.
8. The other corner. The opponent can be cut too. Show the opponent's eye band (the map has this), and show when their corner stops or sends on. A bout won because the other corner pulled out should read as a result with a reason.

## Useless
- "Leave it" when a cutman is on staff, and "Coach does it" when the cutman is there: dominated, as above. Keep the coach card only for no-cutman gyms (amateur gyms without the $50 hire), where it is the real choice.
- The three dots on the eye card ("Cannot see Maria's right hand", "Doctor looks at the bell", "Cutman on staff") mix a fact, a rule and a staff note. Only the first helps a decision, and then only as a number (the blind-side edge).
- "Back training in 1 week" on Pull is true but not a reason; as drawn the green ticks make Pull look as good as Send. Put the layoff for each path in one column instead.
- "Cards so far" as coloured R1, R2, R3 pills without scores. Red and blue are not explained. Show the points (19-19).

## Frequency
From the map: amateur 3 rounds, loser's eye hurt 15%, closing 5%, doctor band 1%. Pro: hurt 30%, closing 12%, doctor 4%. A club with two boxers fighting about 26 bouts a year, half of them losing, sees the stop-or-go card roughly once a year in amateurs, three or four times a year in pros. That is the right rhythm for a drama moment (the Football Manager "serious injury" call is felt for the same reason: rare, and it sticks). The eye-worker row at "hurt" (15 to 30%) is a bout in every four to seven: fresh enough, not noise.
The trouble is the cutman as a hire. $150 a pro night (and $50 an amateur show) for something that matters once a year in amateurs reads as dead money, and the player will fire him after the second quiet month. He needs a use in every bout (see "Connections").

## Connections
- Staff and money: the cutman is already a $150 hire that halves layoffs. The map adds a second job without a reason to hire early. Give the cutman a rating (stars) that sets the lift (8 to 12) and a second use in every bout (a cut opens later when the cutman did the pre-bout work). Then it is a hire a manager argues about.
- Injuries: "Marks easily" is the strongest thread in the map. It should be real: a boxer who marks easily starts every later bout with the eye 8 lower (the left card says it), is shown in the file, and appears as a scouted weakness when an opponent has it. Then "Aim: the left eye" is a plan line, tied to the tape room and the game plan.
- The opponent doing it to you: the symmetry is in the map (Aim offers "The cut eye"). Draw it: the opponent's eye band on the right panel, and a line in the Why when the opponent's corner made the call.
- The fighter's file: a cut stoppage should sit on the record with its own tag, and the Why line the map proposes is good. Add the choice and its price to the History tab.
- Plan and scouting: a rival with "marks easily" found on the tape makes the plan line "work the left eye" worth writing. This is the strongest link to the game's star (the plan).

## Scores (out of 10)
- Look: 6. Dark slate and wood match the kit, the layout is calm and the gold card leads the eye. But the egg face is a placeholder against the painted portraits, the Pull card is half empty, and the Send card is a paragraph.
- Clarity in 5 seconds: 7. "Left eye closing, 28" and the two choices read at once. Lost points for the card saying "Cutman" is gold and pre-picked in feel, and for the number 28 not meaning anything until the player knows the lines.
- Depth of the decision: 5. One dominated row and one lopsided row. The facts are good; the trade is not yet there.
- Excitement: 7. A closing eye at the end of round 2 on a level card is a very good beat, and the player can see the risk. It would be 9 with the forecast and a boxer who argues back.

## To reach 10/10
1. Add the forecast: the eye band ladder (fine, hurt, closing 35, doctor 15) with the eye now, after the cutman, and the range after another round like the last.
2. Put the win chance beside each path, with the two-good-eyes baseline, so the price of the eye is a number.
3. Make the cutman's minute scarce: one job among two or three wounds (eye, brow cut, ribs), so the eye is a trade, not a free gift. Keep "Coach does it" only for gyms without a cutman.
4. Add the middle path: go on and circle away from the blind side (smaller blind-side bonus, less output, a smaller lasting-cut chance).
5. Add the towel trigger so stop-or-go can be decided during round 3, and an exchange-by-exchange "throw in the towel" shout for the player and the same for the AI corner.
6. Add a small tail risk (serious eye injury) and the boxer's say (Heart, morale, trust), so the choice has a person in it.
7. One table, not two cards: choice, win chance, doctor stop, lasting cut, weeks out, morale. Words are in the hover.
8. Make "Marks easily" real: lower eye start in later bouts, shown in the file and as an opponent tell the plan can aim at.
9. Give the cutman a star rating and a use in every bout (cuts open later; a pre-bout job), so the hire is argued, not forgotten.
10. Show the opponent's eye and corner decision; give a retirement win a line in the Why.
11. Cards panel: show the points (19-19) and say which colour is the player's boxer.
12. Replace the egg face with the boxer's painted portrait and the closing eye drawn on it.
