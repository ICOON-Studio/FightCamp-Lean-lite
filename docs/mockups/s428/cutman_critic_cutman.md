Depth here means linked decisions with a real rival. The thread idea (hire, tell, plan, minute, scar, heal, trust) is the right shape and the best thing on the page. But most of it fires only in a cut bout, and the cut bout is rare.

## Where the boxing is wrong or thin
1. **The head-clash rule is useless in the player's first pro fight.** "Survive to round 4 turns a no contest into a decision" cannot happen in a 4-round debut: the bout ends at round 4. Commissions scale the threshold (many use 2 completed rounds for a four-rounder; John to confirm the BBBofC and the commission the game copies). The worked "survive is right when ahead" case does not exist for the first dozen pro bouts.
2. **A clash cut the other boxer then punches is ruled a punch cut.** In practice the referee rules the cause at the stoppage, and a cut that is hunted becomes a punch cut. With your hunt (+1 landed a round per exchange), "head-clash = cards" is almost never reachable. Say who rules the cause (the referee) and that continued punching converts it.
3. **No cut reopens.** Real cutmen do not "close" a cut with a chance. They stop the bleeding with pressure, adrenaline 1:1000 on a swab, a thin jelly layer, Avitene or thrombin if the doctor allows, and it holds until the next clean shot, then it is worse. "Closed" must be a thin patch that reopens on the first solid hit on the spot. That reopen is the drama of rounds 3 and 4, and it kills the idea that closing is a solved problem.
4. **The doctor is not a number at 70.** He looks at where (brow, lid, cheek, lip; lid cuts and cuts running into the eye are the dangerous ones, brow cuts the survivable ones), whether blood is getting into the eye, whether the boxer can see, and whether the corner can control it. The referee calls him (timeout, boxer walks to the doctor mid-round, a minute of tension); the doctor advises, the referee decides. The design only checks "next round". Add the mid-round look. Amateur doctors and referees stop far earlier than pro; a title night gets more rope than a club show.
5. **Swelling is the sister problem, not a side need.** Swelling splits skin and a closed eye is a stoppage on its own (the referee asks "can you see?"). The design halves "eye loss" but never links swelling to cut chance or to the doctor. In the corner it is one person, one minute, one job: pressure and the enswell/ice. It is realistic that the same hands cover cut and swelling, which you did, but not that swelling is a pure lookup.
6. **A cut costs the boxer nothing until the doctor arrives.** Real effect from the first drop: blood in the eye, worse vision, slower defence on that side, and judges who read the bloodied boxer as the loser of a close round. Without a penalty before 70, a cut at depth 30 is a free tag.
7. **Pro corner size.** Pro corners are usually the chief second, the cutman and one more second (a limit of about three in the ring). That is your real "two pairs of hands" rival: hire a cutman and you stop having a bucket man. See my fix 3.
8. **Amateur cutmen.** In England Boxing and World Boxing there is no hired cutman role at a club show; the registered coach works the corner with limited items, and the doctor/referee stop cuts early. The amateur tier prices ($50/$120) and "club closes 50%" are fiction. If you keep an amateur tier, call it "accredited second" and show the doctor stopping much earlier (amateur depth about 45, no race, no 8-second grace). John check.
9. **No "can you see?" test, no mid-round towel.** A chief second can throw the towel in at any moment in pro, not only in the minute. Both are missing options.
10. **Cut side matters.** A lead-side brow takes the jab all night; a rear-side one takes crosses. "Turn the cut side away" has no meaning without a side and stance.
11. **Corner retirement is a TKO loss on the record, with the same suspension.** "Pull out ... a loss, eye is safe" understates it: RTD is a stoppage loss. Pulling out does not dodge the 30-day layoff; stitches add more. Your "Rest 4 weeks" choice then overlaps a layoff the stoppage already imposed (free for a loser, a real cost only after a win with a cut).
12. **Adrenaline and the like are legal topical, but only with a licensed cutman**; "pro, no cutman: coach works it at half strength" is a game rule, not a boxing one (real pro corners are licensed per commission). John check, and keep it as a game simplification if so.

## Arithmetic and consistency problems (these matter for balance)
- **Money does not add up.** A "Name" cutman at $700 on a pro night against a gym share of 25% of a $1,500 debut ($375) is 187% of the share, not "about a quarter". Club at $150 is 40% of a debut share; Seasoned at $300 is 80%. So on tune-ups nobody sane hires anything but None. The design says so, which makes the whole tier decision a one-line lookup: "None until scarred or a ranked night".
- **The hunt swamps the words.** A hunt of +1 landed per exchange x +4-8 depth over six exchanges is +24 to +48 a round. A cut at "45, unlikely" is at the 70 line inside the round if hunted and not closed. The doctor words (under 50 "unlikely") would lie to the player. Either the words must account for the hunt, or the depth increments must be smaller.
- **The range leaks the truth if centred.** "Range 35-50" with the true value always in the middle gives exact depth. The true value must sit at a random point inside the range.
- **Cut rate collides with the pack.** The pack's bout injury roll gives about 6% a bout with 20% cuts (about 1.2%). This page makes 14% a pro bout. One of them must go, or the post-bout injury roll must stop producing cuts, or the layoffs double count.
- **The existing Cutman staff job ($150 a pro night, half the layoffs) vanishes** into the tier table. Keep "halves the layoff" in the tiers; it is the benefit the player pays for every night, not only on cut nights.
- **No starting depth** for a new cut is given. That one number decides everything (closing odds, doctor).
- **The trust table is one-sided.** A truthful "I'm fine" pulled out: -6. Sent out and stopped: -4 (or -8). Sent out and won: +5. So pulling out is never rewarded and is never better than sending out. There is no row for the boxer who says "I can't see". That boxer, sent out and hurt, should be -10; pulled out, +4.

## Dominant strategies found
1. **Never buy a cutman until scar 40+ or a ranked night** (see money). The $50 and $150 tiers pay for a 6-14% event with no continuous return.
2. **Never spend a plan line on "Protect the eye".** It fires in 1 bout in 7 and a plan has 4-6 places. The free shout "Hands up, move your head" (halves hits on the cut) and the minute's "Survive" (switches the same line on) do the same job for nothing. Three defensive tools, one role: nobody needs the line.
3. **Spread 1-1-1** on the cutman's three points. First points are worth 100% on every need, so stacking only wins when the cut is near 70. Given the hunt math, "all on the cut" is right whenever a cut is open. Together: a near-fixed answer.
4. **Pull out is dominated.** A doctor stop and a pull out are the same stoppage loss. The only thing pulling out saves is scar (+2 a landed punch). Going on keeps the chance to win. The reason to stop in real life is the lasting eye (retina, orbit, vision), and the design has no such risk, only scar. Add a small "lasting damage" tail (a few per cent, rising with scar and depth) and the stop-or-go becomes a gamble, not a table.
5. **The three-way choice reads straight off the card:** ahead and clash = survive; behind and punch = race; scar and doctor close = pull out. Every input is printed as a word on the card, and "Why no option always wins" is the lookup table. Add hidden information that the player has to price: the boxer's honesty, the cards that are only an estimate, the doctor's temper, the referee's ruling on cause.
6. **Style change -25% for good** for six weeks of defence drills a boxer would do anyway: nearly free forever. Surgery at $2,500 for -40 scar is a priced no-brainer for any pro near a title.

## One pro four-rounder with a cut in my head
- Pre-fight: hire a cutman? Price vs share says none. No tape session finds a Cutter tell (weapon opens first). Nothing to do.
- R1 (86 in 100 bouts, no cut): minutes show swelling only: one point on the eye, always the same. The cutman card is dormant. The whole design waits.
- R2, cut opens, 14%: this is the only time everything is alive. Minute: three cutman points, three coach points, one of three exits. It is a fine moment (the squeeze of cut and closing eye is real).
- R3: the hunt runs, their corner is hunting, you shout Hands up. Doctor look: not modelled; the stoppage lands without warning.
- R4: if it holds, you are in the last round with a decision to see through; the card says nothing new.
So in most bouts the player has nothing to do with this system, and in the rare bout the answer is mostly read off the card.

## Missing options and useless ones
Missing: mid-round doctor look and a timeout; towel at any exchange; "how many fingers" test; cut location and side (brow, lid, cheek, lip, nose); bloody nose and mouth as common small jobs for the same minute; cutman traits (hopeful, blunt, panicky) that bias his read; scouting the other corner's cutman; prevention (jelly on both brows before R1, cutting scar-prone cuts by about a third); pre-fight medical checking an old scar; a sparring cut in camp that does not heal by fight night; a referee's ruling on cause that can surprise you.
Useless or overlapping: the amateur tier prices; the "Protect the eye" plan line beside the free shout; "Pace Save" beside "survive" (Survive is just Pace Save plus the line); the three doctor words, which are threshold readings of one number; the Head-clasher tell as written, since nothing answers it.

## To reach 10/10 (most important first)
1. **Make closing a thin patch, not a roll.** A closed cut starts at a low depth and reopens to 40+ on the first clean shot on the spot; the opponent's hunt aims at it. This creates rounds 3 and 4 and ends "all points on the cut" as a solved move.
2. **Give the cut and the doctor a place and a person.** Add location (brow, lid, cheek, lip) and side, with a doctor/ref check during the round (timeout, boxer walks over, the corner gets a short window), a doctor who varies (club show, ranked, title), and bleeding into the eye as an immediate cost on vision, defence and the cards. A cut at 30 must already cost something.
3. **Replace the hands split with seats.** A pro corner has three seats: chief (talk and plan), cutman (cut, swelling) and one other (water, breath, stool). Hire a cutman and one seat (the bucket man) is gone. That makes the hire a real trade with the RECOVER points, ties hiring to the minute, and removes the free 3+3. Amateur: two seats, no cutman.
4. **Fix the head-clash rule for the player's path.** 4-round pro: threshold at 2 completed rounds (John to confirm); 6+ rounds: after round 4; and say the referee rules cause, with a clash cut that is punched further turned into a punch cut. Amateur rule per England Boxing (John).
5. **Give "stop it" a real cost on the other side: lasting damage.** A small, rising chance of a lasting eye problem (retina, orbit, vision) when you send out with a deep or lid cut. It is the reason real corners stop fights. Then pulling out becomes a gamble with a price, not a dominated option. Pulling out is recorded as a corner retirement (stoppage loss) with its layoff; say so.
6. **Make the cutman a person and a continuous benefit.** Traits (hopeful, blunt) that bias his read within the range; the range's centre set at random inside it; prevention before round 1 (cuts a fraction of cut chance); the existing "halves the layoff" kept. Then the price is paid for value every bout, and the tier is no longer "None until scarred".
7. **Make small things common.** Swelling, bloody nose, split lip happen in most bouts and use the same cutman card, so the card has something to do in R1. A real cut is the rare big one. Tie swelling to cut chance and to the closed-eye stoppage.
8. **Reconcile the numbers.** Fix the money lines (a Name cutman is more than the whole debut share); make the doctor words account for the hunt; pick one cut rate (this page's 14% or the pack's roughly 1.2% injury cut) and remove the other; state the starting depth; put the cut-side hunts in the tests.
9. **Merge the three defensive tools into two with different jobs.** Free shout (cheap, partial, costs energy), plan line (stronger, only if drilled, costs a plan place) and the minute's Survive that only sets Pace. Give each a distinct condition so none is a duplicate.
10. **Fix trust and healing.** Trust rows for "I can't see": sent out -10, pulled out +4; truthful "I'm fine" pulled out -6 stays. Rest should be free after a stoppage layoff, a real choice only after a win with a cut. Style change must cost a permanent edge (output skill), not six weeks of drills. Surgery price tied to the scar (a high scar costs more and takes longer).
11. **Draw the card as a real picture:** a range bar with the cutman's band, a hunt ghost and a closed ghost; three seats with pips; cards as the corner reads them (dots, a coach's line); the doctor box with "if stopped now: punch / clash"; the exits under a divider with three prices each (win chance, doctor stop, scar and lasting risk); Ready locked until one is picked. See cutman_10_cutman.png.
12. **Probe targets to add:** share of cut bouts where each exit is best (not only "20%"); a four-round pro bout run with and without a cutman; the share of bouts where the cutman tier pays back its price.
