## What is right
- The core is true: bouts are made coach to coach, the host club's matchmaker builds the card, the region only permits, the Supervisor has the last word, and scarcity at weight, age and experience is the real wall. "A reply that can be yes, a counter, or nobody, with a reason" is exactly what a phone call feels like.
- Thin pool shown as a word before you ask is good. Women at 57 and 75 kg really can go a season with two possible opponents.
- Pull-outs from strangers more than from clubs you hold a favour with: true.

## Where the boxing is wrong or off
1. THE UNIT IS WRONG. In England a coach does not ask "a Saturday within N weeks". Shows sit on a fixed calendar months ahead and bouts are made show by show, mostly 2 to 6 weeks before the date, bout sheet closing a few days before. The "8 to 12 weeks" is the host club's permit lead for running a show, not the matchmaking lead. As written, a request open up to 3 weeks plus an 8 to 12 week lead means 11 to 15 weeks to a bout. That kills the first-hour pacing and the nationals entry (5 bouts, entries close week 10). The ask must land on a SHOW from the fixtures list (host, date, card fullness, away or home), not on a window.
2. FAVOURS ARE ONE-SIDED. Real favours are reciprocity: you bring boxers to their show, they bring boxers to yours; a club that sends you three and never takes yours gets a cold phone. "A clean show earns a favour, a pull-out costs one" is a counter, not a relationship. Needs a two-way balance (sent / received) and the host-away split.
3. YOUR OWN CLUB SHOW IS MISSING. Hosting ($3,500) is the strongest matchmaking power a coach has: you pick the opponents. The design never links the two. Hosting should be an ask mode of its own (you ask others to fill your card, and they ask you).
4. INCOMING ASKS ARE MISSING. Half my phone calls are other coaches asking for MY boxers. The player should answer those (yes, no, not at that weight) with a price and a favour.
5. WEIGH-IN. Club shows normally weigh in the morning of the show, with a doctor's check. The pack has the day before; code wins, but a matchmaker will notice. More important: the weight is a negotiation tool the design leaves out: "will you box at 61?", moving a class up or down to find a pool.
6. NO NAMED OPPONENT. Rematches, a rival gym's boxer, or "the girl one place above her in the table" are the real asks, and they tie straight to the regional table that exists in the game. The design only asks for a kind.
7. SPARE LIST / FILL-IN. Cards are overbooked, a third of bouts fall through on the day, and matchmakers keep a spare list ("any bout at 60 kg Saturday"). A boxer who is weight-made and ready is gold on the night. Absent.
8. REPLACEMENTS. "Take it or pull her, nothing lost but the camp's weeks" is too soft. She has already cut the weight, the club loses the ticket cut, and the host remembers. It is a threshold choice the player will solve with one number (win chance above X = take it). Add: ask the host for another, a spare slot at the next show, the sunk cut.
9. SUPERVISOR ON THE NIGHT. The decision is not only the ask: the Supervisor can refuse a bout that looked fine on paper. Put that risk on the kind of fight (step up refused ~15-20%), and let the coach's reputation lower it.
10. ONE COACH, ONE CORNER. Two bouts on one Saturday at two clubs needs staff in two places. Link accepts to the staff and travel.
11. SKILLS BOUT counts 0.5 and is only for the green (juniors, first bouts); useless for most fighters in the sim. Keep, but grey out with the reason when she has more than a few bouts.
12. THE APP at $10 a month is trivia next to $14,600 start cash. It is a gate, not a cost. Make the price a real trade (per ask, or the lost favour and the no-show rate), or the thin-pool boxer simply always pays.

## One season in my head (a 3-boxer club, 14 shows)
Week 8: first ask. Readiness says ready. I pick coach to coach, like for like, window 4 weeks. Reply in 2 days: yes. Accept. Camp. Fight. Week 14: same ask, same channel, same kind. Week 20: same. Nothing changes the answer, so the screen is a vending machine. The player has something to do only when (a) favours run out, (b) a thin pool says nobody, or (c) a pull-out happens. That is three moments a season out of fourteen asks.

## Dominant strategy
Yes. Coach to coach + like for like is best for nearly every boxer in nearly every state while a favour is in hand: free, 1 to 3 days, the fairest match. The show matchmaker is the same with a worse date. The app only wins for a thin-pool boxer, so the thin-pool word tells the player which channel to press: a lookup. Step up is also gated by the readiness band (nearly = like for like, ready = step up), so the "kind" choice collapses to reading the band. Fix: make the best channel and kind change with the state (the calendar, the goal, favours owed, who is booked, how many boxers you have), and make the readiness read noisy and overridable.

The probe bar "no channel best more than 60% of asks" measures what the bot chose, not whether the game has a real choice. Measure regret: in what share of states is each channel the best by (match quality, days, cost). Each should win in at least 20 to 25%.

## Option checks
- Missing: show choice from the fixtures; named or ranked opponent; weight class up or down; ask two shows at once at a price; provisional vs confirmed bout; spare list; incoming asks; host mode; opponent coach's word attached to a reply (so accept or ask again has content); scout the replied opponent before accepting; regional coaches' group chat (the real free, wide, public channel: everyone sees her record).
- Useless as written: the readiness button when it is only a band; the app's flat fee; the Mail / Home duplication unless the reply carries something to decide.
- What each should change: the show chosen sets date and the nationals count; named opponent moves the regional table; weight class changes the pool and her cut; favours spent change the odds, the replacement and the forgiveness.

## Anticipation and reward
A 3-day wait has no tension: base-phase training continues anyway, so waiting costs nothing. Give the wait a clock the player can lose: the bout sheet closing, other coaches asking the same host, the nationals deadline. The reply should arrive as a moment (a Monday page line, a flag on the top bar), not a mail count. Reward is thin: the reward for a good ask is only a fair bout. Make a good ask pay: a name on the table, a favour banked, a ticket cut at home, the Why line praising it.

## To reach 10/10 (most important first)
1. Make the SHOW the unit: ask against the fixtures (host, date, card fullness, home or away, bout-sheet close), drop the free window; replies tie to the calendar; bout goes provisional, confirmed, weigh-in, Supervisor.
2. Make favours a two-way ledger with each club (sent / received, home / away) that can be spent for visible effects (hold a slot, name an opponent, a better replacement, forgiveness for a pull-out), shown on the button.
3. Add targeted asks: a named or ranked opponent, a rematch, a move of class up or down with the pool and cut it creates, and a spare list ("any bout at 60 kg").
4. Make the kind of fight depend on goals and state: nationals count and entry date, regional-table entry at 4 wins, season, tiredness, staff; make the readiness read noisy by coach quality and let heavier sparring improve it. Add the Supervisor's chance of refusing on the night.
5. Make the fall-through a real tree: take the replacement, ask the host for another, pull her, or move to a spare slot at the next show, each with the sunk weight cut, ticket cut and favour effects.
6. Add host mode and incoming asks: your own show is where you pick the opponents; other coaches ask for your boxers.
7. Replies carry content: the opponent's coach's word, record, one hidden tell; a counter that lists real alternatives; a nobody that gives two exits, each with price and odds.
8. Show the pool as numbers (4 women within 40 miles, 2 free before the entry closes), not just a word; show what a class move would give.
9. Give the wait a clock: the bout sheet closing, rival coaches asking the same host, a reply that lands as a flag on the top bar. Make the app a real trade-off (per ask, no-show rate, no favour).
10. Replace the probe bars with regret per state (each channel and kind best in 20+% of states), the nobody rate by pool (30-50% thin, under 10% thick), and a season-length bar: the player faces at least 4 real decisions in 14 asks.
