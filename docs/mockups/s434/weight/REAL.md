# WEIGHT: the real-world numbers (S434, for WEIGHT_MAP_S434)

The owner asked for a stronger weight mechanic in seven parts. Before a game number is set, this page gives the real-world
number for each part, with its source. Every figure is tagged:
SOURCED (read in a primary source on 2026-11-11: a rule book, a commission code or a peer-reviewed paper),
ABSTRACT (only the abstract or index record was read), PRESS (news or a secondary source), or
JUDGEMENT (our reading; not a measurement).
Rule books were downloaded and read as text: England Boxing 2026, World Boxing Nov 2024, USA Boxing 1 Jan 2026,
BBBofC 2026, IBF/USBA Apr 2025, IBF second-day guidelines, and WBC Dec 2024.

## 0. The numbers at a glance (for the map)

|---|---|---|

## 1. Walking weight and the class choice

### 1a. How far above the class fighters walk

| Figure | Tag | Source | Quote |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pro super-middleweight (76.2 kg), 11 contests over 5 years. Each camp started at 86.0-93.2 kg and he lost 12.4 ± 2.1 kg (13.9 ± 2.0%, range 11.3-18.2%). Making weight took 11 ± 4 weeks (4-16) | SOURCED | Morehen et al. 2021, IJSNEM, World Champion boxer case study. https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/15469/ | "Absolute and relative weight loss for each contest was 12.4 ± 2.1 kg (range: 9.8 to 17.0) and 13.9 ± 2.0 %" |

| Earlier amateur samples (Italy, England) cut less: 1.8-4.2% usually, 4.1-5.5% at most | SOURCED (as cited in Zhong) | same | "amateur boxing (1.8%-4.2%)" |
| English amateurs cut 5.16% in one week before championships | ABSTRACT | Hall & Lane 2001, BJSM 35:390, PMID 11726472 | "boxers reduced their body weight by an average of 5.16% of body weight" |

**By class (lighter classes cut and refill more):**
- Pro: "A significant negative correlation was found between the 1st weight and the WG" (Daniele 2016). Lighter boxers gain more back. ABSTRACT, PMID 27564844.
- Amateur: "The lightest men's weight category displayed significantly greater relative BM regain than all other divisions" (Reale 2017, PMID 27834565). ABSTRACT.
- Between seasons: athletes in the lightest classes put more weight back on (Pelissier 2023, doi 10.1080/00913847.2021.2014285; title: "Lightest weight-class athletes are at higher risk of weight regain"). ABSTRACT.
- JUDGEMENT for the game: the walking gap should be a percentage of the class, not a fixed number of kg, with a little extra for the lightest classes.

### 1b. How fast a fighter drifts back up between bouts

| Figure | Tag | Source | Quote |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amateurs regain 3.2 kg (4.8%) after a competition. This is the RWLQ item on the week after | SOURCED (table) | Zhong 2024, Table 2 | "Post-competition weight regains (%) 4.8 ± 2.3" |
| Weight-class athletes (France, 168): lose 4.1% in about 13 days and regain 3.5% in a week | ABSTRACT | Pelissier 2023 | regain "3.5 ± 2.8% of body weight in a week" (from the search abstract) |
| Pro boxer, over his career: fat mass at the start of camp rose from 12.5 kg (contest 1) to 16.1 kg (contest 11), and lean mass fell from 69.8 to 67.5 kg | SOURCED | Morehen 2021 | "progressive increases in fat mass (i.e., 12.5 and 16.1 kg for contest 1 and 11)" |
| Between camps: overeating and low drive to train | SOURCED (anecdote) | same | "his 'desire to keep eating and lack of motivation for training'" |
| An MMA pro found each cut harder and missed weight 3 times out of 14. After two misses at bantamweight he moved up | SOURCED | Kasper et al. 2019, IJSNEM case study, https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/9413/ | "it was becoming increasingly strenuous to make weight" |

JUDGEMENT for the game: after a bout, half the cut comes back in week 1 (about 3.5-5%), then the fighter drifts toward
the walking weight over the next 2-4 weeks. A fighter who cycles hard every bout creeps up one or two kilos over a
career (Morehen).

### 1c. Why fighters move down or up a class

| Figure | Tag | Source | Quote |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fighters go down to face smaller opponents | SOURCED | Zhong 2024 | "compete in a lower weight class, to perceivably compete against athletes of smaller proportion" |
| Making weight feels like part of being a fighter, and like an edge | SOURCED | ACSM Expert Consensus 2021 (Burke et al.), https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/17789/ | "a feeling of advantage over an opponent" |
| UFC fighters who changed class for good: 107, of whom 90 moved down and 17 up. They won 52% afterwards (both directions) against 57.1% before | PRESS | Bloody Elbow 2015, https://bloodyelbow.com/2015/08/05/ufc-fighters-little-success-changing-weight-classes-mma | (figures from the search summary; not peer-reviewed) |
| Heavyweights who had also boxed in lower classes were 19.9% less likely to win at heavyweight | ABSTRACT | Int J Sports Sci Coach 2025, "Somatometric profiles of successful male professional heavyweight boxers", doi 10.1177/17479541241311094 | "were 19.9% [31.1-5.6%] less likely to win a bout in the heavyweight class" |
| California makes fighters pick the lowest class they can make safely, and repeat missers must move up | PRESS | CSAC 10-point plan, 2017 (theScore, Fightful, Sherdog reports; ACSM 2021 cites it) | "intent of CSAC's 10-Point Plan is to get athletes below 10% weight cut" (CSAC minutes 2018, as summarised) |

Takeaway (JUDGEMENT): most moves are DOWN, made for size. Moving up is usually forced, by misses or by a harder body. Either
way the win rate afterwards is about a coin flip, so the choice is a real trade-off and neither move is a sure buff. This
supports the owner's part 1 as written.

### 2b. Does the size actually win fights?

| Figure | Tag | Source | Quote |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pro: the weight gap between opponents averaged 1.94 kg (largest 7.1 kg). Neither the gain nor the gap changed outcomes. Bigger gainers trended toward losing and bigger gaps toward winning, neither significant | ABSTRACT | Daniele 2016 | "Both WG and WD did not affect match outcomes" |
| Amateur boxing: regain made no difference to who won | ABSTRACT | Reale 2017 (100 boxers) | "No differences in BM regain were found between finalists and nonfinalists, winners and losers" |
| Judo: the medallists regained more | ABSTRACT | Reale 2016 (judo), via ACSM Table | "Medalists had greater RWG than non-medalists" |

JUDGEMENT for the game: the prize is real but small. Show it in kilograms ("about 3 kg heavier on the night") and keep its
effect modest: a little power and chin. Real data cannot even prove it wins fights in boxing, so the price must be able to
cancel it.

### 2c. The price during the camp: training on a cut

| Figure | Tag | Source | Quote |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long energy deficits lower power and strength. At about 0.7% a week the losses were minimal | SOURCED (cited in ISSN) | ISSN 2025, citing Garthe et al. | "minimal losses in performance measures ... at rates of 0.7% per week" |
| Pro boxer over the camp: low energy availability (under 20 kcal/kg lean mass) for most of each camp | SOURCED | Morehen 2021 | "low daily energy availability (i.e., <20 kcal.kg-1 FFM) throughout the majority of each training camp" |

numbers are the weekend effect above (non-athletes) and the UFC miss counts in 5. JUDGEMENT for the game:
- A low-discipline fighter slips on about one weekend in two. Each slip is +0.3 to +0.6 kg (about 5 times Racette's daily
  figure, for a fighter eating out freely).
- A high-discipline fighter almost never slips.
- A hard cut (over about 1% a week, or the final days' water) drops mood by an amount the player can see.

### 4c. The last day's water (TRAINING_MAP T8: "up to 2% water")

| Figure | Tag | Source | Quote |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2-3% from fluid and sweat, at levels seen in normal training, does little harm when followed by a proper refill. Over 3% does real harm | SOURCED | ACSM 2021 | "(e.g., 2% to 3% BM reduction) are likely to have few disadvantages ... (>3% BM reduction) may have substantial negative implications" |

## 5. Graded consequences of missing weight

### 5a. Professionals

| Rule | Tag | Source | Quote |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nevada: missing the contracted weight costs 25% of the purse, paid to the opponent. Nothing is due if the fighter makes weight within the hour or is 1 lb or less over | PRESS (Justia copy, current to Dec 2024; the official NV site blocked the fetch) | NAC 467.522, https://regulations.justia.com/states/nevada/chapter-467/general-requirements-for-unarmed-combatants-and-other-licensees/section-467-522 | "forfeits twenty-five percent of his or her purse"; "weighs 1 pound or less outside the agreed limits, no forfeit" |
| BBBofC: 1 hour to make weight, then no more tries. The miss is reported and "disciplinary action may be taken" | SOURCED | BBBofC 3.2.4, 5.13 | "he is allowed 1 hour from the time of the weigh-in to achieve the correct weight" |
| IBF: 2 h to make weight. A champion who misses forfeits the title on the scale; the bout may go on for the vacant title (only the challenger can win it) | SOURCED | IBF 1.A.1 | "the title shall be forfeited on the scale" |
| IBF: if the challenger misses, the champion keeps the title even if beaten | SOURCED | IBF 1.A.2 | "the Champion will retain their title whether he wins or loses" |
| BBBofC titles: the same rule | SOURCED | BBBofC 22.2.2-22.2.3 | "the Champion shall retain the title even if he loses the contest" |
| WBC: over any safety-check limit, the WBC may revoke the sanction or impose fines, suspension, removal from the ratings or a vacated title | SOURCED | WBC 4.14 | "imposing fines, suspensions, removal from the ratings, revocation of challenger status and opportunities, or vacating a title" |
| Real case: Ryan Garcia, 3.2 lb over 140 (April 2024). The fight went ahead; he forfeited "upward of $600,000" of his purse, plus a $1.5 M side bet, and could not win the title | PRESS | ESPN, https://africa.espn.com/boxing/story/_/id/39976264/sources-ryan-garcia-misses-weight-deal-fight-devin-haney | "Garcia would forfeit upward of $600,000 of his purse to Haney" |
| MMA case: three misses in 14 pro bouts, each costing 20% of the purse, and every bout still happened | SOURCED | Kasper 2019 | "all contests still occurred after conceding 20% of his purse" |

So the usual pro outcome is "the bout goes on and part of the purse goes to the opponent". Today the game calls every
miss off (Bouts.gd:328-345) and charges the club BoutRules.MISSED_WEIGHT_COST $300 (BoutRules.gd:88), with a 0.05 kg
tolerance (BoutRules.gd:87, :171-172). The owner's ladder matches the real one: a little over = the bout goes on and a
share goes to the opponent; well over = off.

Where to put "well over" (JUDGEMENT, from the rules):
- Nevada's free band is 1 lb (0.45 kg).
- The IBF fight-morning limit is +10 lb.
- The BBBofC 3-days-out title gate is 3%.
- A game line: 0.5 kg over or less = the bout goes on with a 20-25% purse share to the opponent; about 2% over or more =
  the opponent's camp calls it off.

### 5d. The crash cut: injury and health
