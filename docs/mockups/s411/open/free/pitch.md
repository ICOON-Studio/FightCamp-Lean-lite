# THE WEEK IS A COMIC PAGE

**The idea.** Your career is a graphic novel. Every week is one inked page of seven panels, one per day. You do not read a menu; you lay out the page, then the game plays it as a short montage and turns the page.

**Minute to minute.** Monday: a blank page, with the week's story beats already pencilled in (the coach's draft). You drag painted cards into panels: roadwork, bag, pads, sparring, rest, a weigh-in. Panel size is intensity (a big panel is a hard day). A panel that tells the story gets a small shine: the fight date is circled in red marker. Press the red NEXT PAGE stamp. The week plays as seven quick painted beats; bars change on the page as the boxer's drawing changes (tired eyes, a heavier jaw, a new stance). Surprises (a twist card, an injury) arrive as a torn-in panel you answer.

**Reaching things.**
- Training: the page itself.
- Booking a fight: a poster arrives pasted on the brick-wall panel; tear it off to accept or screw it up to pass.
- Fight plan: a storyboard strip of balloons ("When she presses -> step back") you arrange like a shot list.
- Fight night: the climax splash page. Between rounds you pick one of three balloon cards, thumb in the foreground.
- Money: a ledger tucked in the page's corner, a coin-stack panel when it is tight.
- News: a glued newspaper clipping.

**Year three.** The shelf of past issues. Flip back to see a boxer's drawing change over years, rivals who recur as characters, a fight that went wrong on page 12. Each fighter is a series; your gym is the publisher.

**Hard to build.**
- Playing a week as a montage from painted panel templates plus fighter variants (keep it to a few dozen reusable shots).
- Drag-to-panel layout that stays readable.
- Keeping real numbers visible without it turning into a form (numbers live on hover and in a margin ruler).

**To reach 10/10**
1. A real week-to-montage system: about 40 painted shots composed from fighter, move and place, so no two weeks look alike.
2. Panel size, order and neighbours must change outcomes (a rest panel after a hard one), so layout is a decision.
3. Fighter art that visibly ages and wears (cuts, weight, mood) across issues.
4. Honest numbers: margin rulers and hover cards on every panel.
5. The issue shelf: flip back through past pages, with the Why shown as an annotated page.
6. Hand-lettered type that is real text, not generated lettering.
