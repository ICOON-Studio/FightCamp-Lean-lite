# The Coach's Eyes

**The one idea.** There is no interface. You are the coach, seen through your own eyes, standing in your gym. Every option is a real object: the clipboard in your hands is the week, the rotary phone is offers and the promoter, the corkboard is the fight poster, calendar and rival news, the cash tin and bills on the desk are money, a boxer who walks up and waits is a conversation. Look at a thing, pick it up, act. Painted, warm, first-person; the gym floor lives behind the boxer.

**Minute to minute.** You walk the floor (a few fixed spots: floor, office desk, ring, door). A boxer steps up and wants an answer. You write their week on the clipboard by sliding coloured tags into days. The phone rings: you lift it, the promoter speaks, you take or pass. Time moves with one gesture: tear off the calendar day.

**Reached how.** Training: clipboard plus pad-mitts on the wall for combinations. Booking: phone. Fight plan: a chalkboard you carry, arrows you draw, then it goes in your back pocket to the corner. Money: tin, bills, rent notice on the desk, true numbers when you hold them. News: the newspaper and the corkboard.

**Fight night.** You are on the apron. Water, ice, towel and chalkboard are in your hands; you choose which to give the fighter, as in the second image.

**Year three.** Faces age, scars stay on posters, the wall fills with your champions and rivals; rooms change as you spend. The game is people and consequences, not tabs.

**Hard to build.** First-person painted scenes need many handmade angles per boxer and per state; an object-driven week editor must still show real totals; accessibility and one-click reach from anywhere (needs a fast "look at" shortcut); AI lettering does not replace real text.

## To reach 10/10
1. A real, readable week clipboard with true totals, fatigue and the goal line, not decoration.
2. A fast hotkey ring (1-5 jump to desk, floor, ring, phone, board) so reach stays one click.
3. Consistent painted boxer art in every state (fresh, hurt, happy, low mood).
4. The gym floor shows the people and training actually planned.
5. Hover/pick-up gives real stats on every object, nothing ornamental.
6. Corner minute fully diegetic, with the Why shown as a tape-up replay board.
7. Persistent wall: poster, trophies, news clippings accumulate over years.
