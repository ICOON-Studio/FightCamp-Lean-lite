# PEOPLE: the game is faces, not rooms

**The one idea.** No building on the main screen. The screen is a painted graphic-novel poster of your boxer, with the next opponent's torn fight poster behind her. A strip of faces on the right (coach, promoter, landlady, your second boxer) is how you reach everything. The road to Saturday runs along the bottom as three posters. Navigation is people: you do not open a menu, you turn to someone.

**Minute to minute.** Press CONTINUE and the week plays as short scenes. Each face wears a mark when they want you. The coach leans in and you set the week with big painted dials (morning goal, night, diet). The promoter slides a poster across; accept it and it joins the road. The landlady arrives with the envelope: money is a conversation about rent, with two or three painted choices. News is the rival's poster getting new graffiti. Fight week turns the weigh-in, press and face-off into playable scenes (the moment image): stare, smile or walk changes nerve and trust. Fight night uses the existing corner calls over close-up faces.

**Reaching things.** Training = coach. Booking = promoter. Fight plan = tape on the opponent's poster (pull a tell, pin your answer). Money = landlady. Roster = the strip. News = the rival's wall. Real stats sit one click behind each face.

**Year three.** Each boxer and rival has a story that grows: scars on the portrait, poster history, grudges. The wall becomes your career as a gallery. Faces age, and the road gets longer and more dangerous.

**Hard to build.** A large amount of painted, consistent portraits (expressions, ageing, scars); keeping scenes short so they never feel like a dialog tree; making dense sim decisions (30 stats, planner) readable in a few words; scripting and writing enough scene variety.

**To reach 10/10**
1. A portrait pipeline: one art style, 5 expressions per person, wounds and ageing layers.
2. Scenes that are playable, not text: the dial, the tape pull, the face-off each with real stakes.
3. A fast "ledger" back door so power players reach every number in two clicks.
4. Animated life: breathing, light flicker, poster peeling as fight day nears.
5. Writing and variety for scenes so week 40 differs from week 4.
6. Sound: the strip's faces speak one line each.
