## 1. Two weeks played in my head
CAMP WEEK (Lucy v Kovacs, 19 days out, Danny Flat)

- Tue. NOTHING TO DO. The tape lands by itself (band 38-52 to 41-49, a new problem). The game's Tape Room is not on this day.

  always right when Thursday will stop. The sparring itself (who, how hard, what the rounds are for) is "no choice". In a real camp
  that is the night the corner decides most.

- Sat. Ringside (one of two bouts) or my own hands for a boxer. REAL: this fight against the next one. Best call of the week. Next
  Mon: three lines, the habit row is back.
Count: 2 real decisions (Saturday; Thursday only when the rival claim can truly land), 2 obvious, 2 empty days.

## 3. Anticipation and reward
- Thursday: nothing to look forward to; the reveals are Tuesday and Wednesday and both arrive without me. Friday has no outcome, so
  I never wonder how a session went. Clocks count down to a known result: dread, not suspense. Next Monday's returning rows work.
- Fight night: the plates. "PRESSED: STEP BACK" lit or grey, traced to a named evening. I would wait all camp for this.
- Next season: house line, plan place, senior, a name on the ask, a habit struck off. Right kind (a slot, not a number). But three
  of them hand back the scarce thing (an evening, hands), so the squeeze loosens as the player earns; only the 5th plan place adds
  demand. And nothing opens before roughly bout 5: weeks 1 to 8 have no "opens next" that lands.

## 4. What rests on invented numbers
THE ONE THAT CARRIES IT: hands are scarce. Reid has two evenings, each takes ONE claim (today a pad night splits 50/50 or 70/30), a
senior is worth half and a bag a quarter. Offers usually come 8 to 12 weeks out; a move is usable in week 2, a combination in week
4. Unless tape arrives late, every answer is owned long before taper and no clock bites. If scarcity fails (seniors near Reid, or
THURSDAY2 finds under two problems), the squeeze, the clocks and "comes back Monday" are a queue whose order does not matter. Left:
the card and its ghost, firing by level with plates, the Why, ringside, blocks. Good, and thin.
Others: the band (the card lies), the habit under 2 points (decoration), Flat against Steady (the club claim is a label).
IF THE THURSDAY STOP FOLDS INTO MONDAY: the camp week is Monday and Saturday with two reveals between. The Wednesday tap then stops
being dead (the only way to react to news) and must stop the clock. Things to do drops a point; depth does not move, because
Thursday's pick was mostly forced anyway.

## 8. To reach 10/10 (most important first)
1. NEW AND RAW, OR OLD AND SHARP. Every tape find opens on Tuesday in the Tape Room with two answers side by side: teach the move that
   Beats it (fires 1 in 2 by the bell, costs evenings) or point a line already owned at it (Works, fires 3 in 4, no evening, Friday
   freed for the habit or the other boxer). Always offered, never a fallback. Tune so neither wins 7 times in 10 across opponents.
2. WEDNESDAY'S ROUNDS GET ONE JOB, AND IT COSTS THE BODY. Before sparring pick what the rounds are for: an answer under fire (counts
   as one evening of pads for that line), the habit (partner told to punish it), engine rounds for the camp stat, or light rounds.
   Hard jobs cost +6 tiredness, so the scale's roadwork (+5) has a rival inside 80. Reid's sentence reports on the job picked. The
   START / NOTE tap goes.
3. THE QUIET WEEK NEEDS A CALL EVERY WEEK. From a block's second Friday: CALL THE TEST (three rounds with the senior or a partner;
   pass chance shown as a band; pass = met early, evenings freed; fail = the mood cost and a week) or ONE MORE WEEK. Saturday is then
   chosen on Friday, after the result. In the open spar the player picks which line is shown; showing less learns less.
4. A HABIT HAS STAGES. Two evenings: holds while fresh (rounds 1 and 2, worth a band this fight). Four: gone. Show its price against
   this opponent as a band. Now the habit can win a camp Friday.
5. DRAW THE CAMP TO THE BELL. Every evening before taper as a slot (Reid, you on Saturday, senior, a paid pad man at $120 so the
   club's money reaches the camp); problems sit on slots; next week's Monday shows in ghost. Give the bag a body price. Beside it
   THE OPPONENT: "Kovacs on tape, 2 of 3 found", each line tied to its problem, the third saying where it can be found.
6. FRIDAY HAS AN OUTCOME. A pad night can click (one evening saved) or stall, at odds shown on the tile and moved by tiredness and
   mood. One line on Saturday morning. Clocks stop being arithmetic.
7. THE CAREER LINK MUST BITE. A problem that ran out leaves a mark with a rule: the missed answer is half learned (one evening
   cheaper next camp) and rival corners list the gap until it is closed. A quote in the Why is not a consequence.
8. REWARDS THAT ADD DEMAND, AND SOONER. One small unlock by bout 2 (the second sparring job, or the Saturday pad man). Later unlocks
   add problems as well as hands: the 5th plan place, a second boxer's camp overlapping, a longer plan for longer bouts.
9. Block tiles speak about THIS boxer, without "right when". Name the twist card's place in the week. Fix the day counts, the ghost
   band's width and the pronouns in screen text. Faces at 76 px or more on the fight card: the two boxers are the week's poster.
