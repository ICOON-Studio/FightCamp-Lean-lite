# Fight display (approach B) - critic review, lens: a new player at a first fight (S419)

Read: CRITIC_PACK, CRITIC_ACCESS, FIGHT_HUD_BRIEF, fight_b.png (and fight_b.html for its parts), fight_a.png, fight_c.png. No scripts, no other critics.
Drawing: `fight_critic_newplayer.html` / `.png` (1920x1080). Numbered gold circles in the drawing are review markers, not part of the display.

## 1. The look: 6/10 (not capped)
Hard fails checked. Type under 22 px: none. More than 45% covered: B is about 41%, passes. A bare number with no icon or word: the 41/49/63/58 and 74/90 badges sit on a body part (an icon of where), so they pass narrowly; the three numbers under the tanks (58, 72, 61) have only a small icon far above them, and the punch counts "4/12, 3, 1" have a glove and two body icons but no sign of what "4/12" is. A new player cannot tell "landed of thrown" from "round of rounds". These two rows are the weakest. A list where a picture should be: no. A meter whose meaning needs a manual: the three tanks with dashed band ticks at 35/55/80 and no word for them, yes (the ticks mean nothing at first sight). So no cap, but two near-fails.
What is good: dark, warm, one face and one body per boxer, a real picture of where the damage is, big tag "BIG HOOK", the call card with icons. It belongs to the game's wood-and-chalk look.
What hurts: it is TWO full human figures per side (face disc plus full body) on a screen that already has two boxers in the ring. A new player looks at the ring, then at a body that is not the one in the ring, and has to work out that the red body is the red boxer. The six numbered circles read as a score grid, not as "where it hurts".

## 2. What it shows, element by element (new-player view)
- Six damage places and numbers: CHANGE. Show colour first (green, amber, red outline on the place), a number only on a place that is hurt (under 55) or worse. A green 90 teaches nothing and costs reading time; four numbers around a face read as noise. The player learns "which place is in trouble" and does: pick Hands up (head), Gloves high, or move the plan off a bad side. Pips (small dots) for how many shots that place has taken this fight are the missing piece (hint 1).
- Face close-up: KEEP. The best thing on the screen: a face that gets a purple eye teaches damage faster than any number. The player learns who is hurt in half a second.
- Cut and closing eye: CHANGE. Add a lid icon (half-closed eye) on the eye place and a small red cross that says "the doctor looks at this". Today the closing eye is a purple blob. The player decides: stop worrying about the head and shout cover up, or accept the risk.
- Three tanks (energy, legs, guard) and band ticks: CHANGE. Fill colour = band (green fine, amber hurt, red critical), faint amber and red zones painted in the tank instead of white tick marks; one big number only for energy; legs and guard get their number on hold (a key) not always. Learn: how much is left. Do: Breathe / Save pace.
- Drain arrow: KEEP, and give it a ghost. "down 4" is a bare number to a new player. A dashed ghost outline at the level the tank had at the bell shows the fall with no reading.
- Punch counts: CHANGE. One ring (landed over thrown, so the player sees "he lands a third of what he throws"), the number inside the ring, and head and body counts next to the ring. Learn: who is landing and where. Do: body or head call.
- Exchange blocks, clock: KEEP. Six blocks in the corner colours are the best "who is winning this round" read on the screen. Put round tokens (a red dot for a round won) beside them.
- Fight bar (tug of war): KEEP, add two faces at its ends and a crown on the leader. A new player does not know what "FIGHT" means as a word; a crown on a face is read at once.
- Plan chip: KEEP. Learn: what the fighter is supposed to be doing. Missing: it should show if the plan is being followed (hint 8).
- Scouted habit chip: KEEP, and echo it in the ring (hint 4). Unscouted: a dashed chip with an eye and a question mark.
- Unknowns on the opponent: KEEP the dashed ring plus "?". For a new player a blank is scarier than a number, so show what IS known: the pips of the player's own hits are honest and need no scouting.
- Corner's call and its three effect chips: CHANGE the chips. "+12, -2, -1" are meaningless numbers to a new player. Use arrows: one to three green up arrows for good, red down for cost, on the icon of what changes (shield, bolt, face). The call name stays big. This is where a new player learns "Hands up is for when my head is hurt".
- The two other calls: KEEP but add a tiny target icon (here a torso with a green arrow for BODY) so the call's AIM is visible on the card.
- Timer ring: KEEP. Learn: I have only one call per 30 seconds. Do: choose now.
- Big-shot burst: KEEP. Add a screen-edge flash (the side of the boxer who was hit) for 0.4 s with the thud sound.

## 3. THE HINTS (ranked by how much they change a decision)
Each: TRUE / SHOWN (no sentence) / DONE.

1. TRUE: the head has taken four shots and the guard is falling. SHOWN: the head place is amber with a number and four small red pips beside it; the guard tank has a dashed ghost at the level it started (88) and a gold dashed forecast above the present level (what the shouted call will add). The call card's guard arrows (green) line up with that ghost. DONE: shout Hands up; in the corner minute pick Gloves high; training next week: Defence. Always honest (own boxer, all known).
2. TRUE: the call just shouted is not working. SHOWN: a LAST chip at the right of the call bar with the call's megaphone and a red cross (or a green tick), and the chip pulses once at the next bell. DONE: shout something else or accept the cost (a Low-morale fighter stops listening). Honest always; costs nothing to scout.
3. TRUE: the opponent is tired and the guard drops when tired. SHOWN: blue drops falling off the portrait, a hatched energy tank with a bracket (a range), the TIRING chip, and the opponent's silhouette in the ring goes a shade greyer for the round. Unscouted: only the range, never a number; an unscouted guard stays a hatched tank with "?". DONE: Let your hands go, Go to the body, Go for the finish; plan: Push pace next round.
4. TRUE: the opponent always drops the right hand after the jab (scouted habit). SHOWN: the chip on the panel, and when the habit FIRES the same eye icon pops over that glove in the ring, gold dashed ring, small arrow down, held 1.2 s, with a soft chime. Unscouted: nothing pops, so the player only gets the lesson after a film or a tape study (the payoff for scouting is seeing it light up). DONE: a combo ending on that side, the Set-up call, a plan line for next week.
5. TRUE: a body place has taken shots (the liver is the right ribs). SHOWN: a pip lands on the place with a ripple of white rings that fades in 1.5 s (drawn in the picture at the ribs); a second hit adds a pip and the ring pulses amber; at the hurt band the place gets a number. Honest both sides: pips of the player's own shots on the opponent are known without scouting; the opponent's place state stays "?". DONE: Hands up with Elbows in (body guarded), or go to the body yourself if the opponent's ribs have three pips.
6. TRUE: an eye is closing and the next clean shot may bring the doctor. SHOWN: lid icon under the eye badge; its number ring thickens and a red cross sits at the 15 mark; below 25 the lid closes more and the cross pulses. DONE: the cutman job in the corner minute (close the cut / ease the eye), Hands up, or accept the stop risk.
7. TRUE: the call the player is about to shout shows its aim. SHOWN: a gold dashed forecast on the tank it changes (drawn: guard), or a gold dashed bullseye on the opponent's body place for a body call. DONE: choose between three calls by looking at which target lights, not by reading.
8. TRUE: the plan says "Work the body" but only one of the four punches landed on the body. SHOWN: the plan chip carries a four-pip strip under it; amber when under half followed. DONE: shout Go to the body or change the plan in the corner.
9. TRUE: the opponent has landed more in the exchanges. SHOWN: the six exchange blocks (blue, blue, red) plus the crown on the opponent's portrait in the fight bar. DONE: more pace, a different call.
10. TRUE: energy runs out around round 3. SHOWN: a tiny empty-marker (clock icon) on the energy tank, at the level the tank will reach by the end of the fight at the present drain; it moves up when the drain gets worse. Honest on your own boxer; for the opponent it is a range, the marker drawn hatched. DONE: Save pace, Breathe, shorter combos; a training note for conditioning.
11. TRUE: a hit has hurt (rocked) the boxer. SHOWN: the portrait wobbles, its ring turns white for 1 s, the whole panel dims 20% and the corner of the screen on that side flashes red. DONE: Tie up, Hands up; in the corner Recover (clear the head).
12. TRUE: the opponent is the player's mirror: legs gone means no movement. SHOWN: the leg tank turns red and the opponent's silhouette in the ring flat-footed (a shadow under the feet smears). DONE: Jab and move, keep the range; the opponent cannot get away.

Honest only when scouted: 4 (the habit chip and its pop), the opponent's guard and legs in 3, any number on the opponent's damage places. An unscouted one is a dashed ring or hatched tank with "?" (never a made-up number); what the player has seen with their own eyes (pips, who won exchanges, the tiring clue) is shown for free so the opponent never looks empty.

## 4. Missing and useless
Missing: pips on every damage place (where each shot went, honest unscouted); the call's aim shown on the target (hint 7); the last-call result (hint 2); the plan being followed (hint 8); a leader mark on the fight bar (a crown); rounds won on the cards in tokens; a hold-to-show-all-numbers key for the player who wants the numbers (energy, legs, guard, all six places); the ring figure's own state (hurt, tired) so the screen's pictures agree.
Useless for a new player: the three numbers under the tanks (move to hold-to-show), the tick marks on the tanks (replace by painted zones), "+12 -2 -1" (arrows), the second full body next to each ring figure (if the face and the pips are kept, one body per side is enough, and it could shrink 25%), the word FIGHT on the bar (faces and a crown), and the opponent's three question-mark circles around the face if they show nothing known (show a single dashed ring instead and pips when hit).

## 5. The moments (one line each)
- Knockdown and count: the whole screen desaturates, the down boxer's panel fills red edge to edge, a big count 1 to 10 in the clear centre (the only time the centre is used) with a ten-segment ring; the call bar becomes "stand up" icon only; the opponent's panel shrinks.
- A cut: the cut place on the face drips a short red line and the place pulses once; the doctor cross appears; a cutman icon lights in the corner minute.
- A hurt (rocked) boxer: wobble, white ring, panel dims, edge flash (hint 11); the Tie up call pulses gold.
- Last 10 seconds of a round: the clock goes red and ticks larger, the six blocks' last one pulses, a bell icon fills, the call bar closes (no more shouts).
- Corner minute opening: the ring blurs, both panels slide to the middle, the bottom bar becomes the corner card (what hurt, one fix, recover) and the timer ring shows the minute.

## 6. Scores (0 to 10)
- Look: 6 (good faces, wooden look; two bodies and number badges read as a spreadsheet of circles).
- Read in two seconds: 5 (who is hurt: the face yes; who is winning: only by learning the blocks; what to shout: the call card is clear but its numbers are not).
- Depth for the longer look: 7 (six places, three tanks, counts, habit, effect chips: all real; the unknowns are honest).
- How well it teaches: 4 (nothing connects a cause to a call; no hint shows why a call is best; the player sees numbers but not what they mean).

## 7. HOW TO REACH 10/10 (most important first)
1. Replace the effect-number chips and the numbers under the tanks with arrows and colour; keep one number per element only when it is the number that matters (energy). Numbers on hold with one key.
2. Number badges only on a place that is hurt; every other place is a coloured outline; add hit pips on every place, both boxers (honest unscouted).
3. A forecast ghost: the shouted call shows where it will change a tank or which opponent place it aims at (gold dashed), and the last call shows its result (tick or cross) at the next exchange.
4. Make the scouted habit fire in the ring (eye pop over the glove, 1.2 s, a chime) so scouting has a visible payoff; unscouted opponents show a dashed eye chip.
5. A leader mark: a crown on the face at the end of the fight bar, round tokens for rounds won, the six blocks coloured by corner; remove the word FIGHT.
6. A ghost on every tank for the level at the start of the round plus a small "runs out" marker, and the drain arrow with a short number.
7. One ring for hit rate (landed over thrown) with head and body counts next to it; tooltips in words on hover, never always on.
8. A doctor cross on the closing eye (lid icon; ring shrinking at 15).
9. Shrink the bodies 25% so the screen is 35% covered, keep the face discs large.
10. Moment states: edge flash, wobble ring, red clock, count in the centre, corner minute slide.
