# Dream fight screen, game-feel critic (S419, second drawing)

Picture: `docs/mockups/s419/fight_dream_feel.png`. Base is the first drawing (fight_critic_feel); everything untagged exists today (the live data of FIGHT_HUD_BRIEF_S419; the ghost bars, ripples and ring pulses are display effects on data the fight already has). Gold NEW = needs something the game does not have. 33 words on screen (names aside), smallest type 22 px, about 56% clear. Not drawn but missed: see the end.

Ranked by how much each is missed (for a player of fighting, sports and management games):

1. HIT-TIED NUMBER (comet) + HIT-STOP LINES. What: the damage number travels from the impact in the ring to the damage ring, arriving with a 0.08 s freeze-frame and a 4 px camera nudge. Needs: the fight event (place, size, time) must exist per punch and match the 3D hit time; the 3D fight "only shows the settled round", so the event list must be exported with timestamps. Why expected: every fighting game ties damage to the blow. Changes: the player learns which punch cost which place. Size: medium (data export from the settled round plus a display queue). Unsure whether the settled round already stores per-punch time: the pack only says six exchanges of 30 s.
2. COMMENTARY LINE ("ERIN SMELLS BLOOD"). What: one short line of TV commentary, max 3-4 words, triggered by states (rocked, cut, tired, comeback). Needs: a state-to-line table, no flavour text rule exists (pack: "no flavour text"), so lines must be facts in other words. Why: sports games all have a voice; it carries hints in words so the display can stay quiet. Changes: the player gets the state named once, 2 s. Size: small.
3. MOMENTUM ("smelling blood") METER. What: a flame bar showing who is on a run (last 3 exchanges plus damage plus tiredness); an arrow toward the boxer who has it. Needs: a momentum value in the fight rules that also changes results (a run raises the winner's edge a little), or it is only a readout of the existing edge; the pack lists no momentum. Why: boxing is about runs; players feel them already. Changes: decides push or save, and the Go-for-the-finish call. Size: medium (rule plus balance test).
4. HOLD AND RELEASE SHOUT. What: HOLD a call and release in a gold window for a hard version (more effect, more energy cost); the pack gives one call every 30 s with no timing, only a "NOW!" set-up. Needs: input timing, a sweet-spot window, and a rule for the hard version. Why: a player wants a hand in the moment; the 30 s wait is long for a screen. Changes: the call becomes a skill, not only a choice. Size: medium. Risk: the owner's rule says the 3D never decides; the hold must tune the settled round's edge only.
5. JUDGES' CARDS REVEALED (R1 three squares). What: after each round three small cards flip one by one (1 s apart, a short pause before the last), colour of who won. Pack: scorecards after each round are "held back"; scoring exists after the bout. Needs: per-round judge scores shown live (the three judges' rule already exists; the owner held it back so the owner must say yes). Why: judging is the TV drama of boxing. Changes: the player knows the real score, so the call to push or hold is honest. Size: small to medium.
6. CROWD ENERGY METER + SOUND BED. What: a meter of crowd heat that rises with exchanges won and big shots, and the crowd sound follows it (low murmur to roar). Needs: a crowd value and audio layers (the sound engineer seat exists; crowd roar and loop already in assets). Why: it makes the hits feel big and gives the 3D a mood. Changes: little in decisions (a home crowd could add a small judge edge: no rule today); mostly feel. Size: small for display and sound, medium if it affects scores.
7. TWO MORE BODY PLACES (jaw, solar plexus). What: six places become eight; the jaw is the knockout place, the solar plexus the wind place. Needs: the damage model has six places; two more places change the knockdown and count rules. The owner asked "do we only have 2 hit points on the whole body?"; more places answers him. Why: players expect the jaw and the solar plexus in a boxing game. Changes: new aims for calls (Go to the body splits into ribs and belly) and for the cutman. Size: large (rules, tests, balance).
8. REPLAY PICTURE-IN-PICTURE. What: after a big shot or at the bell, a small replay of the shot with a pin on where it landed, 2 s, slow motion. Needs: the 3D fight must be able to replay a moment and mark a place; it plays the settled round today with skip controls. Why: every fight game replays the knockdown. Changes: confirms what the player saw and what it cost. Size: large for the 3D, small as a still frame.
9. STAKES BANNER (NATIONALS SEMI). What: a label of what the bout is worth, with a trophy icon; for a rivalry it names the rival. Needs: the data exists (nationals bracket, pro rank, belts); only the display is new. Rival gym and rematches are "agreed, not built". Why: pressure tells the player the round matters. Changes: reasons to push or hold; stronger payoff. Size: small.
10. ACCESSIBILITY MODE (eye icon). What: a toggle for colour-blind patterns on the tanks and rings, bigger numbers, reduced flashing, and a sound-cue-only mode for hints. Needs: a settings entry (settings exist: text size, sound sliders; nothing for this) and pattern fills for each colour. Why: shipped games must offer it; the flashing hints need an off switch. Changes: more players can read it. Size: small to medium.

BUILD FIRST: number 1, the hit-tied number with hit-stop. It is the teacher of the whole display: every other effect follows from knowing which punch did what. It also makes the commentary (2), the replay (8) and crowd heat (6) cheap, since all need the same per-punch event list.

NOT DRAWN, also missed: the doctor's stop-or-go card at the 15 line (agreed, not built; unsure whether the 3D corner has the choice); a corner minute strip with fixes; an "adapted" note from round 2 (agreed, not built); a post-fight Why told as three pictures (plan, against you, next); an energy breath/fog and sweat visual that follows the tank.
