## 1. Two weeks in my head
CAMP WEEK
- Mon: one real choice (who gets Reid's Friday). Then "Start week". Good.
- Tue tape lands: spot one thing. A decision only if the Tape Room offers more than one answer; if the best answer is obvious it is a cutscene.
- Wed sparring: NO decision, Reid speaks. Dead day by design (it feeds Thursday).
- Thu: the one change. Real on paper, but see section 2: the card prints Buys / Takes / If it waits with exact numbers, and a "Reid's pick" tag. That is a lookup.
- Fri: Danny's offer expires (take, pass, ask later): real. Pads are spent as chosen Monday/Thursday: no new input.
- Sat: ringside or pads. Real once, then a habit.
- Sun: nothing, correctly.
- Next Mon: three-line readback. Fine.
QUIET WEEK
- Mon claim, Tue tape from ringside, Wed sentence, Thu "does not stop", Sat every other week. Realistically 1 decision (Mon) + a maybe. From week 3 I hand this to "Reid's week" and it plays nearly the same. The design admits it ("I let that stand"). That is the chore weeks, and a quiet week is most weeks in a long career.
AUTO-BUTTON CANDIDATES: Monday claim in a quiet week (best line toward house line, forever); Saturday pads; Thursday when Reid says "nothing new".

## 2. Is each decision real?
- Claim: an answer the plan needs. Right case exists (lands before taper). Real. Chain is true: tape -> answer -> evenings -> taper -> fight. This is the one measured chain, and it is good.
- Claim: a habit. Right case: opponent punishes it. DEAD risk: the card shows "+3 wins in 100 every fight" next to "+6 against Kovacs"; once numbers are printed the player just reads the bigger one. Always-answer-first unless the answer cannot land in time.
- Claim: the best line toward a house line. Right in a quiet week only. In camp it is dead. In a quiet week it is ALWAYS right (no competition except newcomers). CHORE.
- Claim: the other boxer. Real only via "feels second"; good, that is a warning with a deadline.
- Claim: newcomers. Right after a win, wrong when few joined. Pure arithmetic on a visible number: CHORE-ish, but cheap and honest.
- Thursday changes: move Friday to answer, move to habit, scale mornings, swap line, no change. The scale option is the only one in a different currency (tiredness); that cross-currency tension is the best thing on the screen. The "weaker, free line" option is dead unless the answer cannot land; it is the escape hatch, always the worst. "No change" is right only when green: it is a status, not a choice.
- THE LOOKUP PROBLEM: the card states Buys, Takes, If it waits as exact figures. The player can sort three cards by one number. A real decision hides the exact number (ranges, Reid's confidence) or makes the options not comparable. Right now "Reid's pick" is on the answer card: the game tells you the answer, then asks you to press it. The design's own kill list says a one-in-ten lookup kills it; I expect Thursday to be a lookup 6 times in 10.
- Chain check: one week's choice shows up next week (owned answers), the fight (peak camp, firing) and the career (house line, plan place). That link is real for answers and the house line. It is only stated, not felt, for habits, newcomers and ringside: the weakness arrives as a number nobody sees until a Why.
- The week is locked and all three problems arrive on the same Thursday with one slot. Scarcity is real; but the three are not rivals by nature: tape and sparring problems are independent of each other and of the scale. Forced pick of "which to carry" is artificial unless a wrong pick has a later, visible, owned consequence.

## 3. Anticipation and reward
- Looks forward to: Tuesday's tape, Wednesday's sentence, three camp lights, fight night. Good rhythm: a reveal each day.
- Next Monday: three-line readback. Fine, small.
- Season: draw, house line, fifth place. These are real new choices (not bigger numbers). "Opens next" is the best reward element. But: a house line is a status; "its evenings come free" is the only new choice and it is tiny. The fifth plan place gives a real new decision. Good.
- Fight night: plate when a line fires is a strong payoff. Peak camp is already paid.
- Weakness: the reward for a good week is mostly a +number in a forecast. The player never SEES the week's choice succeed until the bout. I want a win-chance forecast on Home that moves when I pick.

## 4. What rests on invented numbers
Most of it. The one that collapses it: ONE CHANGE A WEEK ON THURSDAY, i.e. that most camp weeks open at least 2 real, non-obvious problems. Its own measurement (20 camp weeks) can kill it; if under two problems or best-change-obvious 7 in 10, Thursday becomes a lookup and a calendar remains (Monday claim + Saturday). Secondary: how often a line fires by drill level; sparring-night sentence novelty (1 in 3).
What is left if it fails: Mon claim, tape, ringside, the card in pictures, the plate on fire, house line, plan places. That is a decent skeleton, maybe 5/10.

## 8. To reach 10/10 (most important first)
1. Hide exact payoffs and make options incomparable: show a band ("+3 to +8") and a risk, drop "Reid's pick" and the Buys/Takes/If-it-waits table; Thursday is a trade of different currencies (fight, career, body, money), not a sort by wins.
2. Put a live fight-night forecast on Home (win chance with range, answers owned by taper, listens, peak-camp lights) that moves in ghost when a choice is hovered. The player must SEE the week's choice change the fight.
3. Make the quiet week a different game: a real standing decision each week (which line to carry toward house, whether to sell a banked weakness into an ask, who to put on a show bill) with a trade-off that is visible. Otherwise auto-button from week 3.
4. Make Thursday's problems rivals, not independent: tie two to the same resource (Friday evening AND the tiredness budget) so picking one visibly starves the other next week, with the starved one coming back on Monday as a named problem.
5. Let a bad pick bite later: carry the "if it waits" outcome into the Why and next camp ("late answer, 2 of 4 owned") so mistakes are learned from.
6. Give ringside a real stake: pick which of two bouts to watch with different odds (a known name vs a possible next opponent), not one button.
7. Fix the dead options: "weaker, free line" gets a case where it is the best (a late short-notice offer); "no change" shows its reward when taken on a green board.
8. Screens: collapse Danny's row, cut Sun, move the senior lock into Opens next, no label-value tables, plain words ("4 stay, 2 leave").
