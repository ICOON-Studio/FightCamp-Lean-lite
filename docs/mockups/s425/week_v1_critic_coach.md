# Week design v1 - critic review, head coach of a small amateur club (S425)

Read: brief, CRITIC_PACK, WEEK_DESIGN_V1_S425.md, week_v1.png and .html, training_b.png. Not read: scripts, tests, WEEK_DEPTH_*.

## 1. Two weeks in my head
CAMP WEEK (Lucy 19 days, Danny Flat, six newcomers)
- Mon: twist card, Reid's three evenings (3 claims), sparring night (partner, how hard, line), Danny's offer card. Four boxes,
  roughly nine picks, before the first click of CONTINUE. Real decisions: Monday pads for the missing answer, the offer.
  Chores: how hard (Working every time), line tested (the plan already says which line is new), partner when Danny is Flat.
- Tue: nothing. Honest, but it is a dead day. Wed: a tell flag, sparring runs. Nothing to do, only waiting.
- Thu: the call. Two changes from four options. If the report says "right drops after the jab", the answer is always "fix habit"
  plus one. I would auto-button this from week 3.
- Fri: Danny's offer (take / later / pass). The one decision with a real clock.
- Sat, Sun: nothing. Monday: read the board back (a reading task, not a choice).
- Fight week: corner note, swap or keep. This is the best moment and it is two weeks away.
QUIET WEEK: Mon lays three evenings (pads on a back-up, newcomers, eye on open sparring), asks for a fight. Thu fires "only on
news". Real club reality: a quiet week with one boxer and a coach is pads, bag work and the class. Here it is a menu of
three claims where the answer is "whichever Reid does by default" unless one boxer is booked. Days with nothing to do: Tue,
Sat, Sun in both weeks. Auto-button from week 3: sparring "how hard", newcomers' night, "Technical/Working/Hard".

## 2. Is each decision real?
REAL (a case where it is right, and it links onward)
- Pads on a missing answer before taper: right, and it shows up in the fight (2 to 14 wins in 100). The only chain that is measured.
- Danny's offer card (tune-up for a Flat boxer): right, links to morale and to the career. Real coach thinking.
- Hold a line for the bigger night (regional, national): real in principle, hangs on the measurement that may kill it.
- Taper bars the claims: "no pads in taper" makes the calendar bite. Good.
DEAD or NEAR-DEAD
- Both tire as a sparring partner: in the picture Danny is Flat and no Reid for two weeks; a Flat boxer sparring a camper is
  wrong, so the option is dead on this very screen. It is right only when both bells are over 10 days off.
- Clubmate vs presser: clubmate is the free default; presser costs $160 and is right only when the opponent is a presser
  and known. Before scouting it is a coin flip. Dead until the tape room has run.
- Technical: only right for Flat/tired; Hard: wrong inside 10 days (this boxer: 19 days, taper in 12). So the live row on the
  screen has one sane value, Working. Three buttons, one answer.
- Half-and-half pads: wrong for combinations, the design's own text. Dead.
- Second coach: bad buy with two boxers by the design's own wording. Dead until the third boxer.
- Extra night on Reid's free evening: right under 70 tiredness, wrong at 74. A threshold, not a choice.
- "Show / hold / swap / keep the read line": only meet a corner that reads tape from bout 3 or 4 (Doyle scripted). Until then
  every seen-line choice is unplayed. Four options, zero consequence in the first season.
CHORES: the sparring night's three picks weekly; newcomers' evening ("4 of 6 stay" is a result, not a decision); Monday review
readback; the Thursday call when the report just names the habit. Class off for $115 is a one-time trade, not a rhythm.
CHAIN CHECK: pads -> missing answer -> fight is real. Sparring -> report -> Thursday change -> fight is stated but the middle
link (the report) is a printed sentence ("won 4 of 6") whose truth the game cannot yet produce. The film-on-you link to the
fight ("waited for from the first bell") is stated; today the measured fact is that being read costs 6.5 points and nothing
wins it back. A link that exists only as a design sentence.

## 3. Anticipation and reward
- Thursday: a phone icon and a report. I look forward to it only if it can tell me something I did not know. In a camp week,
  it is a habit I could see on the pads myself. As a coach I would watch the sparring and know. The design makes me wait
  for what a real coach already sees. Anticipation is manufactured by delaying the information.
- Next Monday: green board "4 landed". That is bookkeeping, not joy.
- Fight night: strong, unchanged by this design except the corner note.
- Next season: the house line (a line that reaches Signature becomes named). This is the best reward in the design: it opens
  a choice (a free evening) not a bigger number. Everything else is a tick or a green chip.
Reward is mostly a bigger number or a green mark. Only the house line, a kept newcomer who becomes a prospect, and the
readiness to take a short-notice offer open a new choice.

## 4. What rests on invented numbers
The one that collapses it: a seen line is waited for from the first bell, and a drilled back-up wins points back. Today a
swap wins nothing back. If the first two measurements fail, the design's own kill list says "5 or 6". What is left: the
week board from dates the game already has, the card's four prices, the pads claim. That is a good calendar, not a mechanic.
Second: Thursday as a fixed day with exactly two changes. Third: sparring reports that name a line's win rate (the game has to
earn those words). Invented numbers I do not mind: feels second -5, promise +5/-10.

## 5. Wired and shown
Right: pads on Training with a coach row, Playbook marks seen/held, Fights offer card, Club People rota. Home as the front.
Wrong: Home carries the week board, Reid's Friday, sparring, plan, offer, the call and the bell. The sparring night is a
Training thing and Lucy's plan is a Playbook thing; both are copied onto Home and he cannot act on Lucy's plan there (bars
with white markers, "Seen", "Fri ?", "Held", "reads tape"). Shown but not actionable: "Last week: 4 landed" (a score),
"class on +$115" chip, the 12-day taper chip, "Unseen" tags on a plan he reads only on the Playbook.

## 6. The screen, element by element (1920x1080, type 24+ px, but see the hard fails)
- Nav, date chips: KEEP. "Last week: 4 landed / 1 late": CHANGE into one tappable chip that opens the review.
- THIS WEEK board: KEEP the idea (best thing on the page). CHANGE: day columns are right but the row cells carry glyphs
  (clapper, clipboard, scale, envelope) with labels like Tell/Report/Offer that need a manual. Add one line of plain words
  under each ("Tell: you see his right hand"), or use a face and one word. Dashed Thursday column with an empty hatched Reid cell
  is dead space. The '?' cell on Friday is the strongest element: grow it.
- Reid's Friday: CHANGE. Three claims each with one tick line and one cross line is a text block of six sentences. Make it
  three big picture cards (face, number of wins gained and lost as a bar), not "Danny Low: 70%". "Newcomers: Both wait"
  means nothing. KEEP the "nearest bell" Reid pick as a default stamp.
- Danny's Tune-up: four tiles (85% Flat, 2 of 4 by taper, 1-2 seen, 0 wks clash) = a table of label and value rows in
  disguise. "1-2 seen", "0 wks clash" and "85%" with a speech bubble all need a manual. CHANGE to one sentence-sized picture per
  price: a bar for chance, four pips for answers, an eye or no eye, a calendar strip for the overlap. Hard fail.
- Thursday call: CUT as drawn. Two empty dashed slots "1, 2" and a row of four icons "2 changes" is a blank form the player
  cannot understand before Thursday. Show it only on Thursday; on Monday it is a one-line promise on the board.
- Sparring (Wed): CHANGE. Three faces, three words, a three-step seg row, two chips: ok, but "Step back", "Reid watches",
  "Both tire" are unexplained. Seg buttons repeat Training's intensity. Move to Training; Home shows "Wed: spar a presser, $160".
- Lucy's plan: CUT from Home. Five rows of bars with markers is an Excel list; it is the Playbook.
- Bell and button: KEEP. The best clear block. "stops Thu" next to it is honest.
Hard fails: table of label/value (Danny's tiles, plan rows), a block of text (Reid's Friday), labels that need a manual
(Tell, Report, "clash", "seen", "Held"). Look capped at 4. Too much for one screen: seven boards, thirty-plus items.

## 7. Scores (0 to 10)
- Look 4 (wood and slate right, but crowded, icon-only labels, tiles as table).
- Clarity in five seconds 3 (I cannot tell what I do first; there is no one thing).
- Depth 5 (pads, offer and the corner swap are decisions that lead into each other; the film on you is a design sentence).
- Things to do in a week 6 (Mon, Thu, Fri; Tue, Sat, Sun empty; two of the four are chores).
- Anticipation 6 (the Fri ? and fight week; the Thursday call anticipates a report I could read myself).
- Reward for playing on 4 (green ticks and numbers; the house line is the exception).

## 8. How to reach 10/10 (most important first)
1. Put ONE thing in the middle of Home: the week board with Friday's '?' as the question. Everything else is a drawer
   from a face or a day. Home shows no Playbook, no plan bars, no sparring picks.
2. Give Tue, Sat, Sun something: a Tuesday "watch a tell on the tape" with a cost, or a Saturday club night that pays or
   costs (ticket, members, a fighter's nerves). A week where three days never ask is not a week to play.
3. Make the Thursday call carry news I could not see myself: the engine faded in round 3, the right hand that drops, the
   scale 0.4 kg off. Only these three, each as a picture; two changes from three or four, each priced in wins and evenings.
4. Prove the swap-back rule or drop the read. Measure first. If the swap wins nothing back, replace "waited for" with a
   simpler fact: the corner has seen this line, so it costs a few points, and a back-up costs one evening. One rule.
5. Cut dead options: Both tire (when Danny is Flat), Hard inside 10 days, half-and-half for combos, second coach until the
   third boxer. Show an option only when it can be right today; grey it with the reason, not a label.
6. Danny's offer as four pictures not four labelled tiles: chance bar, four pips (answers), an eye, a calendar overlap bar.
7. Reward that opens a choice: the house line frees a Friday; a kept newcomer joins the Friday claims as a new card.
8. Replace "4 landed / 1 late" with a Monday card of 2 or 3 things: one landed, one late, one surprise with its reason.
9. Real-club truth: let the coach talk after the sparring night. Reid's one sentence per boxer is worth more than a tick.
