FIGHTCAMP STRUCTURE DESIGN (S395, 2026-11-07)
=============================================

Written by Claude as designer. Paper only: no game code, assets or tests were changed, and no Godot job was
run. It goes with docs/GAME_BIBLE.md and docs/PLAYER_PATH.md. Where this page differs from them it says so,
and nothing here replaces them until the operator has answered section 9.

THE VERDICT, ON ONE PAGE
------------------------

Where we stand. If a reviewer scored FightCamp today as a finished game, I estimate about 4.4 out of 10. On
the same scale, Football Manager 24 is 8.0, Corner King, our closest live rival, is 5.8, and World
Championship Boxing Manager 2 is 3.1. This is my estimate from real reviews and our own critics, not a
measurement: no stranger has played the game. An earlier critic gave us 6.5, but that was a preview, which
judges promise and not length.

The biggest gap is not the menus. The game stops. A boxer who turns pro gets no more offers. Nobody ages or
retires, and nobody new arrives in the pro ranks. Our audience plays for hundreds of hours; we run out in a
few. Adding more fights is not enough either: "repetitive" is the reviewers' main verdict at 7 and under. Each
stage of a club's life has to bring new problems.

The second gap is the manager's real job. In boxing, the manager's skill is choosing who to fight and when,
and we cannot ask for a fight at all. The top-rated bad reviews of World Championship Boxing Manager 2 say the
same thing: no rankings, no choice of opponent, "What's the point?"

The third gap is size. The game is built for one or two fighters. The coach already writes the week, but
offers, camps, plans and fight nights all wait for you, and the pages do not work for eight.

Corner King changes the picture. We read its installed files. It already ships most of the long game this
design proposes: about 1,450 pros, 15 organisations, title fights, asking for a fight, poaching, retirement, a
hall of fame, seven starts and an editor. Those are the price of entry now, not selling points. We will not
beat it on size.

What we have that it lacks. Across this kind of game, players' first complaint is that numbers do not visibly
cause results. In Corner King nothing tells you why a fight went as it did, every boxer shares one brain with
no styles, you cannot author a plan, the gym can never fail, and rival fighters appear never to improve. Our
corner minute, our "what decided it" screen, our Playbook and a club that lives on members' dues and can
really close are the answer. So the game must be deeper, not wider.

The design in six moves.
1. Make the plan matter more, make fighters fight differently, and show it working in the ring.
2. Add matchmaking: ask for a fight, see the whole road ahead, scout before you say yes.
3. Make the world live, on a smaller scale than Corner King's: boxers age, improve, retire and arrive, with a
   pro ladder and titles. The save never has to end.
4. Make it a stable of up to eight, where you choose what to run yourself and leave the rest to the coach.
5. Give each year a shape: fixed dates, a year review, awards, a record book.
6. Finish the agreed menu cut, with the first fight at about minute 25 and the "why" screen by minute 35.

Seventeen missing systems are proposed for your approval in section 5.7, each with a build size. Section 5.9
sets us against Corner King. docs/STRUCTURE_MOCKUPS_S395.html shows the main screens beside the real game.

What we can honestly reach. About 7 to 7.5 if all of this is built well. That is a ceiling, not a forecast,
and plans always score better than games. Corner King is also improving every month. A 9 from a reviewer who
loves this niche is possible. A broad 9 average is not a sensible target: of the games checked, only Crusader
Kings III and Against the Storm reach 91 with a real panel of critics. The better target is what small sims
reach on Steam: Football Coach: College Dynasty sits at 95% and Pro Wrestling Sim at 97%, both with plain
looks.

The cost. My rough guess: 6 to 10 working sessions to reach the stranger test, then 30 to 45 more for the long
game, before Early Access. I raised this after seeing how much Corner King needed. That delay is the biggest
price in this document, and it is why question 1 is yours.

Order. Structure, then strangers, then the look. Your decisions are in section 9.

What separates 9 from 7: tests 2 and 3. Reviewers of a 7 say "good, but it repeats". Reviewers of a 9 tell you

What looks matter for: less than we have been spending on them. Plain looks were forgiven again and again;
slow clicks and hidden causes were not. But our operator's rule stands: the game must not look broken.

How realistic is 9? Football Manager's best in the editions checked was 85, Motorsport Manager 81. OOTP's 96
and 89 rest on 5 and 8 critics. Indie sims rarely get enough reviews for a score at all, so what matters most

Why each low score
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

- Long game, 2. Pros get no offers (scripts/state/Bouts.gd:115). Age is used only to check who may enter a

  fixed list of rivals in each division with records that move.
- Optional depth, 5. The week is already delegated: the camp phase writes the coach's week, it repeats by

  for you, and there is no per-fighter "coach runs it".
- Own stories, 4. The signing card is the best screen we have, and Records exists. But the ambition on the
  card does nothing, the opposition barely changes, and the world forgets.
- First hour, 4. Scored 3 before the guide; never rescored. See below.
- Trust, 4. No autosave in the code. Settings and Handbook are "not built yet" pop-ups
  (scripts/ui/Main.gd:363, :365). The release plan also lists a DEBUG window title and a camp bonus counted

1. Opens Settings to set resolution or text size, and gets "not built yet". This happens before they have

5. THE STRUCTURE DESIGN
-----------------------

5.1 What the game is
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

You are the head coach and owner of a small boxing club. You are not a promoter or a tycoon. The coaches you
hire are your assistants, so "Coach runs it" means your assistant runs that fighter for you. You find
unknowns, decide what they become and who they fight, stand in their corner, and read honestly why it went the
way it did. Your club lives on the members who pay dues.

What a reviewer must remember: "the corner minute, and the screen that tells you why." Both seats kept the Why
panel as the thing to remember, but my brief pointed them there, so that is agreement and not independent
proof. The evidence is the niche's first complaint.

Store line, for testing: "Run a real boxing club. Pick the fights. Work the corner. See exactly why you won."

5.2 The loops by time scale
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Times are targets until strangers are timed.

* Scale: Day
    What happens: Sessions run, gauges move, the world fights. No daily job
    What the player decides: Nothing, unless a red pin stops the clock (fight, weigh-in, injury, a last-day
      offer)
    Real time: Seconds
    Ends with: The next stop, named on CONTINUE

* Scale: Week
    What happens: Monday page, then CONTINUE
    What the player decides: Answer pins. Step in on any fighter. Quiet week: one click
    Real time: 1-2 min quiet, 5 busy
    Ends with: A tease: what is next

* Scale: Fight cycle
    What happens: Offer or ask, scout, accept, camp, plan, weigh-in, fight, why
    What the player decides: Who and when. Camp focus and cost. The plan. Corner calls. What to train next
    Real time: 20-30 min for a fight that matters; 2-3 for one you simulate
    Ends with: What the result changed

* Scale: Fight night
    What happens: Tape, rounds, corner, result, why
    What the player decides: Watch full, highlights, or sim. At each break, pick from the corner's few options
    Real time: About 6 min for 3 rounds; about 20 for a 12-round title fight on highlights
    Ends with: Three lines on why, and one button to act

* Scale: Year
    What happens: Fixed dates: regional, nationals, title dates, new arrivals, member renewals, year review on
      the first Monday
    What the player decides: Who to push, who to rest. What to spend on. Who to let go. Which three club goals
      to chase next
    Real time: 3-5 hours with a full stable, if you play about 6 fights and simulate the rest (52 weeks at 1-2
      min, 6 fights at 25 min, 18 at 3 min). Playing everything takes 10 or more
    Ends with: Awards, record book, new faces

* Scale: Career
    What happens: A boxer's whole road: unknown to title to decline to retirement
    What the player decides: When to step up. When to stop. Who comes next. A retired boxer can join as a
      coach
    Real time: First title about 15 hours (a guess); save never has to end
    Ends with: A place in the club's history

The rules that make this work:
- Days have no decisions. S390 found a daily pick is a solved chore, and Dominus is now being marked down for
  waiting. Both seats wanted a daily step; I reject that below.
- The clock keeps the agreed Monday stop. One change: once a player has a stable, a setting lets CONTINUE run
  to the next pin and skip quiet Mondays. Reviewers punish slow seasons, and a player 200 hours in should not
  click 52 times a year.
- The roster sets the pulse. One boxer fights a few times a year. Eight fighters means a fight about every
  fortnight. This is the real answer to "nothing interesting between fights", and it only works if most of
  those fights can be run in minutes.
- Not every fight is an event. You write the plan in two minutes and simulate the night. Tests 4 and 5 punish
  forced watching. See the plan rule in 5.3a.

5.3 The six big changes
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

1. The hook bites harder. Measured: good plan 59%, no plan 45%, bad plan 41%, and it flips about one exchange
a fight. Target: in a close match, at least 30 points between a good and a bad plan, with the better-trained
fighter still usually winning. A bad plan must hurt. Ring position must show on screen, so the plan is seen
working rather than read in a caption. Pressure as a score that cuts the opponent's output, and a stamina
ceiling that shrinks with clean hits (both from S392) give the Why screen real things to report. Hints must
stop giving the answer away.

2. Matchmaking. The manager's real job, and a loud complaint about World Championship Boxing Manager 2. The
rules that stop it being a solved choice are in 5.3a.
- The Road: one picture of the whole ladder, club shows to world level, with your fighter on it. It shows on
  Fights and on the fighter's Overview.
- Ask for a fight: pick a fighter, see three to five opponents in reach, each with risk, money, rank effect
  and date. Promoters say no if you have not earned it. Offers still arrive by themselves.
- Scout before you say yes: a small cost in money or days narrows the range. Today the range only narrows
  after you accept.
- One good number: replace "33-90%" with a plain band plus what is unknown, for example "Likely, unless her
  power is real. Unscouted."
- Few terms: date, weight, purse. Deep contract screens are out: reviewers want trade-offs, not forms.

3. A living world. This needs rules and data, and almost no art, which is what we are best at producing.
- Generated boxers in every division, amateur and pro. They age, peak, decline, retire, and new ones arrive.
- Fictional organisations and belts: area, national, continental, world. Invented names keep us clear of real
  bodies, and many TEW players prefer a good fictional world. This includes clubs: scripts/state/Data.gd still
  names real English clubs, which the release plan's legal list does not yet cover.
- Careers end honestly: age, plus the lifetime damage ledger with a fights-left forecast (S392). This gives
  every fight a cost beyond the week.
- A record book that remembers: champions, title reigns, best fights, rivalries, your club's history.
- All of it held in plain data files, so fans can edit it later.

4. A stable, not a planner. The coach already writes each fighter's week from the phase, and the player edits
exceptions. That stays. What is new is a per-fighter choice of Coach runs it or I run it, covering offers,
camp choices and twist cards as well as the week. The S354 mechanics stay as the hands-on layer: mornings
summing towards a goal, evenings on moves and combos, the plan. Coach-run training must be good, and slightly
worse than a player who cares. Coach skill sets how much worse, so staff have a real value. Limit: 3 places at
the start (two boxers and the BJJ athlete), up to 8 with staff and upgrades.

5. A year with a shape. Fixed dates everybody can see. A year review on the first Monday. Three visible club
goals a year, chosen from a short list, such as "reach 40 members", "win an area title", "develop a national
medallist". Club standing rises through named tiers, and better prospects and better offers come with it.
Success is judged against your own size, so a small club can have a great year.

6. Fighters as people. Rolled traits as numbers on one sheet feeding one shared brain (S392). Habits you only
discover by watching, which can improve (S390, the fresh part). The goal agreed at signing is a promise: keep
it and loyalty grows; break it and they may leave. A named rival coach and gym that crosses your path for
years. Age, injuries and a fighter who sometimes says no. Twist cards stay only where the choice changes
something you can see.

5.3a Three rules that hold the design together
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

These answer the critic's three holes. Numbers are targets to test, not decided numbers.

Rule 1: the plan beats the coach, and you can skip the fight and keep the plan. Otherwise a good coach makes
your plan pointless, or a bad one forces you to watch everything.
- A plan you write counts in full, whether you watch the fight or simulate it. Writing it takes two minutes
  and is the best value in the game.
- If you write nothing, the coach writes one. Its worth depends on coach skill: from close to no plan at all,
  up to about half of a well-read plan. It never reads an opponent as well as you can.
- Watching adds the corner, worth a little more on top. A simulated fight takes your assistant's corner.
- Note the two sizes: coach-run *training* is slightly worse than yours; a coach-written *plan* is clearly
  worse. Training is routine, and the plan is the star.
- So on any night: your plan and your corner is best, your plan and the coach's corner is close, and the
  coach's plan is clearly worse but safe.
- Test: bots on each of the three, over many bouts, in close matchups.

Rule 2: the right fight is not simply the one you will win. Today +5 stats wins 72% and +10 wins 95%, so most
opponents are near-certain one way or the other. If win chance were the only thing, the answer would always be
the easiest legal opponent. So every offer pulls in four directions:
- Rank: beat someone above you and you take their place; beat someone below you and nothing moves. Corner
  King's rankings work this way with no points table, and it is simple to read.
- Growth: soft fights teach little, hard fights teach more, and losses teach most. A padded record leaves a
  fighter unready.
- Money and crowd: purses follow rank and ticket sales. A dull mismatch sells fewer tickets and may cost
  interest.
- The unknown: an unscouted opponent's level is a range, so "+5" may really be level.
- Fights are limited by a promoter's rule: you can ask only within a band of your rank, and a promoter refuses
  if you are not selling or have ducked fights. A refusal always says why.
- Test: a bot that always takes the easiest fight must do clearly worse than one that steps up at the right
  time. An amateur version runs over two game years at rank 4; the full five-year version runs once the pro
  ladder exists. If it fails, matchmaking has failed, as the coach tokens did in S390.

Rule 3: a career ends by choice and decline, never by a timer. Boxing Gym Story's forced retirement of your
best boxer is its most hated rule. Age and damage slow a fighter and raise the risk, and the ledger gives a
forecast. You and the fighter decide when to stop. Only a medical refusal at the far end can force it, with
plenty of warning.

Rule 4: the plan must be a puzzle, not a lookup. If scouting shows a weakness and the plan simply answers it,
a 30-point swing is free, and that is a solved choice.
- Scouting shows part of the picture, never all of it. Habits stay hidden until seen.
- A plan can only use what your fighter has drilled. Camp time is short, so you cannot answer everything, and
  a line built on a raw move works less well.
- Opponents adjust. A line that works early loses its edge if you lean on it, so the corner has something to
  do.
- Lines compete: staying long costs you inside, and pressing costs energy for later rounds.
- Test: a bot that only reads the scouting report and answers it must fall clearly short of a bot that also
  weighs drills, energy and adjustment. If it does not, the plan is a lookup, and rank 3 is not done.

5.3b New problems at each stage
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Endless is not the same as fresh. Long saves in Pocket Stables go stale, and late games in Pound for Pound and
Boxing School turn into clicking. Mobile Boxing Manager's champions run out of opponents. So each stage of a
club asks a different question.

* Years 1-2: survive
    The question: Can we pay the rent?
    What makes it hard: Dues and classes are the income, and one fighter's stipend already costs more than
      they bring. First fights, first loss

* Years 3-5: step up
    The question: Who is ready to turn pro, and when?
    What makes it hard: Too soon wrecks a career. Pros cost more than they earn at first. Two camps collide

* Years 6-10: hold on
    The question: Can we keep what we built?
    What makes it hard: Bigger gyms poach at contract time; kept promises are your defence. Mandatory
      defences. A risky payday against a safe build-up

* Years 10+: rebuild
    The question: Who comes next?
    What makes it hard: Your star declines, and one should stop but will not. Former boxers become coaches.
      Rival gyms have caught up

Three more rules for the late game:
- Members stay the heart of the club. 40 members at $35 is $1,400 a month, which a title purse dwarfs, so dues
  alone cannot carry the identity late on. So members also become the talent pipeline: spending on classes and
  a youth section raises the chance your next unknown comes from your own club. The best prospects cost
  nothing if you built the club that grew them.
- Stars earn less for the gym as they grow. They renegotiate, and the gym's share falls from 35%. Big fighters
  also want better staff and camps. Money stays tight for longer.
- The ladder never empties. Challengers are generated to keep every ranking full, weight changes are possible,
  and rival gyms keep developing talent.

These must be checked in 20-year runs: a strong club should still have bad years, and a champion should always
have a credible next fight.

How eight fighters get paid for. Today one amateur on a $250 stipend already costs more than the opening
income of about $590 a week, and eight would cost $2,000. So the stable cannot be eight stipends:
- Most fighters cost little. Real amateurs pay dues and train; a stipend is a special deal for one or two top
  prospects.
- Pros pay their way, or do not. They live on purses, the gym takes its share, and a pro who cannot sell
  tickets is a decision to make.
- Income grows with the club. Members, evening classes, sponsors and ticket cuts all rise with club standing.
- The roster limit is partly money. Places open with staff and upgrades, so a big stable has to be earned.
- These are shapes, not numbers. The balance must come from real club figures and be checked in the 20-year
  runs before any screen shows eight fighters.

First sizes to try (starting guesses, to be checked against real boxing before any number goes in, by our
real-world rule): four divisions at first; about 20 ranked pros in each, with more below; four belt levels;
fighters from about 18 to 38; new arrivals each year to match retirements.

5.8 P12 in detail: the coach at the start
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The problem. The operator finds the coach ugly, and the corner films show why: he fills the frame in the
game's best moment. Today the corner coach is one fixed staff body, and "the coach" you hire is a name on a
list. The player has no face and no past.

A character generator is not the answer. I agree with the verdict already given. Bodies are finished
MetaHumans with no morphing at run time, and MetaHuman is the only allowed source. A generator would be a
large job for something reviewers do not score.

How other games handle it (read this session; small sample, details in the research notes):

* Football Manager
    What you pick: Look, past level, badges, styles, traits; can be skipped
    Changes play?: Yes: reputation, attributes, job level

* Motorsport Manager
    What you pick: Name, look, one of five backstories
    Changes play?: Backstory only, though sources disagree on the numbers

* NBA 2K25 MyGM
    What you pick: Five backstories or custom

Does it matter to a 9/10? Hardly. No review we opened scored a game up or down for this. Boxing Club Manager
has the choice at 57%. Corner King has a full creator at 90%, and in the top reviews nobody mentions it. It is
a minor nicety for the score, so our cards need not match its creator. It is worth doing for two other
reasons: it costs little, and it replaces a character the owner dislikes in the scene that sells the game.

The proposal.
- First step of a new career, under 30 seconds, and skippable. Quick start takes the first card.
- Type a name. Pick one of four coaches on picture cards.
- Each background gives one clear edge, small enough not to decide a career: Ex-pro (one extra corner call a
  fight), Amateur coach (better walk-ins), Strength coach (slightly better training), Old hand (cheaper camps,
  closer weight cuts). No option without a bonus: a Motorsport Manager guide calls that one "stupid".
- Pick a start: small club, in debt with one good boxer, or rich with big expectations. This doubles as
  difficulty, which reviewers praised in Motorsport Manager's team choice. Corner King players mention the
  same thing.
- You are the head coach in the corner. Luke Reid becomes your first assistant, which also fits P5.

Size. The step itself is S and can go into the first hour (rank 5), using whatever pictures we have. The four
well-made corner bodies are M of art: new or reworked MetaHumans, rendered through the existing pipeline, at
rank 14 with the fight view. The coach cards in the mock-up use boxers' faces as stand-ins, and they are
labelled so.

5.9 Corner King: our closest live rival
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Read from its installed files, in our own words. It is in Early Access, sells for about the same as we plan
to, and is updated often, so this list will age.

What it does better than us today

* The whole career exists
    What it has: 9 weight classes, about 1,450 pros, 75 rival gyms, 15 organisations from regional to world,
      25 ranks a class, title fights, mandatories, unification

* Booking
    What it has: Request a fight, a named opponent can refuse with a reason, negotiate purse and share, lead
      times, rematches

* Training
    What it has: Trainer fit: pairing coach and fighter is a real choice, with personality, specialty and
      overload

* People
    What it has: Relationships that change training, contract acceptance shown as a percentage, poaching with
      a counter-offer and a buyout

* A big world feels near
    What it has: Everyone is scored by closeness to you, and rivals drift towards your divisions

* First hour
    What it has: 7 starts as difficulty, 11 coach-led tasks with cash rewards, 7 first-use tutorials

* Trust basics
    What it has: Autosave choice, text scale 75-200%, 6 languages, Handbook, named saves, global search, back
      and forward, Simulate Days, editor

* Colour
    What it has: Two voiced commentators, 3D press scene, face creator, 56 email types, story banners

What we do better

* Why you won
    Us: Real causes, replayed from the dice
    Corner King: Facts and money only: no screen says why

* Authoring a plan
    Us: Playbook, combos, plan lines against this opponent
    Corner King: One pick per break, 3 shouts a round. It owns the punches

* Fighters differ in the ring
    Us: Traits, habits, signature moves, styles
    Corner King: 8 numbers, one shared brain, no styles

* Stakes
    Us: The gym can close
    Corner King: No failure state in the code

* Mood matters on the night
    Us: Morale is read in the fight
    Corner King: Happiness never reaches the fight

* A real club
    Us: Members, dues, evening classes, grassroots
    Corner King: Income from fighters, reputation and purses; amateurs are a scouting feed

* Amateur road
    Us: Played: bouts, nationals, a career
    Corner King: Simulated around you

* Speed
    Us: Monday stop, one click for a quiet week
    Corner King: Continue per day, or Simulate Days

* Fewer places
    Us: 5 rail buttons, one home per job
    Corner King: 17 sections, most decisions buried in email

* Numbers explained
    Us: Hover for every number
    Corner King: Odds shown, never explained

Where we must surpass it
1. Why. Make it undeniable. Their fight is a real-time physics sim, so causes are hard to replay. Ours is
   built from exchanges, so we can report true causes. A copy of our panel would be hard for them, and this is
   our longest lead.
2. The plan. They could add a Why screen, but authoring a plan means a new fight game. Keep this at the
   centre.
3. Styles. Their top complaint is our P17. Two equal fighters must look and fight differently.
4. A world that grows. Their rival fighters appear never to train, so divisions should weaken over years. Ours
   must develop, on a smaller, closer cast.
5. Stakes. Keep the closing gym, and keep money tight for longer than they do.
6. Clarity. Beat their clutter: every decision on the screen it belongs to, none hidden in a mailbox.

What not to chase: their size, their own-promotion mode (the promoter's seat is crowded and it is a big job),
voiced commentary, the face creator, a 14-column table for every fighter in the world.

Ideas to take, as shapes only: trainer fit; take-their-place rankings; refusals that give a reason, as their
sponsor page does; closeness to the player; a Simulate Days that stops at the next pin, which also backs our
quiet-Monday skip; acceptance shown as a percentage. Hidden traits that show themselves over time are also in
their game, so our habits are not as fresh as S390 thought: the fresh part is wearing them down.

A risk I must name. If they fix styles and add a Why screen within a year, our lead narrows to the plan and
the club. That is why the plan and strangers come first: Gate 1 now also answers "is this worth playing
instead of Corner King?" Each tester should play both if the operator agrees.

9. QUESTIONS FOR THE OPERATOR
-----------------------------

Each one is yours. My recommendation comes first.

1. Should the career be open-ended? I recommend yes: a living pro ladder, ageing and retirement, and a save
   that never has to end. It replaces "ends near first pro fights" and the proposed 10-15 hour arc. It costs
   rules and data more than art, but it is the biggest job here: my rough guess is 30 to 45 more working
   sessions before Early Access. Corner King makes this a requirement, not a nicety: a game that stops at the
   pro debut would be compared with one that does not. A yes also lets the cloud helpers start the world rules
   before the stranger test, an exception to the rule that work outside the current gate waits. If the
   strangers fail, that work may be wasted. The alternative is a fixed arc to an area title, which I expect to
   score no higher than 6 to 7.
2. May we have a plain list of gym upgrades? I recommend yes. Bible section 8 rules out facility building.
   This would be a list with prices and plain effects, with no gym floor and no 3D, so money stays a real
   choice.
3. BJJ rule. Your S389 decision stands: BJJ stays. I recommend one rule on top: it shares every system with
   boxing, and if it is not at the same bar when Early Access is ready, it ships in the first update rather
   than holding the game back. Yes or no?
4. Roster limit of 8? I recommend yes, growing from 3 places. The bible says "small roster". Eight is the most
   I believe a person can follow as people.
5. Close the Camp Board card? I recommend closing the token loop, which failed its own test, and keeping
   habits you discover. Its board look lives on in Home.
6. Keep the world in open files so fans can edit it later? I recommend yes. It costs little if done now, and
   TEW and Pro Wrestling Sim players value it highly.
7. Is the positioning right? "Deeper than Corner King, not bigger": a real club, fighters who fight
   differently, your plan, and the reason you won. This is taste, and it shapes the store page.
8. Which proposals in section 5.7 do you want? I recommend all of them, in the build order. The ones I would
   drop first are the press card (P14) and the editor (P16).
9. Choose your coach (P12). I recommend four picture cards and a choice of start, not a generator. It adds
   about 1 session, plus art for four new corner bodies.
10. Budget, still open from the bible: what will you spend in total on capsule, trailer and lawyer?
