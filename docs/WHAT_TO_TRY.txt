FIGHTCAMP - WEEK 6 BUILD, UPDATE 8 (2026-10-30): FIGHT NIGHT LIKE A FILM - THE INTRO AND THE CORNER MINUTE
=====================================================================================================

NEW IN UPDATE 8 (10 minutes: watch one whole fight on Highlights, 1x, and make your call in the corner)
 1. THE INTRO. Before round 1: both boxers in their corners with their teams on the apron; the announcer names each
    (corner, club, record) while she raises a glove and her coach gives a thumbs-up; they walk to the centre and
    touch gloves; "Seconds out"; the bell. About 13 seconds. "Skip to the bell" (top right) jumps to round 1.
 2. THE CORNER MINUTE. After every round that does not end the fight, a real-time minute shot like a film (black bars
    top and bottom, close shots with soft backgrounds): both walk to their corners and sit on the stool; her coach
    gives her water, an ice pack goes on the back of her neck; her coach crouches in front of her, face to face.
 3. YOUR CALL, WHILE THE MINUTE RUNS. When the coach is face to face with her, the corner card comes up (what
    worked, what hurt, his advice) with the three calls. Pick one: the coach says it to her (caption) and the camera
    turns on him. No pick by "Seconds out!" = the coach says his own call. The clock top left counts the minute.
 4. PRO BOUTS: the cutman comes in mid-minute and works on her cheekbone.
 5. THE OTHER CORNER: a short cut-away to the opponent's coach talking down to her.
 6. "SECONDS OUT!": the team climbs out, she stands up, both walk to the centre, the next round starts on your call.
    "Skip to the bell" (top right) skips the rest of the minute at any time.
 Known limits: nobody climbs through the ropes on screen (the team is in place by the cut that shows them); faces do
 not move (no lip sync); no sound yet.

UPDATE 7 (5 minutes: watch one fight on Full, 1x, then book two more against different opponents)
 1. UPSETS HAPPEN. A slightly better boxer now wins about two fights in three, a clearly better one about three in
    four, a much better one nearly nine in ten (real pro records, researched). Before, 5 rating points decided it.
 2. ROUND 3 IS HARDER. An unfit boxer tires through the fight and her punches lose power from round 2 on; the
    corner call and the scout's notes still matter.
 3. BODY SHOTS HURT. A body shot that lands folds her at the waist, knees give, hands drop, then back to guard. The
    next punch no longer wipes the reaction out (before, most body-shot reactions were cut off within 0.3 s).
 4. QUICKER, SMOOTHER REACTIONS. A hit boxer reacts on the blow, then eases back into her guard from where she is,
    not by snapping to the same start pose.
 5. SLIPS GO THE RIGHT WAY. She slips a straight punch to the outside and rolls under a hook in the way it travels.
    A blocked body shot is taken on the elbow, gloves still up.
 6. A SLIP NOW MOVES HER. Your note: "sidestep ... returns back to the same point". The slip's step now stays taken:
    she ends about 20 cm to the side, so the pair turns to a new angle. After a weave she steps off the line the
    same way. Look at the corner posts behind them: they shift after a slip.
 7. FEET STAY PUT. When the boxers circle, step in or out, or close the gap, their feet no longer skate across the
    canvas. A foot stays where it stands and takes a quick small step when the body has moved off it. Watch their
    boots while they circle.

FIGHTCAMP - WEEK 6 BUILD, UPDATE 6 (2026-10-29): A REAL BLOCK
==============================================================

UPDATE 6 (2 minutes: watch one fight on Full, 1x, look at the moments a jab lands on her gloves)
 1. THE BLOCK IS A COVER-UP. When a punch is blocked, the boxer now brings both gloves up to her temples and goes
    straight back to her guard. Before, she lifted her back glove straight up above her head for most of a second.
    (Measured: every block clip in the motion library did that; the cover-up keeps the glove at temple height.)

WHAT WE MEASURED THIS UPDATE (your question from update 5: "is round 3 harder?")
 - No, not yet. Over thousands of simulated bouts she wins round 3 MORE often than round 1, even when unfit: the
   corner call (+1) and the opponent fading outweigh tiredness, because tiredness only costs her when energy falls
   under 20 and in three rounds it never does. A fix is coming to you as a choice, with the numbers.
 - A bout is decided almost entirely by ratings: 5 points better wins ~100%, 5 points worse ~0%. Real boxing has
   more upsets than that; the real favourite win rates are being researched before we change anything.

NEW: COMBOS AND ENERGY (your "build combos" + "combos should also be associated with energy")
 1. FIGHTERS > Training: a new COMBO row - pick the combination she drills on the pads this week. Only combos whose
    every punch is already a weapon can be picked. Her page > Attributes and moves has the whole COMBO BOOK (10 real
    combos: 1-2, 1-2-3, hook to the body then the head...); locked ones say which punch they still need.
 2. A combo becomes USABLE in about three weeks of pad time (a four-punch one takes about twice as long); the news
    tells you. It is never sharper than her weakest punch in it.
 3. In the fight her exchanges are her real combos, her best ones most often, and a well-drilled combo lands
    quicker. Each learned combo adds a small edge to her rounds.
 4. ENERGY. Combos cost energy, longer ones more; her conditioning decides how fast she tires and how much the
    minute's rest gives back. Between rounds the corner screen shows both boxers' energy at the bell. "Calm her
    down" saves energy, "Go to the body" spends more. A tired boxer throws shorter combos, gets less from them and,
    nearly empty, loses power. The energy numbers are first estimates; real sport-science numbers are being researched.
