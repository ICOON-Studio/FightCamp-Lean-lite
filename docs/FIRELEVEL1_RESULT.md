## Five sentences

1. **Sharp is worth 1.8 points over usable under shares A** (39.8% vs 38.0%), and **signature is worth 2.7 points over sharp** (42.5% vs 39.8%). Under shares B, sharp is worth 2.8 points over usable, and signature is worth 2.7 points over sharp.

2. **The rule makes an UNDRILLED plan worse than today's game by 3.3 to 4.3 points** (a usable line at mastery 35 wins 38.0% or 37.0% vs today's 41.3%). This is a risk for new players: a fighter who has only just learned a combination (mastery 30-59) will hesitate when throwing it, losing 3-4 points of win rate compared to today's game where planned lines always fire.

3. **The 1-1 (double jab) is the line worth sharpening most** - it is the only combo in the GOOD plan, and the only line whose mastery can be set below 60. The other three lines (step_back, pivot_out, tie_up) are steps/defences that always fire at signature (mastery 100).

4. **The design keeps the rule if sharp is worth at least 3 points over usable and signature at least 1.5 over sharp.** Sharp is worth 1.8 (A) or 2.8 (B) over usable - only B meets the 3-point threshold. Signature is worth 2.7 over sharp in both A and B - this meets the 1.5-point threshold. The rule partially meets the criteria.

5. **What I could not measure:** the probe only tests one opponent (Maria Ortega, pressure fighter) and one plan (GOOD). Different opponents and plans may shift the numbers. The probe also sets all combos to the same mastery; in a real camp, different combos have different masteries, and the interaction between them is not measured here.
